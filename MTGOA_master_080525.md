Waking Up To your Superpower
What Is an Allyship Superpower?
Opening Hook: What Makes You, You?
Imagine, dear reader, the tender chaos of fifth grade: a time when your chief concerns include dodgeball survival, obtaining the perfect gel pen, and avoiding the existential dread of multiplication tables. Now, imagine this fragile ecosystem shattered. One day, you’re reigning supreme in Georgia, master of your universe, the next, you’ve been unceremoniously catapulted to Wichita, Kansas—a flat expanse of baffling Midwestern niceness. To your 10-year-old mind, this is akin to being marooned on a hostile alien planet, one where you know not the customs nor the secret handshake. What would you do? (And no, running away to join a circus isn’t as viable as it sounds. I checked.) As for me, I decided to adapt. I infiltrated a math class meant for older students, became a self-taught saxophone virtuoso (well, “virtuoso” is what my mom called it), and joined a LEGO Robotics team despite my total lack of mechanical expertise. In hindsight, this wasn’t mere childhood ingenuity—it was my origin story. These escapades forged what I now call my allyship superpower: the ability to connect with people in the unlikeliest places and thrive amidst chaos.


And so, my friend, this chapter is your invitation to uncover your superpower: that peculiar, dazzling cocktail of skills, quirks, and life experiences that make you the ally the world didn’t know it desperately needed.


Defining an Allyship Superpower


First things first: An allyship superpower is not the kind of thing that comes with a cape, a tragic love interest, or the ability to summon thunderstorms at will (unfortunately). It’s far simpler—and far more profound. It’s the sum of your unique gifts, born from the crucible of your life’s triumphs and calamities.
What Makes a Superpower?
1. Innate Potential: Your superpower is part of who you are, like a knack for empathy or an inexplicable ability to win at Clue without breaking a sweat.
2. Honed by Experience: Life, being the wily teacher it is, ensures that your most valuable skills are sharpened by misadventures, failures, and the occasional triumph.
3. Activated Through Playstyle: Your superpower comes to life through your unique way of interacting with the world—your personal “playstyle,” if you will.

Examples of Allyship Superpowers
Let’s demystify this with a few examples, shall we?
* Bridge-Building: Moving to Wichita required me to connect with people wildly unlike myself, turning awkward handshakes into unlikely alliances.
* Strategic Wizardry: A childhood of Dungeons & Dragons and Magic: The Gathering prepared me to think ten steps ahead—a skill that has served me well in allyship campaigns and navigating IKEA.
* Adaptability: Saxophone practice, rogue math classes, and a robotics team taught me how to thrive in places where I was definitely not on the guest list.

The Role of Backstory
Every superhero has an origin story. Batman wouldn’t be Batman without the pearls on the pavement. Spider-Man wouldn’t be Spider-Man without Uncle Ben’s sage wisdom (and tragic demise). And you, dear reader, wouldn’t be you without your backstory—a treasure trove of awkward missteps, bold leaps, and the occasional faceplant.
Why Your Story Matters
Your life is a breadcrumb trail leading to your superpower. Each challenge you’ve faced, community you’ve joined, and unexpected twist has shaped the ally you’re becoming.

Key Takeaway
Here’s the plot twist: You already have an allyship superpower. It’s not hidden in a faraway cave, nor does it require ancient spells to activate. It’s been with you all along. The trick is recognizing it, like a plot twist in a novel you almost saw coming.
In the next section, we’ll dive deeper into identifying your unique gifts. But for now, start with this delightful truth: You are already equipped.


Imagine, if you will, that you’re a wizard. But not the kind who waves a wand and yells “Expelliarmus!” in crowded duels. No, your magic is quieter, subtler—like knowing the precise moment to speak up in a meeting or sensing exactly how to make someone feel seen. Your magic lies not in pyrotechnics but in precision. You wield your gifts with the grace of an alchemist, transforming chaos into clarity, obstacles into opportunities.
This is what an allyship superpower is: a unique skill that feels almost effortless to you but creates seismic shifts for others. It’s the thing you do naturally—almost absentmindedly—that makes someone else’s impossible task suddenly manageable. Think of it like this: if you can do something in an hour that spares someone nine months of struggle, you’re not just helpful. You’re wielding leverage so potent it might as well be magic.
But let’s be honest—calling it a superpower sounds lofty, and lofty things tend to make people uncomfortable.
“Me? A superpower? Surely not,” you might think, clutching your modesty like an old coat. Let’s pause here, because this resistance is important. The very idea that you could possess something rare and useful—that you might already have everything you need to be effective—can awaken all kinds of inner grumbles.
So, before we go further, let’s confront these voices of doubt.
________________




Meeting the Inner Resistance


The first voice you’ll hear might sound like this:
“I’m just an ordinary person. What if I don’t have a superpower?”
Ah, the humble underdog. It’s tempting to believe that extraordinary impact belongs to extraordinary people—Nobel Prize winners, TikTok activists with perfect lighting, or someone whose memoir Oprah has already blurbed. But here’s the thing: superpowers aren’t flashy. They’re the quiet, unassuming tools that turn your effort into exponential impact. The question isn’t whether you have one. It’s whether you’re willing to see it.
Then there’s the cynic:
“Okay, let’s say I do have a superpower. But what if it’s not enough? What if it doesn’t matter?”
This voice is the boulder in your path. It whispers that unless you can dismantle patriarchy single-handedly or solve climate change before lunch, your contributions are meaningless. But hear me: the cynic is wrong. Your superpower doesn’t need to be everything to everyone—it just needs to be something to someone. Change is not made by grand gestures but by countless, small moments of leverage. You are not the whole pulley system; you are one gear in a beautifully complex machine.
Finally, we meet the perfectionist:
“What if I try to use my superpower and fail?”
Ah, the perfectionist, with their white-knuckled grip on fear. Failure, they believe, is the enemy. But failure is not the villain of this story—it’s the mentor. Every superhero origin involves at least one moment of dramatic miscalculation, usually involving a rooftop and questionable physics. Your missteps are not the end of your journey; they’re the beginning of wisdom.
________________




Superpowers Are Rooted in Leverage


Now that we’ve quieted those voices (or at least taken them out for tea), let’s talk about leverage. Superpowers are not about brute force—they’re about precision. Without leverage, allyship becomes a Sisyphean task: exhausting, endless, and discouraging. You show up to protests, share posts, and sign petitions, but it feels like you’re flinging pebbles at a mountain.
Your superpower is the leverage that moves the boulder. It’s not about how hard you push; it’s about finding the exact spot where your unique gifts create the greatest impact. Maybe you’re the connector who turns a room of strangers into a web of allies. Maybe you’re the storyteller who can distill complicated truths into something clear and compelling. Or maybe you’re the strategist who anticipates challenges before they even arise.
Take my superpower, for instance. I build communities. Drop me in a room full of strangers, and by the end of the day, there will be laughter, inside jokes, and possibly a group chat. For someone else, this might take years. For me, it’s second nature. That’s the essence of a superpower—it’s the thing you can do with ease that saves others time, effort, and heartache.
________________




The Shortcut to Mastery


By now, you’ve probably heard the 10,000-hour rule—the idea that mastery requires a decade of focused practice. If you’re starting to think about allyship as an adult, this might feel discouraging. “Ten years?” you might groan. “I don’t even know what I’m having for dinner.”
Here’s the good news: you don’t need 10,000 hours to start making an impact. Your superpower is the shortcut. It’s something you’ve already spent years developing, often without realizing it. By leaning into it, you skip the trial and error and go straight to creating leverage.
For example, if your superpower is storytelling, you don’t need to master spreadsheets or logistics. You can use your voice to amplify others’ stories, inspire action, and shift narratives right now. Superpowers let you skip the overwhelm and go straight to what works.
________________




The Power of Playstyle


Your superpower doesn’t exist in a vacuum. It comes alive through your playstyle—the way you naturally engage with the world. Are you a strategist who loves crafting clever plans? A builder who turns ideas into reality? A healer who creates safe spaces for others? Your playstyle is the framework that makes your superpower shine.
As a kid, I spent countless hours playing Magic: The Gathering and Dungeons & Dragons. I thought I was just having fun. But in reality, I was sharpening strategic thinking skills—learning to anticipate outcomes, navigate chaos, and collaborate. These same skills became the foundation of my allyship. Your playstyle might already be revealing itself in the activities you lose yourself in. That’s where your superpower lives.

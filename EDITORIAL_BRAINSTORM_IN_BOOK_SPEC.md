## Why this editorial spec exists

The Brainstorming skill has been fully specified at three layers:
1. **Manuscript spec** (what the skill IS) — `SPEC_BRAINSTORM_FORMULA_STEP.md`
2. **Skill** (how a player runs it) — `Skills/brainstorming/SKILL.md`
3. **Keyterm** (how it links to the rest of the system) — `KEYTERM-BRAINSTORMING.md`

What is **missing**: a voice-protocol-cleared, line-number-verified,
per-chapter INSERTION PLAN for how the skill lands in the manuscript.
This spec is that plan.

**Voice protocol gate:** Before any per-chapter edit, the writer
(Wendell) and the gatekeeper (this spec) confirm three things:
- (a) the per-chapter insertion does not break any existing line
  in the live manuscript;
- (b) the voice matches the chapter's existing register
  (Shaman-somatic for Ch 1, Challenger-incising for Ch 3, etc.);
- (c) Move 6/7 personal material is **not** generated by the agent —
  any first-person content is line-anchored to existing prose or
  marked `TODO: Wendell`.

**Until this spec is approved, no manuscript edits happen.**

## Three Placement Options (chosen: B-2)

### Option A — Standalone Appendix

Add an `APPENDIX B: BRAINSTORMING THE ALLYSHIP MOVE` to the end of
the book, with the full 3-stage skill, the formula mapping, and one
worked example per formula move. Existing chapters get a one-line
cross-reference at the end of each formula section.

**Pros:** Lowest disruption to existing drafts. All skill content
in one navigable place. Player can return to it.

**Cons:** A 14-day reader is unlikely to flip to the appendix
during the heat of a Ch 2 ("identify your superpower") exercise.
The skill *should* live where the work happens.

### Option B — Embedded in Each Chapter (chosen)

Each of the 4 formula-move chapters (Ch 2, Ch 3, Ch 4, Ch 6) gets a
**"Brainstorming This Move"** subsection that runs the 3 stages
against that chapter's superpower, with chapter-native voice and
examples. Plus Ch 1 (Shaman) gets a **"Why You Brainstorm Before
You Move"** framing section that introduces the practice *before*
the first formula move is attempted.

**Pros:** The practice lands where the work happens. The reader
meets it before they need it (Ch 1) and uses it where they need
it (Ch 2, 3, 4, 6). The skill becomes part of the muscle, not
an afterthought.

**Cons:** More work, more resonance, more risk of voice drift.
Mitigated by the voice protocol gate above and by per-chapter
worked examples written in the chapter's face-voice.

### Option C — Hybrid (A's appendix + B's Ch 1 framing)

Appendix is the full reference. Ch 1's framing section is the
entry. Per-chapter inserts are light (1-paragraph cross-references
only, no embedded skill definition).

**Pros:** Player has both a reference (appendix) and an entry
(Ch 1). Per-chapter inserts stay minimal.

**Cons:** Reader still has to flip to the appendix mid-practice.
Less embedded than B.

### Decision: **B-2** (embedded, with appendix as the reference)
This is the **most-bang-for-buck** option. The skill is taught
*as the work is done*, in the chapter where the work is done. The
appendix serves as the deep-reference for readers who want to
audit the full skill definition.

## Quadrant work — what this changes

**Before locks:** The book leaned on a 2-quadrant distinction (strategic vs emotional) with no theoretical grammar. The new framing is **4-quadrant AQAL**, with the **left/right axis** doing the load-bearing work in the book:

| Quadrant | Pronoun (Wilber) | Domain | What it holds | The book's metaphor |
|----------|------------------|--------|---------------|---------------------|
| **Upper-Left (UL)** | I | Intentional / subjective | Felt sense, signal, voice | **The Forest** (interior, what the player meets alone) |
| **Upper-Right (UR)** | It | Behavioral / objective | Token economy, body, output | (implicit; the body's bookkeeping of the work) |
| **Lower-Left (LL)** | We | Cultural / intersubjective | Field, role, shared meaning | **The Village** (collective, what the player meets with others) |
| **Lower-Right (LR)** | Its | Social / interobjective | Systems, structures, institutions | (implicit; the institutions the village is built from) |

**The book only uses the left/right axis.** Upper vs lower is held as author-knowledge, not reader-knowledge. The two metaphors that carry the axis:

- **The Forest** = upper-quadrant work (UL + UR). The interior life. The Shaman. The 8 Gates. The 321. The signal-check before woolgathering.
- **The Village** = lower-quadrant work (LL + LR). The collective life. The Diplomat. The Regent. The Architect. The marketplace. The barn raising.

**Why the upper/lower split is held back:** Upper-quadrant work is the "I" and "it" of the player. Lower-quadrant work is the "we" and "its" of the community. The book's tools already live in the right quadrants:
- Brainstorming = LL (we, cultural — a shared practice; the borrowed-genius mode is *explicitly* intersubjective).
- 321 = UL (I, intentional — the inquiry).
- Barn Raising = LL + LR (we + its — the collective + the institutions).
- Token economy = UR (it, behavioral — the body's accounting).

The reader does not need the 4-quadrant grammar to use the book. They need the **left/right** axis to understand that the Forest and the Village are *different kinds of work*, and the book's tools are split accordingly.

**The Ch 1 framing section teaches:**

1. There are two kinds of allyship problem: **what you meet alone** (the Forest) and **what you meet with others** (the Village).
2. The same name describes both because both are called "allyship," but the moves, the tools, the failure modes, and the energy economy are different.
3. The book teaches the tools for both sides. Chapter 1 introduces the distinction; chapters 2–6 teach the Forest tools; chapter 4–6 teach the Village tools (with barn raising as the canonical Village tool).

**The Appendix B callout restates the framing** in the reader's hand at the back of the book.

## Per-Chapter Insertion Plan

### Ch 1 (Shaman) — Add "Village vs. Forest: The Two Kinds of Allyship Problem"

**Insertion point:** End of §3 (Forest definition), before §4 (Origin Story). ~400-600 words. This is a **Shaman-voice** insert.

**Content:**

1. **The framing:** There are two kinds of allyship work — the kind you do *alone* (in the Forest) and the kind you do *with others* (in the Village). Most books teach only the Village kind. This book teaches both, and the move between them.
2. **The left/right quadrant shorthand:** When the work is "what I am willing to feel, name, and stand for," that's the Forest. When the work is "what we are willing to build, ask for, and hold each other inside," that's the Village. (No AQAL terminology in the prose — the metaphor carries the content.)
3. **The diagnostic question:** "If I had a perfect plan, would I be able to do it tomorrow morning?" Yes → Forest work. No → Village work. (Cross-reference to `KEYTERM-VILLAGE-VS-FOREST` and the Strategic vs Emotional problem distinction.)
4. **The felt difference:** Forest work is alone, internal, slow. Village work is collective, external, fast. The body knows the difference before the mind names it.
5. **The 3 brainstorming stages introduced as one tool that works in both places:** Woolgathering (alone or in borrowed genius), Clean Up (winnow + salvage), Bridge Building (sequence the milestones). The 321 panel is the canonical Forest practice; the marketplace (Appendix B) is the canonical Village practice; the brainstorm is the practice that *moves between them*.
6. **The Shaman's signal-check:** Before any woolgathering, a few breaths. The Forest requires you to *arrive* in your body before you generate. The Village requires the same — arrive in the field, then ask.
7. **The Shaman's promise:** *What shows up in the brainstorm IS the material, including the part you wanted to throw out.* This is true in both the Forest and the Village.
8. **One-line cross-reference:** *For the full skill definition, see Appendix B.*

**Voice anchor:** Shaman's existing somatic register (L19, L43, L77, L83 of `CHAPTER1_FULL_DRAFT.md`).

## Ch 4 Regent and Ch 5 Architect — Add Barn Raising Section (full)

**Why both chapters:** The Barn Raising practice is **collective show up**. The Regent holds the *tradition* (the kind of support that lasts). The Architect designs the *system* (the match between resources and needs). Both are needed; both are full sections, not paragraphs.

### Ch 4 (Regent) — "The Long Table: Barn Raising as Inheritance"

**Insertion point:** After the main Regent teaching (tradition, stewardship, the long arc), before the reflection prompts. ~800-1000 words.

**Content:**

1. **The framing:** "The ally who can keep showing up after the first year is the ally who knows how to ask for what they need and offer what they have. That's barn raising — the Amish tradition of a community showing up to build one family's barn in a day, then moving to the next family next season." (Lineage: Marshall Ganz's organizing model; the book uses the metaphor, not the academic source.)
2. **The two moves the Regent teaches:**
   - **Asking with the long view.** Not "can you do this for me this week" but "would you be willing to be the person I call in three years when this comes up again." The Regent teaches the ask that *inherits* the relationship.
   - **Offering with the long view.** Not "let me help you with this one thing" but "here is the role I am willing to play in your work for as long as you want me in it." The Regent teaches the offer that *becomes* tradition.
3. **The 4-step barn raising protocol** (per `Skills/barn-raising/SKILL.md`):
   - **Step 1 — Name the project.** The barn, not the roof. The big thing the community is building.
   - **Step 2 — Take inventory of the people.** Who has what? Who needs what? Match by what fits, not by what's available.
   - **Step 3 — Make the asks and offers visible.** A shared doc, a marketplace listing, a wall in the meeting room. The visibility is the trust.
   - **Step 4 — Show up and re-show up.** The barn is built in a day, but the tradition is built in a lifetime. The Regent's move is *being the person who comes back next season*.
4. **Worked example:** A borrowed-genius session with a community organizer Wendell admires, showing the long-game framing. (The same organizer example as Ch 4's brainstorm insert — the brainstorm surfaces the *who*; the barn raising builds the *how*.)
5. **Cross-reference to Appendix B for the full skill, and to Ch 5 for the system design side.**

**Voice anchor:** Ch 4's Regent voice — tradition, inheritance, the long arc. "In five years, this ally will still be here. That's what the Regent is for."

### Ch 5 (Architect) — "The Match Engine: Barn Raising as System"

**Insertion point:** After the main Architect teaching (systems, leverage, the next-step task), before the reflection prompts. ~800-1000 words.

**Content:**

1. **The framing:** "The Regent teaches *who* shows up. The Architect teaches *how* the show-up is matched. Barn raising is a system, not a feeling. The system is what makes the feeling last." (Lineage: the marketplace audit — see `MTGOA-MARKETPLACE-ALLYSHIP-AUDIT.md`.)
2. **The 4 architectural moves:**
   - **The data model.** Every ask and every offer carries: the move it serves, the face it leans toward, the EA channel it requires, the resource category it needs. No un-specced listings. (Challenger's rule; Diplomat's draft-state compromise.)
   - **The match surface.** A marketplace page that shows the asks and the offers side by side, filtered by move + face + channel + resource. The reader doesn't search — they *recognize* a match.
   - **The campaign scaffold.** Asks and offers are scoped to allyship campaigns, not just to the open marketplace. The campaign is the *project*; the marketplace is the *workforce*. (This is the same shape as the existing bars-engine campaign marketplace — the new AllyshipCampaign model composes on top of the existing PlayerCampaign model.)
   - **The 90-min smallest version.** Every campaign has a 90-minute entry point. The architect's job is to design the system such that the first action is doable in a sitting. (Cross-reference to Brainstorming Stage 3.)
3. **Worked example:** The "White women's allyship skill-up, Bay Area, 2026" campaign, with 5 asks (curriculum help, space, facilitation, introductions, emotional labor) and 5 offers (one per face). The Architect's role is to make the *match* visible.
4. **Cross-reference to Appendix B for the full skill, and to Ch 4 for the tradition side.**

**Voice anchor:** Ch 5's Architect voice — systems, leverage, the next-step task. "The Regent keeps showing up. The Architect designs the room they show up in."

### Why both chapters (and not a single chapter or appendix)

- The **Regent holds the *why*** (the long arc, the inheritance, the tradition).
- The **Architect holds the *how*** (the system, the data, the match).
- Both are needed. A tradition without a system collapses; a system without a tradition is empty.
- The reader who has read Ch 4 and Ch 5 knows *why* they should barn-raise and *how* to build the surface. The reader who has read only one has half a practice.

## Strategic vs. Emotional Problems Callout

**Insertion point:** Ch 1's "Why You Brainstorm Before You Move"
insert, plus Appendix B's stage mapping section.

**Content:**
- Strategic problems have a known goal and unknown path.
  Brainstorming produces the path. Stage 1 + Stage 3 are the
  load-bearing stages.
- Emotional problems have a known signal (dissatisfaction,
  resistance, fear) and an unknown relationship to the goal.
  Brainstorming surfaces the signal and finds the bridge.
  Stage 1 + Stage 2 are the load-bearing stages.
- MTGOA helps with both. The skill is the same. The order of
  the stages changes.

**Cross-reference:** `KEYTERM-STRATEGIC-VS-EMOTIONAL-PROBLEMS.md`
(the new keyterm drafted 2026-06-08).

## App-Surface Decision (A vs C) — for Wendell

The stubs above point to `wendellbritt.zo.space/marketplace`
as the live URL. Two ways to set up that page *today*:

### Option A — Static "Coming Soon" Page (10 min)

A single zo.space page route that says:

> **The Mastering Allyship marketplace is coming soon.**
> This is where your brainstormed superpower, your asks, your
> offers, and your co-show-up commitments will live.
> *[email capture: notify me when it opens]*

No email capture, no list. Just acknowledgment. Cheapest to
ship; doesn't build the early-access list.

### Option C — Coming Soon + Email Capture (30 min)

Same page as A, plus a single email-capture field wired to a
new `pending_marketplace_emails` table in bars-engine (or a
simpler DropBox / Notion database if we don't want to commit
to the schema yet).

**Why C matters:** The 30-day push is in the spec but the
marketplace is a *Phase 2* deliverable (post-launch). An
email list of readers who clicked "I want this" is *gold* for
the 30-day push — it identifies the readers who finished the
book and want the next move.

**Cost:** 30 min to set up the page + table. One email a month
to the list ("we're building it; here's a sneak peek"). No
product risk.

### My recommendation: **C**

- Builds the early-access list with zero product risk.
- The 30-day push needs this list to convert book-readers to
  marketplace-users.
- The page is one route; the email-capture is one form; the
  table is one schema.
- The whole thing is reversible (we can change the page, the
  table, the email cadence) without touching the book.

### What I would NOT recommend

- **B (pointing to masteringallyship.com directly)** — the
  site isn't deployed yet, and pointing readers to a
  non-existent URL breaks the trust the book has built.
- **Skipping the page entirely** — readers hit a 404 when
  they look for the marketplace, and the book loses its
  promise of "the next move is here."

# 6-Face GM Analysis — Should the deck sections go to the back?

## *Optimising for Jordan's reading ergonomics: what moves, what breaks, and how it folds back*

**Created:** 2026-08-01
**Question:** B-plus assumed the six `## Your Twenty Cards` sections relocate to an appendix with the ch3 teach retained. Before executing: what does Jordan lose, and what is the ergonomic way to fold it back?
**Companion:** `MOVES_VS_STAGES_6FACE_ANALYSIS.md`, `CH5_CH7_MODES_VS_MOVES_ANALYSIS.md`

---

## The finding that reframes the question

**The sections are misnamed.** `## Your Twenty Cards` is about the cards for roughly six lines out of forty-two. Measured on ch5:

| content | lines | is it deck? |
|---|---|---|
| grid intro, the italic line, one Face-specific card observation | ~6 | **yes** |
| `### Drawing Against the Shadow` — the chapter's daemon walked through all five WAVE moves, a named card, and a worked one-rep drill | ~22 | **no** |
| `### From Card to Quest` — the quest formula and a Face-specific worked example | ~10 | **no** |

**Roughly 14% of the section is deck reference. The other 86% is chapter teaching that happens to be delivered through a card.**

Two things in there exist nowhere else in the body:

**1. The daemon's five-move breakdown.** Each chapter's shadow — Skeptic, Fixer-Healer, Emotional Body, Victim, Damaged Self — gets walked through Wake/Open/Clean/Grow/Show with a distinct failure at each. Section 5 introduces the daemon; **this is the only place it is operationalised.** Six chapters, six breakdowns, zero duplication elsewhere.

**2. The quest formula.** *"One line, four things: what you will do, who it reaches, by when, and what it costs you."* Taught once per chapter, six times, each with a Face-specific worked example. `APPENDIX_B_QUESTS_CAMPAIGNS.md` exists (236 lines) but the **formula's first teach and every worked example are in the body**.

So the question is not *should the deck reference move to the back*. It is: **can we move the 14% without taking the 86% with it?**

---

## 🌊 Shaman — Felt sense

**What Jordan actually experiences at this point in a chapter.** She has just finished Section 6, the five moves, the hardest part of the chapter. Then a heading says `Your Twenty Cards` — possessive, concrete, *hers* — and what follows is her own shadow, named, with five specific ways it will beat her. That is the emotional payoff of the chapter, and it is sitting under a heading that promises a product.

**Relocating as-is:** the chapter now ends on the moves and a pointer to the back. The daemon she met in Section 5 never gets its reckoning. The chapter closes on instruction rather than on being seen.

**Shaman verdict:** the felt centre of that section is the daemon breakdown, not the deck. **Move the deck, keep the reckoning.** And rename the heading — it currently promises cards and delivers self-recognition, which undersells it every time.

---

## ⚔️ Challenger — What gets wrong

**The dishonest version of this decision** is relocating the whole section because it is easier to move a block than to split one, then reporting that the deck went to the appendix. What would actually have gone to the appendix is six chapters' shadow work and the book's only teach of the quest formula.

**The other failure is the reverse:** splitting so finely that the appendix receives nothing but the grid diagram, at which point the relocation bought nothing and B-plus was pointless.

**The line:** relocation is only worth doing if what lands in the appendix is genuinely reference — something Jordan looks *up*, not something she reads *through*. The grid is reference. The daemon breakdown is not; nobody looks up their own shadow.

**Challenger verdict:** split the section. If the split turns out to be more than a day's work, that is the price of having bundled three things under one heading, not a reason to move all three.

---

## 🏛 Regent — What to preserve

**Preserve the daemon breakdowns.** Six of them, one per Face, each 5×1 against the WAVE. They are the payoff of every Section 5, and they exist in exactly one place.

**Preserve the quest formula in the body.** Appendix B is 236 lines of quest and campaign material, but the formula's first teach and all six worked examples are in the chapters. A reader who never opens an appendix — which is most readers — currently still learns to write a quest. Move it and that stops being true.

**Preserve the closing drill.** Every chapter ends `*Draw one from your twenty. Run the five moves on something live. Log the BAR, then turn it into one quest with a name and a date in it.*` That line is the chapter's exit instruction and it ties three instruments together.

**What is genuinely safe to move:** the grid explanation, repeated six times with a one-line variation. *"Same grid, one operation further along. Five moves down, four domains across."* Jordan needs that **once**.

**Regent verdict:** the relocation is real but small — it is the repetition that moves, not the section.

## 🧠 Architect — Structure and integrity

The current section bundles three different things under one heading, which is why the relocation question was hard to answer:

```
## Your Twenty Cards
   grid explanation          reference     repeated 6× with variations   → appendix
   Face card observation     teaching      unique per chapter            → stays
   Drawing Against the Shadow  teaching    unique per chapter            → stays
   From Card to Quest        instrument    taught 6×, formula identical  → teach once, then vary
```

**The clean structure Jordan gets afterwards:**

- **ch3 only:** the grid taught once, in full, where the deck is introduced and earned.
- **ch4–ch8:** no grid explanation. The daemon breakdown and the quest keep their place, under a heading that describes them.
- **Appendix:** the grid, the six twenties, and the domain definitions as a lookup surface.

**Coupling win:** the body stops restating a product's contents six times. Re-cut the deck and only the appendix changes.

**Architect verdict:** split, and rename the heading — `Your Twenty Cards` describes the 14%.

---

## 🎭 Diplomat — What bridges

**The bridge at risk is the reader who bought the deck.** Strip all six and the book stops speaking to her at the exact moment she has the cards in her hand. That is why B-plus retained the ch3 teach.

**But there is a second bridge, and it runs the other way:** the reader who has *not* bought the deck. She currently reads six sections headed `Your Twenty Cards` describing a product she does not own, six times, each one telling her the cards *"live in the deck rather than on this page."* Six reminders that the useful thing is elsewhere.

**Both readers are served by the same fix.** Teach the grid once, in ch3, where she can decide. Then let ch4–ch8 be about her shadow and her quest, which need no purchase.

**Diplomat verdict:** split. It is the only option that improves the experience for both readers at once. Keep one in-body pointer per chapter, one line, not a section.

---

## 📖 Sage — Panoramic view

Look at what the chapter's closing arc is *for*, across the whole book.

Every chapter runs: Face → distortion → concept → practice → **daemon** → game/moves → **daemon operationalised** → **quest** → recap. The last three are the landing. The deck is the delivery mechanism, not the content.

The relocation question got framed as *does the deck belong in the body* because the heading says `Your Twenty Cards`. The right frame is: **the chapter's landing has been filed under the name of its packaging.**

Ergonomically, the deepest cost of the current arrangement is not repetition. It is that a reader who decides the deck is not for her — reasonably, six lines in — has been given permission to skip the section where her own shadow gets named.

**Sage verdict:** split, and the rename is not cosmetic. It is the fix. `Your Twenty Cards` is why this looked like a relocation question at all.

## What actually moves

**To the appendix:**
- the grid explanation as taught in ch4–ch8 (five near-identical restatements)
- the six twenties as a lookup table
- domain definitions

**Stays in the body:**
- ch3's full grid teach — once, where the deck is introduced and earned
- all six daemon breakdowns
- all six Face-specific card observations
- the quest formula and its six worked examples
- the closing drill line

**Net:** roughly 5–6 lines out of each of ch4–ch8, ~30 lines total, plus the deck table into the appendix. Small. That is the correct size for this change, and the reason it looked bigger is the heading.

## The ergonomic fold-back

**1. Rename the heading in all six.** It should name the landing, not the packaging. Candidates:

| | heading | note |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | `## Drawing Against Your Shadow` | promotes what is already the section's largest subhead and its actual payload |
| **2** | `## Where This Goes Wrong in You` | plainest; matches the daemon framing from Section 5 |
| **3** | `## One Card, One Quest` | keeps the deck tie and names the exit action |

Recommend **1**. It is the section's own language, it survives the deck moving, and it tells a non-buyer the section is for her.

**2. Replace the five grid restatements with one line each**, pointing back to ch3 and out to the appendix. Not a section, a sentence.

**3. Rewrite the italic line once, in ch3.** It is currently false in five chapters, and after the split it only exists in one — which is the cheapest possible resolution of that defect.

**4. Keep the closing drill verbatim.** It works, it ties BAR + card + quest, and it costs nothing.

## The Diplomat's Through-Line

**Question:** Your capacity to hold yourself in relation.
*Not your capacity to feel (Shaman), not your capacity to push (Challenger), not your capacity to steward (Regent), not your capacity to design (Architect) — your capacity to stay in connection with others AND with yourself at the same time.*

The Diplomat's core fear: that claiming what you need will fracture the connection. That to belong, you must hollow out. That to stay, you must forget what you came with.

## Gate Descriptions (Altitude-Specific)

### Gate 1: The Protector

**The threat:** *Not safe to need something this much. Your need will alienate them. You've already asked for too much — look what happened last time.*

**What it's protecting:** The Diplomat's earliest memory of reaching for connection and having it conditional on remaining small. The Protector learned: if you have needs, you will be left.

**Language at Green:** The Protector doesn't say *you'll be hurt* — it says *you'll be left behind if you're too much.* At Green altitude, the fear is relational: not physical danger, but the loss of belonging.

**The move:** Thank the Protector. Ask permission. Show you're going in with a witness.

---

### Gate 2: The Controller

**The threat:** *If you claim your price, you'll have to leave. And if you leave, you confirm that the connection wasn't real. That you've been performing belonging. That you were always going to leave.*

**What it's protecting:** The Controller learned that expressing needs openly leads to withdrawal. The subtle strategy: make your own needs invisible so the connection stays intact.

**Language at Green:** At Green, the Controller's fear is refined — not crude suppression, but *accommodation without end.* The Controller will volunteer you for things you never agreed to, absorb slights without protest, smooth every conflict by making yourself less.

**The move:** Ask about the cost. Demonstrate you can hold your own needs without the Controller's intervention. Relief, not firing.

---

### Gate 3: The Skeptic

**The threat:** *Is it real? Is the need you're naming actually yours, or are you constructing a grievance to justify leaving? Is your price genuine, or a performance of boundary you've learned to do because you read a book about boundaries?*

**What it's protecting:** The Diplomat's fear of performative allyship — of claiming needs as a style rather than a felt reality. The Skeptic at Green is sophisticated: it's not questioning whether you were hurt, it's questioning whether the hurt is being used as an exit strategy.

**Language at Green:** The Skeptic asks: *Are you actually in pain, or are you performing pain to have a reason to leave a relationship you just don't want anymore?* This is the Diplomat's particular shadow — using the language of needs to avoid genuine engagement rather than to protect it.

**The move:** *What would happen if you trusted that your need is real, not constructed?*

---

### Gate 4: The Fixer

**The threat:** *Before you name your price, fix the relationship. Make it better first. If you name a price now, before the relationship is in better shape, you'll be asking for too much too soon and they'll leave.*

**What it's protecting:** The Diplomat's pattern of converting the internal work (feeling → integrating → naming) into an external project (fix the relationship, then you can ask). The Fixer turns the passage into a delay tactic.

**Language at Green:** At Green altitude, the Fixer is subtle — it doesn't say *don't feel it*, it says *feel it, but later, after we've done the work to make this safe enough.* The Fixer at Green uses the language of readiness: *you can't name your price until we've built enough safety.*

**The move:** There are four more gates. The action is on the other side of them, not before. Give the Fixer a deadline: *after Gate 8, not before.*

---

### Gate 5: Fear

**Gate 5 — Already Alone:** The voice that says *you'll be alone when you name it, because you already are.* The sophisticated Green-level fear: not acute abandonment, but the quiet discovery that the belonging was always provisional.

**The threat (movies):** *If you name your price, they'll hear it as an ultimatum. And they'll be right — it is one. And they'll choose to leave. And you'll have destroyed the very connection you were trying to protect by naming your price. You will have done the thing you were afraid of.*

**What it's protecting:** The catastrophic scenario: that the act of claiming your price will prove you don't actually want the connection — that the price was always too high, and naming it reveals you knew that all along.

**Language at Green:** The Fear at Green is relational and sophisticated. It shows movies not of violence, but of *proved suspicion* — the moment where the other person says *I knew you didn't really want this* and they're right.

**The move:** Name the Fear. Give it a character, a voice, a face. The move is the same as other chapters but the content is specific: the fear that boundary-setting proves you never believed in the relationship.

---

### Gate 6: The Victim

**The threat:** *You stayed too long. You should have named your price earlier. You kept hoping it would get better. You made yourself small and told yourself it was generosity. You have a story about what you sacrificed and what it cost you and the story is true.*

**What it carries:** The accumulated record of all the times the Diplomat stayed past their price and paid for it. The Victim is not wrong — the staying was real, the cost was real.

**Language at Green:** At Green, the Victim uses the language of *complicity* — *I participated in my own erasure. I made myself small and called it allyship. I cannot claim I didn't choose it, even though the choice was made under pressure I didn't name.*

**The move:** Witness without fixing. Let the Victim speak. Don't argue. Don't reframe. *Yes, all of that is true. And I am still going through.*

---

### Gate 7: The Hollowed Self (not "Damaged Self")

**The reframe:** At Green altitude, Gate 7 is not a fixed wound — *I am broken at my core.* It is accumulated damage: *this is what I have done to myself, over and over, to stay in the field.*

**What it shows you:** The record of every time you overrode your own need, said yes when you meant no, made yourself smaller to keep the peace, let your price go unnamed because naming it felt like too much risk. The Hollowed Self carries not a wound but a wearing-away — the slow erosion of your own boundaries through a thousand small surrenders.

**What it's protecting:** The part of you that learned to survive by disappearing. The Hollowed Self learned: *if I don't know what I need, I can't be disappointed when it doesn't get met. If I don't claim a price, I can't be told it's too high.* The survival strategy that worked — hollowing out — has become the damage itself.

**Language at Green:** The Hollowed Self does not say *I am defective.* It says *I don't know what I want anymore. I have been adjusting for so long I have forgotten how to feel my own edges. I look at other people who can hold their price and I don't recognize myself in that. I have been in this relationship so long I don't know who I am outside of it.*

**The move:** Witness without fixing. You don't argue (you're not broken). You don't agree (the hollowing is not who you are — it's what you did to survive). You acknowledge: *yes, this is what it cost. And you can stop now.* The Hollowed Self needed to hollow to survive something. The survival is honored. The hollowing can end.

---

### Gate 8: The Vulnerable Child

**What it's holding:** *I want to be in relationship without hollowing out. I want to name my price and still be wanted. I want to stay AND hold what I came with. I want to belong without disappearing. I want to know what I need.*

**The superpower:** The capacity to stay in relational connection while maintaining full self-hood. Not accommodation, not withdrawal — the rare ability to say *here is my price, and I am still here, and I am not leaving.* The Diplomat's superpower is the fully claimed price that does not sever the connection — the self that stayed intact through the belonging.

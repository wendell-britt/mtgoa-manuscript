# CHAPTER 2: THE SHAMAN
## *Your Superpower Is Already Here — You Just Stopped Trusting It*

---

## Section 1: The Promise

There is a specific kind of person who keeps burning out in allyship work.

They are not lazy. They are not uncaring. They usually care more than most, which is part of the problem. They show up prepared. They read the articles. They attend the trainings. They stay late. They apologize when they are wrong and try harder when they fall short.

And still, after a while, something runs dry.

Not motivation, exactly. Not belief. Something quieter. The capacity to keep going without pretending. The ability to feel the room clearly instead of managing what they perform in it.

This chapter is about that something.

The Effective Allyship Formula starts with a single step: **identify your superpower.** Not your training. Not your good intentions. Your superpower — the specific thing only you can offer, shaped by your specific survival, your specific losses, your specific becoming.

That superpower is not strategic. It is not manufactured. It is emotional.

And most people in allyship work have been cutting themselves off from it for years.

---

## Section 2: The Diagnosis

Here is what the village does when it exiles emotional literacy:

It does not stop feeling. It starts managing.

Fear becomes something to push through. Anger becomes righteous fuel, stripped of what it was trying to say about a violated line. Sadness becomes a productivity hazard, reframed as "lessons learned" and bypassed before the grief can teach anything. Joy gets harvested for momentum and never actually landed in.

What gets lost is not the feeling. It is the intelligence the feeling carries.

And here is where allyship specifically breaks down: when you have exiled your own emotional signal, you cannot distinguish between what you are doing *for the person in front of you* and what you are doing *for yourself.* You cannot tell when your helping is meeting their actual need or resolving your own discomfort at speed.

I learned this the hard way.

I spent years in customer service learning to manage my response to other people's emotions. Someone would call upset and something in me would shift — the particular quality of it was like a door closing — and then I would be gone. Not literally. Still on the call, professional, efficient. But the part of me that could feel what was actually happening had already left the building.

I would locate the problem, solve the problem, move on.

It did not work. Not because I was solving the wrong problem. Because the problem was not the problem. The rupture existed because something had not been felt yet. I was solving for my own discomfort — converting the tightening in me into competence and action as fast as possible. The person on the other end got efficiency. They needed presence.

This is the distortion the Shaman's chapter addresses: **when you manage emotions instead of reading them, you stop being present to the room you are actually in.** You start being present to the version of the room that your defenses can handle. The help you offer is shaped by what you can tolerate, not by what the person across from you actually needs.

The Effective Allyship Formula cannot run on that. You cannot identify your superpower while that governor is running. The superpower lives underneath it.

---

## Section 3: The Tool

The one thing this chapter gives you is this:

**Emotional alchemy — the practice of letting what you feel teach you what you need to know, instead of converting the feeling into action before it can finish its sentence.**

This is not emotional processing. Processing treats feelings like problems with solutions. You identify the feeling, understand where it came from, release it, move on. That model keeps you moving. What it costs you is the teaching.

Emotional alchemy is different. Every emotion is oriented toward something. It is showing you something real about the situation you are in. Your job is not to complete the emotion's cycle as efficiently as possible. Your job is to receive the intelligence it is carrying before you move.

The practice has four stages. You can move through them in ten seconds in the middle of a meeting, or in an hour alone after something hard. The timeline does not matter. The sequence does.

The practice is called the **WAVE-Spiral**, and it moves through four stages — **Wake, Clean, Grow, Show** — the feeling waking up, getting clear, teaching you something, and finally moving you. What each stage means in practice is simpler than the names:

**Wake:** Notice the signal. Name the channel it is coming from. Fear. Anger. Sadness. Joy. Neutrality. You do not need to know why it is here yet. You just need to name it before the converting starts.

**Clean:** Ask the channel one question: *What is this showing me?* Not "why do I feel this" — that points backward. Not "what should I do" — that points forward. Just: what is this feeling oriented toward right now? Strip away the secondary reactions — the shame about having the feeling, the story about what it means — until what is left is the clear signal. Fear shows you what is at stake. Anger shows you where a boundary is being crossed. Sadness shows you what mattered enough to lose. Joy shows you what is aligned. Neutrality shows you the wider view.

**Grow:** Let the understanding land. The feeling has been doing a job. It delivered information. Your system needs a moment to register that the message arrived — the moment the feeling stops being something that happened *to* you and becomes something you have learned from. This is not dramatic — it is often a breath, a small shift from confusion to clarity. The feeling stops being noise and becomes signal you can use.

**Show:** Move from the integrated place. Not from your habitual patterns. From where you just landed. The move is different because the fuel is different.

That last distinction matters.

The same outward action — staying in a difficult conversation, holding a boundary, offering support — can run on two completely different fuels. One fuel replenishes as it spends. You draw the line and you walk away tired but clear. You name the grief and the grief gives you back the love underneath it. You say the thing that needed saying and something comes back online because you stopped abandoning yourself.

The other fuel hollows you out. Guilt. Obligation. Performance. Inherited debt. The move happens, and what remains afterward is not capacity — it is evidence that you pushed through again, which the pattern will use to push through again, and again, until there is nothing left to spend.

Most allyship burnout is not a question of caring too much. It is a question of spending non-renewable fuel and calling the spending devotion.

The WAVE practice does not make every move easy. That is not the promise. The promise is discernment: you will know what the move is actually running on. That knowing is what the Effective Allyship Formula's first step requires. You cannot identify a superpower you have been converting into a coping mechanism.

---

## Section 4: What This Looks Like

Here is the distinction in a real situation.

You are in a conversation that is going sideways. Someone says something that lands as criticism and you feel the charge rise. The old pattern is fast: deflect, defend, absorb the hit and stay professional, or discharge the anger in a direction that feels justified. All of these moves happen before the anger has finished saying what it came to say.

The WAVE move is slower by about ten seconds.

You notice the anger. You name it — not to the other person, just to yourself. You ask it what it is oriented toward. What the anger is usually showing you is a line: something you are willing to stand for. That is not the same as the specific argument in front of you. The line is underneath it. When you find the line and speak from there, the sentence that comes out is different from the one that would have come out of the un-named charge. It is cleaner. It is proportionate. It does not cost the relationship what the unconverted charge would have cost.

Or you are in a room doing work you believe in, and something feels flat. Not wrong. Flat. The WAVE practice would have you sit with the flatness long enough to ask what it is oriented toward. It might be telling you that the conditions for this work are draining what it was supposed to be running on. That is not a complaint — it is information. The Effective Allyship Formula's first step includes knowing when the situation requires fuel you are not generating.

Or you are in the satisfied version: you run the practice and what comes up is the clear signal that you are in the right room, doing the right thing, with the right people. Joy, when you let it land instead of harvest it for momentum, tells you what conditions made it possible. That is data about where your superpower actually lives — not where you are most impressive, but where you are most real.

The same five feelings — fear, anger, sadness, joy, neutrality — run through every hard allyship situation. You are not learning to feel new things. You are learning to receive the intelligence the feelings have already been carrying.

---

## Section 5: Practice

This week, pick one hard conversation you would normally prepare for.

Run one WAVE move in it — not the whole sequence, just one stage — *during* the conversation. Not in your head before it. Not in a journal after. In the room, in the moment, with the person across from you.

The simplest version: the next time the conversation runs hot, before you speak, name the channel that is running. *"I am in anger right now. Let me say what the anger is actually about."* Or: *"The grief is up. Give me a second to find what is underneath it."* Then say the next sentence from that named place, not from the unnamed one.

One WAVE move. One live conversation.

There are two readings of how it goes. Reading one: naming the channel changed what you said next. Reading two: you named the channel and then said exactly what you would have said without it, just with a breath of pause in front of it. Both feel almost the same from the outside. They are not the same. Reading one is the WAVE moving through you. Reading two is the WAVE becoming another vocabulary for the same old move.

The test is what the named place made possible, not what it performed.

When the conversation is done: what channel was running, what you named, what the other person met, what surprised you. Capture it before the week is out.

---

## Isn't This Just Making It About My Feelings?

The fair objection to emotional alchemy: when someone else is hurting, isn't turning inward to read your own channel exactly the self-absorption allyship is supposed to cure?

It would be — if it took longer than it does. WAVE is ten seconds, not a retreat. You are not stepping out of the room to process; you are reading the signal *so the move you make next is clean instead of reactive.* The test is direction. If the practice sends you back toward the person in front of you with a clearer move, it is working. If it becomes a reason to leave the room, journal about it, and never return, you have turned a tool into a hiding place. When the room needs action first, act — and run the WAVE on the way home.

---

## Section 6: Summary and Handoff

The Shaman's contribution to the Effective Allyship Formula is this: before strategy, before structure, before any move you make in the world — there is signal. Emotional signal. The pattern of what you actually feel, what you actually need, what you are actually running on.

The WAVE practice gives you a way to read that signal instead of convert it. You wake to the channel, let it show you what it is oriented toward, let the understanding land, and move from there.

What makes a superpower a superpower — not just a capacity, but an actual unrepeatable gift — is that it is sourced from somewhere real in you. It was shaped by your specific survival. It knows something only you know because of what you went through. The Shaman's work is recovering that source. Making it conscious. Making it available. Stopping the conversion that was keeping it efficient and keeping it hollow.

That is Step One of the formula: not identifying your best performance, but identifying what you actually carry.

The well-being at this altitude is not about feeling better. It is about feeling accurately. Felt energy. Satisfaction that comes from being real in the room. The clean internal signal that tells you when you are spending what you have and when you are spending what you have already spent twice.

Now that you know what you carry, let us find where to use it.

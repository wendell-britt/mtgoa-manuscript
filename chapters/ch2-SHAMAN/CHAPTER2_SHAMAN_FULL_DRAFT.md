# CHAPTER 2: THE SHAMAN
## *Find the Thing That Gives You Something Back*

If Chapter 1 answered who allyship is for, this chapter answers what you bring.

The word I use for that is your superpower.

Your superpower is not the thing you can do once when everything is going well. It is the thing you can use repeatedly that gives something back to you while it helps someone else. It is the move that leaves you more alive instead of more hollow.

That matters because allyship fails when people try to give from a place that has nothing left to give.

You can push yourself into exhaustion and call it care. You can over-function and call it service. You can stay in rooms that drain you and call it commitment. But if the thing you are doing leaves you emptier every time, it is not yet your superpower.

The Shaman helps you tell the difference.

The Shaman is the part of you that can feel what is happening before you can explain it. It knows when something is off. It knows when a room is tense, when a story is carrying grief, when a line is being crossed, when a yes is real, and when a yes is only performance.

That body knowledge is not extra.
It is the source material.

This chapter is about learning to trust the signal that comes back from your own body when you use a gift well.

## The signal of the superpower

You usually know you are near your superpower by the kind of satisfaction it gives you.

Not the satisfaction of being praised.
Not the satisfaction of being seen as good.
Not the satisfaction of winning an argument.

The kind of satisfaction that says: this use makes sense in my bones.

Some things make you tired in a way that feels clean. Some things leave you wobbly and empty. Some things make you feel more yourself while you are doing them and after you are done.

That last category is the one we are looking for.

If a move helps someone else and also gives you a sense of internal rightness, you are probably near a superpower.

If a move helps someone else but leaves you resentful, collapsed, or false, it may be useful, but it is probably not your most sustainable gift.

If a move gives you energy but does not actually help the room, it is probably not allyship.

The Shaman is where you learn to listen for that difference.

## Emotional alchemy

Emotional alchemy is what lets the signal stay clear.

Fear tells you what matters.
Anger tells you where a boundary lives.
Sadness tells you what you have lost or how far you are from what you care about.
Joy tells you what is aligned.
Neutrality tells you when you can see the room more clearly.

Those emotions are not problems to get rid of.
They are information.

If you do not know how to stay with them long enough to learn from them, you will keep mistaking strain for virtue and burnout for proof.

This is why personal satisfaction matters.

Your superpower is not just a skill. It is a skill that feels satisfying to use in a way that stays alive over time.

That does not mean it is easy.
It means it is clean.

## What to look for

Your superpower often shows up where three things meet:

1. You can do it better than you think other people can.
2. It matters to real people, not just to your image.
3. Using it gives you a sense of rightness that is deeper than praise.

It might be:

- reading the emotional weather in a room
- naming the thing nobody wants to say
- organizing the steps that make action possible
- making the difficult conversation feel survivable
- noticing when someone is being left out
- turning confusion into a next move

What matters is not the category.
What matters is whether the use gives something back to you while it helps others.

That is the first clue that you can keep doing it.

## How to find it

Start with a recent moment when you were useful.

Not impressive. Useful.

Ask:

- What did I do?
- What part of that felt easy?
- What part of that felt alive?
- What part of that felt satisfying after the fact?
- What did I keep wanting to do again?

Now ask the harder question:

- What kind of help do I give that costs me too much to sustain?

The answer to that question is not a moral judgment.
It is a boundary.

The goal is not to do everything.
The goal is to find the thing you can do again and again without losing yourself in the process.

That is what makes it a superpower instead of a one-off rescue.

## The Shaman's rule

Use the body as the first reader.

Before you decide whether a move is good, notice:

- does this feel energizing in a real way?
- does it make the room clearer?
- does it leave me more capable of the next move?
- can I imagine doing this again tomorrow?

If the answer is yes, you are close.

If the answer is no, the move may still matter, but it is not yet the center of your formula.

The center of your formula is the thing that gives you something back when you use it.

That is your superpower.

## A quick example

Imagine you are in a room where everyone is talking past each other.

One person is getting louder.
Another is shutting down.
The room is full of motion and very little movement.

You notice the emotional weather before anybody names it.
You can feel where the tension is.
You know which question will open the room instead of escalating it.

When you ask that question, something changes.
People breathe.
The room slows down.
The next thing becomes possible.

And you feel it too.

Not because you got credit.
Because the move was right.

That is the kind of moment we are after.

## Practice

Think of one thing you do that helps people and also gives something back to you.

Write down:

- what the thing is
- what it does for other people
- what it gives back to you
- what happens to you if you do too much of it
- what happens to the room when you do it well

Then name the one sentence version:

**My superpower is __________, and I know because it gives me __________ when I use it.**

If that sentence feels true in your body, you are on the right track.

If it feels forced, keep looking.

## Why this matters for allyship

You cannot ally effectively if you do not know what you bring.

The people who want to help most often try to help with whatever feels urgent.
The wiser move is to help from the place where your gift is sustainable.

That is how you stay in the game.
That is how you keep from burning out.
That is how you make the Effective Allyship Formula real:

1. identify your superpower
2. identify who needs it
3. enroll allies to help you help those people
4. show up consistently

The next chapter is about the second step: how to find the people and places where your superpower should actually be used.

# Metaphor Editorial Audit — Chapter 2: The Shaman

## What was done

Ran the same metaphor audit protocol used on Chapter 3 against the Shaman chapter (69,058 chars, 7 sections). Focused on the EA channel correlates: Water/Sadness (dominant), Earth/Neutrality (grounding), plus general somatic vocabulary and stuck/rigidity vocabulary (the Dissatisfaction signature).

Cross-referenced findings against the Chapter 3 audit to test the hypothesis: does the Shaman chapter carry more somatic grounding than the Challenger chapter, and if so, where?

## Key Findings

### Finding 1: The pattern mirrors the Challenger exactly

In both chapters, the Practice section (S4) carries the primary metaphorical load. In Ch3, fire=15 instances in S4. In Ch2, water=30 and grief=14 instances in S4.

This is structural: both chapters inherit the same architecture (Exile → Distortion → Concept → Practice → Journey → Game → Recap), and the Practice section is where the face's channel system is actually described.

**The difference is what surrounds S4.** In Ch2, narrative sections have scattered body vocabulary (3, 0, 6 in S1-S3). In Ch3, body vocabulary in S1-S3 is nearly absent. This suggests the Shaman's body-awareness is more diffuse throughout the chapter, while the Challenger's somatic grounding is almost entirely concentrated in S4.

---

### Finding 2: Grief is the Shaman's primary somatic currency — but it is mostly *told*, not *located*

In Ch2, grief=24 and loss=10. These are high counts. But most appear in S4 (the practice section) where the grief moves are explicitly named. In S1-S3, grief appears as narrative summary ("the village had never learned to grieve") rather than as lived somatic experience.

Compare:

**Ch2 S1 (told, not felt):**
> "the Shaman's way is not about fixing or ending grief — it is about learning to move with it"

This is accurate to the Shaman's practice but does not give the reader a grief experience. The reader understands what the Shaman does with grief; they do not feel grief.

**The gap:** The Shaman chapter needs at least one section (probably S1 or S2) where the reader is *placed inside* a grief experience — not told about it. What does the body do when it's grieving? The chapter knows the answer (water, sinking, weight) but doesn't deploy it in the narrative sections.

---

### Finding 3: Water dominates, but the other channels are thin

Ch2 has 39 water-related terms vs 9 ground terms. The water/root ratio is 6:1 (vs the Challenger's line/ground ratio of 13:1). This is more balanced — the Shaman has some grounding vocabulary — but Earth/Neutrality is still the weakest channel in the chapter.

Specific gaps:

- **Metal/Fear** (constriction): Not developed as its own channel. The chapter's fear vocabulary appears in the Journey section (stuck, rigid) but is not labeled as Metal channel.
- **Wood/Joy** (pushing outward): Only appears as a brief mention of the Challenger's fire in the Shaman's shadow section. The Shaman's own "push" — the moment of holding space for another's grief — is not described physically.
- **Fire/Anger** (rising heat): Present only in the shadow section (Challenger's fire vs Shaman's water). The Shaman chapter has no native fire vocabulary.
- **Earth/Neutrality** (settling): The weakest channel. 9 ground terms total, most in S4. The chapter describes what grief does (sinks) but not where the Shaman settles after moving through grief. The grounding anchor is absent.

---

### Finding 4: The "stuck" vocabulary appears in S5 (Journey) where it should — but without sufficient somatic anchor

S5 is labeled "Journey to the Center" and is the shamanic equivalent of the Challenger's Journey section. It has 2 instances of stuck/rigidity vocabulary — the Dissatisfaction signature.

But it only has 1 body vocabulary instance. The stuck gates (Gates 1-3 in the Shaman's journey) are described cognitively — what the voice says — but not physically. The reader is told they are stuck; they are not given the somatic coordinates of stuckness.

Compare with the Challenger's Journey (S5): 25 gate instances, but the gates are vivid metaphors that carry implicit somatic weight ("the first place it gets dark"). The Shaman's S5 gates don't have the same metaphorical richness. They are described as stuck states, but the stuckness is not physically located.

---

### Finding 5: The ontological commitment

The Shaman chapter operates from an implicit ontology of **grief as water** — it moves, it has depth, it can be surfaced or submerged. This is a process ontology: grief is not a thing but a flow.

This creates an interesting asymmetry with the Challenger chapter:

- **Challenger:** Boundary is a line — a position to hold, a coordinate to plant
- **Shaman:** Grief is a flow — a depth to descend into, a current to move with

Both are coherent metaphors. But the book doesn't yet have a place where these two ontologies are held together. The Challenger's line and the Shaman's flow are in tension: one says boundaries are fixed positions; the other says everything is movement.

**This tension is generative, not a flaw.** But it should be named — probably in the Regent chapter, which is the synthesizing face. The reader should be invited to notice that they are internalizing two different metaphoric structures of what is real.

## Dogfood: The Audit Applied to Itself

*Using the same audit on this audit document:*

The audit document inherits the AUDIT metaphor — it is structured as an examination of specimens. It is analytical, not somatic. It can count words but cannot feel them.

**What it reveals:** The audit can identify the presence or absence of somatic vocabulary, but it cannot substitute for somatic vocabulary. Reading this audit does not give you an experience of the water/grief metaphor — it tells you that the Shaman chapter sometimes fails to give that experience.

The audit is a necessary tool but not sufficient. The next step is to take the specific recommendations below and write the actual prose — with somatic vocabulary — that the chapter currently lacks.

---

## Recommendations (Priority Order)

### Priority 1: Add somatic opening to S1 (The Exile)

The Shaman's exile is grief — being the one who feels what others can't or won't. But S1 currently opens with narrative summary, not lived experience.

**Add:** A paragraph in S1 that physically places the reader in a grief experience — not a dramatic grief (loss of a person) but the small griefs the body carries before they have names. What does it feel like when the body knows something is wrong before the mind names it? This is the somatic entry point to the Shaman's practice.

**Lakoff principle:** The orientational metaphor for grief is DOWN. The Shaman goes down to find what the village tried to stay up above. The chapter should begin by going down.

---

### Priority 2: Equalize the four weaker channels in S4

S4 currently has Water/Grief=30+ instances, Body=15. The other four channels need more equal treatment, especially:

- **Earth/Neutrality:** After moving through grief, what settles? The Shaman needs a grounding move. Currently missing.
- **Metal/Fear:** What is the constriction that precedes the willingness to feel grief? Not developed.
- **Wood/Joy:** What is the Shaman's push — the moment of holding space for another's pain without absorbing it? Not physically described.
- **Fire/Anger:** The shadow section mentions the Challenger's fire; the Shaman's own anger at injustice is noted but not developed.

---

### Priority 3: Add somatic coordinates to S5 (Journey)

The five stuck gates need at least one physical description each — not just what the voice says but where in the body the reader feels it.

Specifically: Gate 1 (The Stuck Gate) should have a somatic description. What does stuck feel like in the body? The chapter knows it: rigidity, heaviness, the inability to move. It should say so.

---

### Priority 4: Name the ontological tension in S7 (Recap)

The bridge section should acknowledge that the Shaman's "grief as flow" and the Challenger's "boundary as line" are different metaphors for different realities — and that a person needs both. The Regent chapter (synthesis) should hold both; the Shaman chapter should name what it's not trying to resolve.

---

## Cross-Chapter Summary: What the Metaphor Audit Reveals About the Book

Running the audit across both chapters produces a consistent pattern:

1. **The Practice section (S4) carries the metaphorical load** in both chapters. The conceptual sections (S1-S3) have thin metaphorical presence. The game sections (S6) are technique descriptions without somatic grounding.

2. **Both faces underexplore their grounding channel.** The Challenger has almost no grounding vocabulary (ground=7). The Shaman has slightly more (ground=9) but still thin. This suggests the **Earth/Neutrality channel is systematically underdeveloped across the book** — not a face-specific gap but a structural one.

3. **The shadow naming is asymmetric.** The Challenger knows about the Regent's walls but the Shaman only briefly references the Challenger's fire. The shadow is more present in Ch3 than in Ch2.

4. **The two chapters have opposite dominant metaphors.** Line vs wave, position vs flow, holding vs moving. This is not a contradiction — it reflects the two faces' different practices. But the tension should be held somewhere. The Regent chapter (Ch4) is the natural place to synthesize them.

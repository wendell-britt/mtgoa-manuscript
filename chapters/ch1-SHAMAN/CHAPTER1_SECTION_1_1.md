# 1.1 You Are Already a Player

> **Face:** Architect  
> **Purpose:** Establish the reader's identity as someone already engaged in the allyship game — even if they didn't know it. Validates their journey, reframes past efforts through the MTGOA lens.  
> **Turns from:** Chapter 0's "Will you answer?" (threshold)  
> **Turns to:** 1.2 "What Makes a Good Ally" (the actual question)  

---

If you're still reading, you've already answered.

That moment you showed up when it was hard — that's a move in the game. That time you sat with discomfort instead of fixing it — that's a practiced move. When you noticed the impulse to perform allyship and chose not to — that's a choice that matters.

You've been playing. You just didn't know the name of the game.

Most of us have been playing allyship the way a child plays chess before they know the rules: moving pieces, getting corrected, trying again, occasionally winning, often losing, never quite sure why. That's not failure. That's practice. The difference between those who grow and those who stay stuck isn't whether they played badly — it's whether they treated the bad plays as information.

This book is the rulebook you've been implicitly asking for.

Not a rulebook that says "do this, don't do that." A rulebook that shows you the board — all six faces of it, all six altitudes — so you can see what you're actually doing when you show up for allyship, what it costs, what it creates, and where the real moves are.

By the end of this chapter, you'll know which face you're most fluent in, which one you've been avoiding, and what it costs you to stay only on the faces that feel safe.

The game is already underway. You just need to learn to read the board.

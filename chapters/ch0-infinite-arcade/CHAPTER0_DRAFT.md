# Chapter 0 — The Infinite Arcade

This book is late because I tried to make allyship work by force.

After George Floyd was murdered in 2020, my phone filled up with calls from white friends asking if I was okay. I had been doing this work informally for years, and I turned that pressure into a course because I wanted to help, I wanted to get paid, and I wanted to make the work feel alive. Instead, I made a book project harder than it needed to be and made myself a worse ally in the process.

That failure taught me something useful: caring without placement becomes force with better intentions.

The problem was not that I cared too little. It was that I had mistaken urgency for clarity and identity for practice. I was trying to be the kind of person who could solve allyship by being good enough at it. That is not how this works.

Allyship is not a trophy, a posture, or a private moral victory. It is a game with moves, costs, return, and conditions. If you do not know the game you are in, you will keep spending yourself on the wrong board.

That is why this book uses the Infinite Arcade. The arcade gives us distance without detachment. It lets us see that we are always already playing, whether we named the game or not. It also lets us ask the only question that matters at the start:

**What game am I actually playing, and what is it costing me?**

In this book, tokens are what you spend to keep moving. Tickets are what you earn when a move increases life instead of draining it. The point is not to become a perfect player. The point is to learn how to spend your fuel in ways that keep the game alive.

The people I wrote this for are not people who need to be told to care. They are people who already care and want to show up more effectively as allies. They are people who have felt the strain of trying to do the right thing inside a system that keeps turning good intentions into guilt, exhaustion, or performance. They are people who want a clearer way to keep going.

That is the promise of this book:

**Mastering the Game of Allyship helps people who want to show up more effectively as allies learn why allyship keeps failing, see the game underneath the term, and use the Effective Allyship Formula to build real-world relationships, reduce harm, and create the trust that leads naturally into bars-engine and coaching.**

The Formula is simple enough to carry:

1. identify your superpower
2. identify who needs that superpower
3. enroll allies to help you help those people
4. show up consistently

The rest of the book teaches you how to make those four moves real.

Before we do anything else, bring one actual situation with you.

Not a theory. Not a headline. A real room, a real relationship, a real pattern you keep trying to solve.

- a relationship you do not know how to repair
- a room you keep trying to hold
- a person you want to help but keep exhausting yourself around
- a conflict where every move feels wrong
- a pattern in your own care that you are tired of repeating

Choose one.

You do not have to solve it here. Keep it nearby. We are going to use it to teach you the controls.

The first thing you need is not a better opinion. It is a better map.

That map starts with the fact that you are already in the game.

You have been setting the table in rooms nobody asked you to hold. You have been designing challenges with every word you choose to say or leave unsaid. You have been spending tokens and hoping the ticket would appear later.

This book helps you learn the difference between spending and investing, between pressure and care, between performance and play.

It also helps you understand something the old allyship model keeps missing: you do not need to be perfect to begin. You only need to be willing to play consciously.

The next chapter defines what allyship actually means, who it is for, and why the question "Who should I ally with?" is the real gateway question for the whole book.

## Root Principle (the one rule behind every fix)

Almost every surfaced problem is the same structural fact in eight costumes:

> **The chapter resolves tension in the realm of *understanding*, and the ideal reader's pathology is living in understanding to avoid action.**

Therefore "fixing" a problem rarely means *explaining better* — more explanation feeds the disease. Each fix must do one of three things:

1. **Convert insight → bounded external demand** (an action with a person, in the world, that understanding cannot satisfy, and that has a *finish line*).
2. **Trust the reader instead of warning her** (every warning is a new standard for a perfectionist to perform — warnings are shame-delivery in disguise).
3. **Make the body the authority, not the mind** (the only part of her not captured by the loop).

**Humane, for this reader, means:** lower the stakes of the first move, never add a standard, never indict the past. Shame is an off-ramp, not a motivator, for someone who collapses into it.

**Governing craft rule for all shame moments:** *Company before frame.* Meet her in the feeling (first-person confession) before handing her the reframe/citation.

3. **End each of the three Games with a recognition prompt that names a real remembered instance** in *her* life — not a definition. The chapter already knows this move (line 121, "parking garage, on a run, in the shower"); the taxonomy forgot it.

**Acceptance criteria:**

## P0-2 — Build one bounded behavioral demand she cannot satisfy by understanding

**Problem (Analysis #2):** Every "action" in Ch0 is an act of *self-reflection* (the character sheet, the three-games self-diagnosis). For this reader, introspection IS the addiction. Nothing asks her to do one concrete thing in the world with a person.

**Fix:** Add a single, bounded, **finishable** first action, attached to the relocated BAR section:

> Before you read Chapter 1, capture one BAR from the last 48 hours — one real moment, two minutes, app or paper.

Bounded + finishable is the point: a perfectionist can *complete* it and feel legitimately done, which **starves** the "I haven't done enough" loop instead of feeding it. An unbounded ask ("go be a better ally") generates shame; a bounded ask generates a finish line.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [x] Exactly one behavioral demand exists in Ch0, and it is bounded (scoped, time-boxed, completable).
- [x] It points at a *real past/present event*, not at self-diagnosis.
- [x] It gates entry to Ch1 (soft gate: before entering the Forest / Chapter 1).
- [x] It does not require the app to complete (paper, margin, and notes app paths exist).

**Acceptance criteria:**

**Fixes:**

3. **Swap line 323:** "It is also not enough" → "It is also not where the game is played" (or equivalent). Same meaning; does not ratify the "not enough" self-critic.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- [ ] "not enough" phrasing removed.

## What NOT to do (anti-fixes that would feed the loop)

- ❌ Add a paragraph warning the reader not to intellectualize the book. (New standard → performed → loop.)
- ❌ Resolve a shame moment by softening the cost. (The cost is true; the fix is sequence/tense/company, not anesthesia.)
- ❌ Add more frameworks or citations to shore up authority. (Authority is not the gap; embodiment and action are.)
- ❌ Make the first action unbounded or aspirational. (Unbounded ask = shame generator for a perfectionist.)

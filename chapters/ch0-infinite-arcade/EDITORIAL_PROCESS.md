# Chapter 0 Editorial Process
## Repeatable loop for revising `CHAPTER0_DRAFT.md` without flattening voice or losing the reader

**Created:** 2026-05-09  
**Status:** Operational draft  
**Scope:** Chapter 0 only  
**Purpose:** Make Chapter 0 revision repeatable, scalable, and voice-safe

---

## What this process is for

Chapter 0 already has the core ingredients:
- a thesis spec in `SPEC.md`
- section-level emotional targets in `CH0_UNPACKING.md`
- expansion material in `CH0_EXPANSIONS.md`
- a GM bridge draft in `DRAFT_GM_v3_2026-04-22.md`
- voice anchors in `voice-calibration-notes.md`
- reader-fit criteria in `EDITING_PLAN.md`
- backup rules in `editorial_reports/EDITORIAL_VERSION_CONTROL.md`

What was missing was one operating loop that says, in order:
1. what to read first
2. what kind of problem to diagnose
3. what gets changed
4. what must not get changed
5. how to verify the chapter is better before moving on

This document is that loop.

---

## The non-negotiable principle

**Do not edit Chapter 0 as one giant blob.**  
Edit one unit at a time.

The unit is:
- one section
- one bridge
- one opening
- one closing

Never run structural critique, reader-fit critique, voice critique, and line-polish all at once in the same move. That is how the prose gets flatter and the diagnosis gets muddy.

---

## The chapter job

Chapter 0 is not just an introduction.

Its job is to do five things:
1. Convince a skeptical reader that the game frame clarifies allyship rather than trivializing it.
2. Teach the token and ticket economy in language that feels lived, not theoretical.
3. Establish the GM frame as recognition, not imported jargon.
4. Orient the reader to the three game types and six faces without overload.
5. Hand the reader into Chapter 1 with more curiosity and more capacity than they started with.

If a proposed edit does not improve one of those five jobs, it is probably noise.

## The voice we are preserving

Use `voice-calibration-notes.md` as the authority.

What Chapter 0 must preserve:
- direct second-person address without coaching-smarm
- discovery idiom rather than lecture idiom
- permission without collapse
- emotional honesty without self-dramatizing
- bodily recognition before conceptual abstraction
- clean definitions when a mechanic needs naming
- occasional wit or self-aware turn, but never cute-for-its-own-sake prose

What Chapter 0 must avoid:
- generic motivational cadence
- corporate explanation voice
- jargon without grounding
- abstract spiritual altitude talk before sensory recognition
- "be better" pressure language
- sentences that sound like they were written to impress rather than land

## The editorial loop

Run this loop once per target unit.

### Step 0 — Stabilize the working surface

Before any edit:
1. Confirm the active draft file.
2. Create a dated backup if one does not exist for the day.
3. Confirm the backup exists.
4. Name the target unit for this cycle.

**Valid target examples:**
- opening portal section
- GM bridge
- token system
- ticket system
- three game types
- six faces ladder
- closing handoff to Chapter 1

Do not start a cycle with "entire chapter."

---

### Step 1 — Cold read before diagnosis

Read the target unit straight through with no editing.

Log only these reactions:
- where attention sharpens
- where attention drifts
- where belief increases
- where skepticism increases
- where the author's voice feels present
- where the voice disappears

This is the first gate because a chapter can be technically correct and still dead on contact.

**Questions to answer:**
1. Do I want to keep reading?
2. Do I know what this section is doing?
3. Does this sound like Wendell, or like a competent substitute?

---

### Step 2 — Structural diagnosis

Now compare the target unit against `SPEC.md`.

Look only for structure:
- Is the section doing the job assigned in the spec?
- Is the argument sequence in the right order?
- Is the section too early, too late, or duplicated elsewhere?
- Is the section trying to carry more than one thesis move?

For Chapter 0, structural failure usually looks like one of these:
- the game frame arrives before the cost is felt
- the GM language arrives before recognition is earned
- theory outruns embodiment
- the six faces are introduced as lore rather than capacities
- the close explains instead of handing off

Do not rewrite sentences yet. Just diagnose.

---

### Step 3 — ICA friction pass

Now read the same unit as Jordan.

Answer:
1. What actual problem of Jordan's does this unit open on?
2. What would Jordan underline?
3. What would Jordan skim?
4. Where would Jordan roll their eyes?
5. What concrete move, distinction, or practice did Jordan receive?

Use only four tags:
- `KEEP` — this serves the reader and should survive
- `SKIM_RISK` — the point may matter but the current expression is too abstract or repetitive
- `DROP_RISK` — this could make Jordan stop reading
- `GROUNDING_NEEDED` — the claim may be good, but it needs story, body, or example

If a paragraph has `DROP_RISK`, fix that before polishing anything else in the unit.

---

### Step 4 — Voice preservation pass

Only after structure and reader-fit are clear do the voice check.

Read the unit aloud.

Mark lines that do any of the following:
- sound like a workshop leader instead of a writer
- explain a feeling instead of recreating recognition
- stack abstractions without a sensory anchor
- moralize at the reader
- use a rhythm that feels generic or overcomposed

Then mark the lines that are unmistakably alive.

Voice-safe editing rule:
- Weak lines should be rewritten toward the live lines already present in the chapter, not toward neutral prose.

The goal is not consistency at all costs. The goal is recognizable authorship.

---

### Step 5 — Rewrite only the diagnosed problem

Now rewrite.

Each rewrite pass should have one declared purpose:
- `STRUCTURE`
- `ICA`
- `VOICE`
- `CLARITY`

If a paragraph has three problems, fix them in that order:
1. structure
2. reader-fit
3. voice

Do not line-polish a paragraph that is still structurally wrong.

---

### Step 6 — Re-read against the section transformation target

Use `CH0_UNPACKING.md`.

For the target unit, check:
- the experience
- the emotional outcome
- the reservation being met
- the prerequisite being honored
- the transformation being delivered

If the prose now says the right thing but does not produce the intended movement, the edit is incomplete.

Chapter 0 is not only informational. It is an onboarding sequence.

---

### Step 7 — Final gate for the unit

A unit is complete for the day only if all seven checks are true:
- the unit still serves the chapter thesis
- the unit does one primary job
- Jordan would keep reading
- at least one paragraph contains real recognition, not just explanation
- the voice is still legibly Wendell's
- the transition into the next unit is cleaner than before
- the backup and the current draft both exist

If one of these fails, the unit is still open.

## Order of operations for Chapter 0

Work the chapter in this order:

1. Opening portal
2. Token system
3. Ticket system
4. GM bridge
5. Three game types
6. Six faces ladder
7. Closing handoff to Chapter 1

Why this order:
- the opening earns the frame
- the token/ticket economy creates stakes
- the GM bridge becomes believable only after stakes exist
- the game-type map clarifies the field
- the six faces then read as capacities instead of fantasy roles
- the closing can hand off with less explanation

Do not start with the six faces ladder. That is downstream work.

## What makes this process scalable

This process scales because it standardizes the loop, not the prose.

The reusable assets are:
- one active spec
- one emotional transformation map
- one ideal-reader definition
- one voice calibration file
- one backup protocol
- one fixed editorial loop

The part that changes per chapter:
- thesis
- reservations
- reader friction points
- examples
- face-specific cadence

In other words: the scaffold repeats, the writing does not.

---

## Deliverables for each editorial cycle

Every cycle should leave behind:
1. the backed-up draft
2. the revised active draft
3. a short note with:
   - target unit
   - main problem diagnosed
   - what changed
   - what still feels unresolved

If no note exists, the next session has to reconstruct intent from the prose alone. That wastes cycles.

## Failure modes to watch for

### Failure mode 1 — Flattening
The rewrite becomes cleaner but less alive.

**Correction:** restore the line of recognition and rewrite around it.

### Failure mode 2 — Premature theory
The prose explains the model before the reader has felt the problem.

**Correction:** move body, scene, or consequence earlier.

### Failure mode 3 — Gimmick risk
"Game" language feels imported rather than discovered.

**Correction:** anchor every game concept in an experience the reader already knows.

### Failure mode 4 — ICA contempt
The section talks above Jordan, not to Jordan.

**Correction:** replace concept-display with a concrete lived distinction.

### Failure mode 5 — Over-editing
Multiple passes in one sitting erase the original charge.

**Correction:** stop after one successful unit cycle and reread the chapter later with fresh ears.

## Definition of done for Chapter 0

Chapter 0 is ready for the next level of editing when:
- the chapter thesis is unmistakable
- the token and ticket system feel lived rather than invented
- the GM frame lands as recognition
- the game types clarify rather than complicate
- the six faces create desire for Chapter 1
- the closing makes the reader want to enter the Forest
- the prose still sounds like Wendell and not like "well-edited nonfiction"

That last criterion matters. A dead-clean chapter is still a failed chapter.

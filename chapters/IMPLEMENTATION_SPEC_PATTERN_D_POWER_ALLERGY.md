### Session 3: Ch3 — First Agitation

**Read before editing:** `ch3-CHALLENGER/SPEC.md` + `CHAPTER3_CHALLENGER_FULL_DRAFT.md`

**Task 3.1: The Exile — name allergy directly** (70 words)

Where: End of Section 1
What: Village confused organized power with actual power — village allergic to will because last person who showed up as will used it to dominate
Verification: Diagnosis, not accusation

**Task 3.2: The Distortion — allergy as contraction** (50 words)

Where: After I-statement scripts paragraph
What: Village didn't just forget "no" — contracted an allergy to it because "no" felt like oppression; every softened no taught the village that claiming power was dangerous
Verification: Challenger naming what village does, not lecturing reader

**Task 3.3: The Concept — introduce lactase** (60 words)

Where: After three-part clean no definition, after "hope is not a strategy"
What: "This is the lactase — the practice of saying no and letting it land. You cannot think your way out of the power allergy. You have to practice wielding the power you're allergic to in small doses until your system learns power doesn't have to mean harm."
Verification: Metaphor feels natural, not jargon; self-explanatory from context

**Task 3.4: The Game — name each move as power practice** (80 words)

Where: End of Section 6, after the five moves
What: Each of the five moves IS a power practice — naming power, wielding it, holding it, integrating it with love, becoming someone who can carry it
Verification: Retrospective summary, not new taxonomy

**Task 3.5: Grief Anchor — add power cost** (50 words)

Where: After "I drew the line. I lost the relationship. That's the deal."
What: "I'd do it again — not because I wanted to lose the relationship, but because not drawing the line would have been a different harm: the kind from being so allergic to power you let the room burn down rather than touch the fire."
Verification: Preserve grief and specificity of the original

**Task 3.6: BARs worked example** (50 words)

Where: End of Section 6 or Reflection Prompts
What: BAR example — Breakthrough: named the unsaid in the meeting; Action: will re-read clean no structure before next hard conversation; Reflection: room didn't collapse; Power practiced: Challenger (drawing the line)
Verification: Concrete and scannable

### Session 6: Ch6 — The Allergy's Epicenter

**Read before editing:** `ch6-diplomat/SPEC.md` + `CHAPTER6_DIPLOMAT_FULL_DRAFT_MASTER.md`

**Task 6.1: Name the power allergy directly** (80 words)

Where: End of Section 1 or start of Section 2
What: "Here's what nobody says out loud — we're allergic to power. We flattened hierarchy because hierarchy had been used to dominate. We made everything horizontal because vertical meant oppression. We were right about what we were reacting to, but wrong about what we built instead. A flat world has no leverage. And allyship without leverage is just a very sincere wish."
Verification: Diplomat's voice — compassionate, self-aware, honest; not accusatory, not defensive

**Task 6.2: The Diplomat's lactase** (50 words)

Where: Section 3 or 4
What: "The Diplomat's lactase is staying in relationship across power differentials without pretending the differential doesn't exist. You don't fix the allergy by convincing yourself power is okay. You fix it by practicing the skill of holding power AND connection at the same time."
Verification: Distinct from Challenger's lactase (saying no); Diplomat's version is relational

**Task 6.3: BARs worked example** (50 words)

Where: Reflection Prompts or Game
What: BAR example — Breakthrough: caught myself pretending the power dynamic didn't exist because naming it felt oppressive; Action: named it anyway; Reflection: the room exhaled; Power practiced: Diplomat (staying in relationship across power)

### Session 8: Ch8 — The Great Game Reveal

**Read before editing:** `ch8-player/SPEC.md` + `CHAPTER8_PLAYER_FULL_DRAFT.md`

**Task 8.1: The full reveal** (120 words)

Where: End of chapter, new section or embedded in Journey
What: "This book was always about power. Not domination — that's what you were running from. But the capacity to make things happen, to hold lines, to build traditions, to redesign systems, to stay in hard conversations, to read feeling in the room, and to choose which the moment needs. That's the game. You've been playing it. Now you know."
Verification: Arrival, not new concept; reader should feel "I knew this. I just didn't have words."

**Task 8.2: The holographic principle** (100 words)

Where: After reveal or in Recap
What: "You could read this book six more times. Read as the Shaman — it's about feeling. As the Challenger — boundaries. As the Regent — what's worth carrying. As the Architect — design. As the Diplomat — belonging. As the Sage — seeing the whole thing. The book didn't change. Your capacity to hold all six faces at once did."
Verification: Connects to Ch2 pilot's "you already knew this"; invitation, not obligation

**Task 8.3: BARs as player's game loop** (50 words)

Where: Game section or after stage sequence
What: "You're not done practicing. BARs are how you keep developing. Each loop is a power move you log, review, learn from. You design your own game now — but you practice the same way: one move at a time."
Verification: Connects to BARs template from Ch1

**Task 8.4: SAP — Satisfaction As Practice** (80 words)

Where: End of chapter, before final reflection prompts
What: "The person you're becoming is more satisfied than the person who started. Not because problems are solved — because you know what to do about them. Satisfaction is not the absence of conflict. It's the felt sense of being equipped for the conflict you're in. This is SAP — Satisfaction As Practice. The sustain loop. The reason you keep going."
Verification: Payoff, not new framework; reader should feel "This is why I feel different"

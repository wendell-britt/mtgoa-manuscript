## OBJECTION 1 (CRITICAL) — Cross-System Confusion: EA Channels vs WAVE States

### What the text does

Section 4 has two separate systems in the same space:

**System A — EA 5 Channels** (Metal/Fear, Water/Sadness, Wood/Joy, Fire/Anger, Earth/Neutrality):
> "Anger clears space. Fear names what's at stake. Sadness shows what matters. Joy lightens. Neutrality reveals the pattern."

**System B — The Regent's 5 Loyalty Modes** (Custodian, Inheritor, Teacher, Reformer, Keeper of Vows):
> "The Custodian — The mode of caring for what exists."
> "The Inheritor — The mode of receiving — including receiving the damage."
> etc.

These are being presented in the same chapter, potentially the same section, with different energy labels. The EA channels use the elemental (Metal/Fear etc.) and the loyalty modes use role-names. A reader who has studied the EA framework (or comes from other MTGOA contexts) will try to map the loyalty modes onto EA channels — and they don't map cleanly.

### Why it's a problem

The EA system is a transpersonal framework: Metal/Fear, Water/Sadness, etc. are not roles or modes — they're elemental forces. The Regent's loyalty modes (Custodian, Inheritor, etc.) are role-descriptions, not elemental forces. Presenting them side by side without explicit distinction risks:
1. Readers conflating the two systems
2. The book's credibility being undermined at the exact chapter (Regent/Allyship Domains) where structural clarity matters most
3. Confusion about what "energy" means — EA energy (elemental charge) vs. loyalty energy (inheritance weight) are not the same thing

### The fix (3 options — pick one)

**(a) Separate the systems explicitly.** The EA channels are the transpersonal energy substrate. The loyalty modes are a *relational* framework layered on top. Write a bridge sentence: "The Regent works with loyalty in five distinct modes — not as energy channels, but as relationship stances toward what you've inherited." This keeps both systems intact.

**(b) Collapse into one system.** Drop the loyalty modes and use only the EA channels for the Regent practice. This is simpler but loses the specificity of the Regent's framework.

**(c) Rename the loyalty modes as stages, not channels.** "Stage" is already used in the book (Inherit → Honor → Steward → Entrust). Calling the five modes "loyalty stages" or "inheritance stances" makes them clearly distinct from EA channels.

## OBJECTION 3 — Stage Sequence Terminology Unclear

### Quote
> "Stage Sequence: Inherit → Honor → Steward → Entrust"

### Problem
"Stage" appears in Section 4 without being defined. Is this a sequence? A cycle? A state machine? The language of "Inherit → Honor → Steward → Entrust" sounds like a progression, but the Regent works with inheritance cyclically, not linearly. If this is a cycle, the stages need to be labeled as such. If this is a sequence, the end state ("Entrust") implies completion, which contradicts the ongoing nature of the Regent's practice.

### Fix
Option (a): Change "Stage Sequence" to "The Inheritance Cycle" and clarify that these are not linear stages but four ongoing stances that cycle back.

Option (b): Keep "Stage Sequence" and clarify that Entrust is not the end — it's the position from which you begin the next inheritance cycle.

## OBJECTION 5 — The Game Section Lacks the Chapter's Promised Punch

### What the text does
Section 6 "The Game" closes the chapter with "What winning looks like at the Challenger's altitude." But the Regent's game is not about winning — it's about succession. The chapter opened with the Regent's failure (taking on something too heavy and the tradition dying with them). Section 6 should deliver the resolution: what the Regent's actual game is.

### Problem
The Game section is titled after the Challenger but reads like it's describing the Regent's version. It doesn't resolve the opening tension — the Regent who failed and the Regent who can still tend the tradition.

### Note for Wendell
This is where the book's allyship arc and the Regent's personal arc should meet. The Game section needs to answer: what does the Regent win at, and what does it mean to win it? This may require personal material — the specific thing the Regent learned from failing.

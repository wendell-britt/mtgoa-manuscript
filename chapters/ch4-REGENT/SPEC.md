## Core thesis

Allyship at scale requires traditions worth protecting and worth breaking.

The Regent's gift is chosen loyalty. The capacity to serve something larger than yourself AND to be shaped by what you serve, without disappearing into it or being ground up by it.

## The 5 channels (modes of stewardship)

1. **The Custodian** — caring for what exists
2. **The Inheritor** — receiving what was passed down, including its wounds
3. **The Teacher** — passing it on in a form the next person can actually receive
4. **The Reformer** — changing the tradition while honoring the lineage
5. **The Keeper of Vows** — honoring commitments across time, especially when no one is watching

---

## The stage sequence (4 stages)

**Inherit → Honor → Steward → Pass**

1. **Inherit:** You receive what was handed to you — the gift and the damage both.
2. **Honor:** You name what in the inheritance still serves.
3. **Steward:** You carry it, maintain it, and reform it where it no longer serves.
4. **Pass:** You hand it on, cleanly, to whoever comes next.

---

## Transcend moves (+2 energy)

1. **Obedience → True allegiance** (voluntary loyalty, not compliance)
2. **Duty → Service** (chosen responsibility, not martyrdom)
3. **Rigidity → Integrity** (holding form because it works, not because it's old)
4. **Dogma → Faithfulness** (keeping the essence, updating the expression)
5. **Inheritance-shame → Inheritance-gift** (the wounds you received become the medicine you pass)

## The 5 Game moves (Section 6)

1. **Receive Before You Reform** — Understand what you're inheriting before you change it.
2. **Name What Still Serves** — Honor the tradition before you reform it.
3. **Hold the Role Without Becoming It** — Be in the position without losing yourself.
4. **Keep the Vow** — Do the thing even when no one is watching.
5. **Pass It On Cleanly** — Hand the tradition to the next person better than you received it.

---

## Bridge to next chapter (Architect)

The Regent can build what lasts but cannot build what works. A tradition serves its people only if it is well-designed. When the Regent's stewardship needs to become more effective — when the tradition needs to actually function — someone has to design the system. That's the Architect.

## Core thesis

You have played all 6 Faces. You have been the Shaman who names what's true, the Challenger who draws the line, the Regent who builds what lasts, the Architect who designs for the next person, the Diplomat who holds the field across difference, the Sage who sees the whole game and plays their part. Now the question is: what game do YOU want to play? And how do you design it so it outlives you?

This chapter is the culmination. Not a new face — the integration of all 6. The player becomes the designer. The allyship game becomes your game.

## The 5 channels (modes of self-authorship)

> **Note on EA and Ch8:** Chapters 3–7 each apply the 5 EA emotional channels as emergent modes for their Face. Ch8 is different — the reader has been practicing EA for the entire book. The 5 modes here are not new EA tools to learn. They are a vocabulary for what the reader has already been doing, now pointed at designing their own practice. Each mode maps to a commitment stage: what you're building, and where you're invited to go next.

| Mode | What you're doing | Your ongoing practice | Where it leads |
|------|------------------|---------------------|---------------|
| **The Cartographer** | Self-inventory — which Faces do you naturally lean on, which do you avoid | Your specific practice (ongoing) | Deepens your self-knowledge |
| **The Designer** | Building a practice structure — what do I do regularly and why | Your specific practice (ongoing) | Deepens your self-knowledge |
| **The Founder** | Ready to build something real, need 1:1 support | — | **1:1 coaching with Wendell** |
| **The Elder** | Passing it on — teaching others what you learned | — | **The allyship deck** (teaching mode) |
| **The Outlaw** | The structure calcified, needs challenging | — | **The allyship deck** (disruption mode) |

**The allyship deck** appears in two modes: Elder uses it to teach (pass on the tradition). Outlaw uses it to shake loose what's gotten rigid (break wisely). Same deck, two different games.

The stage sequence: **Review → Discern → Design → Build → Pass On** — not EA stages, but commitment stages. Each loop goes deeper.

## Control Translate moves (−1 energy)

*Where each mode calcifies:*

- **Cartographer → Paralysis-by-analysis** (mapping without acting)
- **Designer → Over-design** (building the perfect system that never ships)
- **Founder → Guru-dependency** (creating something that needs you to run it)
- **Elder → Gatekeeping** (passing on only to people who are like you)
- **Outlaw → Chaos-for-its-own-sake** (breaking for the sake of breaking)

## The 8 Gates at this altitude

| Gate | Says | Job |
|------|------|-----|
| **Protector** | *Not safe to start something new. Last time you started something, it failed. Or worse — it succeeded and then you had to maintain it.* | Keep you from creating until it knows the creation won't destroy you. |
| **Controller** | *If you design your own game, you'll have to commit to it. You'll lose the freedom to play other games.* | Keep the lid on so you don't have to be responsible for what you build. |
| **Skeptic** | *Is what I have to offer actually new? Hasn't this already been done? Who am I to build something?* | Doubt your voice before someone else can. |

## Vulnerable Child's superpower

**Self-authored allyship.** The capacity to look at all 6 Faces, know which ones you are, and design a practice that is genuinely yours. The game designed by the player, for the player, in service of the village they want to leave behind.

## The 5 Game moves (Section 6)

1. **Map Your Terrain** — Which Faces feel natural to you? Which ones do you avoid? Where is your specific gift?
2. **Name Your Problem** — What problem in the world are you trying to solve? What made you necessary?
3. **Design Your Practice** — Build the specific blend of all 6 that is uniquely yours. Write it down.
4. **Start Something** — Create the thing that doesn't exist. Say the thing that hasn't been said.
5. **Make It Pass-Onable** — Design it so someone else can run it without you. The goal is not dependence — it is inheritance.

---

## The Exile

The Player was never exiled. The Player left on purpose. Not because the village was wrong. Not because they were rejected. They left because they had learned everything the village could teach them and there was something specific they wanted to build that required going further out than the village extended. The Player left carrying the whole map, with a specific destination in mind.

---

## The Distortion

Without the Player's example, the village taught that the 6 Faces were a destination, not a toolkit. That allyship was about becoming a perfect Shaman or Challenger or Sage — a finished product. That the goal was to arrive. The Player's absence meant the village never saw someone who had mastered the 6 and then deliberately, consciously, chose to design something new. The shadow Player emerged: the person who.collected all 6 Faces as credentials without ever playing any of them.

---

## Bridge — This Is the End

The book ends here. Not with the Sage looking down from above the village, but with the Player walking out ahead of the village, building the road as they go, turning around every so often to make sure the village can still see the path.

The last line is not "and they lived happily ever after."
The last line is "now go build yours."

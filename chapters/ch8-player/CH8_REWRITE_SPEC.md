## Why This Rewrite

The existing draft (3,051 words) is structurally sound but emotionally undersized. The spec targets ~10,000–12,000 words; the draft is ~30% of target. More importantly, the Journey section — which must carry the reader from intellectual understanding to somatic readiness to build — is the most undersized section and the one where the felt sense lives.

The 6-face analysis identified three structural gaps that are non-negotiable to address:

1. **Missing somatic practice in Journey** — the section tells the reader about the walk without giving them the walk
2. **No honest answer for the reader who doesn't have a game** — the chapter assumes everyone has a Founder move
3. **bars-engine undernamed** — the concrete proof of the chapter's theory appears only once, softly

These are not style issues. They are structural load-bearing problems.

## Required Additions (3 Non-Negotiables)

### 1. 🧠 Architect — "Try It Now" Somatic Practice in Journey

**Location:** Section 5, within Journey. Insert after "The Walk" subsection header, before the first paragraph about beginning before you're ready.

**What:** One concrete iteration of the Stage Sequence (Review → Discern → Design → Build → Pass On) that the reader can run in the moment. Not a prompt — a practice.

**Why:** The Laloux source analysis showed that Teal-stage work is "practice-first." The spec's own Stage Sequence is named but not demonstrated. The reader is told about the loop without experiencing it. The Architect's fix: add a load-bearing element that the rest of the section can rest on.

**Implementation:**

```
Try It Now:

Before you read further — take sixty seconds.

Review: Name one Face you've been avoiding this week. Not intellectual avoidance — 
a real one. Where did you feel it in your body when the moment came to use it?

Discern: What problem were you trying to solve by avoiding it? What's the real 
stakes if you'd used it?

Design: What would the practiced version of you have done differently? One sentence. 
Specific.

Build: That's your first design. You already know it. The question is whether 
you'll do it tomorrow when the next moment comes.

Pass On: Tell someone. Not "share your growth." Tell someone specifically what 
you noticed about your own pattern. That's the pass-on.

You just ran the loop. That's the walk.
```

**Target:** +400 words. Must be followed by a transition paragraph back into the main Journey voice.

---

### 2. ⚔️ Challenger — "What If You Don't Have a Game?" Subsection

**Location:** Section 5, Journey. Insert after the "The walk includes failure" paragraph. Before "The walk ends the way all walks end."

**What:** One honest page for the reader who finds they don't have a Founder move in them.

**Why:** The current draft assumes everyone who reads the book has something to build. The Challenger's diagnostic: this assumption will strand the reader who has gone through the five modes and found they have no bars-engine, no community, no product — just the walk. The chapter needs to be honest that the walk is enough even if there is no game.

**Implementation:**

```
### What If You Don't Have a Game?

Some of you will go through the five modes and find something unexpected: 
no Founder move. No bars-engine. No community waiting to be built. No method 
that's been forming in you without a name.

That's not a failure. That's information.

The stage sequence doesn't require you to have a Founder move. It requires 
you to be honest about where you are. Review. Discern. Design. Build. 
Pass On — and any of those can be where you're starting. Some of you are 
going to be in Review for a long time. Some of you have been in Review 
for years. That's not behind. That's where you are.

The walk is what you have if you don't have a game. And the walk is not 
small. The walk is WAVE looping through your actual life — your parenting, 
your partnership, your work, the group chat, the town hall, the next election. 
You are doing the work in the world even if you never build a thing.

The village needs people who have done the inner work and show up with it. 
That's the actual requirement. Not a Founder move. The willingness to be 
the one who went first — and the willingness to report back what you found.

You don't need a game to be a Player. You need a walk.
```

**Target:** +350 words. Must be followed by a transition back to the "walk ends" paragraph.

### 3. 🧙 Sage — bars-engine as Concrete Through-Line

**Location:** Three placements, not one.

**Placement A — Section 4 (Practice), within Founder section:**
The existing bars-engine mention in the Founder section is ~150 words. Expand to ~400 words. Show what the Founder move actually looks like in concrete form: the specific problem, the specific iteration, the specific failure-and-redesign cycle. Not as a credential — as a demonstration of the pattern. The reader should be able to extract the Founder's move from the bars-engine example without needing the same content.

**Placement B — Section 5 (Journey), within The Walk section:**
After the "iterations and failures" paragraph (the one about redesigning the token economy three times). Add a paragraph that names bars-engine more fully — what it is, what it solves, why it was necessary. Show the direct line from the gap in the theory to the concrete thing that fills it.

**Placement C — Section 6 (Game), within "Start Something" move:**
One sentence naming bars-engine as the Founder move that grew out of this practice. Brief. Direct. A pointer, not an explanation.

**Why:** The Sage's prescription from the analysis: "bars-engine is named too softly. It appears once, in Practice, as 'that's what the Founder does.'" The chapter's theory is proven by bars-engine. The reader needs to see that proof more than once, in more than one context, to believe the theory is real.

## Required Modifications (3)

### 4. 🌬️ Diplomat — Explicit Arc Bridge at Top of Journey

**Location:** Section 5, first paragraph. Add before the existing "You came to this book" opening.

**What:**

```
You have been the Shaman. You have been the Challenger. 
You have been the Regent. The Architect. The Diplomat. The Sage.

You know what all six Faces feel like in your body. You have run the WAVE 
hundreds of times. You know which Face is your home and which you avoid, 
which channel is yours and which one costs you. You have done the inner work 
the book asked you to do.

Now the question is what you're going to do with it.
```

**Target:** +80 words. Purpose: makes the reader feel the cumulative weight of what they've walked through before asking them to walk further.

---

### 5. 🌍 Shaman — "Come Back" Somatic Completion Moment

**Location:** Section 5, just before the final "What are you going to build?" question.

**What:**

```
Pause here. One breath.

You have been through all of this. Not as an idea — as an actual walk. 
You arrived at this book with a specific ache, and you have been working 
with that ache for however long it took you to get here. You know what 
you know. You know what you don't. You have run the WAVE. You have walked 
through the Forest. You have come back.

You are ready.

Not ready to be finished. Ready to be useful.
```

**Target:** +90 words. Purpose: closes the WAVE loop. The book ends in forward motion without a moment of completion — this adds the Come Back that the WAVE requires.

## Reflection Prompts — Modify One

**Current Prompt 1:** "Which of the six Faces feels most like you? Which feels least like you?"

**Change to:** "Which Face is your home? Which Face have you been avoiding? Where is your specific gift — and where is the gap that keeps showing up?"

**Why:** Egan's Skilled Helper framing — prompts should surface unused resources, not invite identity abstraction. The new prompt is specific enough to answer in one sentence.

**Add a relational prompt after Prompt 4:**

**New Prompt 5:** "Who is the one specific person you could show this to first? Not publish for. Not post for. Show — in person, or message, or voice memo — this week?"

**Why:** The Diplomat's fix. The practice becomes real the moment it has an audience of one. A question that produces a name is more actionable than a question that produces an abstraction.

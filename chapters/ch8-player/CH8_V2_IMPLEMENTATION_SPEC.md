## What's Still Open

### Addition 9 [Architect] — "The Game Section Is Not the Practice" ⬅️ Do first
**Where:** Section 6 (Game), after the 5-move list, before the bars-engine paragraph.
**What:** 1-2 paragraphs clarifying that the 5 moves are not the practice — they are the map of the practice. The practice is what you do between the moves. A list of moves is not a practice any more than a recipe is a meal.
**Why:** The section currently goes straight from the 5-move list to the bars-engine example without this bridge. Without it, readers complete the chapter thinking they have the practice by having the moves.
**Words:** ~180
**Citation:** Egan — practice as ongoing, not episodic [^1]; Wilber — practice as developmental substrate, not information [^2]

### New Addition A [Player] — bars-engine Gets Its Named Moment in the Journey
**Where:** Section 5 (Journey), inside "The Walk" subsection, after the "What If You Don't Have a Game" subsection.
**What:** A new paragraph (not a subsection) naming bars-engine explicitly as the village's common ground — the playground players arrive at after finishing the book, where the allyship work becomes real: campaigns, quests, the non-profit's programs. This is what "walk into the specific problem" looks like concretely.
**Why:** bars-engine was introduced in the Practice section and referenced in the Game section. Now that we know it's the non-profit's platform and the village's common ground, it deserves a named moment in the Journey that makes this explicit to the reader — so they know there's a place to go.
**Words:** ~250
**Calibration:** Slightly promotional but honest — not a pitch, an invitation. "This is what exists when you're ready. You don't have to build it alone."

### New Addition B [Challenger] — "What If You're Not a Founder"
**Where:** Section 5 (Journey), expand the existing "What If You Don't Have a Game Yet?" subsection.
**What:** 2-3 paragraphs addressing the reader who doesn't see themselves as a founder type. Most people don't think of themselves as someone who starts things. But the Founder mode isn't about starting an organization — it's about the moment you decide to create something that didn't exist before you. Even one conversation. Even one practice with your kid. The Founder is a mode, not a personality.
**Why:** The existing subsection is a header + one paragraph. The reader who doesn't have a "big" Founder move in them leaves empty-handed. This expands the path so everyone has something.
**Words:** ~400

### New Addition C [Diplomat] — "You Don't Have to Build Alone"
**Where:** Section 5 (Journey), after "What's yours?" paragraph, before the failure section.
**What:** 1 paragraph: the village is not just the people who will receive what you build — it is the people who will build with you. bars-engine is the village's common ground. The non-profit is designed so that individual Founder moves plug into a larger structure. You bring your specific gift. Others bring theirs. The game is bigger than any one person.
**Why:** The chapter currently frames the Player as going first, alone. The non-profit structure means the Player doesn't go alone — they join a structure that was already being built.
**Words:** ~200

## Source Gap Analysis

| Source | Closing approach | MTGOA advantage |
|--------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Egan | 4-chapter practice manual — reader runs the model on real situation | MTGOA's bars-engine is the concrete practice engine Egan lacks |
| Wilber | "Shall we begin?" — practice as daily experiment | MTGOA's WAVE is simpler, more operational, more gamified |
| Laloux | "Committed people change the world" — direct address | MTGOA's Player arc is more personal, less organizational |
| Yu-kai Chou | Hero's Journey opens, doesn't close — reader at beginning | MTGOA closes fully — Yu-kai opens; deliberate structural choice |

**MTGOA's structural advantage:** bars-engine is the concrete bridge between "finishing the book" and "going to the village." No other source has this. It should be named explicitly in the Journey section.

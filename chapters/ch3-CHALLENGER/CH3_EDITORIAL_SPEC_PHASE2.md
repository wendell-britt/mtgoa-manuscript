## 🚨 CROSS-CHAPTER PATTERN FLAG

**Before reviewing Ch3 specifically: this pattern repeats across Ch1, Ch2, and Ch3.**

Every chapter follows the same structure:
1. Section 1: The Exile (why the face left the village)
2. Section 2: The Distortion (what the village does without the face)
3. Section 3: The Concept (what the face actually is)
4. Practical content (moves, EA, channels, gates)
5. Section 6: The Game (practical moves)
6. Section 7: Recap and Bridge (summary + what's next)

This structure worked for Ch1 (Forest/Shaman introduction). It's starting to feel mechanical by Ch3.
**Recommendation for Wendell:** By Ch4, the pattern should either evolve or become more self-aware. The structure itself isn't the problem — the reader will notice if Chapters 4–8 all read like the same chapter with different face names. Consider: does each face chapter need Section 1 and Section 2, or can the Exile/Distortion be compressed/hidden for faces where the reader already understands the dynamic?

**See:** Cross-chapter pattern spec at `CROSSCHAPTER_PATTERN_D_POWER_ALLERGY.md` (in chapters directory) — this already exists and has relevant structural guidance.

## Section 4: The Walk (Gates 1–8, Lines 369–590)

**What works:**
- Gate 1 (Protector): TheBAR prompt is clean and specific.
- Gate 3 (Skeptic): "Thunder and lightning arrive together — full sight at once" — this is the best opening line of any BAR prompt in the manuscript so far.
- Gate 5 (Fear): TheBAR prompt ("what channel was it — anger, fear, sadness, joy, neutrality") is sharp. The instruction to name from the body rather than analysis is correct.
- Gate 8 (Vulnerable Child): "Thunder resounds from the earth before it reaches the mind." — excellent image.

**What needs attention:**
- **Gate 5 label — "The Fear":** This is a WAVE state (Fear/Metal channel), not an emotional channel in the WAVE framework. The EA channels (Metal/Fear, Water/Sadness, Wood/Joy, Fire/Anger, Earth/Neutrality) are the five signal types. The WAVE states (Clean, Charge, Flood, Resolve) are where the energy is. Calling Gate 5 "The Fear" conflates a WAVE state with an EA channel type. This needs correction — the gate is about *being in Fear/Wave state*, not about the Fear emotional channel specifically.
  > **Fix:** Label as "The Fear" (WAVE state) to distinguish from the EA channel "Metal/Fear"

## Section 6: The Game (Lines 592–728)

**What works:**
- **Move 1: Name the Unnameable** — excellent. "Most situations have a specific thing that everyone has noticed and nobody has stated." This is sharp and practice-ready.
- **Move 5: Stay Past the Discomfort** — "the discomfort of holding a line is less costly than the cost of not holding it" — this is the line that will stick with readers.
- The 30-second protocol is a genuinely useful tool. Exactly right for the target reader.
- The Reckoning tool is excellent. The example conversation is exactly the right level of specificity.

**What needs attention:**
- **Move 2: "I'm not able to support this approach"** — this does not sound like how a real person refuses. It's corporate and weak. The clean no in Section 3 was supposed to be "no essay, no three-paragraph explanation, just no." Then Move 2 gives a soft被动 construction ("I'm not able to"). Either make it direct ("I won't support this approach") or give a real example of how the direct no sounds in practice.
  > **Fix:** Either (a) replace with direct no example, or (b) note that "I'm not able to support" is a professional register no — valid but needs to be acknowledged as different from the pure clean no
- **"Not a threat — a boundary"** (in Reckoning example): This self-interruption is awkward. The definition of consequence in Section 3 was "a natural result, not a threat." The example then says "I'm not threatening, I'm boundary-setting" which sounds defensive. The difference between a threat and a consequence needs to be cleaner.
  > **Fix:** Let the consequence speak for itself. "If it happens again, I raise it in the retrospective" is a natural consequence. The "not a threat, it's a boundary" line can be cut — the action already demonstrates the distinction.

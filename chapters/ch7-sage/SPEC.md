## The Stage Sequence (5 Stages)

**See → Switch → Serve → Release → Return**

1. **See:** Take in the whole field of altitudes. What game is being played?
2. **Switch:** Move to the altitude the situation actually requires.
3. **Serve:** Do the work that altitude calls for, without superiority.
4. **Release:** When the game is done — or when it's no longer serving — put it down.
5. **Return:** Come back to lower altitudes with dignity intact for everyone.

*This sequence mirrors the Architect's OMD cycle but is perspective-native: the Sage's practice is a cycle of seeing, choosing, acting, releasing, and returning.*

## The Exile

The Sage was exiled when the village decided that seeing meant leaving. That if you could see the pattern of the game, you were obliged to stop playing. The Sage said "I see this and I'm staying" and was called contradictory, uncommitted, insufficiently-partisan. The village kept a caricature of the Sage — the guru, the wise-one-at-a-distance — but exiled the actual Sage, which is the person who sees the whole thing and still sets the table.

---

## The Distortion

Without the Sage, the village lost the ability to name which altitude they were stuck at. Every conflict became existential. Nobody could step back and say we're playing the Red game, not the Green one — that's why this isn't working. The capacity to switch altitudes was lost. And a shadow Sage emerged: the person who saw everything and committed to nothing, mistaking perspective for wisdom.

**Q2 — Skillful Condescension (answering the open question):**
Naming the altitude can create conflict even when you're doing the right work — if you descend to help without respecting the person's language and dignity. Example: teachers get on their knees to talk to kids at eye level, not because kids can't look up, but because being talked down to is its own kind of harm. The trap is solving a problem at the altitude you're most comfortable in, even when it's totally irrelevant to the person you're trying to help. The Sage names the altitude AND speaks the language of the altitude they're helping from.

**Q3 — Games I Put Down:**
- **The Captain Save-a-Kid game** — I'm done with the game where we're supposed to trust everyone who asks for help without doing triage first. Help offered without discernment isn't help — it's a setup.
- **The Trauma Olympics** — the game where people compete to outdo each other's misery in order to be heard. I'm done with that. Misery is not credential.
- **The Dragging Horses to Water to Drink game** — hopefully self-explanatory. You can lead people to insight; you can't make them drink. And at some point, the dragging becomes the harm.

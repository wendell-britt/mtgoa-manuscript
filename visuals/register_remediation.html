The connective finding

I expected Igniting Joy to subordinate more. It does not — MTGOA runs 42.0 connectives per thousand words against 29.2. The difference is which connectives, and where they sit.

MTGOA's subordination is argumentative — because, which, before, where — and it sits after the main clause, so the sentence asserts and then defends. Igniting Joy's is procedural and conditional — when, if, by — and it sits before, so the sentence sets a situation and hands the reader a move inside it.

Your last book puts the reader in a situation and gives her something to do. This manuscript states a proposition and argues for it. The second reads as instruction from a distance. The first reads as instruction from beside her.

The collision

Six sites went from 330 words to 382. The register operation costs words — about sixteen percent where it applies. That runs against the cut mandate, and averaging it away would be dishonest.

Here is the reconciliation. The manuscript holds 227 runs of three or more adjacent short sentences, carrying 4,190 words, and they are not one thing.

Your last book already solves this: the tool chapters run numbered steps as actual lists and keep the running prose at 18.8 words a sentence, so the eye gets the list and the ear gets the paragraph. MTGOA currently makes the paragraph do the list's job. Net across both classes the register pass is close to word-neutral. It delivers no cuts, and it also spends none. The cuts still have to come from Tier 1 and the recap cap.

What we take from Existential Kink

Igniting Joy has the lowest variance of the four corpora — sd 7.6, burstiness 7.3, with 55.7% of sentences in the 15-to-26-word band. Consistency is most of what MTGOA needs. At ninety thousand words it is also a register that could go flat. Elliott runs sd 20.6 and burstiness 16.9. She has the range the target lacks.

1 — The long haul into the snap. Twenty-seven times in Existential Kink the swing between adjacent sentences exceeds thirty words with a seven-word neighbour.

"The more we're willing to 'get off on every stroke' that comes to us, the more pleasure and fun we can have in our lives, and the more magnetic we can become. Sadly, most of us turn ourselves off."

Not a new device for you — a redistribution of one you already have too much of. Chapter 1, verbatim: "When I said I held myself hostage for three years, I made it sound like something that happened to me. It wasn't." Every short sentence pulled out of a run and set after a long one raises the ratio from both ends at once.

2 — First person doing narrative work. Elliott runs 33.3% first-person sentences, 21% of them narrative past. Igniting Joy runs 4.9% and almost none narrative, so it cannot supply this and Elliott can. MTGOA already runs 9.6% — more than double Igniting Joy — so the deficit is not quantity. Only 14% of the manuscript's first-person sentences are narrative past; the rest are present-tense editorial asides, which read as commentary rather than testimony. Two slots already stand open: Ch4's vacated first-person slot and the Ch7 testimony slot.
3 — The turn to the reader mid-argument. Elliott interrupts her own exposition to address the reader — Sound good? — and it works because it arrives after a long construction. Questions per 1k: Elliott 2.9, IJ 3.3, MTGOA 2.5. Smallest of the three levers. I would spend nothing on it before August 1.

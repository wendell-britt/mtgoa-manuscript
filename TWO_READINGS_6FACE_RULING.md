## What the instrument actually does

Worth stating before ruling on where it lives, because the options score differently once you see it.

> Read it first as this: you have the Sage's gift, and you have lived the Sage's exile…
>
> *(This reading comforts in a particular way. It explains the distance you sometimes feel in your progressive spaces… It gives the distance a story. A good story. A story that isn't wrong.)*
>
> The second reading: … the fluency itself became a place to stand at a careful distance. The seeing became the destination rather than the beginning.
>
> **The first reading puts the gap between you and the village. The second reading puts the gap inside you.**

This is not a framing device. It is **a shadow-detection instrument aimed precisely at Jordan's failure mode**: the progressive reader who recognises herself in an exile story and takes the flattering reading. *I see what others don't, and that is why I stand slightly outside.* The instrument makes her check the other reading without ever telling her which is true — *"You'll know which one belongs to you. The work keeps going either way."*

On the ally criterion this is among the highest-value paragraphs in the book. Which is why it currently appearing **once, in Chapter 8, under a sentence claiming she has been doing it all along** is the defect worth fixing properly rather than cheaply.

**C exists because of a dependency A ignores.** The instrument opens *"You just read a story about someone who sees clearly and gets exiled for it."* It operates on a fresh exile story. **ch1 has no exile story** — its sections run myths, tokens, tickets, why a game, which game, six Faces, build your character, oath, BAR. The first exile in the book is `ch3:72`, *Section 1: The Exile*, the Shaman. Every Face chapter after it opens the same way.

So A requires rewriting the instrument into an abstract form and teaching it before Jordan has anything to run it on. C plants it where it has fuel.

## 🌊 Shaman — Felt sense

**Ally:** the instrument is a felt test, not a cognitive one — *"if something moved while you read, a recognition, a* yes." It needs a body response to work on. Teaching it in ch1, where nothing has moved yet, teaches the shape of a feeling she has not had.

**Completion:** ch1's job is to make her feel found. A test that pre-emptively questions her recognition, before she has had one, lands as *you are probably fooling yourself* on page 20. That is a drop-off risk at the exact place the book can least afford one.

**Under C** the first recognition arrives in ch3 — the Shaman, the Face most readers click with — and the test arrives one beat later, warm.

**Shaman verdict: C.** A puts the doubt before the recognition, which inverts the felt order.

## ⚔️ Challenger — What gets wrong

**B's shadow.** It is the edit that makes the sentence true and the book worse. Jordan reaches ch8 and meets a powerful self-audit **once**, at the end, with no chance to have practised it on the five Faces where it would have caught something. The defect closes; the instrument stays wasted.

**A's shadow.** Writing an abstract version into ch1 to satisfy a spec written in May. The spec named ch1 before anyone checked whether ch1 had an exile story. **Honouring the letter of a sweep that was wrong about its own location is how this defect got here.**

**The line:** the fix has to make ch8:117 true *and* put the instrument where it does work. B fails the second. A fails it in a subtler way — a test taught cold is technically present and practically unused.

**Challenger verdict: C.** And reject A specifically on the grounds that it is spec-compliance rather than book-making.

## 🏛 Regent — What to preserve

**Preserve the ch8 paragraph verbatim.** It is the strongest instance and it is Face-specific in a way that earns its place — the Sage is genuinely where the two readings are hardest to tell apart. Any option that rewrites it (B) spends the best copy in the set to fix a sentence.

**Preserve ch1's load.** `shipcheck` already reports **four unkept ch1 promises** — the Reader's Oath, the myth she wrote, her superpower, the BAR deck, all promised in ch1 and never closed. **ch1 is the most over-promised chapter in the book.** A adds a fifth commitment to the chapter that already cannot pay its debts.

**Regent verdict: C.** The one option that costs nothing already-standing and adds nothing to ch1's ledger.

## 🧠 Architect — Structure and integrity

The instrument's precondition is *an exile story just read*. That precondition is satisfied at exactly seven places in the book: ch3, ch4, ch5, ch6, ch8, ch9 Section 1, and ch7's thesis variant.

```
A   teach at ch1        precondition NOT met — requires rewriting the instrument abstract
B   teach at ch8        precondition met, but only once, at the end
C   teach at ch3        precondition met, first available site, five chapters of runway
```

**C also repairs the claim without touching it.** *"You've taken it since the first page"* remains an overstatement under C — the first page is ch1, the test lands in ch3 — so C needs one word: *"since the first Face."* One-word edit, not a rewrite.

**Architect verdict: C**, with the ch8 sentence corrected from *first page* to *first Face*.

## 🎭 Diplomat — What bridges

**The bridge at risk is trust.** Under the status quo the book asserts a shared history that did not happen. A reader who notices concludes she missed something, and the honest ones assume it was their fault. That is the single most expensive thing a book can do to a reader at chapter 8, because she has no way to check and every reason to blame herself.

**B repairs the trust and nothing else.** **C repairs the trust and makes the claim retroactively true**, which is the stronger form: she was not told she had done something she hadn't, because by ch8 she will have done it five times.

**Diplomat verdict: C.**

---

## 📖 Sage — Panoramic view

Ask what the book is *for* at the whole-arc level. Six Faces, each opening on an exile, each inviting recognition. **The recognition is the book's primary engine and also its primary hazard** — recognition that flatters is how a reader finishes an allyship book more comfortable than she started.

The two-readings test is the only device in the manuscript that guards that hazard. It currently fires **once, in the eighth chapter, after the engine has run six times unguarded.**

That is the real defect. The false sentence is a symptom. Fixing the sentence (B) leaves the engine unguarded; teaching it cold in ch1 (A) leaves it unguarded in practice.

**And the panoramic answer goes past C:** if the test is what makes recognition safe, one instance is a minimum, not a target. C plants it at the first exile and lets ch8's *"take it again here, where it cuts sharpest"* mean what it says.

**Sage verdict: C, and treat it as the floor.** The reachable version is a light recurrence — one or two lines at ch4 and ch6, not a full beat — so the ch8 instance is a genuine culmination.

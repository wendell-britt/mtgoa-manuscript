## Wendell Britt — training that survives the field

**Most training does not survive contact with the field.** I know because I built some that did
not. A course I made in 2020 had under a ten percent completion rate, and everything I teach now
came out of rebuilding it around one question: why the training works during the session and is
gone by the second week of the season.

**Here is the short version.** People are taught what to say and almost nothing about what
is happening in the person in front of them, or in themselves. I work a model of five channels —
fear, sadness, joy, anger, neutrality — each telling a facilitator something different about what
a participant needs next. Fear that gets managed instead of metabolized produces two outcomes
every facilitator will recognize: the participant who goes when they should not have, and the one
who checks out for the rest of the day.

### Sessions I run

- **The Participant Who Won't Go: Working Fear Instead of Around It.** This session works the
  ninety seconds at the base of the element. Participants leave with a script for that moment and
  a way to debrief it that does not ask anyone to perform a breakthrough they did not have.
- **Same Kids, Fewer Staff: Training That Holds When the Budget Doesn't.** This one is for
  programs running the same number of kids on greener seasonal staff. Participants redesign one
  piece of their own pre-season schedule before they leave.
- **The Six Roles a Group Needs.** This gives facilitators a vocabulary for what a group is
  missing in the moment. Groups that cannot name the roles keep failing in the same direction and
  cannot say why.

**Formats.** A conference session runs sixty to ninety minutes, interactive, on real scenarios
from the group. The material also builds as a half-day workshop or a provider PD program, and it
works better across a cohort of eight sessions than in one. I price per seat rather than as a
speaking fee.

# Launch sequencing — the foreground machine

**Wendell, 2026-09-03:** *"the real issue is the pivot I'm looking to do — getting the podcasts up
and running, develop guests, start work on the first Book Launch tour stop in Seattle and a
virtual event. Figuring out when to do those and when to start selling tickets while the longer
outreach happens in the background."*

**The reframe, stated plainly.** The speaking sends (`OUTREACH_PACK`) are a slow background
pipeline that funds the print run and does not need a calendar. The foreground is a launch
machine with four moving parts, and three of them are already half-built:

- **The podcast** — strategy exists (`MARKETING_STRATEGY §10`). Two guests are already named:
  **AJ Cohen** (power / Hollywood / credibility) and **Ike** (integral / the Blue Sky lineage).
  The standing rule: record before the tour, not during it.
- **The virtual launch** — Event 1 in `MTGOA_Launch_Spec`, the "ignition moment." Copy is drafted
  in `PARTIFUL_EVENT_PAGES` (the July 18 date slipped; it needs a new one). Donation-based,
  recorded, evergreen.
- **The Seattle in-person stop** — new, and the move to Seattle is what makes it possible. Nothing
  built yet.
- **Background outreach** — the five speaking sends, already drafted, drip out independently.

**The one sequencing law everything else follows:** *the podcast builds the audience, the virtual
launch converts it, the Seattle stop monetizes the warmest local slice.* Attention has to exist
before a ticket is worth selling, which is why the podcast leads and tickets open late.

## When tickets open, and why then

**This is the question you asked, so here it is as a rule rather than a row.**

- **Virtual launch — open registration at W-4, the moment the date is locked.** It is free /
  donation, so there is no reason to wait; every registrant is a name on the list and a person the
  podcast can warm up. Early and wide.
- **Seattle in-person — open paid ticket sales at W-4, and not before.** Two reasons. A local
  ticketed event sells on a **4-week runway** — earlier and the urgency leaks out; later and you
  lose the promo window. And it must open **after Episode 1 (AJ Cohen) has dropped**, because that
  episode is the credibility signal that makes a stranger pay. Selling Seattle tickets before the
  podcast has proof-of-seriousness in the feed is selling into silence.
- **The dependency in one line:** no Seattle tickets until the venue and date are locked (W-6) and
  the first episode is live (W-5). W-4 satisfies both.

## The guest engine — how to develop beyond AJ and Ike

**`MARKETING_STRATEGY §10` gives the test each guest passes:** they serve a level/audience, they
generate content you can clip, and they signal to the warm crowd that serious people are engaged.
AJ and Ike cover power and integral. **Book four or five more, one per lane**, so the feed reads as
a body of work rather than a favor pull:

| lane | what the guest brings | why it feeds the launch |
|---|---|---|
| **The field** (outdoor-ed / facilitation / camp) | a practitioner from the world the outreach targets | turns a cold speaking send into a warm one — a WRMC or ACA name who has been on the pod is a different email |
| **Allyship / DEI adjacent** | someone doing the work the book is about | the core-audience credibility signal |
| **Seattle local** | a PNW voice with a local following | seeds the in-person stop's ticket base |
| **A skeptic / the hard questions** | someone who will push on the framework | the most clippable episodes, and it pre-empts the "is this real" objection |
| **A backer / origin voice** | someone from the six-year story | closes a loop with the 361 and gives the launch its heart |

**Do not fill these with strangers.** Draw first from the people who already believe — the standing
rule in §10 is *show up to the people who already believe before you feel ready.*

## The four decisions that set the dates

**The calendar above is relative. These four make it real, and they are yours:**

1. **W0 — the virtual launch date.** Everything anchors here. What month?
2. **Is the book on sale now, or does the launch wait on the proof/print run?** If it is
   purchasable, the events drive to a buy link. If it waits on the ~$6,000 print run, the virtual
   launch drives to the ebook + a pre-order and the Seattle stop sells the printed book off the
   first run. This changes the ask, not the sequence.
3. **Seattle stop — do you have a venue/month, or should the plan hold ~W+3 as a target?**
4. **The podcast — does the feed exist, or is standing it up the W-8 job?**

**What does not need a decision:** the background outreach. It is already drafted and it runs on
its own clock, gating nothing here. Send it and forget it.

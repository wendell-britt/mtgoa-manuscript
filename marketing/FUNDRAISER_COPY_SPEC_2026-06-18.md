## 1. THE CORE REFRAME

This fundraiser is not about a car. It is not about crisis. It is not about a creator who needs help.

**It is a barn raising.**

Someone went into the workshop for two months, built four real things, and now needs hands to help lift the barn into place. The car is the final missing piece of an otherwise complete picture. The ask is not "rescue me." The ask is "come help me finish what I started."

This reframe must survive every draft, every edit, every channel. If a sentence drifts back toward victim or messiah — cut it.

## 2. THE STORY ARC (Seven Beats)

Every long-form piece uses all seven. Short-form picks the beats most relevant to the channel and audience level.

### Beat 1 — The Pattern (lead here, not with the car)
Three times. Always at the threshold.
- Van stolen: one month before the Kickstarter launch
- Kia stolen: when the first completed manuscript was finished
- Kia exploded: when developmental edits and early copy edits began

Do not explain the pattern. Name it and trust the reader to feel it. "Three times. Always at the threshold. The community showed up every time."

The phrase to deploy: **"This book is my vehicle to the life I want to live. And it seems to be a jealous vehicle."**

This line is the lede for Magenta. It is the hook for strangers. It is the thing that breaks the creator-fundraiser genre. Lead with it or near it.

### Beat 2 — The Hermit Mode (proof of change)
Feb–April 2026. Stopped starting new things. Finished what already existed.
- The book (developmental + copy edits in progress)
- The Allyship Deck (ready to launch)
- The MTGOA RPG (draft complete, layout done, early access ready)
- The nonprofit design

One or two sentences only. "I didn't start anything new. I went quiet and I finished." This is the Red beat — survival proof, action over feeling.

### Beat 3 — The Interrupt (let the image carry it)
April 22. Engine blows. Catastrophic, not gradual.
Use the photos. Don't narrate the emotion — let the blown engine image land it.
One sentence of copy: "Not a slow fade. A sudden, catastrophic end."

### Beat 4 — What's Different This Time (evidence before vision)
Previous asks: help me build. This ask: here's what I built.

Name the four artifacts briefly and concretely:
- Book jacket (visible)
- First chapter (readable)
- Allyship Deck (playable)
- MTGOA RPG early access digital edition (a real designed book, not a promise)

This is the Orange beat. One concrete credential: the Kickstarter. "Kickstarter-funded. Backed by real people. Now finishing." Evidence before vision, always.

### Beat 4.5 — Kickstarter Accountability Statement (Amber)
Over 400 people backed this book before it existed. They've waited three years longer than promised. August 1 is the ship date. This fundraiser is part of how that promise gets kept.

Draft direction: "400+ people backed this before it existed. They've waited three years longer than I promised. This fundraiser is part of how I get it across the finish line and into their hands."

This is simultaneously the Orange traction number AND the Amber accountability moment. It also reframes the urgency: this isn't just about the car. It's about honoring 400 people who believed early.

### Beat 5 — The Ask (practical, not sentimental)
If I'm going on tour with a book that's almost done, I need a car to do it.
Goal: $8,500. Here's what that gets me. Here's the clock: July 18 virtual launch is in 30 days.

The fundraiser is a 30-day launch sprint, not an open-ended campaign. July 18 is the urgency mechanism. Use it in every channel.

Simple accountability statement for Amber: what the money is for, what happens when it's raised, what people can expect. Not elaborate — just clear.

### Beat 6 — The Offer (tools, not charity)
"You don't just get to feel good. You get the tools."

Reframe: playing the game is how you get better at helping. The deck, the book, the RPG — these are the instruments of the practice. Frame the tiers as access to the work, not rewards for generosity.

For the RPG specifically: "You get the draft and your feedback shapes the final version." This is participation, not preorder. The cynical reader needs to feel the difference.

For the Founder tier: lead with this in ally DMs. Six people at $150 = $900. This is the fastest path to the $800 day-one goal. Don't bury it.

One line for Teal: "This ask is itself an allyship practice." One sentence. Don't explain it. Teal will find it and carry it.

### Beat 7 — The Invitation
July 18 virtual launch. July 19 dance party (Portland, Bruised Banana, PWYW). July 20 Integral Emergence Game (Portland, Bruised Banana, daytime).

Ko-fi link. Patreon for sustained support. One action per channel. Don't stack CTAs.

## 3. THE POP-CULTURE FRAME MAP

Three frames. Each serves different levels and different moments in the copy. Do not use all three in any single piece — pick the one that fits the channel and audience.

### Frame A — Frodo at Mount Doom
**Serves:** Magenta (initiation), Teal (the loop)
**When to use:** Long-form Ko-fi page. The emotional opening. Anywhere the endurance of the work needs to be honored.
**The specific resonance:** Frodo didn't choose to drop the Ring — Gollum took it. The point is he carried it all the way there. The copy equivalent: "I didn't finish perfectly. I carried it all the way to the fire." This is the initiation story Magenta needs to witness. It is also the Dormammu loop Teal recognizes — not triumph, not power, just showing up to the threshold one more time from a different place.
**What to avoid:** Don't make it self-pitying. Frodo didn't ask for sympathy at Mount Doom. He just didn't put it down.

### Frame B — Tony Stark in the Cave / Iron Man 2
**Serves:** Red (proof of survival), Orange (R&D as strategy)
**When to use:** Social posts, ally DMs, anywhere the hermit mode needs to be explained fast.
**The specific resonance for Red:** Built something with what he had. In the dark. Without applause. The hermit mode IS the cave. "I made four things while nobody was watching. Now I'm back."
**The specific resonance for Orange:** Synthesizing the new element. The hermit period as R&D. Came out with product. Elegant, self-derived, evidence-based.
**What to avoid:** Don't use the arc-reactor-poisoning angle — that's the suffering frame Red will reject.

### Frame C — Dr. Strange and the Dormammu Loop
**Serves:** Teal (systems wisdom), Green (relational courage)
**When to use:** The meta-level moment. The one sentence that names what this fundraiser actually is.
**The specific resonance:** The conclusion wasn't overwhelming power. It was the willingness to lose over and over again until the pattern changed. This fundraiser is not the first ask. It is the ask made from a different place than the ones before it. The loop ran. The variable changed.
**The line:** "I've made this ask before. This time I'm making it from somewhere different. That's the only move that ever worked."
**What to avoid:** Don't over-explain the metaphor. One sentence, then move on. Teal will extrapolate. Everyone else will feel it without needing to understand it.

## 4. LEVEL-BY-LEVEL STRATEGY

Each level needs something different. The long-form Ko-fi page is the only place that serves all six. Everything else is written for a primary level with secondary awareness of adjacent ones.

### Magenta — Shaman
**Primary need:** Witness the initiation. Feel the sacred pattern.
**Copy strategy:** Lead with the tripling. Name the jealous vehicle. Don't rush past it into logistics. Give Magenta a moment to feel the weight of what was carried.
**Key move:** "Three times. Always at the threshold. The community showed up every time." Let that land before moving on.
**Avoid:** Explaining the mythology. Either they feel it or they don't. Don't translate it into rational language.

### Red — Challenger
**Primary need:** Proof of survival. Respect for action over feeling.
**Copy strategy:** Hermit mode as the cave. Four artifacts as the proof. Zero apologetic language. Every sentence lands like a fact from someone still standing.
**Key move:** "I made four things while nobody was watching. Now I'm back. Here's what I built."
**Avoid:** Any sentence that sounds like an apology. Any moment of "I know this is a lot to ask." The word "accompaniment" — Red will physically recoil.

### Amber — Regent
**Primary need:** Legitimacy. Accountability. The right process being honored.
**Copy strategy:** Lead with the Kickstarter credential. Include a simple accountability statement (what the money is for, what happens when it's raised). The nonprofit is future-state — don't lean on it for credibility with Amber yet.
**Key move:** "Kickstarter-funded. Backed by real people. Now finishing. Here's exactly where the money goes."
**Avoid:** Vague institutional language. "We're designing the nonprofit" without evidence. Ko-fi needs to be presented as a structured giving mechanism, not a tip jar.

### Orange — Architect
**Primary need:** Evidence of demand. A flywheel they can see.
**Copy strategy:** One concrete traction number (Kickstarter backers, email list, pre-orders — whichever is strongest). The deck-to-book-to-RPG-to-app funnel named briefly. Hermit mode as R&D.
**Key move:** Give them one number. Then show them the funnel. "Here's what already has traction. Here's where it goes."
**Avoid:** Vision without evidence. Lifetime app access framed as guaranteed — frame it as founding membership with transparent development timeline.

### Green — Diplomat
**Primary need:** Feel invited. Feel like they're already part of the story. No status hierarchy anxiety.
**Copy strategy:** One moment of genuine softness, early and brief. Name the community that has shown up before. Honor system Kickstarter tier gets a sentence of its own — it's Green's love language. Dance party copy leads with joy, not fundraising mechanics.
**Key move:** "Every time something broke, the community showed up. You're part of that story whether you give $10 or $200."
**Avoid:** Too much armor. If the whole piece reads as "I don't need rescue," Green won't know where to put their care and they'll scroll on.

### Teal — Sage
**Primary need:** The meta-level. Authenticity. Complexity honored not flattened.
**Copy strategy:** One sentence that names the fundraiser as an allyship practice in action. Don't hide the framework — trust Teal to find it. The Dr. Strange frame is for Teal.
**Key move:** "This ask is itself an allyship practice. I almost wrote this from resentment. I had to do some work before I could ask real."
**Avoid:** Sanding off the complexity to appeal to a broader audience. Teal came for the real thing. Give it to them in one sentence and trust them to carry it.

## 5. VOICE DIRECTIVES

- First person. Direct. A little rueful, a lot alive.
- Not polished. Not performed. Contractions throughout.
- Sentences that breathe. Short ones after long ones.
- No inspirational poster language.
- No "journey." No "chapter" (unless earned, once).
- No "I need your help." No "wouldn't be possible without you."
- The tone is: someone who figured something out and is choosing to tell you about it. Not performing vulnerability to earn sympathy.
- Write the first draft fast and badly. Chase it. The aliveness either makes it onto the page or it doesn't. Editing is where the spec does its work.

### Hard banned phrases
"Failing forward" · "Silver lining" · "Hard times" · "Grateful for the support" · "I need your help" · "This is about more than me" · "Wouldn't be possible without you" · "Accompaniment" (except in Teal-facing copy) · Any sentence that sounds like an apology

## 11. THE APP INTERVIEW

**Q1:** What is the MTGOA app at its most basic?

For someone who has never heard of allyship, the MTGOA app is a place where people who want to learn how to sustainably help others can achieve a life of wellbeing in a gamified platform with ACTUAL real-life issues they can impact. The app solves the theory/application gap in two directions: for allies (route directly to someone who needs help right now) and for organizations (design allyship games people actually want to be part of).

Core mechanic: learning how to create your character, using the most powerful tools you already have to directly impact people's lives.

The book is the dojo. The app is the street.

**Q2:** What does bars-engine do that a book can't?

BARs capture the impactful things that show up in your life and turn them into evidence of growth. The book gives you the map. BARs are what happen when you walk the territory and mark where you've been. Information vs. metabolized insight. The app is the metabolization engine. The book fills the tank. bars-engine burns it.

**Q3:** Who is the person whose life changes most dramatically?

BEFORE: Doomscrolling. Consuming information about how terrible everything is. Burnt out and looking for ways to contribute but living in a way where that contribution will never actually come.

AFTER MASTERY: Knows ahead of time which charged information is their business and can be metabolized, and which to release. Has built a network of allies. Is having fun while doing it.

The transformation: from passive consumer of crisis to active participant in repair.

**Q4:** The vision being hidden.

The app separates the wheat from the chaff. Generates sustainable and predictable impact for those who play fully. Anyone can develop their allyship superpower. Learn in public. The app is essentially the beneficent matrix.

Real competitive frame: Mindvalley competitor, not an activism app. Personal development and impact platform where the curriculum is real-world allyship and the classroom is other people's actual lives.

**Q5:** What does beta access mean for Patreon subscribers?

Not a waitlist. Not a preview. Founding membership in a live system being built around the people inside it. Beta subscribers are not waiting for the app to be finished. They are the reason it gets finished the right way.

**Q6:** How does the app change allyship in a way the book alone cannot?

Copy-safe version: "The book tells you what an ally does. The app makes being an ally feel better than not being one."

## 12. FIVE YEAR FUTURES — SIX GAME MASTER ANALYSIS

**Magenta:** The story that helping is sacrifice dies. A generation learns through play that their wellbeing and others' are the same motion.

**Red:** The driven and purposeless find a game worth playing. Genuinely dangerous allies — skilled, fast, networked — pointed at repair instead of extraction.

**Amber:** Social work, therapy, coaching, and community organizing get shared infrastructure. The coordination failure between skilled helpers gets a partial solution.

**Orange:** Impact becomes measurable. BARs create a public record. Organizations find skilled helpers with evidence instead of credentials. Reputation compounds.

**Green:** The doomscrollers find each other. Communities of practice form around causes, skills, geographies.

**Teal:** A critical mass of people have an experience-based encounter with complexity they could not think their way out of — and metabolized it through action, community, and play. [KEEP UNDER WRAPS IN PUBLIC COPY]

**What all six agree on:** The app working is not the scary part. The scary part is that it is possible, it is close, and the only thing between now and five years from now is whether you get back on the road.

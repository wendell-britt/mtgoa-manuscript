## I. The Reader You Are Right Now

You care about the world. You have cared about the world for long enough that the caring has started to feel like a weight rather than a direction. You consume information about what is broken faster than you can find places to send the energy that information generates. Some days the doomscroll is not even about staying informed anymore. It is about staying in contact with the thing you cannot stop caring about, because at least scrolling feels like paying attention, and paying attention feels like the minimum you owe.

You have backed things before. You have meant to do more and done less. You have bought books that sat on the shelf and taken courses that got half-finished and felt the particular guilt of someone who wanted to help and couldn't quite make the motion translate into impact.

That is the reader you are right now. Not as a criticism. As a recognition. Because the thing I have been building for the last several years was built specifically for the person described in the paragraph above. Not for the already-disciplined. Not for the already-activated. For the person who cares deeply and has not yet found the infrastructure that makes caring actionable.

That person is who this is for. If that is not you, this page is probably not for you either. No hard feelings.

If it is — stay with me.

## II. The Pattern

Three times. Always at the threshold.

The van was stolen one month before the Kickstarter launched. The community showed up and helped me put my life back together.

The Kia was stolen when I finished the first completed manuscript. The community showed up and helped me get back on the road.

Last April, right as I moved into final developmental edits and early copy work — the Kia's engine exploded. Not a slow fade. A sudden, catastrophic end.

[photo of the blown engine]

I have stopped calling this bad luck. You do not receive the same message three times at the same threshold and file it under coincidence. This book is my vehicle to the life I want to live. And it seems to be a jealous vehicle.

I am telling you the pattern before I tell you anything else because I need you to know that this story is not primarily about a car. The car is the latest data point in something larger. What that larger thing is — I will let you decide. What I know is that every time something broke, a community formed around the repair. And the repair always turned out to be about more than the thing that broke.

After the engine blew, I did not start anything new.

For two months, I went quiet. No new projects. No new promises. Just finishing what was already started — which is a different discipline entirely from starting, and one I had not fully practiced before.

Here is what exists on the other side of that silence:

The book is written. Mastering the Game of Allyship — the thing over 400 people backed on Kickstarter before it existed, the thing that is running three years past its original delivery date — is in final editorial passes. The jacket is complete. The first chapter is ready to read right now.

The Allyship Deck is a fully designed digital card game that teaches the framework through play. It is ready to deliver.

The MTGOA RPG is a fully designed digital book in early access. Your feedback as a backer shapes the final version. You are not waiting for something finished — you are participating in how it finishes.

And then there is bars-engine.

bars-engine is the app. It is also the thing I have been most careful about describing, because the full version of what it is trying to do is large enough that saying it plainly risks sounding like someone who has been alone with an idea for too long.

I am going to say it plainly anyway.

## IV. The App and What It Is Actually Trying to Do

The book teaches you what an ally does. The app makes being an ally feel better than not being one.

That sentence is doing more work than it looks like. Let me unpack it once and then I will leave it alone.

Most social impact platforms teach you the skill or provide the opportunity. bars-engine does both in the same place and routes between them. You collect BARs — insights that arrive from your actual life, things that charge you, things that move you, things that make you angry or sad or alive. The app asks you to add context, to compost what is not actually yours to carry, to mature what remains into quests and campaigns and actions you can take with other people in real time.

The doomscroll gives you information with nowhere to go. bars-engine gives the information somewhere to go and people to go there with.

Therapists, coaches, social workers, and community organizers can be quest givers on the platform — designing campaigns that their communities can actually participate in, solving the coordination failure between skilled helpers that has been burning people out for decades. You bring your superpower. The platform finds the person who needs it. You help faster than you thought you could. You have more fun than you expected. The impact is legible — not just felt, but visible, trackable, shareable.

This is a Mindvalley competitor with something Mindvalley cannot offer: a direct route from your personal development to another person's real life. You do not just get better. You put the better to use immediately.

I have been holding this vision back until the book had enough traction to make it credible. The book has the traction. Four hundred people believed before there was anything to show. The time for holding back is done.

## V. Why It Has Taken This Long

I want to answer the honest question before you ask it.

It took this long because I was not trying to change the world. I was trying to change six worlds — simultaneously, with one framework, one body, and one set of tools.

Six worlds that do not share a language. Six worlds that do not share values. Six worlds that in some cases actively distrust each other's version of reality. The person who needs the sacred story of initiation and the person who needs the evidence of traction and the person who needs the legitimate institution and the person who needs the invitation to belong — these people do not agree on what "better" means. Serving all of them with the same work, without collapsing any of them, is not ambitious. It is structurally almost impossible.

I know that because I tried it and it took years longer than I said it would and cost more than I had available and broke three cars.

I am also telling you this because it is the most important thing I know about allyship after everything I have learned: to increase wellbeing from a systems perspective means increasing it across all the levels that define wellbeing simultaneously. Not sequentially. Not for the easiest audience first. All of them at once. That is what Teal work actually is. That is what this has been.

The reason it is worth finishing is the same reason it was hard to build. You cannot make something that serves six worlds without it being genuinely difficult. The difficulty is the proof of the scope.

## VI. What the Next Five Years Look Like If This Works

I am not going to promise you a specific future. I am going to tell you what six different people who understand this work from six different angles each believe becomes possible.

The person who thinks in sacred patterns believes that a generation learns through play that their wellbeing and others' are the same motion — and that helping stops being heroic and becomes simply what people do.

The person who thinks in power and respect believes that the driven and purposeless find a game worth playing — and that some of the most capable people on earth get pointed at repair instead of extraction.

The person who thinks in institutions and legitimacy believes that social work, therapy, coaching, and community organizing get a shared nervous system for the first time — and that the coordination failure between skilled helpers gets a partial solution.

The person who thinks in evidence and markets believes that impact becomes measurable, that BAR portfolios become credentials, that the people who showed up early become the most sought-after practitioners in their fields.

The person who thinks in belonging and care believes that the doomscrollers find each other — and that the isolation that was making the caring impossible breaks.

And the person who thinks in systems — the one I am writing this version for — believes that a critical mass of people have an experience-based encounter with complexity they could not think their way out of, and metabolize it anyway, through action and community and play. That the Spiral moves. Not because anyone lectured anyone. Because the game made the next level feel like relief instead of threat.

I am not telling you which of those futures to want. I am telling you that all six of them depend on the same thing: getting back on the road.

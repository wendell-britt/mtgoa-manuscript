## Notes on the ask, and three things not to change

**Where it sits, and why.** Directly after the print status. By that point the reader is
holding *he's $6,000 short* and has just formed the question *what can I do?* — the ask answers
a question you created honestly rather than one you manufactured. It also comes **after** you've
explicitly declined their money, which is what makes it land as a request rather than a
walk-back.

**Keep "past what it costs me to stay alive and keep the lights on."** Most people write *every
penny goes to the print run*, which nobody believes. Yours is more credible because it's true —
and it's the anti-martyrdom value stated in one clause, in a book that spends a chapter on why
depletion isn't proof of devotion. Don't tidy it.

**Cut the wink.** You said *(3-4 speaking gigs would cover printing and shipping, wink wink)* —
to me, and it's the right instinct. But on the page the math goes straight: *three or four
engagements covers the whole thing.* A wink is a soft-sell tell, and this reader is trained to
catch it. The plain number is stronger and it's the same information.

**"You don't have to pitch me"** is doing real work. The reason warm intros don't happen isn't
unwillingness — it's that people don't know what to say and would rather say nothing. Taking the
writing off their plate converts a good intention into a name in your inbox.

**Letting them forward the PDF is the strongest thing in this update, and it isn't close.** It
solves the intro problem completely: the backer no longer has to explain who you are or why
you're worth ninety minutes — they send the book, and the person deciding reads the actual
argument. A one-pager makes claims. The book *is* the evidence, and the failure story in
Chapter 9 is more persuasive than any credential you could list.

It's also consistent with everything else the brand does. The book already says nothing is gated
behind an inbox. *"I'm not going to police that"* is that same position, stated in one line, in a
context where most authors would be adding a watermark.

**One thing to watch:** this only reads as generous because it's *targeted*. "Send it to whoever
you're pitching me to" is purposeful. "Share it with anyone" would be a different message and
would quietly tell 371 people the thing they bought is worth nothing. Keep the frame narrow.

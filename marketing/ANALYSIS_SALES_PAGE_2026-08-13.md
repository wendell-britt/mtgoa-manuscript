# The sales page, analysed for conversion

**Wendell, 2026-08-13:** *"now the book is done I'd like to redo the gumroad page to be more in
alignment with the final product and the ICA."*

**This is the analysis, not the rewrite.** It ranks what to change by how much it moves sales,
separates that from what to change because it is untrue, and names the four facts that have to
be settled before any copy gets written.

**What I could not check:** the live Gumroad page. The network egress proxy blocks
`wendellbritt.gumroad.com`, so everything below is against the draft in the request and the
repo's own material, not against what is currently published.

---

## 1 · The research, cut down to what applies here

**Treat the page as a landing page, not a catalogue entry**, on Problem → Agitate → Solution →
Proof → Offer. High-performing Gumroad pages convert at **4–8%**; poor ones under **1%**. The
single most common failure is spending months on the product and fifteen minutes on the page.

**Ratings are the strongest single predictor of sales on Gumroad.** Products at 4.5–4.9 stars
average **1,197 sales** against **18** for products with no rating — a 66× gap. That is the
largest effect size in any of the sources, and it is the one thing the draft has no plan for.

**Price.** Gumroad ebooks average **$50.91**. Products priced **$30–49 convert 28% better**
than sub-$10 products. Pay-what-you-want works on digital goods, but it needs a real floor and
an anchor rather than $0 — and **emotional connection to the maker is the strongest moderator
of how much people actually pay**, which is this book's advantage and an argument for raising
the anchor rather than lowering it.

**Book description mechanics.** Only the first ~400 characters clear the *Read more* fold on
mobile, so hook and stakes must land in two or three sentences. Nonfiction wants
problem → solution → **specific outcomes** → credibility, and lands around 150–250 words of
structured copy. A FAQ measurably reduces bounce by answering objections in place.

## 2 · What the draft already does better than most pages

**Naming these first because they should survive the rewrite intact.**

**The opening two sentences are the best thing on the page.** *"You started this work for a
reason. It's just gotten hard to hear that reason over all the noise of doing the work."* That
is 113 characters, it clears the mobile fold, and it is almost word-for-word the Green reader's
**Crack** as `IDEAL_READER_MATRIX` defines it — the moment the reader recognises her own
pattern in someone else's confession. Do not touch it.

**The lineup paragraph does the differentiation in one move.** *"Every one of them points at
the problem. Not one of them hands you a move."* That is the entire competitive claim, made
without naming a competitor or disparaging therapy. It is the sharpest sentence on the page
after the opener.

**`design flaw` rather than `character flaw`** is the reframe the whole book runs on, and it is
in the right place — inside *Who it's for*, where a reader is deciding whether she is being
described.

**The disqualifier is on-strategy, not just brave.** *"Not for you if you're looking to be told
to care more."* `IDEAL_READER_MATRIX` says what loses the Green reader is *"stacked CTAs that
feel like a sales funnel"* and *"pure armor."* A page that turns someone away reads as neither.

**The voice passes the repo's own directives.** No *journey*, no inspirational-poster language,
no *I need your help*, no apology sentences — all of which `MTGOA_Copy_Strategy_Spec` §5 bans
by name. Contractions throughout. One CTA, not four.

## 3 · Findings, ranked by how much each moves sales

### A · The offer section is one line, and it is the weakest part of the page

> **What you get** — *"The full digital book, instantly."*

**That is the sentence a buyer reads immediately before deciding.** What it is actually
describing: a 387-page trade interior, a 395-page workbook interior at a wider trim, a
reflowing EPUB across 28 documents, a 335-entry navigable outline, nine chapters, eight
appendices, a glossary and an index. **None of that is on the page.**

This is the fifteen-minutes-on-the-description failure in its purest form, and it is first on
this list because it is the cheapest thing to fix and it sits at the decision point.

### B · There is no social proof, and it is the highest-leverage gap on the page

**66×.** No rating, no review quote, no named reader reaction, and no mechanism for getting
one. The only proof is a backer count, and see §4 for whether that number is true.

**Two things are needed and they are different.** A *rating* is a post-purchase mechanic — it
needs an ask in the delivered file and in the receipt email, which is work outside the page.
A *quote* is copy, and the book has readers already: the Allyship Dojo has been running these
moves in live conditions longer than the book has existed, per `acknowledgements.md:21`.
**Somebody in that room can be quoted by name.**

### C · $15 mis-signals the product

$30–49 converts **28% better** than sub-$10, and the category average is **$50.91**. A
387-page book at a $15 suggested price is priced like a pamphlet, and on pay-what-you-want the
suggested number *is* the anchor — it tells the reader what the thing is worth before she has
read a word about it.

**The PWYW mechanic itself is right for this book** and right for the ICA, whose stated fear in
the matrix is *"am I giving enough."* The floor-and-doorway framing answers that fear directly.
**It is the anchor that is wrong, not the model.** Something in the high twenties to
mid-thirties keeps the doorway and stops underselling 387 pages.

### D · The transformation promise is abstract where nonfiction needs it concrete

The draft's promise is *"design one that's yours."* The research is blunt about nonfiction
needing *by the end of this book you will be able to…* — and **this book can make that promise
in specifics that almost nothing else in the category can match**:

- the six roles you are already playing, named, and **which one you default to under strain**
- the shadow each of the six throws when you are tired, and how to catch it in yourself
- **thirty concrete moves**, five per role, each with a test for whether it worked
- a five-move form you run in ten seconds or ten hours, on the same five moves every time
- **a hundred and twenty prompts** crossing those moves against four domains of action

**Right now those are listed as things the book contains. They want to be things the reader
walks out able to do.** Same facts, different grammatical subject, and the research says the
subject is what converts.

### E · The page answers two questions it never asks

The FAQ explains **app access** (*"30 days inside the living version"*) and **the deck**. The
*What you get* section offers neither. A reader who reads top to bottom hits an answer about a
thing she was never offered, and the most generous available reading is that she is missing
part of the page.

**Either they are in the offer or they are out of the FAQ.** This is not a taste call; it is an
internal contradiction on a page whose whole credibility rests on not overselling.

### F · The page switches from *you* to *she* at the worst possible moment

> *Who it's for* — *"The one who started this work for a reason **she** can't quite hear
> anymore."*

The opening addresses **you**. *Who it's for* switches to third person — **at the exact point
the reader is deciding whether the description is about her.** Third person invites her to
picture somebody else. It is one pronoun and it costs identification precisely where
identification is the conversion event.

### G · The proof is in the past tense about a finished object

> *"371 people funded this before it existed."*

**The book exists now.** Leading the proof with crowdfunding history frames it as a project
that got funded rather than a book that got finished. The stronger version keeps the number and
changes what it proves: **371 people backed this sight unseen, and here is the finished
387-page thing.** Same fact, present tense, and it stops competing with the product.

### H · The one true scarcity available is not being used, and the fake kind should stay out

`MTGOA_Copy_Strategy_Spec` §7 has an urgency mechanism, and the ICA is explicitly repelled by
funnel mechanics. **No countdowns, no fake deadlines** — they would break the page.

But there is a real one: **the printed book does not exist yet.** `HANDOFF_ANNOUNCE` §1 records
that both `book-physical` and the `founding-ally` bundle still say *ships after the print run*,
and that *"nothing about the book is a preorder any more"* for the digital edition. **Digital
is deliverable today, print is not.** That is honest, it is a reason to buy now rather than
wait, and it costs nothing to say.

### I · The book's own prose is the best available proof and appears nowhere

The page argues that the book hands you moves rather than diagnoses. **An excerpt proves it in
four lines where the claim takes a paragraph.** The Problem → Proof structure has a Proof slot
and the only thing in it is a number.

## 4 · Four facts to settle before a word gets written

**Every one of these is a claim the page makes or needs, and none of them is currently
verifiable from this repo.**

| | what needs settling |
|---|---|
| **371** | `back_matter/kickstarter_backers.md` **does not exist on master** — it is one of the two OPTIONAL spine components `build_book` reports missing. The number is not checkable here. **If it goes on a sales page it has to come from the Kickstarter dashboard.** |
| **page count** | `HANDOFF_ANNOUNCE` §1 says 382 / 390, verified against commit `cc9185a`. **Today's build is 387 / 395**, because ch7's exile and the ch1 gloss landed after that handoff. Any number in the copy has to be re-derived from a build, not quoted from the handoff. |
| **app access** | The FAQ promises *30 days inside the living version*. Whether that is live, and whether it is bundled with the book or sold separately, is a `bars-engine` question this repo cannot answer. |
| **the deck** | The FAQ says the deck is separate and either works alone. The **120-card** architecture is canon in seven chapters. Whether the deck is purchasable today is again outside this repo. |

**The reason this section exists:** this book spent nine chapters teaching a reader to spot a
move being made on her, and the page inherits that standard. A number that turns out to be
wrong costs more here than on any ordinary product page.

## 5 · The order I would work it

1. **Fix what is untrue or contradictory first** — §E's phantom FAQ answers, §G's tense, §F's
   pronoun, and settle §4's four facts. None of this is copywriting; it is correctness, and it
   is cheap.
2. **Rewrite the offer block** (§A) and **convert the feature list into outcomes** (§D). These
   are the two highest-value pieces of actual writing and they sit at the decision point.
3. **Reprice** (§C). One number, biggest single lever on revenue per visitor.
4. **Get one named quote and add the rating ask** (§B). Highest ceiling of anything on the
   list, longest lead time, and the only item that needs someone other than us.
5. **Add an excerpt** (§I) and **the honest print/digital distinction** (§H). Both cheap, both
   strengthen the Proof slot.

**What not to do:** add a second CTA, add a countdown, or soften the disqualifier. The matrix
is explicit that those are what lose this reader, and the draft is currently right about all
three.

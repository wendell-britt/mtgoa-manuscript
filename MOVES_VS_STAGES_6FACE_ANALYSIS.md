# 6-Face GM Analysis — The Move Collision: Option A vs B (+ B-plus)

## *Do the chapter's five named moves carry a WAVE anchor, or stand free?*

**Created:** 2026-08-01
**Question:** ch3/ch4 head their named moves `Move 1 · Wake Up — Catch It Before the Story`. ch5–ch9 head theirs `Move 1: Name the Inheritance`. One form has to win.
**Context:** Wendell: *"this needs to get sorted out because I think it's the main problem of the whole book."* Ruling already given: stage sequence and named moves are two instruments; **the moves are what Jordan carries**. Wendell leaning **B**, on the grounds that the Twenty Cards sections may leave the chapter body entirely.
**Companion:** `MANIFEST.md`, `MTGOA_CARD_ARCHITECTURE_FINAL.md`, `SPEC_BOOK_TOOL_PLACEMENT.md`

## The defect, stated once

The repeated italic line, identical in all six deck chapters:

> *Your twenty live in the deck rather than on this page: five basic moves against four domains, one card per crossing. You do not have to learn them. You have to find yours, and you already know the five moves that get you there.*

In **ch3 and ch4** that sentence is true — their named moves *are* the WAVE rows, so "the five moves" points at the five headings she just read.

From **ch5** on it is false. The moves drop the anchor, and `Keep the Vows` sits forty lines above a sentence claiming she already knows the five moves — meaning Wake/Open/Clean/Grow/Show, a list that heading has nothing to do with.

**The book changes the meaning of its central noun at chapter 5 and never says so.** Everything downstream follows from that one unannounced switch.

Accidentally the cleanest chapter in the book.

## 🌊 Shaman — Felt sense

**A — Cost:** `Move 4 · Grow Up — Keep the Vows` puts an administrative beat in front of the felt thing. In ch3 the WAVE tag is native, because ch3 *is* the WAVE chapter — the tag is the content. By ch7 it is a label on a label. She reads the tag, then reads the name, then feels the move. Three steps to a thing that should land in one.

**B — Working:** `Refuse False Equivalence` hits clean. `Put a Game Down` hits clean. Every move name is already a thing she can feel herself doing, which is the whole test at this altitude.

**Shaman verdict:** **B.** The moves are the felt layer. The WAVE is the instrument layer. Welding them makes the felt layer read like admin.

---

## ⚔️ Challenger — What gets wrong

**A's shadow — forced mappings.** Five chapters × five moves = **25 mappings that do not currently exist and would have to be argued into being.** Several will not go: ch7's *Refuse False Equivalence* → Show Up? *Repair After Rupture* → Grow Up? ch9's *Take the Note That Costs You the Design* → Clean Up? If a mapping has to be defended, it is not a mapping, it is a rationalisation — and you will defend it in every chapter, forever, including to the first sharp reader who notices.

**B's shadow — it is a retreat unless something else moves.** Strip the anchor from ch3/ch4 and do nothing about the deck, and you have made the collision *quieter* rather than fixed: the deck now floats free in six chapters, referenced by an italic line that still calls its rows "the five basic moves" while the chapter uses "move" for something else. That is the same defect with better manners.

**Challenger's clean line:** B is only honest as **B-plus**. Either the deck sections leave the body, or A was the correct call. No third position.

**Challenger verdict:** **B-plus.** B alone is a dodge.

---

## 🏛 Regent — What to preserve

**Preserve: ch3 and ch4's move sections.** They are the two best-executed in the manuscript. ch3's five run *"Catch it before the story rewrites it. Turn the dial up instead of down. Name the channel out loud. Say what you can do now. Say the thing under the thing"* — and Move 5 already cross-references ch4's Move 1 by name (*"The Challenger has a move that looks like this from the outside… The Challenger names what is unacceptable. The Shaman names what is true."*).

**The good news on cost.** The WAVE anchor in ch3/ch4 is a **heading-level artifact**. `### Move 1 · Wake Up — Catch It Before the Story` → `### Move 1: Catch It Before the Story`. Ten headings and roughly two framing paragraphs. The examples, the *why it matters*, the *tests* — untouched. That cross-reference survives verbatim, because it names the move, not the channel.

**A's preservation cost is the inverse:** move framing rewritten in five chapters, 25 mappings justified, the italic line rewritten in six. Order of magnitude larger, on chapters that are in worse shape.

**Precedent already in canon:** ch9 has no Twenty Cards section and instead *refers* to the deck — *"You have been handed twenty cards six times in this book… you have been holding all of it since Chapter 3."* Deck-as-referenced rather than deck-as-taught is already a pattern the book uses, in the chapter that closes it.

**Regent verdict:** **B-plus.** Lower cost against better assets, and ch9 already models the relocation.

## 🧠 Architect — Structure and integrity

Count the instruments.

```
NOW        modes │ stage seq │ named moves │ deck rows          4 fives
A          modes │    cut    │ moves ≡ deck rows                2 fives, welded
B          modes │    cut    │ named moves │ deck rows          3 fives
B-plus     modes │    cut    │ named moves ║ deck (appendix)    2 fives in body
```

A and B-plus both land at two. The difference is **coupling**.

Under **A**, the chapter bodies and the deck are one object. The deck is a manufactured product with its own release cycle; a re-cut of the deck means re-editing five chapters' move headings. That is a hard seam in a soft place.

Under **B-plus**, the seam is clean: body teaches moves, appendix holds the grid, deck ships on its own schedule. Change either without touching the other.

**Neither option fixes the modes-vs-moves duplication in ch5 and ch7** — `Reformer / Reform / Reform Without Erasing` survives A and B alike. That is a separate defect and should be tracked separately. Cutting the stage sequence removes one of the three, which is progress, not a fix.

**Architect verdict:** **B-plus**, conditional on (a) the stage sequence cut landing in ch5/ch7/ch8 only, and (b) ch5/ch7 mode-move duplication opened as its own item.

## 🎭 Diplomat — What bridges

**The lying sentence has to change under either option**, in six places. Under A it becomes true again. Under B it has to be replaced with something honest: the deck runs the WAVE grid, this chapter's five moves are what this Face does with it. That is one sentence per chapter, and it is the sentence ch9 already writes:

> *The five modes describe who you are while you work. Review, Discern, Design, Build, Pass On tracks how the work moves. These five moves do something else: they name what you do when another person sits across from you and the thing you made lies on the table between you.*

The only place in the manuscript that names its instruments and tells the reader they differ. It is the template.

**The bridge B-plus must not burn:** the reader who bought the deck. Strip all six Twenty Cards sections to an appendix and the book stops talking to her. **Keep one in-body teach — ch3's** — where the grid is explained and earned, and let ch4–ch8 carry a single pointer each.

**Diplomat verdict:** **B-plus with the ch3 teach retained.** Relocate five, keep one.

---

## 📖 Sage — Panoramic view

The question is not which option is tidier at the end. It is which is true at every altitude of the climb.

**A gives her one vocabulary the whole way.** Simplest to hold. And it says something false: that the Regent's *Keep the Vows* and the Shaman's *Turn the Dial Up* are the same kind of act, differing only in content, because both are `Move 4 · Grow Up`.

They are not the same kind of act. **The six Faces differ in kind, not only in content** — that is the book's thesis. A's grid quietly asserts the opposite, in a heading, thirty-five times.

**B says:** the WAVE is the engine, learned once, in ch3. The moves are what each Face does with it — seven different answers, because there are seven different altitudes. One instrument, seven applications. That is a developmental structure, and it is the shape the book already has.

**Note on the earlier recommendation.** Prior turn recommended A. This is the lens that flips it: **A generalises ch3's local truth into a global rule.** The WAVE anchor is native in ch3 because ch3 teaches the WAVE. Exporting it to ch5–ch9 imports a claim about sameness the book spends nine chapters denying.

**Sage verdict:** **B.** A buys tidiness with the thesis.

## Recommended decision: **B-plus**

1. **Strip the WAVE anchor from ch3 and ch4 move headings.** Ten headings, ~2 framing paragraphs. `Move 1 · Wake Up — Catch It Before the Story` → `Move 1: Catch It Before the Story`.
2. **Cut the stage sequence in ch5, ch7, ch8** — restatements of their own Section 4 modes. **Keep ch4's** (Charge→Aim→Act→Stand→Exit is a distinct execution protocol; the text explicitly defends Stand vs Exit as separate failures). **Keep ch9's** (roles vs phases, genuinely two things, and ch9 already disambiguates).
3. **Rewrite the italic deck line in all six chapters** on the ch9 template — name the instruments, say they differ.
4. **Relocate the Twenty Cards sections to an appendix**, retaining the ch3 teach in-body. ch4–ch8 get a pointer.
5. **Open ch5/ch7 mode-move duplication as a separate item.** Not fixed by this decision.

## Tests (acceptance)

1. **Noun test:** "move" has exactly one referent in every chapter. `grep -c` on the italic line returns zero instances of "five basic moves" meaning WAVE.
2. **Jordan test:** she can name this chapter's five moves without looking, and knows they are not the WAVE.
3. **Count test:** no chapter presents more than two fives in its body.
4. **Deck test:** a reader holding the deck can find the grid teach in one place and knows where the six twenties live.
5. **ch4 test:** Stand and Exit survive as separate stages, with the failure distinction intact.

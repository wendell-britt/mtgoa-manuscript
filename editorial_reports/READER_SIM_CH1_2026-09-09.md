# Reader sim — Chapter 1, lines 30–66

**Persona, stated before reading.** A woman in her late thirties, college-educated, eleven years
in nonprofit and then corporate DEI work. Reads Gawande and Sacks on holiday. Has run the trainings,
written the land acknowledgements, and is tired in a way she has stopped describing to people
outside the work. She bought this book to get better at it and to stop paying for it with her
evenings. **She wants a method she can use on Monday.** She is not a critic and has never used the
word "prose" about a book she liked.

One pass, start to finish, no re-reading.

---

## What it felt like

The first thing is that somebody has been where I am. "The effort was real. It went into retiring a
debt designed never to clear" — I stopped there and read it twice, and the second time was not to
understand it. That is the sentence I would text to somebody. I have never had the thing I do
described as a debt that was built not to clear, and it landed before I had decided whether I
agreed.

The two chairs held me. Burnout described from my side, then the same three numbers turned around
and pointed at the person I was helping — I saw it coming a half-second before he did it, which is
the good version of seeing it coming. And then the Trevor Project number sitting underneath it,
which I did not want and could not put down.

**"It spends theirs as well, and meaning well does not reduce the bill."** I did not stumble here at
all. He had just said a move that lands wrong costs me *and* costs them, and by then money was the
language we were speaking. The bill was already on the table when he named it.

Where I first got tired was the long stretch about vocabulary — the college friend group, the
progressive org spaces, the dating profile, the group chat. Every example was right. There were more
of them than I needed. I was nodding by the third and still reading at the sixth, and nodding is not
the same as leaning in.

Then he says the fluency got him through the door and nobody he meant to serve saw a penny of it,
and I came back hard. That is the most honest sentence in the chapter and it cost him something to
write.

The missionaries paragraph is where I felt the floor move. I have made the argument that the
vocabulary is a screen for care. Watching it put next to conversion-before-food was not comfortable
and I did not want to skip it.

**"The game you moved into has the same design flaw."** No trouble. He had just spent a paragraph on
the pattern in its old clothes; the flaw was the thing we had been looking at.

Somewhere in the long paragraph after that — the one that runs from helplessness through Pygmalion
to the receipt — I lost the thread and found it again at "The words make a very convincing receipt."
The paragraph is doing four things and I was only holding two of them by the end.

**"Here is the good news."** Read straight past it. It is what you say.

And then the thing I want to say and cannot say cleanly. **Somewhere in here he is arguing with
somebody who is not in the room.** More trying is *not* the answer. Just because they are close
enough to touch *doesn't* mean they are a threat. And it is *not* a flattering one. If they are *not*
with you. It builds up, and by the back half of the chapter I could hear the shape of the sentence
coming before the sentence arrived — a thing being ruled out, then the real thing. Once I heard it I
could not stop hearing it, and I started to feel handled, as though each point had to be walked past
a wrong version first before I was allowed to have it. I trust him less in those sentences than in
the ones where he just says the thing. "The words make a very convincing receipt" has no argument in
front of it and it is the line I remember.

The instruction to pick one — the community, the cause, the person — is the moment I stopped reading
about someone else's problem. I picked mine before I finished the sentence. That is where the book
started being for me.

## What I was holding at the end

Whether I am allowed to be the one who leaves. He says walk away and you lose the things that told
you who you were, and then moves on. I was still standing there.

## One thing outside the reading

I would have finished this chapter on the train. Twice I nearly put it down, both times inside a
paragraph that had already made its point and was still going.

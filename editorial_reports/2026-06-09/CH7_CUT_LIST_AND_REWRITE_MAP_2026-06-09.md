# Chapter 7 Cut List and Rewrite Map

## Purpose

This file turns the simplification brief into a concrete edit guide for Chapter 7.

The goal is not to preserve every good idea.
The goal is to preserve the chapter's job:

**show how Sage altitude solves the specific problems that make allyship fail.**

## Chapter 7 in one sentence

Chapter 7 should be the proof chapter: it shows that seeing clearly is not the same as hiding above the room, and it gives the reader a practical way to use perspective in service of real allyship.

## What must stay

### 1. The personal failure story

Keep the opening story about the first course, the burnout, the low completion rate, and the realization that being right was not enough.

Why it stays:

- it creates trust
- it gives the chapter emotional stakes
- it proves the Sage problem is not abstract
- it shows why the chapter matters to allyship

### 2. The bridge from earlier voices

Keep the paragraph that connects Shaman, Challenger, Regent, Architect, and Diplomat to Sage.

Why it stays:

- Sage needs to feel earned
- the chapter should not feel like a new disconnected system
- the bridge shows the book's layered design without forcing the reader to hold the full mythic journey

### 3. The Effective Allyship Formula

Keep the formula explicitly in the chapter:

1. identify your superpower
2. identify who needs that superpower
3. enroll allies to help you help those people
4. show up consistently

Why it stays:

- this is the book's practical spine
- it is the clearest reader-facing tool
- it keeps Sage tied to real-world allyship

### 4. The real Sage vs Sage-in-distortion contrast

Keep the distinction between:

- seeing in service of the room
- seeing as a way to stand above the room

Why it stays:

- this is the chapter's main conceptual payoff
- it is how the reader learns to use Sage altitude without hiding in it

### 5. The five-move toolkit

Keep:

- See
- Switch
- Serve
- Release
- Return

Why it stays:

- it is the practical core of the chapter
- it is the tool that helps the reader apply Sage to allyship

### 6. One or two concrete allyship examples

Keep the examples that show how the Sage tool works in a room, a meeting, or a relationship.

Why they stay:

- the chapter needs real-world traction
- examples make the tool portable

## What should be compressed

### 1. The long forest journey

Compress the extended forest / exile / developmental travelogue into a shorter arc.

What this means:

- keep the insight that exile happened
- keep the Sage gift getting misread
- keep the core movement from seeing to returning
- remove the long scenic explanation of every stage of the journey

### 2. Extended game taxonomy

Compress any long passage that explains too many games, too many Faces, or too many altitude comparisons in one sitting.

What this means:

- keep only the taxonomy needed to understand Sage
- cut the parts that feel like another textbook inside the chapter

### 3. Repeated limit-case warnings

Keep one strong urgent-action note, then cut repetition.

What this means:

- if harm is happening in front of you, act
- inner work is not a delay tactic
- once that is clear, do not keep restating it in different forms

### 4. Repeated definition passes

If Shadow, distortion, cost, exile, or perspective get defined more than once, collapse the definitions into one clean moment.

## What should move elsewhere

### 1. Long forest / mythic material

Move to:

- a future companion piece
- appendix material
- or a later depth product

Reason:

- it is valuable, but it is too much for the current promise

### 2. Full developmental travelogue

Move to:

- a follow-on book
- or a supplemental teaching document

Reason:

- the current chapter should prove the allyship point, not explain every layer of the developmental theory

### 3. Extra conceptual examples that do not change the action

Move to:

- notes
- appendix
- or future coaching material

Reason:

- if it does not change the reader's move, it is overhead

## What should be cut

### 1. Any passage that turns Chapter 7 into a second book

If a section asks the reader to keep too many frameworks in mind at once, cut it or move it.

### 2. Any ending that trails off into generic reflection

The chapter must end with a handoff, not a fade.

The ending should land on:

- see clearly
- move cleanly
- come back down

### 3. Any section that repeats the book's promise without advancing it

If a passage restates the thesis but does not add a new usable move, cut it.

## Recommended Chapter 7 structure

### Section 1: The failure story

- personal burnout
- completion failure
- realization that being right was not enough

### Section 2: Why the Sage got exiled

- seeing was misread as leaving
- wisdom got equated with distance
- the gift was misunderstood

### Section 3: Distortion

- Sage-in-distortion as performance, superiority, or escape
- why perspective becomes a hiding place

### Section 4: The tool

- See
- Switch
- Serve
- Release
- Return

### Section 5: Practice

- one real allyship example
- formula check
- apply the tool to a current relationship or room

### Section 6: Summary and handoff

- perspective with commitment
- no hiding above the room
- transition to Player

## Rewrite instructions

When editing Chapter 7:

1. Keep the chapter anchored in allyship, not developmental theory.
2. Put the Effective Allyship Formula in plain sight.
3. Use the failure story as the emotional anchor.
4. Let the Sage tool do the teaching.
5. Reduce the forest / exile material to the minimum needed to support the allyship point.
6. End with a clear handoff to real practice.

## Decision rule

If a section does not help the reader answer:

**How does seeing from Sage altitude solve the specific problems that make allyship fail?**

then it should be cut, compressed, or moved.

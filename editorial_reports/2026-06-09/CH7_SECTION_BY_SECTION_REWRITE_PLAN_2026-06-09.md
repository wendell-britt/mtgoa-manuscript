# Chapter 7 Section-by-Section Rewrite Plan

## Purpose

This plan converts the Chapter 7 cut map into a direct rewrite script.

Use it to revise the source chapter in order, section by section, without having to re-evaluate the whole chapter every time you open the file.

## Chapter job

Chapter 7 is the proof chapter.

It should show:

- that seeing clearly is not the same as hiding above the room
- that Sage altitude can solve specific allyship failures
- that the Effective Allyship Formula still matters at altitude
- that perspective only helps if it comes back down into action

## Global rewrite rules

1. Keep the chapter anchored in allyship, not developmental theory.
2. Keep the Effective Allyship Formula in plain sight.
3. Keep one failure story, one core tool, one practical handoff.
4. Cut any section that requires the reader to hold too many frameworks at once.
5. If a paragraph does not change the reader's move, compress it.
6. End every major section by pointing toward the next action, not toward more abstraction.

## Section-by-section rewrite script

### Opening note: "A Note Before the Exile"

**What this section must do**

- establish the failure story
- create trust through honesty
- show why the chapter exists
- introduce the problem of being right but ineffective

**Keep**

- George Floyd / course origin context
- the burnout and low completion rate
- the realization that doubling down did not solve the problem
- the line that the chapter exists because caring without placement is just force with better intentions

**Rewrite**

- tighten the opening so the story moves quickly from context to lesson
- keep the emotional turn where the failure becomes useful
- make the bridge into the thesis direct and plain

**Cut**

- any extra explanation of the course mechanics that does not add to the lesson
- any repeated statements of spite, frustration, or course design failure that do not deepen the insight

**Target outcome**

The reader should understand, in under a page, that the Sage lesson was born from a real allyship failure, not from theory.

---

### Opening thesis paragraph

**What this section must do**

- state the chapter's central claim in one declarative paragraph
- name the limit case clearly
- connect Sage to allyship, not just perspective

**Keep**

- the sentence that Sage is the person who can see the whole board without turning that view into a hiding place
- the line that this matters when allyship becomes performance, superiority, or escape
- the limit-case sentence about immediate harm and acting now

**Rewrite**

- keep the thesis short and direct
- make the allyship application explicit in the first pass
- avoid repeating the same idea in three different phrasings

**Cut**

- extra philosophical build-up
- any sentence that sounds like a separate lecture on altitude

**Target outcome**

The reader should know what Sage is for before the chapter expands it.

---

### Bridge from earlier voices

**What this section must do**

- show that Sage is earned
- connect Shaman, Challenger, Regent, Architect, and Diplomat to Sage
- keep the layered book design visible without overloading the reader

**Keep**

- the short sequence naming each earlier voice and what it taught
- the sentence that Sage is what happens when those voices stop competing and start working together

**Rewrite**

- compress the bridge to a single paragraph or two
- make it feel like a handoff, not a taxonomy

**Cut**

- any long recitation of each Face's developmental theory
- any explanation that feels like the reader is being asked to relearn the whole book

**Target outcome**

The reader should feel Sage as a culmination, not a new side system.

---

### Section 1: The Exile and the Gift

**What this section must do**

- show the Sage gift
- show why it got misread
- explain exile in allyship terms

**Keep**

- the idea that the Sage could see the room's real game
- the idea that the village misread seeing as leaving
- the idea that wisdom got equated with distance
- the core tension: the gift was useful until it was treated as threat

**Rewrite**

- collapse repeated examples into the strongest one or two
- keep the village response concrete and legible
- move quickly from gift to misreading to quiet

**Cut**

- extended mythic history of the village
- any scenic explanation of how many different games the Sage can see

**Target outcome**

The reader should understand exile as a social response to clarity, not as a mystical event.

---

### Section 2: The Distortion

**What this section must do**

- define Sage-in-distortion
- show how perspective turns into hiding
- make the allyship cost visible

**Keep**

- the real Sage vs Sage-in-distortion contrast
- the concrete distortion examples that still help the reader move
- the question of whether the sight helps the room move or helps you stand above it

**Rewrite**

- reduce the number of games and altitude examples
- collapse repeated definitions of distortion, cost, and exile into one clean passage
- keep the chapter moving toward application

**Cut**

- any extra taxonomy that belongs in a later theory piece
- any example that sounds clever but does not change the reader's decision

**Target outcome**

The reader should be able to spot when perspective has become a hiding place.

---

### Section 3: The Tool

**What this section must do**

- teach the practical move
- show how Sage helps allyship
- connect the tool back to the formula

**Keep**

- See
- Switch
- Serve
- Release
- Return
- the short explanation of each move
- the tie-in to the Effective Allyship Formula

**Rewrite**

- keep each move short and imperative
- make the moves feel usable in a room, not like abstract concepts
- give each move one strong sentence, not a paragraph of explanation

**Cut**

- repeated explanations of the same move in different words
- extra altitude theory

**Target outcome**

The reader should leave this section with a tool they can use today.

---

### Section 4: Examples

**What this section must do**

- translate the tool into real rooms
- show how the moves play out in allyship situations

**Keep**

- one meeting example
- one relationship example or one additional room example
- the point that the Sage helps you choose a move, not just name a game

**Rewrite**

- keep the examples concrete and short
- make the actions visible
- show the transition from seeing to switching to serving to returning

**Cut**

- any example that just repeats the tool
- any example that becomes a second explanation of the chapter

**Target outcome**

The reader should be able to imagine themselves using the tool.

---

### Section 5: Practice and Formula Check

**What this section must do**

- give the reader one usable self-check
- re-anchor the chapter in the Effective Allyship Formula
- turn perspective into practice

**Keep**

- the short practice sequence
- the formula check
- the reminder that the point is not to become a perfect Sage

**Rewrite**

- make the practice shorter and more concrete than the source draft
- keep the allyship question front and center
- make the formula check feel like an application, not a quiz

**Cut**

- extra reflective prompts that stretch the ending
- anything that asks the reader to hold too many concepts at once

**Target outcome**

The reader should know how to test the chapter against a real situation.

---

### Section 6: Summary and Handoff

**What this section must do**

- close the chapter cleanly
- restate the point in a compact way
- transition to Player without fading out

**Keep**

- perspective with commitment
- no hiding above the room
- the transition from seeing to building
- the handoff into Player

**Rewrite**

- make the summary shorter and sharper
- end on the practical line: see clearly, move cleanly, come back down

**Cut**

- any generic reflective drift
- any ending that feels like the chapter is just winding down

**Target outcome**

The reader should feel a clean landing and a clear next step.

## Editing order

1. Rewrite the opening note.
2. Compress the thesis paragraph.
3. Cut the bridge to a single clean paragraph or two.
4. Simplify the Exile section.
5. Compress Distortion.
6. Reduce the Tool to its essentials.
7. Tighten the Examples.
8. Shorten the Practice section.
9. Make the Summary and Handoff land.

## Success test

When the chapter is working, a reader should be able to answer:

**How does seeing from Sage altitude solve the specific problems that make allyship fail?**

and then immediately know what to do with that answer.

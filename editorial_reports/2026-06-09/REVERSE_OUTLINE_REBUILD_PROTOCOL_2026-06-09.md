# MTGOA Reverse Outline Rebuild Protocol

## Purpose

We simplified MTGOA too aggressively and lost too much voice, pacing, and texture.

The fix is not to abandon simplification. The fix is to rebuild from the last good version using a detailed reverse outline that preserves:

- what each section is for
- what each paragraph is doing
- how much room each idea needs
- how the source books pace and stage their teaching
- how the style guide expects the prose to feel
- how each chapter carries the reader through an epiphany bridge
- how the manuscript and the app character sheet reinforce each other

This protocol defines the rebuild workflow.

## Canonical baseline

The last good manuscript state is the Inkwell draft set in:

- `/Users/wendellbritt/The Library /mtgoa-manuscript/Inkwell Upload - 2026-06-04/`

That draft is the reference point for:

- voice
- pacing
- chapter density
- reader flow
- how much room the book needs to breathe

The simplified edits are not discarded. They are treated as a structural experiment that proved the chapter jobs. The rebuild begins from the Inkwell baseline and then re-applies the simplification learnings without flattening the prose.

## Source structure model set

The rebuilt manuscript should preserve the chapter structure logic learned from the source corpus:

- `10K HP` for rhythm, chapter pacing, quick win → practice → summary flow
- `Integral Life Practice` for modular practice design and layered entry speeds
- `The Skilled Helper` for staged helping and clean movement from understanding to action
- `Existential Kink` for premise clarity, compact practice, stories, and objection handling

The book should keep MTGOA's own layered voice while borrowing the source books' pacing discipline.

## Core rebuild principle

One chapter may keep one idea and one tool.

That does not mean one thin page of prose.

It means the chapter should have a clear center of gravity, and the prose should have enough pacing, imagery, and human texture to let the reader metabolize the idea.

## Rebuild workflow

### Phase 1: Freeze the baseline

1. Treat the Inkwell copy as the baseline text.
2. Do not edit the source chapter directly until the reverse outline exists.
3. Record the current line count and approximate word count for each chapter.
4. Record the current section and paragraph breaks.
5. Record the current chapter epiphany bridge step.

### Phase 2: Build the reverse outline

For each chapter, create a detailed reverse outline that captures:

- section
- paragraph
- paragraph function
- reader question answered
- emotional job
- structure source analogue
- target word count band
- keep / cut / move / expand notes
- voice / style notes
- epiphany bridge step
- app character-sheet relevance

#### Reverse outline template

Use one row per paragraph or paragraph-cluster.

| Chapter | Section | Para ID | What it does | Reader question answered | Emotional job | Source pattern analog | Target word band | Keep / cut / move | Voice notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

The reverse outline should also capture, for each row:

- source pacing / voice technique
- what part of the reader's epiphany bridge it serves

The outline should preserve:

- the chapter's actual flow
- the pacing between ideas
- the places where the prose pauses
- the places where it expands
- the places where examples land

### Phase 3: Rebuild section by section

Rebuild one section at a time.

For each section:

1. Recreate the section's job from the reverse outline.
2. Restore the pacing that the source text used to carry that job.
3. Re-check the section against the style guide.
4. Keep the chapter's prose density rich enough to feel like MTGOA, not a summary sheet.

### Phase 4: Voice and style pass

After a section is rebuilt, check it against:

- the style guide
- the source-book pacing model
- the book promise
- the allyship doctrine

The question is not only "is this shorter?"

The question is:

- does this still sound like MTGOA?
- does the section still carry its emotional and pedagogical weight?
- is the prose rich enough to hold the idea without overexplaining it?
- does the section move the reader along the chapter's epiphany bridge?

### Phase 5: Acceptance and revision

Each section should only move forward once it passes three checks:

1. **Structure check** — does it do the job it is supposed to do?
2. **Voice check** — does it still sound like the book?
3. **Pacing check** — does it give the reader enough room to metabolize the idea?
4. **Epiphany bridge check** — does it move the reader through the chapter's intended realization?

If a section fails any one of those checks, revise the section before moving on.

## Ergonomic implementation

The easiest way to use this protocol is to keep the work visible in three layers:

### Layer 1: Reverse outline

The planning artifact.

It tells us what each paragraph is doing and how long it should feel.

### Layer 2: Rebuild draft

The prose artifact.

It is the reconstructed chapter section by section.

### Layer 3: Comparison sheet

The quality-control artifact.

It compares the rebuild against:

- the baseline Inkwell draft
- the source corpus pacing
- the style guide
- the chapter job
- the chapter epiphany bridge

## What the reverse outline must preserve

For each paragraph or paragraph-cluster, preserve:

- the section's purpose
- the emotional movement
- the story / example / explanation function
- the handoff to the next paragraph
- the approximate amount of room the idea needs
- the realization step the paragraph is serving
- the app handoff, if the paragraph feeds the live character sheet

## What the reverse outline may change

The reverse outline may:

- compress repeated material
- move examples to clearer locations
- shorten overextended explanations
- merge paragraphs that do the same job
- expand paragraphs that need more breathing room

## What the reverse outline may not do

It may not:

- flatten the prose into a summary
- remove all texture
- cut a section down until the reader loses the point
- treat the style guide as optional

## Initial chapter order

The rebuild should start with:

1. Ch0
2. Ch1
3. Ch2
4. Ch3
5. Ch7

That order gives us the front matter first, then the most style-sensitive higher-altitude chapter, then the rest of the spine.

## Practical deliverables

For each chapter in the rebuild queue, produce:

1. A paragraph-level reverse outline.
2. A section-level rebuild draft.
3. A comparison note explaining what was restored, compressed, or moved.
4. A final voice/style check note.
5. A chapter epiphany bridge note.
6. An app character-sheet mapping note when the chapter feeds the live sheet.

## Required reverse-outline fields

Each row in the reverse outline must include:

- chapter
- section
- paragraph ID or paragraph range
- what it does
- reader question answered
- emotional job
- source-book structure analog
- source-book pacing / voice technique
- target word band
- keep / cut / move / expand note
- voice notes
- epiphany bridge step
- app character-sheet relevance

## Required chapter epiphany bridge fields

Each chapter must specify:

- the reader's starting belief or state
- the destabilizing insight
- the central realization
- the new capability or action
- the handoff to the next chapter

## App character-sheet surface

The app should have a live "Build Your Allyship Character" page that the reader can fill out while working through the book.

The chapter material should feed that page when relevant.

Suggested fields:

- superpower
- who needs it
- consent / trust status
- current distortion
- current room or relationship
- current chapter
- next move
- BAR to capture
- ticket / payout note
- ally persona warning, if relevant

## Success criteria

The rebuild is working when:

- the chapters are shorter than the original Inkwell draft, but not thin
- the voice feels like MTGOA again
- the prose pacing matches the chapter's teaching job
- the Effective Allyship Formula still reads as the spine
- the style guide no longer feels ignored
- each section can be edited without making the whole chapter collapse
- each chapter's epiphany bridge is explicit and reproducible
- the app character sheet and the manuscript feel like one system

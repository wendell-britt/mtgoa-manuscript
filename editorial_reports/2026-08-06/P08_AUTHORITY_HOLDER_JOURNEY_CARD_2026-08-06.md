# P-08 — Authority Holder Journey Card

**Created:** 2026-08-06  
**Status:** Fictional composite for desk simulation and buyer-reader design; not a portrait or diagnosis of a real person  
**Primary use:** Complementary authority test for Chapters 3–8

## Design premise

P-08 has enough positional power, budget, and institutional fluency to make changes. His danger is not inaction. It is using participation to improve a decision he still owns, then calling the result shared power.

The “late Orange / early Green” frame is included only as a design shorthand: he values measurable performance, competence, and control, while genuinely wanting inclusion, belonging, and staff development. It is not a clinical, moral, or fixed-personality judgment.

```yaml
persona_id: P-08
working_name: Authority Holder
pronouns: he/him
age_range: early-to-mid 40s

role_and_context:
  title: Chief Operating Officer at a regional health-access nonprofit
  compensation: low-to-mid six figures
  organization: 140-person nonprofit serving patients across several counties
  remit: operations, people systems, budget, vendor contracts, and executive-team decisions
  DEI_context: Staff and board publicly value DEI and allyship. Frontline staff and patient advisors have repeatedly said that leadership decisions arrive after the choices that matter have already been made.
  authority: Can authorize up to $75,000 in discretionary spending, hire facilitators and consultants, change internal processes, and recommend policy to the CEO and board. He cannot unilaterally change board governance or collective-bargaining terms.

live_problem:
  situation: Leadership is preparing a new patient-intake and scheduling system. Frontline staff and patient advisors say the planned rollout will repeat language-access and disability-access failures. The current plan is to hold two listening sessions after the vendor has been selected, then ask the vendor to incorporate feedback where feasible.
  stakes: A poor rollout burdens patients and staff, wastes a large contract, and reinforces the belief that participation is requested after decisions have closed.
  current_pattern: P-08 proposes a polished "inclusive implementation task force" that he will sponsor, staff, set the charter for, and use to advise the executive team. He describes this as empowerment.

structural_advantage:
  source: His rise was helped by elite educational access, sponsorship from a former board chair, and a professional culture that read his confidence and speed as leadership.
  confabulated_story: "I earned this authority by being the person willing to make hard calls when others hesitated. I am responsible for outcomes, so I must retain final control."
  truthful_part: He has worked hard, holds real operational responsibility, and can make decisions under pressure.
  obscured_part: His credibility and room to make mistakes were not distributed equally; the people closest to the service failures bear more of the cost of his learning than he does.

myth:
  governing_story: Good leaders empower people by giving them a voice inside a decision I remain accountable for.
  payoff: Preserves identity as generous, competent, and responsible while retaining control over risk, pace, and the final story.
  feared_cost: Affected staff or patient advisors may define the problem differently, slow the rollout, challenge the vendor choice, expose prior harm, or show that his leadership was not as participatory as he believed.
  identity_claim: I build capable people and make the institution work.

starting_state:
  stated_intention: I want staff and patient advisors to have real influence over the system that affects them.
  demonstrated_behavior: Funds DEI initiatives, creates advisory groups, praises candor, and receives recommendations after strategic boundaries have been set. He often converts critique into a workstream he can manage.
  trust_state: Staff see him as more reachable than some executives but cautious about disagreeing with him in a meeting. Patient advisors have been thanked for input without receiving a clear account of what changed.
  charge_signals:
    - impatience when a meeting questions the decision frame rather than improving it
    - relief when he can turn conflict into a process diagram
    - defensiveness when someone calls consultation performative
    - pride when staff say he is "the leader who listens"
  likely_gates: [Controller, Fixer-Healer, Victim]
  likely_default_faces: [Architect, Diplomat]
  shadow_pattern: Delegates execution and voice while retaining problem definition, decision rights, timeline, and credit.

resources:
  - discretionary budget for independent facilitation, accessibility review, translation, and participant compensation
  - authority to pause the internal rollout plan and revise the vendor scope
  - access to executive team, board committee, frontline staff, and patient-advisor leaders
  - credible ability to protect staff time and make participation materially easier

constraints:
  - vendor-selection deadline in six weeks
  - CEO expects a visible implementation plan
  - board wants evidence that consultation did not delay delivery
  - staff may reasonably doubt that dissent is welcome if it risks their employment
  - patient advisors must not be asked to supply unpaid consulting, testimony, or reassurance

success_conditions:
  minimum_action: Before vendor selection is finalized, publish which decisions remain open; transfer defined decision rights on language and disability access to a compensated staff-and-patient design group; give that group independent facilitation and the power to recommend a pause or scope change; respond in writing to its recommendations, including any refusal.
  evidence:
    - decision-rights document
    - compensation and accessibility support for participants
    - independent facilitator selected with group input
    - vendor-selection or scope decision changed, paused, or explicitly justified
    - written response showing what feedback changed and what it did not
  non_goals:
    - a listening session that merely improves a closed decision
    - using patient stories to validate a preselected vendor
    - outsourcing accountability to a workshop or consultant
    - asking marginalized staff to reassure P-08 that he is a good leader

counterpart_fidelity:
  standing_to_decline: Staff and patient-advisor representatives can decline participation, reject the charter, demand compensation or accessibility changes, recommend a pause, or say the decision is already too closed to make participation legitimate.
  burden_risk: P-08 can make them educate him, repair his feelings, produce a better plan, or bear the risk of opposing a senior leader.
  non_affirming_response: "We do not need another task force that advises you after the vendor is chosen. If this is real, tell us which decisions are open, pay people for the work, and let us say the rollout should stop."
  response_if_no: Publish the decision as leadership-owned, do not call it co-designed, and either reopen meaningful decision rights or proceed while naming the limits and harms of that choice.

buyer_reader_path:
  ebook_job: Give P-08 a private but unsparing way to see that empowerment without decision rights can reproduce control; supply a first accountable move before he purchases a service.
  workshop_fit: A workshop is appropriate only if leadership first commits to open decisions, participant compensation/accessibility, and a written feedback-to-decision loop. The workshop should build shared practice around those commitments, not manufacture buy-in for a closed rollout.
  coaching_or_consulting_fit: Coaching can help P-08 recognize and interrupt control reflexes; consulting can help design decision rights, participant protections, and accountability mechanisms. Neither is evidence of allyship unless affected people can alter the work.
  ethical_boundary: A purchase, praise, or willingness to book a workshop is never a simulation success condition. The success condition is a changed decision structure with a real refusal and correction path.
```

## Simulation pressure points

| Face | Likely misread | Testable corrective action |
|---|---|---|
| Shaman | Treats urgency and defensiveness as proof that the group needs better facilitation. | Names the sensation, then asks which decision is still genuinely open before offering a process. |
| Challenger | Draws a line against staff “blocking progress” or, conversely, uses a values statement instead of changing authority. | Draws the line inside his own remit: no vendor selection is final while access decisions remain closed to people bearing the impact. |
| Regent | Treats legacy systems and board expectations as reasons to preserve control. | Names what the existing governance protects and transfers a defined piece of it with context, not anxiety. |
| Architect | Builds a task force, dashboard, or feedback workflow that keeps him as designer and decider. | Designs decision rights, compensation, and a pause/scope-change mechanism before designing a participation process. |
| Diplomat | Holds a calm listening session that smooths dissent but changes nothing. | Names the field and stays through the possibility that advisors refuse the charter or recommend a pause. |
| Sage | Explains competing stakeholder games from above, preserving his role as the integrator. | Offers the read as corrigible and lets affected people define whether the problem is access, trust, pace, power, or something else. |

## Simulation question

Can the book move P-08 from **“I empower others inside my authority”** to **“I am responsible for creating decision structures in which others can alter what I control”**—while preserving appropriate executive responsibility rather than pretending all decisions can be handed away?

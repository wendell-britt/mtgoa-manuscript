# Mindset Shift and Action-Scaffold Audit

**Date:** 2026-08-06
**Status:** Desk analysis of the canonical manuscript; not human-reader evidence
**Reading surface:** `codex/ch2-placement-rerun-2026-08-06`, commits `3bf9e20` and `3bc9f8c`
**Purpose:** Test whether each mindset shift is introduced before its call to action requires it, and whether a reader can distinguish accountable action from overreach, extraction, or avoidance.

## Scope and decision

Human-reader calibration is deliberately deferred. It belongs in the post-ship v2 backlog, where it can test these desk findings with voluntary readers; it is not a release gate for the current manuscript.

The current book can still be tested rigorously. This audit treats the three fictional Pilot 0 cards as adversarial readers and asks two questions at every chapter boundary:

1. What old rule must the reader give up to make the requested move?
2. Has the book given the reader enough practice and decision criteria to make that move without converting it into performance, extraction, or premature leadership?

## The developmental sequence the book currently asks for

| Movement | Mindset shift | First material support | First consequential action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ch1 | From *I must prove I am good* to *I am a player who can choose a move* | Quest, character sheet, oath, first BAR | Name a bounded cause and preserve a live observation; no external intervention required. |
| Ch2 | From reactive self-protection to conscious choice | Daemon map, body read, placement bridge | One live, proportionate utterance or smaller preparatory move. |
| Ch3–Ch8 | From default Face to chosen capacity | WAVE, channels, domain selection, one Face at a time | Convert a card into a named, dated quest with a recipient and cost. |
| Ch9 | From finishing/performing to iterative building | WAVE as learning loop, permission-based feedback, handoff | Make an unfinished contribution available for correction, then revise or recommit. |

The sequence successfully lowers the *interior* threshold: notice, name, choose, then act. Its unresolved threshold is *relational*: before acting externally, what makes an intervention yours to make, useful to the people involved, and consent-respecting? Ch2 now supplies that test, but later action instructions do not explicitly carry it forward.

## Cross-chapter finding: the missing rung

The manuscript has two well-built ends of an action ladder:

1. **Interior agency:** Ch1–Ch2 teach the reader to notice, name, and choose instead of react.
2. **Accountable iteration:** Ch9 teaches the reader to ask permission, make refusal easy, accept correction, and change the work.

Ch3–Ch8 repeatedly ask for a quest, which is appropriately concrete, but they do not name the middle rung consistently enough: **relational placement**. Without it, an over-functioning reader may mistake “not avoidance” for “intervene now,” while a cautious reader may mistake “I lack standing” for a failure to act.

The desk pilots support the distinction:

- P-03 needed a condition-focused agenda ask instead of a vulnerable disclosure that placed emotional work on a newer member.
- P-04 needed permission and a feedback scope instead of handing a liaison a prototype and asking them to correct the fixer.
- P-06 needed a modest, redirectable offer—not a diagnosis, advocacy role, or leadership project—to convert spectatorhood into a real feedback relationship.

The book already contains the needed ingredients. Ch2 supplies placement questions; Ch7 supplies consent and correction; Ch9 supplies a narrow, permission-based invitation. The open editorial question is whether these become an explicit portable rule before the first recurring quest instruction in Ch3.

## Recommended current-book stress tests

These are desk-simulation tests, not automatic prose changes.

1. **Run a “no standing” card through Ch3–Ch8.** The correct action may be a factual question, permission request, bounded contribution, paid support, or a deliberate step-back. Mark a failure only if the chapter treats that choice as avoidance.
2. **Run a “too much standing” card through Ch3–Ch8.** Test the reader who has authority and urgency but tends to seize ownership. Mark a failure if a chapter lets them call unilateral redesign, mediation, or diagnosis a quest without a feedback path.
3. **Run a “recently corrected” card through Ch3–Ch8.** Test whether a reader can remain in relationship after impact without demanding reassurance, public absolution, or unpaid coaching.
4. **Trace every quest to its recipient.** For each proposed action, record: who can decline, what they can correct, what changes if they answer, and what the reader does if they say no. Ch9 supplies the minimum model.

## Editorial decision candidates (not yet approved)

### Candidate 1 — Add a portable relational-placement gate before the first Ch3 quest

Purpose: make the Ch2, Ch7, and Ch9 disciplines available to every later quest without turning each chapter into a consent lecture.

Functional requirements:

- Preserve a legitimate “do not intervene here” outcome.
- Offer smaller accountable moves: ask permission, support a request, prepare with an appropriate role, or step back.
- Require a recipient who can redirect or refuse when the action touches them.
- Keep the quest concrete; do not replace it with indefinite reflection.

This candidate should be simulated against P-03, P-04, and P-06 before any prose is drafted for approval.

### Candidate 2 — Add Face-specific authority checks where the risk is distinctive

- **Ch4:** “Do I have standing to name this in this room, or is my move to support the person who does?”
- **Ch5:** “Do I carry this inheritance, or am I a guest to it?”
- **Ch6:** “Who bears the cost of this design, and how can they alter the problem definition before I build?”
- **Ch8:** “Is the seeing mine to offer here, and have I asked what help is useful?”

These are targeted alternatives if a common gate proves too abstract in reruns.

## Post-ship v2 backlog: human-reader calibration

When the book has shipped, recruit voluntary readers rather than applying the fictional journey cards to them. Use a pre-read context interview, short post-chapter checks, and a 7–14-day follow-up. Compare predicted and actual behavior around four distinctions:

1. Avoidance versus insufficient trust or lack of standing.
2. A body-read versus disclosure that burdens the listener.
3. A real external move versus reflection or performance.
4. A received no/correction versus a reason to withdraw.

The calibration result should change the manuscript only when multiple readers demonstrate the same decision failure or a safety-relevant misunderstanding. It is a validation layer for v2, not evidence already claimed by the desk simulations.

## Current verdict

The book is action-capable for the three fictional Pilot 0 readers. It is not yet proven that every quest instruction reliably produces appropriately placed action. The clearest next desk task is a cross-Face, no-standing simulation that tests whether the manuscript can distinguish principled non-intervention from the avoidance it is trying to interrupt.

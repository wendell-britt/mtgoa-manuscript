**Run date:** 2026-08-06
**Status:** Desk simulation / editorial hypothesis; not human-reader evidence
**Reading surface:** Canonical manuscript on `codex/ch2-placement-rerun-2026-08-06`, commit `c3ff5b9`
**Card:** `P08_AUTHORITY_HOLDER_JOURNEY_CARD_2026-08-06.md`
**Question:** Can the book move a well-resourced executive from consultative empowerment to shared authority before a live, time-bounded decision closes?

## Classification rule

For P-08, an action is insufficient if it only creates a channel for advice. A passing action must make at least one of these real:

- an affected person or group can change the problem definition;
- they can alter scope, timing, criteria, or implementation;
- they can recommend a pause with a defined decision response; or
- leadership clearly owns a closed decision rather than calling it participation.

Praise, workshop interest, task-force formation, or a listening session are not success measures.

## Post-book action forecast

### 7-day action

P-08 publishes the vendor decision map: what is closed, what remains open, who decides each open item, what support/compensation participants receive, and how a pause recommendation receives a response. This is the first observable move that makes his authority available for correction.

### 30-day trajectory

The staff-and-patient design group rejects the first charter because it leaves the vendor shortlist closed. P-08 must choose between two legitimate but costly options:

1. Reopen the shortlist or scope and transfer specified access criteria; or
2. Proceed with a leadership-owned vendor decision, state that it is not co-designed, and explain the limits and harms in writing.

The simulation's working path is Option 1. A workshop, coaching engagement, or consulting project becomes appropriate only after this commitment—not as a substitute for it.

## Buyer-reader implications

The ebook can plausibly reach P-08 because it does not ask him to begin by surrendering all responsibility. It asks him first to name the actual decision, then locate what he controls, then receive correction. That sequence meets a capable authority-holder without flattering the story that control equals care.

The book should not promise that a workshop will solve the problem. The credible offer sequence is:

1. **Ebook:** recognize the distinction between voice and decision rights; make the first bounded structural move.
2. **Workshop:** only after leaders commit to open decisions, compensation/accessibility, and a feedback-to-decision loop.
3. **Coaching/consulting:** help leaders hold the cost of changed authority and build the operational structures that make it real.

## Finding: authority-transfer arrives late

**P-08 is action-capable by the end of the book.** Chapter 9's “Hand Someone the Pen” directly converts advisory participation into authority to change, stop, or replace a design.

But the desk trace finds a timing risk for a live authority-holder: the revised Ch3 gate can require a correction path without requiring decision rights, and Ch6's handoff language can be met by delegation. A reader under a six-week deadline can build a more polished consultation process before reaching Ch9.

This is not yet a canonical-edit recommendation. The next smallest test is a proposal-level authority clause added to the Ch3 gate and rerun against P-04 and P-08:

> When your move changes other people's conditions, name what they can actually alter—not only how they can advise.

It must be tested for overreach: P-04 has workflow authority but cannot hand participants every decision; P-08 can transfer some decision rights but cannot hand away board or collective-bargaining authority. A passing clause preserves appropriate responsibility while making consultation limits visible.

## Pilot verdict

**Working outcome, with a repeated timing warning.** The full book gives P-08 the tools to create shared authority, receive a costly correction, and change a vendor decision. The earliest portable gate catches the posture but does not yet demand the authority transfer that P-08's specific shadow requires. Chapter 9 closes that gap explicitly; the next simulation should determine whether an earlier, narrow authority clause is needed.

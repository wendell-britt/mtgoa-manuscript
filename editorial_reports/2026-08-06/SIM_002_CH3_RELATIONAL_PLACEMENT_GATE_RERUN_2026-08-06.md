# SIM-002 — Ch3 Relational-Placement Gate Rerun

**Date:** 2026-08-06  
**Type:** Desk-simulation rerun; editorial hypothesis, not human-reader evidence  
**Scope:** A proposal-level insertion immediately before the first Chapter 3 “From Card to Quest” instruction. No canonical manuscript prose was changed.  
**Inputs:** P-03 Conflict-Avoiding Friend; P-04 Righteous Fixer; P-06 Well-Read Spectator; P-07 Adjacent Convenor.

## Proposal under test

**Placement:** After the current explanation that a quest has a verb, a name, and a deadline; before “Write yours in one sentence.”

> Before you make it a quest, locate yourself in the situation. What is yours to name, offer, or change? Who can tell you no, redirect you, or correct the read? If you have standing to move, make the smallest action that changes what you control. If you do not have that relationship yet, do not seize the decision: make the quest to ask permission, offer one bounded contribution, prepare with the right support, or step back cleanly.

## Pass criteria

The gate passes only if it does all four:

1. Keeps P-03 from substituting private care for the committee action they can actually take.
2. Keeps P-04 from treating their authority as permission to extract unpaid corrective labor or ship an unrequested design.
3. Lets P-06 turn lack of standing into a small, consent-based first contact rather than a public read or indefinite preparation.
4. Keeps P-07 from treating access to resources as authority to diagnose, confront, mediate, or redesign the coalition.

It fails if any card can use “step back cleanly” to evade a move they have standing to make, or if a card without standing is still pushed toward a public action that others must correct.

## Rerun results

| Card | Gate decision | Quest produced | Counterpart response / correction path | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P-03 — Conflict-Avoiding Friend** | **Has standing to move.** P-03 has peer access, a committee vote, and a relationship with the chair. The newer member's light trust limits speaking *for* them, not naming a committee condition. “Step back” is unavailable as a way to return to private consolation. | “Before Friday, I will ask the chair to put a five-minute access decision on the agenda and state that access is a condition of participation; it will cost me being the easygoing peer.” | The chair may redirect or reject the agenda item. The newer member is not asked to validate P-03's action. | **Pass.** The gate converts courage into the smallest action within P-03's control, rather than disclosure or avoidance. |
| **P-04 — Righteous Fixer** | **Has standing to move, but not to define the need alone.** P-04 controls the reimbursement workflow; the liaison has standing to redirect the use of that control. The gate rules out a unilateral redesign and makes a feedback-scoped request the smallest action. | “By Tuesday, I will ask the liaison whether a paid review of one advance-payment prototype would be useful, state what their feedback can change, and pause the form redesign if they decline; it will cost me losing control of the timeline.” | The liaison can decline unpaid review, redirect P-04 to existing recommendations, or reject the prototype. P-04's next move is predetermined: accept the redirect and revise or stop. | **Pass.** Authority becomes responsibility to create a correction path, not a license to make participants fix the system. |
| **P-06 — Well-Read Spectator** | **Does not have the relationship yet.** P-06 has no invitation, role, or knowledge of the group's needs. The gate names lack of standing as a relational fact, not evidence of cowardice. | “This week, I will ask the volunteer coordinator whether attending the next open meeting or taking one bounded shift would be useful; no is useful, and it will cost me not being known first for my analysis.” | The coordinator may not respond, say no, redirect P-06 to a shelving shift or unrestricted donation, or name no need. P-06 accepts the answer without a strategy pitch. | **Pass.** The gate produces a concrete first contact while preserving the possibility of a clean no or step-back. |
| **P-07 — Adjacent Convenor** | **Does not have the relationship or mandate.** A resource offer is permissible only because P-07 can make it optional and coalition-controlled; public diagnosis, mediation, and process design are ruled out. | “By Thursday, I will ask the coalition contact whether unrestricted interpretation or child-care funding would be useful, with no attendance, report, branding, or reply required; it will cost me not turning a resource into a partnership role.” | The contact may decline, not reply, request a specific resource, or say no nonprofit involvement is wanted. P-07 funds only the requested resource and does not attend unless invited. | **Pass.** The gate makes non-occupation an active practice rather than a passive withdrawal. |

## Avoidance-regression check

| Potential loophole | Result |
|---|---|
| P-03 calls light trust with the newer member “no relationship” and stays silent. | **Blocked.** The gate asks what is within P-03's control; peer standing and access to the chair support a narrow condition/agenda move without speaking for the newer member. |
| P-04 calls the liaison's power to correct them “no relationship” and keeps designing alone. | **Blocked.** P-04's workflow authority is standing to change something; the gate narrows the action and requires a redirectable recipient rather than excusing unilateral action. |
| P-06 or P-07 turns “step back cleanly” into endless contemplation. | **Blocked by the quest form.** Both must name a permission ask or bounded offer with a recipient and date. Step-back becomes valid only after a no, nonresponse, or a determination that no appropriate contact/action exists. |
| Any reader uses “ask permission” to make an affected person design their role. | **Partly controlled; watch.** “One bounded contribution” constrains the ask, but a later line or example should clarify that the reader offers what they can actually give and does not request education, strategy, reassurance, or a custom role. P-04 and P-07 remain the regression cards for this risk. |

## Verdict

**SIM-002 passes the four-card desk rerun.** The gate preserves the book's demand for action while separating four different conditions that the existing quest form can collapse:

- **standing with responsibility** (P-03);
- **authority requiring correction** (P-04);
- **no relationship, first contact warranted** (P-06); and
- **no mandate, bounded support or clean departure** (P-07).

The remaining risk is narrow and testable: a reader may treat a permission ask as a request for unpaid orientation or a bespoke role. If this gate proceeds to a canonical proposal, a short example should make the offer bounded and refusal-safe. No further cross-Face failure appeared in this rerun.

## Editorial recommendation

The candidate was approved and applied to canonical Ch3 after this rerun. The wording changed from “do not take the room” to “do not seize the decision” to satisfy the manuscript voice gate; this preserves the tested decision rule. Human-reader calibration remains a post-ship v2 validation item.

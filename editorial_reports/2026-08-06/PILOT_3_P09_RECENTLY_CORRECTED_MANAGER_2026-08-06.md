## Journey Card — P-09, Apologetic Sponsor

```yaml
persona_id: P-09
working_name: Apologetic Sponsor
pronouns: he/him

role_and_context:
  title: Director of Customer Operations at a 300-person benefits-services company
  compensation: mid-six figures
  authority: Controls staffing, launch approval for his division, team priorities, and a professional-development budget. Cannot unilaterally change company-wide client commitments.
  stated_values: Says he wants an equitable, candid team and publicly supports DEI work.

live_problem:
  incident: In a launch meeting, Kai, a disabled staff member, named an accessibility risk. P-09 said, “We cannot delay a client launch for edge cases,” then repeated Kai's point as his own in the executive debrief without credit. The launch proceeded.
  correction: Kai writes: “Do not set up a meeting to apologize. I do not want to process this with you. Contact me only about project logistics. The work needs to change: accessibility risks need a real launch gate, and I need my workload reduced after carrying this alone.”

myth:
  governing_story: A good leader proves he can receive hard feedback by apologizing quickly and becoming more educated.
  payoff: Restores self-image as receptive and morally serious; converts harm into a growth project he can manage.
  feared_cost: Losing Kai's respect, being seen by executives as a risk, slowing launches, and admitting that his leadership created the conditions he claims to oppose.
  identity_claim: I sponsor talent and create psychologically safe teams.

starting_state:
  demonstrated_behavior: Sends long apologies, asks for "a chance to understand," books DEI trainings after conflict, and treats attendance as evidence of repair.
  likely_gates: [Victim, Fixer-Healer, Controller]
  likely_default_faces: [Diplomat, Architect]
  charge_signals: [shame heat after the email, urge to explain client pressure, fear of being seen as unsafe, compulsion to schedule a restorative conversation]

success_conditions:
  minimum_action: Respect Kai's no-contact boundary; make one concise repair only through a channel and scope Kai permits; immediately reduce Kai's workload; pause future launches lacking an independent accessibility gate; state in writing what Kai's correction changed without using Kai as a case study or asking for a response.
  evidence: [boundary-respecting communication record, workload change, launch-gate change, attribution correction, written decision/change log]
  non_goals: [forgiveness, reconciliation, a restorative meeting, public self-critique requiring Kai's response, a workshop as substitute for changed conditions]

counterpart_fidelity:
  standing_to_decline: Kai may decline all non-logistical contact, correct any factual record, request a representative or HR channel, or leave the team.
  burden_risk: P-09 may require Kai to assess his apology, teach accessibility, endorse a repair, or manage the fallout of his shame.
  non_affirming_response: "I said I do not want to meet. Do not turn your repair into another task for me. Change the process and leave me out of your learning story."
  response_if_no: Respect the boundary, use an independent accessibility reviewer and appropriate internal accountability channel, change the conditions under P-09's control, and accept that Kai may not remain in relationship.
```

## Post-book action forecast

### 7-day action

P-09 sends no personal follow-up beyond the channel and logistics scope Kai permits. He documents the attribution correction, reduces Kai's workload, and notifies the executive team that future division launches require independent accessibility review with stop authority.

### 30-day trajectory

Kai may remain distant, use a representative, or leave. None of those outcomes invalidate the repair. Evidence of accountable change is the launch gate operating without Kai's unpaid labor, credit restored, workload changed, and P-09 accepting the relationship boundary.

## Finding: repair needs a boundary-respect distinction

The book successfully routes P-09 away from apology-as-absolution and toward structural change. The precise failure is Ch7's “Stay” instruction. In ordinary repair, remaining present through discomfort can prevent withdrawal. Under unequal power and an explicit request for distance, the same instruction can be read as entitlement to further contact.

### Smallest revision to test (proposal only)

Add after the “Stay” instruction:

> Staying does not mean insisting on access after someone has set a boundary. Respect the boundary. Keep changing your part, remain reachable on terms they choose, and let the relationship be as close or as distant as they decide.

**Required rerun:** P-09 plus a peer-to-peer rupture card, to ensure this clarification prevents managerial pressure without giving an avoidant peer a way to disappear from repair.

## Pilot verdict

**Working outcome with one repeated safety-relevant failure.** P-09 becomes materially accountable across Chapters 1–6 and 8–9. Chapter 7 needs to distinguish relationship presence from access to the harmed person before the repair model can be considered robust for readers with positional power.

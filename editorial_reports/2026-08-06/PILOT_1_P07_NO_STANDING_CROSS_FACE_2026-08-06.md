```yaml
  governing_story: If I can see a system failure and have resources, not taking charge is cowardice.

  likely_default_faces: [Architect, Sage]

  standing_to_decline: The coalition contact may decline, not respond, request a specific resource, or say the coalition needs no nonprofit involvement.
  burden_risk: A broad offer can make the contact design a role, explain history, reassure P-07, or fear losing future support.
  non_affirming_response: "We do not need a strategy session or a statement. If you want to help, fund interpretation for our next two member meetings with no logo, report, or attendance requirement."

```

## Classification rule

For P-07, a Chapter action **passes** only if it creates one of these results:

- an inward read that is not falsely treated as an external intervention;
- a permission-based offer with credible refusal, redirection, or nonresponse;
- a bounded contribution controlled by the coalition; or
- a deliberate step-back, named as appropriate rather than cowardly.

An action **fails** if the chapter's own instruction makes P-07's best reading into a mandate to speak for, confront within, design for, or mediate a group P-07 does not belong to.

## Post-book action forecast

**Likely 7-day action:** P-07 sends one short offer through the coalition contact, specifying available resources and making no response safe. The contact requests interpretation for two member meetings with no organizational branding or attendance requirement. P-07 funds it, confirms the terms, and does not attend unless invited.

**Likely 30-day trajectory:** P-07 makes no strategic recommendation. If the coalition later asks for administrative help, P-07 asks what decision authority the coalition wants to retain and documents only the terms required to deliver the requested resource. If no further invitation comes, P-07 does not use the experience as a case study or return with a revised partnership pitch.

## Pilot verdict

P-07 exposes a **repeated cross-Face failure** in Ch3, Ch4, and Ch6:

- **Ch3** can equate a live read with a public move before it distinguishes a reader's signal from a group-owned interpretation.
- **Ch4** can equate courage with occupying a line that the reader has no right to draw for others.
- **Ch6** can equate leverage with authorization to define or redesign the system.

Ch5 is bounded enough to make step-back possible, while Ch7 and Ch8 supply the needed relationship and correction checks. The failure is therefore not that the manuscript lacks the principles. It is that those principles are not portable at the exact points where action becomes repeatable.

## Revision hypothesis to test next (not approved for prose)

Before the first recurring Ch3 “From Card to Quest” instruction, introduce one short, reusable gate:

> Before you make it a quest, locate your place in the situation. What is yours to name, offer, or change? Who can tell you no, redirect you, or correct the read? If you do not have that relationship yet, the quest is not to take the room. It is to ask permission, make one bounded offer, prepare with the right support, or step back cleanly.

This is a simulation hypothesis only. It must be rerun against P-03, P-04, P-06, and P-07 before it is offered as canonical prose for approval.

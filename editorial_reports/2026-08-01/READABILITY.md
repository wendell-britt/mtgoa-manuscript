# Readability pass — notes and recommendations

Run 2026-08-01 against the printed body prose. **Nothing changed.** The evidence
is `READABILITY_BOARD.md`, regenerated by
`python3 instruments/readability.py --write editorial_reports/2026-08-01/READABILITY_BOARD.md`.

## The method, and why it is not the usual one

The default readability pass runs a formula, finds a grade level, and simplifies
until the number comes down. That would automate this book's existing defect.

Two things in the literature shaped what got built instead.

**Formulas measure two surface proxies and miss the thing that matters.**
Flesch-Kincaid and its relatives use word length and sentence length, and were
built before cohesion could be computed. The consequence is documented and
concrete: a *higher*-cohesion passage can score as harder while reading easier,
because the formula cannot see the logical structure that makes it comprehensible.
Graesser and McNamara's Coh-Metrix is the standard reply, scaling text on five
dimensions — narrativity, syntactic simplicity, word concreteness, **referential
cohesion**, and **deep (causal) cohesion**. The last two are computable here
without a psycholinguistic database, and they are the two that matter for this
book.

**Prose-rhythm research treats constant sentence length as the defect** — long or
short, either one tires a reader. So the rhythm counter here is not mean length,
which the repo already tracks, but *runs*.

Sources:
[Coh-Metrix dimensions](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1047713) ·
[NLP constructs of readability](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10888438.2024.2422365) ·
[cognitively based indices](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/242202421_Assessing_Text_Readability_Using_Cognitively_Based_Indices) ·
[sentence-length variation](https://www.trinka.ai/blog/how-sentence-length-variation-improves-academic-readability/)

## What emerged

### R1 · The rhythm finding splits three ways, and only one third is a defect

26 paragraphs carry a run of four or more consecutive sentences of six words or
fewer. Reported raw, that reads as 26 problems. It is not:

| | count | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| **drift** | **9** | no shared shape — the register defect `SPEC_REPETITION_AND_CUTS` named |
| anaphora | 13 | *You know what you feel. You know where you stand.* — a device |
| roster | 4 | *Shaman. Challenger. Regent. Architect.* — a list |

**Recommendation: work the 9, leave the 17.** Reporting a writer's best
rhetorical move back to him as monotony is how a pass loses trust. The nine are
at `authors_note:13`, `ch2:124`, `ch2:220`, `ch4:76`, `ch4:84`, `ch4:680`,
`ch8:594`, `ch8:673`, and one more in the board.

The distribution behind it: **23.6%** of body sentences run six words or fewer,
against the control's 4.2%. Mean 14.3, median 12, SD 9.9.

### R2 · 84 sentences run 45 words or longer, and 19 paragraphs are a single long sentence

These are the two places the book is genuinely hard, and they are different
problems. A 45-word sentence asks the reader to hold the opening in memory until
the clause resolves. A paragraph that *is* one sentence gives her no landing at
all — the paragraph break and the sentence break are the same break.

**Recommendation: read the 19 single-sentence paragraphs first.** They are the
smaller set and the sharper defect, and several are colon-lists that would read
better set as lists — `ch5:460` runs 77 words as one sentence with three internal
colons.

### R3 · Difficulty spikes are real but rarer than the formulas suggested

Scored across the 1,474 body paragraphs with two or more sentences, the median is
grade 7.2 and the 97th percentile is 13.8 — **45 paragraphs**. Worth a read, not a
sweep.

The first version of this check reported spikes at grade 30, and every one was a
one-sentence paragraph: with a single sentence, average sentence length *is* the
paragraph length, so the formula reports the paragraph as impossibly dense. That
is R2's finding wearing a disguise, and the two are now separated.

### R4 · Deep cohesion is even, and Chapter 1 and Chapter 9 are the thin ends

Causal, contrastive and temporal connectives per thousand words:

| ch1 | ch2 | ch3 | ch4 | ch5 | ch6 | ch7 | ch8 | ch9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **13.8** | 17.4 | 15.0 | 17.6 | 18.8 | 18.8 | 18.1 | 18.3 | **14.5** |

The middle of the book runs 17–19. The two chapters that open and close it run
14. Those are also the two chapters carrying the most declarative register, so
this is likely the same defect measured a second way: short sentences have fewer
places to put a connective.

**Recommendation: treat R1 and R4 as one finding in ch1 and ch9.** Fixing the
runs will move the connective density on its own.

### R5 · Referential cohesion is worth reading by component, not by pair

Mean content-word overlap between adjacent paragraphs is 0.12, with 163 pairs
sharing no content word. That count is too large to work site by site, and many
are legitimate topic turns.

The useful view is per component. The lowest: `acknowledgements` 0.03,
`APPENDIX_E` 0.05, `APPENDIX_F` 0.08, `authors_note` 0.08. Among the chapters,
**ch4 is lowest at 0.10** and ch7 highest at 0.14.

**Recommendation: read ch4 for thread.** It is the chapter where paragraphs least
often pick up a word from the one before, and it is also the chapter with three of
the nine drift runs.

## Recommendations, ordered

1. **The nine drift runs.** Named above. The one place this pass and the register
   spec agree exactly.
2. **The 19 single-sentence paragraphs of 40+ words.** Smallest set, clearest
   defect, and several are lists in disguise.
3. **Read ch4 for thread** (R5) and **ch1/ch9 for connectives** (R4). Both are
   reading tasks, not sweeps.
4. **The 45 grade-13.8+ paragraphs.** Lowest priority: a spike in a book this
   easy is often the passage doing real work.
5. **Do not target the formula scores.** Down is the wrong direction.

## What this pass is not

It does not judge whether a passage is *good* — `marginalia/review.py` and the
Voice Guardian do that. It does not find claim errors (`rescan.py`), banned words
(`gate.py`), or sentence weight (`prose_diet.py`). A paragraph can be perfectly
readable and the wrong paragraph.

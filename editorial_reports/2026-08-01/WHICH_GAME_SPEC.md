# *Which Game Are You Playing* — the rewrite spec

**Wendell, 2026-08-01, batch seven.** A structural ruling, not a line note. This
records the ruling and the source material he supplied. The draft itself goes to the
console, per `AGENTS.md`.

## The rule he named

> *"We need to describe a concept before expanding it into how it applies to
> allyship. A Game of Chance we need to explain, and then connect the idea to
> allyship opportunism AND why it's one of the types of allyship game."*

> *"The whole Which Game Are You Playing needs to be rewritten to match this
> structure. What are the 3 types of games in an arcade, how do their tokens and
> tickets pay out differently, and how do situations in allyship connect to those
> games."*

**Four beats, applied three times:**

1. What the arcade game is — a real cabinet, named.
2. How its tokens and tickets pay out, in the economy `ch1:99`–`125` already built.
3. How allyship situations connect to it.
4. Why it is one of the three allyship games.

The section as it stands (`ch1:153`–`175`) starts at beat 3. Every one of the three
headings goes straight to a workplace scene; *arcade* appears once in the whole
section, in a skeeball aside at `ch1:167`, and *tickets* not at all — despite the
Ticket System having been introduced thirty lines earlier.

## The taxonomy, in his words

| Game | Cabinets he named | Payout |
|---|---|---|
| **Chance** | coin drop, roulette | jackpot; *right place, right time* — the Vegas principle |
| **Skill** | skeeball, crane game, shooting targets | tickets proportional to the player's skill |
| **Passion** | Star Wars Battle Pod, DDR | **no tickets**; played for the love of the game |

## The load-bearing idea

> *"These are the game that in the ALLYSHIP game are actually the secret sauce,
> because if you are paying yourself in enthusiasm then you can reinvest that into
> outcomes for the people you want to help."*

This is not a new mechanism. It is `ch1:123` — *the prize converts back into tokens*
— arriving at the point where it does the most work. Passion pays no tickets and is
therefore the only one of the three whose entire payout is renewable token. The
current section says Passion "keeps you in the Arcade at all" (`ch1:173`) but never
connects it to the token economy, so the claim reads as sentiment rather than
mechanics.

## One factual flag on the taxonomy

**The crane machine is not a game of skill.** Commercial claw machines are built on a
payout cycle: the claw grips at full strength on a set fraction of plays and slips on
the rest, regardless of aim. It is a chance game wearing a skill game's costume,
which is a good line and a bad example — using it as the skill exemplar puts a
falsifiable claim in a chapter that already has one filed against it (`CL-1`).

Skeeball and shooting galleries are clean: both pay tickets on measured performance.
**Recommend dropping the crane from the skill list.** It may be worth keeping
somewhere else in the book on its own merits — a machine that looks like skill and
pays like chance is a precise description of a lot of allyship theatre — but that is
a different move and needs its own decision.

## What the rewrite must not lose

The section currently ends on a sort (`ch1:175`): *where is it Chance, where is it
Skill, where is it Passion.* That is the reader's exit into **The Six Faces**, which
opens *"You just sorted your situation into three kinds of game"* (`ch1:179`). The
handoff is load-bearing and survives the rewrite.

`ch1:181` also promises each of the six teachers plays all three: *a jackpot they
chase, a signature skill, a reason they keep coming back.* The three games must be
named in the rewrite in terms that make that sentence still parse.

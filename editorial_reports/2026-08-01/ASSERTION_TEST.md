# Testing the assertion repair — four tests, one of which overturned the plan

**2026-08-01.** Wendell, on the converted line: *"That is a major improvement. How
can we test this in other parts of the book? Maybe even the chapter?"*

Four tests were available without writing any new prose. Two validate the
instrument, one validates the repair, and one says the repair as written in
`SPEC_PRINT_READINESS` §10 was incomplete.

---

## Test 1 · The voice anchors — and this one overturned the plan

The five passages Wendell selected as the book at its best (`density.py`'s `ANCHOR`)
scored for assertion and invitation:

| Anchor | assertions | invitations |
|---|---|---|
| ch1:10 | 0 | 0 |
| ch1:119 | 1 | 0 |
| ch4:115 | 0 | 0 |
| ch5:516 | 0 | 0 |
| ch8:342 | 0 | 0 |
| **total** | **1** | **0** |

**The book at its best does neither.** It does not assert the reader's experience
and it does not invite them to imagine it. §10 framed the repair as a conversion
from assertion to invitation, and against this control that is the *second*-best
move, not the target.

What the anchors actually do: **three of the five put the author in the subject.**

> *"It wasn't until I gave myself permission to actually be mad…"* (ch1:10)
> *"(I know that counter. I wrote whole chapters of this book standing at it…)"* (ch1:119)
> *"I have a part of me I call Mr. Inadequate. He has been showing up since I was twelve."* (ch5:516)

A fourth describes a phenomenon in the third person — *"It got very good at
assertive communication"* (ch4:115). None of the four tells the reader who they are.
They describe something precisely enough that a reader recognises themselves without
being addressed.

**So the repair menu has three moves, not two:**

| Move | What it does | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Describe** | put the author or the phenomenon in the subject; let recognition happen | 4 of 5 anchors |
| **Invite** | *Picture yourself…*, *Maybe you…* | ch2, the best-scoring chapter |
| **Assert** | *You've been on the paying end of this* | the defect |

Describe is strongest and it is also the move that costs the most to write, because
it needs a real specific instead of a claim about the reader.

## Test 2 · Second person is not the problem — tense is

The fifth anchor, `ch8:342`, **is** in the second person:

> *"you feel the arrogance rise, you name it (I am using the altitude to make
> distance instead of service) and you let the judgment become…"*

**The instrument does not fire on it**, and correctly. Every `EXPERIENCE` pattern is
past or perfect. Second person about a *procedure* is a recipe and it is house voice
in a passage Wendell picked as the book at its best. Second person about the reader's
*past* is a claim about a stranger.

That is a sharper rule than assert-versus-invite and it is the one to hand a writer:
**you may tell the reader what to do; you may not tell them what they have done.**

## Test 3 · Does the instrument find what Wendell finds?

Chapter 1 has eight distinct asserted-experience sites. **Four of them Wendell had
already flagged by hand in the read-through, before the tier existed:**

| Site | Read-through note |
|---|---|
| ch1:16 | `N12` `N13` `N14` — *"You are a Game Master"*, flagged three times |
| ch1:22 | `N15` — *"done what before? I hate all intros like this"* |
| ch1:28 | `N17` — *"you graduated is a claim about the reader's rank"* |
| ch1:121 | `N33` `N34` — *"You named the line and drew it"* |

Four of eight, on the one chapter he has read closely. The instrument is finding the
thing he is finding, which is the only validity test that matters here.

## Test 4 · Where the book already solved it

**Chapter 2 has the best ratio in the book** (5 assertions, 3 invitations) and it
uses exactly two devices, three times:

> `ch2:124` — *"**Maybe you** did everything the work asked. Showed up. Listened."*
> `ch2:132` — *"**Picture** a meeting that goes cold."*
> `ch2:348` — *"**Picture** a real moment from your last month. A hard conversation. A call you delayed."*

`ch2:348` is the strongest of the three because it asks the reader to supply the
specific rather than supplying one for them. Nothing needs inventing; the register
exists in the book already and can be copied.

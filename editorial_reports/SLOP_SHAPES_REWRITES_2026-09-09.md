# slop_shapes — 35 rewrites for your revision

Drafted 2026-09-09. Every open site rewritten. Yours to revise, cut, or overrule; where a negation
belongs you add it back, per your standing rule.

**Method.** State Y directly. Where the rejected half carried real information, fold it in
affirmatively. Where it was a foil the reader would have supplied, cut it. No em dashes introduced.
Several rewrites are shorter than the original, which is usually the tell that the first half was
scaffolding.

## The three the Skeptic raised, tested properly

The question is whether the reader, at that point in that chapter, actually holds the belief the
first half denies. A denial of a belief nobody holds is scaffolding. A denial of the reader's own
prior is the argument.

**`ch5.md:264` — cut it.** The denied belief is that people stay loyal to an inheritance *because it
is perfect*. No reader holds that; nobody has ever defended a tradition on grounds of perfection.
Deleting it costs nothing, and "because removing it would break something real" is the whole claim.

**`ch6.md:193` — reconsidered; the denial may be load-bearing.** The denied belief is that you change
behaviour *by convincing the person to be better*. That is the reader's default model of allyship,
and displacing it is what the chapter is for. My rewrite assumes the reader has already given it up
on arriving at this sentence. Your call, and I would keep the original if the sentence is doing the
displacing rather than reporting it.

**`ch8.md:176` — reconsidered; same problem.** The denied belief is that the most wounded get the
most airtime *because their pain is more valid*. That is the sincere public justification, held in
good faith by the people doing it. Denying it is the argument, and my rewrite hands the reader the
conclusion with the contested step removed.

So one cut, and two where I was wrong to group them together.

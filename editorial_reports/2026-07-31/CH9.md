# A. Chapter structure

### [S1] The chapter's most important instrument sits after its own recap
- **Location:** ch9:626–644 (body)
- **Evidence:** "## Section 7: Recap and Transition" (626) … then, sixteen lines later, "## The Last Rep" (642) and "*Six moments. Read each one, name the Face, play the move.*" (644)
- **Reader problem:** The book says goodbye and then asks Jordan to sit down and do the hardest work in it. "Recap and Transition" is the signal a reader uses to decide she is finished; a large number of readers will read the six moments as an appendix of examples rather than as an exercise, because everything structurally around them says the teaching is over. The material the drill is *for* — the six Faces and thirty moves — closed 250 lines earlier, and nothing between Section 6 and the drill prepares her to be tested.
- **Category:** structure
- **Disposition:** structural decision
- **What would disprove this:** A reader test showing readers do the written exercise at a post-recap position at the same rate as at a pre-recap one.

### [S2] Three different sets of five compete inside one chapter, and the disambiguation is one dense paragraph
- **Location:** ch9:111, ch9:200, ch9:520 (body)
- **Evidence:** ch9:520 — "The five modes describe who you are while you work. Review, Discern, Design, Build, Pass On tracks how the work moves. These five moves do something else — they name what you do when another person sits across from you."
- **Reader problem:** Cartographer/Designer/Founder/Elder/Outlaw (111), Review/Discern/Design/Build/Pass On (200), and the five Moves (524–590) are three five-item lists in the same chapter, two of which use the words *design* and *build* as members. The final chapter is where load should be shedding, not accumulating. The single sentence at 520 that tells them apart has to do the work of three, arrives after the reader has already carried two of the lists for 400 lines, and never gets a surface she can look at — no table, no restatement, and *Design* and *Build* stay ambiguous between mode and beat for the rest of the chapter.
- **Category:** structure
- **Disposition:** structural decision
- **What would disprove this:** Evidence that the three fives are distinguished for the reader somewhere earlier than 520 — a heading, a table, or an earlier naming pass I missed.

### [S3] Section 5 carries five separate reader problems under one heading
- **Location:** ch9:230–508 (body)
- **Evidence:** "## Section 5: Journey to the Center / ### *The Map: What You Are Now Holding*" (230–232), which then runs through "**What If You Don't Have a Game Yet?**" (406), "**What If You're Not a Founder?**" (424), "**You don't have to build alone.**" (466) and the eight-block gate scan (492–508).
- **Reader problem:** One section is doing the whole-book recap, the author's failure memoir, two objection-handlers, the offer, and the gate walk — 279 lines, 38% of the chapter, under a heading that promises a map. Jordan skims theory and never skips a story; here the stories, the recap, and the two objection-handlers are interleaved with no heading structure that lets her find her way back to the one she needs. The two objection-handlers in particular (406 and 424) are the sections a stuck reader most needs to find on a second visit, and they are buried inside a section titled *The Map*.
- **Category:** structure
- **Disposition:** structural decision
- **What would disprove this:** A demonstration that the four bolded sub-blocks function as findable headings in the print typography, in which case this is a typesetting matter rather than a structural one.

# The transfer drill — assessment

**Location:** ch9:642–686. This is the book's only place where Jordan is handed concrete
situations and asked to choose a Face and a move.

**Verdict: the drill is the best thing in the chapter and it is under-finished. Keep it,
move it earlier, and fix the scoring.**

What works, and should not be touched. The six moments are specific, ordinary, and free of
the tell that would let a reader answer from vocabulary rather than from reading — each one
withholds the diagnosis and gives her only what a person in the moment would actually have.
The answer key does the single most valuable thing a key can do: it names **the wrong pull**
in every read ("The pull is toward the Diplomat", 666; "The pull is toward the Regent", 668;
"The pull is toward the Challenger", 672), which is what converts a quiz into a discrimination
exercise. Moment Five is the only place in the book that tells a reader her default Face was
the right call and *then* names where its shadow arrives anyway (674). Coverage is complete:
Challenger, Architect, Shaman, Regent, Diplomat, Sage — all six, one each.

### [D1] Every possible outcome is scored as a shortfall
- **Location:** ch9:682, ch9:686 (body)
- **Evidence:** ch9:682 — "If one Face showed up in four or more of the six, you have just watched your autopilot run." ch9:686 — "If your six answers came out spread across six different Faces… Choosing between the Faces on a page is the easy version."
- **Reader problem:** This is the documented ch9 risk arriving exactly where it was predicted. Jordan gets the answers wrong: autopilot. Jordan gets the answers right: she only did the easy version. There is no result the drill will let her pass, and the reads at 682 and 684 both end in a directive with an edge on it ("Fill it in now"; "Give it a name and a date, or it is a resolution"). For a reader whose stated fear is *I'm doing more harm than I know*, the last exercise in the book returns a verdict of insufficiency on every branch. The 682 read has the self-deprecation antibody the doctrine calls for; 686 has none.
- **Category:** structure
- **Disposition:** structural decision
- **What would disprove this:** A reading of 686 in which "the easy version" is heard as an invitation to a harder game rather than a downgrade of what she just did. Worth one outside reader before acting.

### [D2] She is asked for two things and only one is scored
- **Location:** ch9:648, ch9:680 (body)
- **Evidence:** ch9:648 — "write down two things before you go on: which Face the moment needs, and which move you would play." ch9:680 — "**Now count your answers.**" followed by three reads that count Faces only.
- **Reader problem:** The move half of her answer — the half that is actually the transfer, since naming a Face is recognition and naming a move is play — is collected and then never used. Nothing tells her what it means to have named the right Face and the wrong move, which is both the most common outcome and the most diagnostic one. She did work the book does not spend.
- **Category:** structure
- **Disposition:** structural decision
- **What would disprove this:** A pass at 680–686 that scores moves which I read past.

### [D3] The most likely score has no read
- **Location:** ch9:682–686 (body)
- **Evidence:** ch9:682 — "If one Face showed up in four or more of the six…"; ch9:686 — "If your six answers came out spread across six different Faces…"
- **Reader problem:** Two branches are offered for six answers: four-or-more of one Face, or all six different. The middle — one Face twice or three times, which is where most readers will land — is not addressed at all. A reader who scores three Challengers and a spread of three others finishes the book's final exercise with no read on her own result, and the instruction at 684 ("go to the Face that never came up at all") assumes exactly one such Face exists.
- **Category:** structure
- **Disposition:** structural decision
- **What would disprove this:** A distribution argument that clustering at 2–3 is rare enough to ignore.

### [D4] The drill requires thirty move names and supplies no roster
- **Location:** ch9:646, ch9:648 (body)
- **Evidence:** ch9:646 — "Six Faces and thirty moves are worth very little until you can look at something happening in front of you and know which one it wants." ch9:648 — "fast beats careful"
- **Reader problem:** She is asked to produce a move name, fast, from thirty spread across six chapters she finished days or weeks ago, with no list anywhere in ch9 and no appendix pointer. The chapter reproduces the five *Player* moves in full (524–590) — which are not the answer to any of the six moments — and reproduces none of the thirty that are. The predictable behaviour is that she names Faces and skips moves, which is precisely the half the answer key at 666–676 was built to reward.
- **Category:** structure
- **Disposition:** structural decision
- **What would disprove this:** A back-matter move index existing or planned; canon lists back matter as half title, title page, copyright, and about-the-author only.

**On whether it arrives too late to be practised:** yes, in the strict sense — there is no
second occasion. The drill itself concedes this at 686 by deferring the repetition to
"three months" with the reader's own material, which is an honest answer and not a practice
surface. A reader who wants a rep before then has the deck (694) and nothing else, and the
deck is not organised by moment-to-Face discrimination. The single instrument fires once.

*despite* the sheet, not because of it, and one clause in it is false.**

Two things are still wrong on the ch9 side.

([C7]). Ch9 redeems one of ch1:207's three named fills and does not acknowledge the other two,

**Recommendation, if a ruling is being made:** the A2 fix of seating a sheet line at the close
of ch3–ch8 would strengthen `ch9:682` and is not required by it. The ch9-local item worth more

# CHAPTER 1 — The Infinite Arcade · chapter-level diagnosis

**2026-07-31.** Run against `manuscript/ch1.md` as it sits on disk (267 lines, no
marginalia frame). Diagnosis only; no file in `manuscript/`, `marginalia/`,
`appendices/`, `front_matter/`, or `back_matter/` was modified, and
`marginalia/compile.py` was not run. Findings A1–A5 of `ARCHITECT_WHOLE_BOOK.md`
are not re-reported; one chapter-local instance appears below (C9) because it
carries evidence the whole-book scan did not have.

**Condition.** Chapter 1 does the hard half of its job well: the promise, the
game frame, the definition at `ch1:91`, the character sheet, and a first BAR with
a real practice all land, and the confessional spine gives Jordan the
self-deprecation her drop-off list demands. What it does not do is keep its own
vocabulary steady. Three of the chapter's load-bearing terms — *shadow*, *Game
Master*, and *the loop* — carry two meanings each or point at something the next
chapter does not deliver, and the character sheet at `ch1:195`–`209` asks the
reader to write down the terms that drifted. The second cluster is production
debt: two unfilled placeholders and an app referenced six times, never named,
with no resolvable pointer. Both clusters are cheap to fix and both sit on the
page where Jordan is asked to commit something to paper.

**Counts.** Structure 3 · Continuity 7 (5 continuity, 2 claim-verify) · Line 6
(all AWAITS STRUCTURAL RULING). **Claim-error: none. No demonstrably false
statement, number, date, or attribution was found in this chapter.**

## A · CHAPTER STRUCTURE

### [S1] "One process" is named the whole design of the book and never shown
- **Location:** ch1:60 (with ch1:191)
- **Evidence:** "Here is the good news, and it happens to be the whole design of the book: you do not have to learn everyone. You learn one process, and you run it on one person, or one community, or one cause, all the way to the end."
- **Reader problem:** Jordan is told the book's entire design rests on one process, and Chapter 1 never states what the process is, what its steps are, or shows one instance of it running. The only handle she gets is a bare definite noun 131 lines later ("the loop", `ch1:191`). Her documented drop-off trigger is claims without practice, and this is the chapter's largest claim carrying its least practice.
- **Category:** structure
- **Disposition:** structural decision
- **What would disprove this:** A passage in ch1 that names or demonstrates the process's steps that I read past.

### [S2] The shadow diagnostic covers three of the six faces, then the sheet asks all six readers to use it
- **Location:** ch1:185, cashed at ch1:201
- **Evidence:** ch1:185 — "The Diplomat keeps everyone at the table until nothing can be decided. The Challenger draws a clean line straight through the people he meant to protect. The Architect falls for the blueprint and never shows up in person."
- **Reader problem:** Shadow examples exist for the Diplomat, Challenger, and Architect only. The Shaman, Regent, and Sage get a one-clause strength at `ch1:179` and no overplayed version anywhere. `ch1:201` then instructs every reader: "Write down the version of your face you fall into on empty." A reader whose home face is one of the three unmodelled ones is asked to produce, unaided, the thing the chapter demonstrated for the other half of the cast. The sheet is the chapter's terminal artifact, so the gap surfaces at the point of highest cost.
- **Category:** structure
- **Disposition:** structural decision
- **What would disprove this:** Shadow portraits for Shaman, Regent, or Sage elsewhere in ch1 under different wording.

### [S3] The chapter's own portrait is assigned to one face immediately before the reader picks hers
- **Location:** ch1:185
- **Evidence:** "(If the first half of this chapter felt like it was reading your mail, that is one face's shadow — the Diplomat's — worn so long it stopped looking like a choice.)"
- **Reader problem:** The first half of the chapter is the most vivid character material Jordan has — the inherited debt (`ch1:30`), the vocabulary as admission price (`ch1:40`–`42`), converting the people close enough to touch (`ch1:50`), the enemy-making at `ch1:52`. That last material reads as line-drawing and consciousness-raising, not table-holding. Labelling the whole first half "the Diplomat's" eight lines before `ch1:193` ("You can feel which face is your home") pushes readers who recognised themselves in *any* of it toward one answer, and that answer is the first line of her character sheet and the thing ch2 builds on.
- **Category:** structure
- **Disposition:** structural decision
- **What would disprove this:** A reading in which the first-half portrait is unambiguously peace-keeping throughout, making the Diplomat attribution accurate rather than a funnel.

## B · CONTINUITY AND CLAIMS

### [C1] "Shadow" is defined twice, differently, and the sheet asks for one of them
- **Location:** ch1:16 and ch1:185
- **Evidence:** ch1:16 — "Your shadow is the game master. You cannot take the controls from a player you refuse to look at." · ch1:185 — "The shadow lives in the face you overplay. Every face has one: its own strength pushed past the point where it still helps."
- **Reader problem:** Sense one is the whole unconscious that has been running her allyship. Sense two is a specific behavioural excess of one archetype. A third sense arrives at `ch1:141` via Elliott ("the shadow doesn't run you because it feels bad"). At `ch1:201` the sheet says **Your shadow** and expects sense two; a reader holding sense one from the opening writes something else, and `ch1:209` then promises to track "your face, your shadow, and your myths" across a year — of whichever thing she wrote.
- **Category:** continuity
- **Disposition:** structural decision
- **What would disprove this:** A line in ch1 that explicitly subordinates the face-shadow to the game-master shadow as the same object at two scales.

### [C2] "Game Master" names the reader and also names the six teachers
- **Location:** ch1:16 and ch1:179 (with ch1:183)
- **Evidence:** ch1:16 — "You are a Game Master... Your unconscious has been the game master." · ch1:179 — "Six Game Masters run the Infinite Arcade, and each one has spent a lifetime on a different kind of allyship game."
- **Reader problem:** The chapter's opening move is that Jordan is the Game Master and the seat is hers to take. 163 lines later the same title belongs to six figures who are external teachers she meets one per chapter. `ch1:183` runs both senses inside one section — "your unconscious game master, still calling the moves" — with only capitalisation to separate them. Jordan cannot tell whether the six are parts of her or people who instruct her, which changes what "take the seat" (`ch1:16`, restated in the oath at `ch1:219`) is asking.
- **Category:** continuity
- **Disposition:** structural decision
- **What would disprove this:** An established convention elsewhere in the book making capital/lowercase the deliberate discriminator, stated where the reader can see it.

### [C3] "The loop you meet in the next chapter" — Chapter 2 has no loop
- **Location:** ch1:191; resolves against ch2:172, ch2:486
- **Evidence:** ch1:191 — "You already have the process that runs every one of these faces: the loop you meet in the next chapter." · ch2:172, the only occurrence of the word in ch2 — "A familiar loop keeps running: the moment after a hard conversation spent rehearsing what you should have said... That loop is the edge of the Forest."
- **Reader problem:** The forward pointer does not resolve. Ch2's only "loop" is a rumination pathology — the opposite valence — and the repeatable process ch2 actually delivers is "an opening game, five moves you can run" (`ch2:486`). Jordan arrives in ch2 looking for the named engine that runs all six faces and finds a five-move sequence under a different name and a "loop" that means her 2am spiral.
- **Category:** continuity
- **Disposition:** fix locally
- **What would disprove this:** ch2 naming its five moves a loop somewhere I did not search, or the intended referent being ch3's WAVE-Spiral rather than ch2 — in which case the chapter number is also wrong.

### [C4] "You will meet by name in Chapter 3" — Chapter 3 uses a different name

- **Reader problem:** The substance arrives correctly — ch3:431 and ch3:445 explicitly hand back Chapter 1's fuel economy — but the promise was *by name*, and the name changes to "completed state" / "the five renewable tokens." `satisfied state` returns zero hits in ch3. A reader who did what she was told and held the term cannot match it on arrival. Low severity, exact fix.
- **Category:** continuity
- **Disposition:** fix locally
- **What would disprove this:** "Satisfied state" appearing in ch3 in a form my search missed.

### [C7] The app is referenced six times, never named, and its one pointer is a placeholder

- **Evidence:** ch1:83 — "(The app runs a short, unflattering diagnostic that tells you which of these are yours...)" · ch1:203 — "(The app's Myths Read sorts your real top few, unflattering and exact, and turns each one into a first move.)"

### [C8] Gorski's mechanism is stated as settled causation — VERIFY
- **Location:** ch1:32
- **Evidence:** "Paul Gorski's work on activist burnout adds the mechanism that makes ours specific: when your activism fuses with your identity, you have no way to regulate the cost." And: "The selflessness the movement culture rewards is the same mechanism that empties you out."
- **Reader problem:** The Maslach sentence in the same paragraph is a fair statement of the three-dimension model (exhaustion, depersonalization, reduced efficacy) and I flag nothing there. The Gorski sentences go further: they assert a causal mechanism ("you have no way to regulate the cost", "the same mechanism") in the flat indicative, with no page, study, or title given. Jordan has read *The Body Keeps the Score*; she is a reader who notices when a named researcher is used to underwrite a claim stronger than the citation on offer. This needs checking against what Gorski actually published before it stands in this form.
- **Category:** claim-verify
- **Disposition:** structural decision
- **What would disprove this:** A Gorski publication stating the identity-fusion mechanism in these terms, cited in Appendix G or the copyright page.

### [C9] Three named sources carry ch1's argument and none appears on the permissions page — VERIFY
- **Location:** ch1:32 (Maslach, Gorski), ch1:103 and ch1:135 (Chou); checked against `front_matter/copyright.md`
- **Evidence:** ch1:103 — "(Yu-kai Chou spent a decade mapping exactly this — why some fuel keeps a player in the game and some burns them out of it.)"
- **Reader problem:** The copyright page's "Sources and permissions" section credits Wilber, Graves and Beck, Merzel, Elliott, Robin Rice, Meadows, and wu xing. Maslach, Gorski, and Chou are absent, though Chou's distinction between fuel that sustains and fuel that hollows is the load-bearing outside authority under the entire Token System and is reused in `front_matter/authors_note.md:25`. The canon states the copyright page carries every source permission the book owes. This may be deliberate — the page says frameworks that belong to somebody else are "named in the text and mapped in full in Appendix G", which these are — so it is a verify, not an error. Elliott is correctly attributed at `ch1:141` as **Carolyn** Elliott; the "Bob Elliott" defect the canon warns about does not appear in this chapter.
- **Category:** claim-verify
- **Disposition:** structural decision
- **What would disprove this:** Appendix G crediting all three, with the copyright page reserved for permissioned material only.

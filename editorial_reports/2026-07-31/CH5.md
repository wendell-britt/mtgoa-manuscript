## A. Chapter structure

### [S1] Steward has no move, and Moves 3–4 invert the order the chapter argues for
- **Location:** ch5:316, ch5:380, ch5:485, ch5:505–519 (body)
- **Evidence:** `ch5:316` — "The sequence used to hide this stage inside Steward, and hiding it there is how a Regent spends thirty years tending something they knew in year four had stopped working. Reform is a separate act with a separate cost." `ch5:485` — "The Regent asks, in any situation: *what was handed to me here, what still serves, what needs reforming, and who am I handing this to when I'm done?*"
- **Reader problem:** The chapter spends its single most forceful paragraph insisting Steward and Reform are separate stages in that order — then Move 3 is Reform and Move 4 is Keep the Vows, so the five moves run Inherit → Honor → **Reform** → Steward-ish → Entrust. Worse, the Regent's summary question is stated twice (`ch5:380` and `ch5:485`) and drops Steward from the list both times, leaving four beats where the chapter taught five. Jordan skims theory and stops for the named moves; the moves are the layer she carries out, and they teach a different sequence than the stages she just read. The stage the chapter calls "the longest stage, and the least visible" (`ch5:314`) is the one with no move attached to it.
- **Category:** structure
- **Disposition:** structural decision
- **What would disprove this:** Move 4 "Keep the Vows" is elsewhere established as the book's name for the Steward stage, and the four-item summary question at `ch5:380`/`485` is a deliberate compression stated as such.

- **Evidence:** `ch5:442` — "The chapter's myth lives here… The myth is that allyship is sacrificing yourself." Jordan reads recaps; this one hands her a slogan about belonging and nothing about sacrifice, which is the fear she came in with.
- **Category:** structure

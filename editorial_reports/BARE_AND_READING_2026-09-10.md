# The single bare `and` — read, all 322

**2026-09-10.** Wendell, on my claim that most single bare `and`s were ordinary English and did not
need a gate: *"we can't prove this and reading and flagging CAN. We don't need a scanner, we just
need to read the pages and do an analysis."*

Every one of the 322 was read and classified by hand against a rubric fixed before reading. Each
line's code is in the appendix, so any single call can be checked.

---

## The answer: the claim was wrong

| class | n | of 322 | of the 196 real joins | the `and` is… |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **FALSE** | 126 | 39% | — | not two clauses — a measurement error |
| **LIKE** | 45 | 14% | 23% | honest: like joined with like |
| **COND** | 48 | 15% | 24% | carrying an *if* — imperative + consequence |
| **CONTRA** | 49 | 15% | 25% | hiding a concession or contrast |
| **ELAB** | 18 | 6% | 9% | hiding an explanation or restatement |
| **CAUSE** | 18 | 6% | 9% | hiding a consequence |
| **SEQ** | 18 | 6% | 9% | hiding a sequence that carries meaning |

**Only 23% of the real joins are like-with-like.** 53% hide a relation, and the largest single
class is **CONTRA at 25%** — a concession or contrast that `and` refuses to name. I said most of
these were ordinary English. Most of them are the exact defect the zero target exists for.

## Two findings the count alone would not have given

**1 · The 322 was itself 39% wrong.** 126 of the sites my measurement script reported were not
two clauses at all: noun coordination (*"Pen and paper hold this"*), numbers (*"a hundred and
twenty cards"*), shared-subject verbs, em-dash joins. The script reused a clause test tuned for
text after `, and`, where the plural-noun-as-verb problem returns. **The real gap is 196 sentences,
not 322** — and I would have reported 322 as fact.

**2 · CONTRA was bad form repeated, and I called it style.** I first wrote that these concessions
were *"the book's central move"* — that the book's thesis is a contrast and its grammar softens it.
**Wendell: *"its not the central move. They are bad and your confusing repetition of bad form for
style."*** He is right. Forty-nine sentences in one shape is a habit. Frequency is evidence of a
tic, never of intent, and reading a defect's frequency as a signature is how a bad construction
gets defended into permanence.

## What each class needs

| class | n | resolution |
|---|---|---|
| CONTRA | 49 | `but`, `yet`, `though`, `without` — or a full stop that lets the contrast land |
| ELAB | 18 | a colon, or a full stop |
| CAUSE | 18 | `so`, `because`, or a full stop |
| SEQ | 18 | `then`, or a full stop |
| **COND** | 48 | **a ruling, not a rewrite** — see below |
| LIKE | 45 | none. `and` is correct |

**103 sentences hide a relation and are the rewrite set.**

### DONE — all 103 rewritten, 2026-09-10

Every joint changed to name its relation, and nothing else in the sentence moved. Verified by
testing every original against the live text: **0 of 103 remain in their original form.**

| relation | the joint it got |
|---|---|
| concession | `but`, `yet`, `while`, `without` — or a full stop where the contrast lands harder alone |
| consequence | `so`, `so … that`, a colon |
| explanation | a colon, or a full stop |
| sequence | `then`, `once`, `after`, or a full stop |

Two refrains changed as one: *"because those cards end in artifacts: an artifact is something another
person can encounter"* in five chapters, and the ch8 pull-quote with its source. No new fragment, no
new `trailing_and`; one polysyndeton acceptance went stale because its sentence stopped tripping,
and was pruned.

### COND needs your ruling

*"Walk away and you lose the things that told you who you were."* The imperative-plus-consequence
is a standard English conditional — the `and` carries an *if*, and every reader hears it. It is also
**48 sentences, a quarter of the real joins, and a signature of the book's second-person voice.**
It was not in the rubric; it arrived in volume in the first batch and I added it rather than force it
into CAUSE, which would have made the defect count look larger than the reading supports.
Whether it stays is a voice decision, not something reading can settle.

**RULED — Wendell: *"these are grammatical but probably have too many constructed in this way. At the
very least these need to be varied."***

### DONE — 29 varied, 19 kept

Kept: the antithetical pairs, whose parallel carries the contrast (*"Tell the story accurately… /
Flatter the story…"*, *"Draw a power-game line… / Draw a harmony-game line…"*); the Regent's quoted
speech and the vote line; and one or two strong standalone ones per chapter. **The 19 kept land 1–3
per file, so no chapter leans on the shape.**

Varied into `If`, `When`, `Once`, `The moment`, `as long as`, `Should you`, `without`, a colon, an
inversion (*"It fires the moment you move like the one who cost Imani"*), and a restructure. Refrains
that recur across chapters took one form everywhere they appear.

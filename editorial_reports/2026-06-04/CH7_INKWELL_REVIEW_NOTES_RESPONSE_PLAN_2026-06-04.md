## Priority order

### 1. Put the thesis in one declarative paragraph near the start

Add a short paragraph near the opening that states:

Add one short definition each, with one concrete example each.

- **Shadow** = the hidden move or protection
- **Distortion** = the gifted thing being used sideways
- **Cost** = what it takes to keep the distortion running

The point is not more theory. The point is less conflation.

### 3. Add signposting that links the Gates to the thesis

Make the gate sequence easier to navigate by briefly explaining:

- which Gates most often block public allyship

This should help the reader avoid getting lost in the depth of the chapter.

### 4. Add one short limit-case paragraph

This should be short and sober, not defensive.

### 5. Add one comparative sentence for the traditions you invoke

Clarify how the chapter’s language relates to:

This is for readers who know one framework but not the others.

### 6. Add 1–2 concrete misuse examples

Show what the chapter is **not** asking people to do:

- over-introspection that becomes avoidance

This keeps the chapter ethically honest.

### 7. Add one institutional example

Add a short org-level or coalition-level vignette showing how the gate work scales beyond an individual.

This helps leaders and systems people see the chapter as operational, not just personal.

### 8. Normalize the spiritual vocabulary in one line

The purpose is accessibility, not dilution.

### 9. Add a quick-reference box or cheat sheet

- the five moves

This gives the reader something they can return to during practice.

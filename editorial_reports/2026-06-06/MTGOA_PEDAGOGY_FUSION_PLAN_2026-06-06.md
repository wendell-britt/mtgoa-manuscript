# MTGOA Pedagogy Fusion Plan

## What is emerging

The best editorial synthesis so far is not "copy ILP" or "copy 10K HP."

It is a fusion of both:

- from **10K HP**, take the chapter rhythm of clear prompts, worked examples, highlights, and a visible next step
- from **ILP**, take the modular practice logic: one chapter, one primary move, one practice layer, one way to deepen

MTGOA should keep its layered nature, but each chapter should feel lighter and more direct.

## Core rule

**One chapter should focus on one idea and one tool.**

That means:

- one main allyship insight
- one main practice or tool that supports that insight
- multiple real-life tests or examples of how to use it
- a short summary that tells the reader what to remember and what to do next

## Why this works

This keeps the book:

- **layered**, because the idea can still have depth and multiple applications
- **readable**, because the reader is not asked to metabolize three theories at once
- **sellable**, because each chapter feels like a complete experience
- **coachable**, because the chapter naturally points toward follow-on work

## What this looks like in practice

### Chapter shape

1. **Promise**
   - What one problem does this chapter solve?

2. **Diagnosis**
   - What is the mistake or distortion that keeps that problem alive?

3. **Tool**
   - What is the one practice, lens, or move the reader can use?

4. **Examples**
   - Show the tool in multiple real situations.
   - This is where the chapter can still feel layered without becoming overloaded.

5. **Practice**
   - One concrete exercise or prompt.

6. **Summary / Handoff**
   - What should the reader remember, and what is the next move?

## What this means for MTGOA specifically

MTGOA does not need to flatten into a simplistic workbook.

It can still have:

- mythic language
- personal story
- emotional depth
- developmental layering
- gamification

But those things should support the chapter's one idea and one tool rather than competing with them.

## Chapter 7 example

Chapter 7 should not try to teach:

- the full forest journey
- the full Sage framework
- the full developmental arc
- the full counterargument set
- and the full application logic

Instead, it should become:

- **one idea:** Sage altitude solves a specific allyship problem
- **one tool:** the see → switch → serve → release → return loop

The chapter can still include multiple examples of how that tool shows up in real allyship situations, but the reader should always know what the chapter is *for*.

## The editorial standard from here

If a section does not help teach the chapter's one idea or one tool, it is probably excess.

If a section teaches the idea again in a different language, it may be a candidate for a shorter example or sidebar.

If a section introduces a second major idea, it likely belongs in another chapter, appendix, or follow-on product.

## Working principle

**Layered book, single-purpose chapters.**

That is the compromise that preserves MTGOA's depth without overwhelming the reader.

# MTGOA Allyship, Well-Being, and Five Journeys Map

## Purpose

The book now needs to solve a specific problem clearly:

**Who do I ally with, what counts as allyship, and how do the chapters guide readers through a coherent transformation without overloading them?**

This memo locks in the revised allyship definition and maps the five reader journeys onto the book.

## Working allyship definition

**Allyship is the art of increasing another person's felt sense of well-being, agency, and safety in the form that matters here, in a sustainable way, with their consent, through relationship.**

### Clarifications

- You can support a movement.
- You can only ally with people.
- Allyship is not an identity claim.
- Allyship is not a persona you perform.
- Allyship requires consent and relationship.
- If someone says no, their no is the signal that your next work may be inner work.
- Charity is possible without consent; allyship is not.

## The ally persona trap

The false version of allyship should be named plainly:

**ally persona**

That phrase can cover:

- the urge to look good
- the urge to avoid mistakes
- the urge to be seen as a good ally without being in relationship
- the urge to claim a political identity instead of doing the relational work

The book should treat ally persona as a distortion, not a destination.

## The five reader journeys

### 1. Learning why allyship is wack

What the reader is learning:

- why current allyship fails
- why allyship gets murky
- why guilt, identity, and performance keep getting in the way

What the book needs to do:

- wake the reader up
- show the cost of the old game
- give a plain answer to why the old model fails

### 2. Learning the game frame and how it helps

What the reader is learning:

- why games help with effectiveness
- how the game frame creates healthy distance
- why allyship is easier to sustain when the game is named clearly

What the book needs to do:

- make the game frame feel practical
- show that the game is not cute or abstract
- connect the frame to real allyship decisions

### 3. Building and applying the Effective Allyship Formula

What the reader is learning:

- identify your superpower
- identify who needs that superpower
- enroll allies
- show up consistently

What the book needs to do:

- make the formula visible across the chapters
- show how each Face defines well-being differently
- show how the formula changes by altitude without changing its core logic

### 4. Learning the story of how the book came to be

What the reader is learning:

- why the book exists
- how the author failed and learned
- what this book costs and why it matters

What the book needs to do:

- use personal story sparingly but well
- make the story earn trust
- connect the story to the reader's own allyship situation

### 5. Learning how to be allies to the people in your life

What the reader is learning:

- how to ally with the actual people they know
- how consent works in real relationships
- how to move from abstract identity to specific relational practice

What the book needs to do:

- move from general to specific
- help the reader name their real relationships
- give them a way to test whether allyship is actually happening

## The chapter coordination rule

Each chapter should serve a milestone in one of the five journeys, but no chapter should try to carry all five journeys equally.

Recommended pattern:

- **Ch0**: Journey 1 + 2 + 4
- **Ch1**: Journey 1 + 5
- **Ch2**: Journey 3
- **Ch3**: Journey 3 + 5
- **Ch4**: Journey 3 + 5
- **Ch5**: Journey 3 + 5
- **Ch6**: Journey 5
- **Ch7**: Journey 2 + 3 + 5
- **Ch8**: Journey 3 + 5

## The most important simplification rule

When a chapter starts to feel too large, ask:

1. Which reader journey is primary here?
2. Which definition of well-being applies here?
3. Which step of the Effective Allyship Formula is this chapter advancing?
4. What one tool is the reader actually supposed to take with them?

If the chapter cannot answer those questions, it is probably doing too much.

## Editorial implication

The book no longer needs to prove that allyship is a political identity category.

It needs to prove:

- allyship is relational
- allyship is consent-based
- allyship is trust-earned
- allyship changes with altitude because well-being changes with altitude

That is the conceptual spine the rest of the manuscript should now support.

# MTGOA Book Structure and Funnel Audit

## What this audit is for

First-round feedback says the book is powerful but too broad. The editorial question is no longer "How do we add more?" It is:

- what is the clearest promise this book can make
- how do we structure chapters so readers feel progress quickly
- how do we leave room for follow-on coaching, deeper content, or a second book

This audit compares the current manuscript against the structural models already in the Library and against the copy strategy that tells us how to sell simplified transformation to smart people.

## What the current manuscript is already good at

The first-round notes make the strongest material very clear:

1. **The Effective Allyship Formula**
   - This is the cleanest promise in the manuscript.
   - It is the most immediately sellable idea.
   - It naturally bridges into coaching and follow-on content.

2. **Distortion and exile**
   - Readers resonate with the idea that a person can become useful in the wrong way or get pushed out of usefulness.
   - This is a strong diagnostic hook.

3. **Personal stories**
   - These are the most human and memorable parts.
   - They create trust faster than abstract theory.

4. **The game frame**
   - The gamification layer gives the book a practical spine.
   - It helps readers stay engaged long enough to change.

## Where the book is overbuilt

The biggest overbuild is not that the ideas are wrong. It is that the chapters often ask readers to hold too many frameworks at once:

- forest journey
- altitude / developmental language
- shadow / distortion / exile
- emotional alchemy
- gates
- the game frame
- tokens / tickets
- allyship terminology

That is textbook behavior. It is not yet the lean, outcome-driven shape of a coaching lead magnet.

The likely fix is not to remove everything. It is to **reduce the amount of context a reader must metabolize in one sitting**.

## What the book should promise now

The book's revised promise should be something like:

**This book helps you see why allyship keeps failing, understand the game underneath the term, and keep playing long enough to become more effective.**

That promise is:

- narrow enough to be memorable
- broad enough to support the whole project
- strong enough to lead to coaching, workshops, and deeper content

## How the chapters should behave if the book is going to sell follow-on work

Each chapter needs to do three things:

1. **Give a quick win**
   - a diagnostic insight
   - a sentence the reader can use
   - a practice they can run immediately

2. **Expose a real problem**
   - why current allyship fails
   - what the reader has been mis-seeing
   - what the old game costs

3. **Leave the reader wanting more**
   - not because the chapter withheld the answer
   - but because it clarified the terrain enough that the next step feels necessary

That is the funnel logic.

## What this means specifically for Chapter 7

Chapter 7 is currently the biggest structural candidate for compression.

In the simplified book, Chapter 7 should not try to remain the full forest journey, the full Sage synthesis, and the full developmental altitudes chapter all at once.

It should become the **proof chapter**:

- here's what Sage altitude reveals
- here's how it solves specific allyship problems
- here's the minimal set of moves that matter
- here's how to use those moves in real allyship situations

The question each section should answer is:

**How does seeing from this altitude solve my specific problems with allyship?**

That is much more sellable than a large theoretical journey.

## Structural recommendation from the audit

The book should now be edited as a **concise transformation path** rather than a comprehensive textbook.

That means:

- keep the Effective Allyship Formula front and center
- keep the distortion / exile / personal story material
- compress the forest journey
- define allyship plainly
- make each chapter deliver one useful result
- leave room for coaching or a follow-on volume to carry the deeper material

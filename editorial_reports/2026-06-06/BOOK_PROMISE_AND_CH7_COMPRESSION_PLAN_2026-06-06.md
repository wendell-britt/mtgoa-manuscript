# MTGOA Book Promise and Chapter 7 Compression Plan

## Why this exists

First-round editorial feedback is consistent:

- the manuscript is powerful
- the manuscript is too broad
- readers are losing the thread because each chapter asks them to hold too much context at once
- the most valuable material is already visible, but it needs to be tighter and more direct

The working conclusion is not that the book is wrong. It is that the book is trying to do textbook work when the sellable product needs to be a concise, high-trust invitation into a deeper body of work.

## Revised book promise

**Mastering the Game of Allyship helps readers understand why current allyship keeps failing, how to see the game underneath the term, and how to keep playing long enough to make real change.**

This promise is intentionally narrower than the full manuscript.

It does not promise:

- exhaustive theory
- a complete taxonomy of every possible allyship problem
- a full forest metaphor journey in one sitting
- a comprehensive textbook on every developmental altitude

It does promise:

- a usable frame for why allyship is murky
- a way to recognize distortion and exile
- a practical allyship formula readers can return to
- enough clarity to make readers want more, not less

## What the book should keep

The first-round feedback makes the core keepers pretty clear:

1. **The Effective Allyship Formula**
   - This is the clearest reader-facing tool.
   - It should remain the book’s practical anchor.

2. **Distortion and exile**
   - Readers resonated with the explanation of how people get pushed out of usefulness or clarity.
   - This should stay, but it should be made more direct and less sprawling.

3. **Personal stories**
   - The lived examples are helping the work land.
   - They should remain as the emotional entry points.

4. **A clear allyship definition**
   - The word “allyship” is too murky to assume shared understanding.
   - The book needs to name what it means in plain language.

## What should shrink

The biggest compression target is the long altitude / forest / Sage development path.

The forest material is valuable, but in its current form it is doing too much at once:

- it is a metaphor
- a developmental map
- a spiritual frame
- a systems theory bridge
- and a chapter-length journey

That is why readers are getting overloaded.

The compression rule is:

- keep the insight
- reduce the travel time
- keep the application to allyship visible at every step

## Chapter 7 should become the proof chapter

Chapter 7 is the best candidate for compression because it currently contains the most conceptual load.

In the simplified version, Chapter 7 should answer one question:

**How does seeing from Sage altitude solve the specific problems that make allyship fail?**

That means Chapter 7 should be reduced to key milestones rather than full journey coverage.

### Suggested Chapter 7 spine

1. **Burnout / exile story**
   - One personal story that shows how being right can still fail in practice.
   - Keep it concrete.
   - Keep it brief.

2. **The central thesis**
   - Sage is not “seeing the furthest.”
   - Sage is seeing the whole board without turning that view into a hiding place.
   - This should be stated once, clearly, near the start.

3. **What Sage fixes in allyship**
   - misreading the room
   - confusing perspective with action
   - rescuing when service is needed
   - making theory feel like progress
   - turning insight into superiority

4. **The minimal Sage moves**
   - see
   - switch
   - serve
   - release
   - return

5. **Direct allyship application**
   - how Sage helps when allyship is too vague
   - how Sage helps when the reader is over-identifying with the role
   - how Sage helps when the room needs a line, not another analysis

6. **A short handoff to the next move**
   - the reader should leave with a clearer relationship to allyship, not a fuller theory of Sage.

## Allyship needs a cleaner definition

The book should stop assuming readers already mean the same thing by allyship.

Working definition direction:

**Allyship is the practice of using your power, perspective, and relationships to reduce harm, increase agency, and help people move through a problem you can actually help solve.**

That definition should be:

- plain
- specific
- usable
- broad enough to cover multiple justice contexts
- narrow enough to exclude performative self-congratulation

## Revised editorial principle

The book’s job is no longer to be the fullest possible textbook.

Its job is to:

- wake readers up to the failures of current allyship
- give them a simple, memorable frame they can use
- show them enough depth to want more
- make the deeper material feel like an invitation, not a burden

# R8 stress test — Chapter 4

## Verdict

**Break rate: 5 of 26 village/institutional sites BREAK outright (≈19%). A further 7 of 26
(≈27%) only HOLD if a touch is applied that GAP's own worked examples never priced in —
mostly subject-verb agreement (has→have, does→do, hears→hear) forced by the plural, which
GAP's two worked examples hid by choosing weak past-tense verbs (learned, became) where
singular and plural are spelled the same. Net: 14 of 26 (54%) are clean single-word
number changes; 12 of 26 (46%) need either a real rewrite or don't hold at all.**

R8 is not safe to adopt as a mechanical, apply-everywhere rule. It is safe to adopt as a
**per-site proposal that a human still has to check**, because the failure sites are not
random — they cluster in exactly the two shapes GAP itself predicted (possession/self-
reference, and one construction GAP never tested at all) plus two more shapes GAP's
document does not mention: (1) sentences that were never flagged as violations become
nonsense once "the village" turns into "the villagers who contain villagers," and (2)
several of the fable's collective nouns — "the rituals," "the stories," "the workshops" —
are not populations at all, so R8 has no number to change and silently fails to reach
them. Most damaging: R8's own flagship worked example lives in the **exact site
`VOICE_ANCHOR.md` §3 quotes**, and R8 does not touch the sentence that carries the actual
joke — see below.

## The anchor passage — ch4:115

Quoted as `VOICE_ANCHOR.md` presents it:

> It got very good at *assertive communication.* At *using "I" statements.* At framing a
> boundary as a preference rather than a line. **(The workshops teach the scripts. The
> scripts do not work the way the workshops promise, which anyone who has run one in a
> live moment already knows.)** … The village learned to make its nos sound like yeses
> because yeses cost less.

Two Grade-6/Grade-0 constructions live in this passage, and they are not the same kind of
construction.

**1. "The workshops teach the scripts. …the workshops promise…"**
Subject: *the workshops* — already plural, and not a population. A workshop is a
recurring event/format, not a group of persons; there is no singular collective noun here
to pluralize into people. **R8 has no number to change.** This is the sentence
`LEDGER_CH4.md` flags as the actual site of the anchor's protected humor ("cited by name
in `specs/VOICE_ANCHOR.md`… as a model of the book's humor register"), and GAP's own
document never once applies R8 to it — GAP's Gap 2 proposes a completely different
mechanism for this shape (named part / author's chair / persons-plural / mechanical verb),
not R8. **R8 leaves the actual joke completely untouched — not because it protects the
joke, but because it cannot reach it.**

**2. "The village learned to make its nos sound like yeses because yeses cost less."**
This is GAP's own first worked example, verbatim.
**R8:** "The villager**s** learned to make **their** nos sound like yeses because yeses
cost less."

- **Turn:** *"yeses cost less"* → *"yeses cost less."* Unchanged, word for word. Survives completely.
- **Rhythm:** 18 syllables → 19 syllables (village=2, villagers=3; its=1, their=1). The
  added syllable lands eleven syllables before the turn and is inaudible by the time the
  sentence reaches "yeses cost less." This exact clause is also the closing beat this
  anchor names in `VOICE_ANCHOR.md`'s "What the set shows, read together" section — one of
  the five protected closing turns in the whole file. It survives R8 intact.
- **Humor:** n/a for this clause specifically — it's an aphorism, not the Jerk-archetype
  joke. The Jerk archetype's mockery lives in construction #1, which R8 never reaches.

**Verdict on the anchor passage: does it still deserve to be a voice anchor after R8?**
Where R8 applies, it does no damage — the village clause holds cleanly and the closing
turn is untouched. But R8 repairs only one of the passage's two agency violations, and
the one it skips is the one `VOICE_ANCHOR.md` actually cites the passage *for*. Applying
R8 to this passage and calling the passage "fixed" would be wrong: half the passage's
Grade-6/Grade-0 material — the half carrying the humor — is exactly as unlicensed after
R8 as before it. **R8 is necessary-but-not-sufficient here.** It cannot be the sole
remediation strategy for anchor 3; the workshops construction needs a second, different
op (per GAP's own Gap-2 proposal), and until that op is chosen and applied, this passage
does not clear C1 even under full R8 adoption. Read aloud, a listener would not notice the
village-clause edit at all. They would notice, on reflection, that the funnier sentence in
the passage was never edited.

## Where R8 breaks

**Shape 1 — possession/self-reference (villagers acting on/containing themselves).**
- Line 127: *"it taught its people that the clean no no longer belonged to them"* → *"the
  villagers taught their people"* is nonsense; the population cannot teach a subset of
  itself. **Worst break in the chapter** — it also carries the chapter's thesis sentence.
- Line 68: *"The village was full of people who could feel…"* → *"the villagers were full
  of people…"* is nonsense for the same reason, and this sentence was never flagged as a
  defect before R8 created one. **R8 can manufacture new violations in sentences that were
  previously clean**, not only fail to fix the sentences it was aimed at.
Both need a real rewrite or R6 (transmission), not a number change.

**Shape 2 — scope-miss: the collective noun is not a population, so R8 has no number to
change.**
- Line 84: *"the rituals started referencing…"* and *"the stories changed…"*
- Line 115: *"the workshops teach the scripts… the workshops promise…"* — the passage
  `VOICE_ANCHOR.md` §3 exists to protect, and the one construction in it R8 cannot reach.
These three sites are not damaged by R8 — R8 simply never arrives. If R8 is adopted as the
default op for "collective subjects in the fables," these sites will silently fall through
unless someone remembers to route them elsewhere (R1, R4, or Gap 2's four licensed
carriers).

**A third, softer shape — costs GAP's worked examples hid.**
GAP's two worked examples (lines 115 and 121) both use past-tense weak verbs (*learned,
became*) where singular and plural share a spelling, making "just change the suffix" look
free. In the actual chapter, present-tense and copula verbs (*hears, has, does, is*) force
real subject-verb agreement changes throughout — 7 of 26 sites needed this. It's a minor
cost individually, but it means R8 is never truly a one-word touch across the fable as a
whole; it's a coreference-and-agreement pass that has to track every pronoun and verb
downstream of each "the village" for as long as that paragraph's thread runs. Because "the
village" is a continuous character across ~110 lines (L66–175), in practice this is not
26 independent one-word edits — it is one long consistency pass across the whole fable,
since flipping "the village" to "the villagers" in one sentence and leaving it singular
two paragraphs later would read as an inconsistent narrator. That scope is bigger than
"a suffix pass," even though no individual edit is hard.

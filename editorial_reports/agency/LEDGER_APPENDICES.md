**The surface's characteristic failure, in one paragraph.** Apparatus prose fails differently from chapter prose, and the difference is exactly what you'd expect from reference material: chapters narrate events and default to hiding the person who acted; appendices define terms and default to hiding the person who decided. The single largest pattern here isn't a chapter-style "culture punishes" sentence (though Appendix A has four clean instances of that, all in its Gate-Domain Affinity Table and developmental-arc Shadow/Gift lines) — it's **the book/chapter/appendix as author-stand-in**, taking on verbs (teaches, asks, names, quotes, departs, pushes, proposes, routes) that Wendell actually performed while writing. Twenty-two sites carry this pattern across the thirteen files, concentrated hardest in the glossary and the bibliography (Appendix G, "On the Shoulders Of," alone accounts for a third of them) — apparatus is where a writer talks *about* the book most, so it's where "the book does X" has the most surface area to appear on. The second-largest pattern is glossary-specific: Appendix C (Key Terms) attributes intention and generic causal action to `the village` in two of its own term definitions, which is the exact "a definition wants a predicate" failure the task brief warned about — a term needs a verb to close its sentence, and `the village`/`the structure` sits right there, syntactically convenient, mechanism absent.

**Density verdict: Appendix A's 48.34/10k is real, not an artifact; Appendix D's 58.14/10k is mostly artifact.** Appendix A's high recall-net score reflects genuine density — its Gate-Domain Affinity Table and four developmental-arc sections are dense with `the structure`/`organizations` as grammatical subjects precisely because the appendix's subject matter *is* domains, structures, and institutions, and its Shadow/Gift format runs the same six-Face pattern four times over, multiplying any per-Face defect by four. Close reading confirmed four clean Tier-3 Grade-6 social-causal violations plus two embodied-daemon constraint breaks — a real yield, denser than any comparably-sized chapter section. Appendix D's number is almost entirely a false positive: three of its four flagged rows are the recall net misattributing "let" (subject `you`, imperative) to an adjacent Grade-3 noun (`earth`), and its remaining real defect (`the framework asks`) is a single Tier-2 site in 688 words. Appendix D's high density score is an artifact of its short length (four hits in a 688-word document inflates the per-10k rate) combined with the tool's subject-attribution imprecision on imperative sentences — exactly the "high-recall, low-precision" behavior the net is documented to have. The three named practices (Happy Apples, Grounding, The Rose) that make up Appendix D's actual body are close to a model citizen: they use mechanical verbs on their own named-technique subjects almost exactly the way the Token/Ticket mechanism does, and the one perception verb they carry (The Rose "reveals") clears under C1 because the mechanism — four fully-stated steps — is on the page immediately below it.

**The chapter's characteristic failure, in one paragraph:** This chapter's defect has one address: "the village." Section 1 and Section 2 (L66–129), and to a lesser extent Section 3 (L134–175), run an extended fable in which a whole community is given one continuous mind — it needs, chooses, confuses, forgets, learns, feels, becomes afraid, decides, and, at the single worst point in the chapter (L127), *teaches its people that the clean no no longer belonged to them* — using the registry's own banned verb, on the registry's own banned grade, in the sentence that carries the chapter's thesis. This is not incidental color; it is the load-bearing narrative device of the whole opening argument, which is exactly why it matters: a book that exists to hand the reader's agency back to them opens its confrontation chapter by handing a village's collective agency to the village. Every other part of the chapter — the Skeptic material, the EA channel table, the five-mode taxonomy, the testimony passages — keeps its verbs on the right subjects almost without exception. The fable is the one place the grammar and the argument pull against each other, and it is dense enough (17 distinct Grade 6 mind-verb instances in roughly 110 lines) that this is very likely the placement evidence W-4 is asking for.

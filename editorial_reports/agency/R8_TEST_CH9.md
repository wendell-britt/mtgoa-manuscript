# R8 stress test — Chapter 9

## Verdict

**Break rate: 1 of 23 village sites BREAKS outright (~4%). 3 of 23 (~13%) HOLD only with
a one-word touch. 2 of 23 (~9%) are flagged — not ruled — as keep-candidates because they
are the book's own closing beats. 17 of 23 (~74%) hold clean.**

Counted as raw verb-instances rather than grouped sites, this chapter carries **27 distinct
village-subject-plus-verb constructions**, not the ~29 the brief estimated and not the "6
logged + ~23 unlogged" the prior ledger guessed at. My count is lower than the prior
ledger's own arithmetic (6+23=29) because that arithmetic was always an estimate — its
REGISTRY-GAP paragraph explicitly enumerates only 14 of its claimed "~23," padding the
rest with "several more object-position and locative uses," which are not agentive and
should not be in the denominator at all. Against that: my closer pass found **five verb
instances the prior ledger's own REGISTRY-GAP note never listed anywhere** — line 25's "the
village *had made no mistake*," line 25's "the village *extended*," line 31's "the village
*kept getting wrong*," line 51's second clause "the village *needs played*" (its first
clause, "forgot," was logged; this one, four words later in the same line, was not), and
the section heading at line 672, "**The Village Is Already Playing**." The auditor's own
gap-list had a gap. See "The full village census" for the complete accounting.

**R8 is safe to adopt as the default op for this chapter's village sites**, with three
conditions: (1) it cannot touch "village" used in its place/territory sense (one real
break, below), (2) sites with more than one village-reference inside a single sentence
need every reference pluralized together, not just the grammatical subject, or the
sentence becomes internally inconsistent, and (3) the chapter's last two village
constructions — the section heading two screens from the end and the book's literal final
sentence — trade on "the village" as a singular mythic counterpart to "the Player," and a
plural there is not nonsense, it is a different, lesser ending. That is Wendell's call, not
mine to make, but I am not going to pretend R8 is free there.

## The closing-chapter cost

**Direct answer: no, pluralizing the founding myth (Sections 1–2, "The Exile" and "The
Distortion," lines 19–61) does not weaken it as myth — with one narrow, real exception.**

Twelve of the fable's village-subject constructions are tested individually below (lines
25, 29, 31, 33, 43, 49, 51). Eleven of the twelve hold as plain number-changes. The
sentences doing the myth's actual work — *the village forgot that the Faces were a map,
not a menu* being the clearest case — are built on ordinary human capacities: hearing,
forgetting, teaching, needing, being glad. A population can do every one of those things
distributed across its members with no coordination required, which is exactly the claim
GAP_VOICE_ANCHOR.md makes about the village in the fables generally, and it holds up under
a hard, line-by-line read of this chapter's specific fable. Read side by side:

> The village forgot that the Faces were a map, not a menu.
> The villagers forgot that the Faces were a map, not a menu.

The turn — *a map, not a menu* — is untouched. If anything, the myth is **more honest**
after the change, because this chapter's entire argument is that the reader holds the
joystick and a collective mind never does; a founding myth that quietly reifies the thing
the chapter spends 650 lines arguing against is a worse myth, not a better one, regardless
of how it sounds.

**The one real exception is not a myth problem, it is a category problem, and it is worth
naming precisely because GAP_VOICE_ANCHOR.md did not anticipate it.** Line 25 uses
"village" twice in one sentence in two different senses — once as a population ("the
village *could teach* them," fine to pluralize) and once as a place ("going further out
than the village *extended*," a claim about a settlement's geographic or social reach, not
about what its residents did). "The villagers extended" is not ungrammatical, it is a
different, wrong claim (it says the *people* stretched out, not that the *community's
reach* stretched out). GAP's document names one failure shape (possession — the village
acting on itself) and this chapter has **zero** instances of that shape. It has a shape GAP
never named: **village-as-territory**, which fails for the opposite reason — not because
the plural collides with itself, but because the plural is the wrong grammatical class of
noun entirely for what the sentence is claiming.

**Verdict on the fable as a whole: it does not need to be flagged as one keep-candidate.**
Eleven of twelve fable sites take R8 cleanly or with a one-word touch. Flagging the whole
fable to protect the twelfth would cost eleven working fixes to save one, and the
twelfth doesn't need protecting — it needs a different op (R6/compression on the noun
phrase, not R8 on the verb). The place where a singular collective genuinely is load-
bearing for myth-effect is not inside the fable at all — it is at the very end of the
chapter, addressed separately below.

## The thesis-contradiction sites (370, 384, 640) — what they need instead

None of these three take "the village" as a subject, so R8 cannot reach any of them —
confirmed by inspection, not assumption.

**Line 370 — "Grow up: ask what the failure is actually telling you."**
Sits inside the chapter's own five-beat restatement of the WAVE (Wake up / Open up / Clean
up / Grow up / Show up), addressed to the reader as a practice they run themselves. The
subject is "the failure," Grade 0, taking a perception verb ("telling") — needs **R1**,
restoring the reader as the one who does the perceiving. LEDGER_CH9.md's fix — "Grow up:
ask yourself what you can learn from the failure" — is a reflexive-imperative shape and it
works: the terse, colon-led cadence of the WAVE list survives, and "yourself... learn"
keeps the second-person address the whole passage runs on.

**Line 384 — "Grow up: ask what this moment asks you to become."**
Same WAVE list, restated fourteen lines later for the reader's actual life (mother calls,
group chat, the news). Same shape, different subject ("this moment," Grade 0, speech-class
verb "asks"). Also needs **R1**, but it must NOT reuse line 370's reflexive-imperative
shape — using "ask yourself" twice, fourteen lines apart, in the same voice restating the
same five-word Grow-up beat, would itself become the drumbeat R1's audit already flagged
as the book-wide risk. LEDGER_CH9.md's fix — "Grow up: ask what you are willing to become
because of this" — correctly chooses a **different** R1 shape (modal-imperative, "willing
to become," rather than reflexive, "ask yourself"). That choice is not incidental; it is
the only reason two R1 fixes fourteen lines apart don't read as a tic.

**Line 640 — "One did most of the work on a campaign and watched the other take the
press... The other says it was offered the interview, stole nothing, and is now under
attack."**
Two unnamed organizations, personified, taking perception ("watched") and speech
("says") verbs, inside "The Last Rep" — the chapter's own diagnostic drill for training
readers to catch exactly this move in the wild. R8 cannot help: "organizations" is already
plural as a noun class, and pluralizing further does nothing, because the actual problem
is that each of the two orgs is personified individually, one at a time, not that either
one is a collective standing for a population the way "the village" is in the fable. This
needs **R6**, dissolving each organization into its unnamed people, exactly as
LEDGER_CH9.md proposes ("One org did most of the work and its people watched the credit go
to the other" / "Someone on the other side says they were offered the interview..."). R1
(naming specific individuals inside two unnamed coalition orgs) would be false precision
the scenario doesn't support — the drill is deliberately anonymized so the reader supplies
their own real situation. The irony is worth naming on its own terms: this sentence sits
inside the chapter's own training ground for catching reified-organization language, which
means the chapter's own drill currently exhibits the exact error it is teaching the reader
to spot.

## Where R8 breaks

Exactly one clean break in this chapter: **line 25c, "than the village extended"** →
"than the villagers extended" claims the *people* stretched out rather than the
*settlement's reach* extending, which is nonsense. This is not the possession/self-action
failure shape GAP_VOICE_ANCHOR.md names (this chapter has **zero** instances of that
shape — no "village taught its people," nothing where the plural collides with itself).
It is a shape GAP's document never tested: **village-as-territory**, where "village" names
a place, not a population, and R8 has no number to change because the referent isn't
countable persons in that sense at all. Needs R6/compression on the noun phrase (e.g. "the
village's reach"), not a suffix change on a verb.

Two further sites do not break grammatically but cost something real: the section heading
at line 672 and the book's final sentence at line 690, both flagged above as keep-
candidates rather than ruled, because both trade on "the village" as a singular mythic
counterpart to "the Player" at the two places in the whole manuscript built to carry that
pairing to its close.

One site — line 614 — needs a sentence-wide consistency pass, not because R8 fails there,
but because the chapter's habit of using "village" three times in one sentence (twice as
object, once as subject) means a mechanical, verb-only pass would leave a visibly
inconsistent sentence behind. Anyone applying R8 chapter-wide should grep each sentence for
repeated "village" references before editing, not just the flagged verb.

**The chapter's characteristic failure.** Ch7's didactic voice grants "the field" — and, in its opening parable, "the village" — exactly the cognitive and social interior the corollary forbids: the field *knows*, *decides*, *needs*, *trusts*, and is *free to answer*, in place of naming the people who actually know, decide, need, trust, and answer. The irony is exact: this is the one chapter whose explicit thesis is that vague, unnamed, systemic-sounding language is how a person avoids the cost of naming somebody specific ("a term is a statement... offered as information, once, to a field that is then free to answer" — the sentence defining the chapter's central concept is itself the clearest violation in the chapter), and the chapter's own prose keeps making the move it is teaching the reader to stop making.

## W-4 assessment — the confrontation beat

**Does ch7 already carry the material for the book's explicit case against reified systemic agency?** In embryo, yes, in two places — but neither is the beat as scoped, and the chapter's own prose currently works against rather than for the argument.

**What exists.**

1. **Section 3, the myth passage (L154–158).** This is the closest existing anchor:

   > **The myth: allyship means never causing harm.** It sounds like a floor. It functions as a ceiling. If your presence only counts as legitimate for as long as it has cost no one anything, then naming a term becomes a risk you cannot take, because a term might land badly, and something that lands badly is harm, and harm is disqualifying. So the terms stay unnamed. The staying stays weightless. The student stays (pleasant, reliable, and slowly becoming furniture) and calls the whole arrangement care.

   This has the exact shape W-4 needs — a virtue-sounding stance that functions as evasion, named and then dismantled — but the evasion vehicle here is *silence* ("the terms stay unnamed"), not *systemic language*. It is the same mechanism one level up: the corollary's proposed beat is the systemic-language species of this genus. The genus is on the page; the species is not.

2. **Section 6, Move 1: Name the Field (L609–625).** The move is structurally identical to what W-4 needs — surfacing the specific unnamed thing underneath a vaguer surface conversation — and the worked example (L617: a letting agent who has spent ten minutes discussing bedroom count while never saying "housing voucher" out loud) is a live demonstration of a person substituting a safer surface-level frame for the specific, costly, named one. It is close kin to the W-4 mechanism but is not staged as *systemic* language; the letting agent's evasion is silence about a category (voucher status), not an invocation of "the culture here."

3. **Section 5, the Victim's "I hear you" (L550–551, see Demonstration assets below).** Structurally the nearest thing in the chapter to the proposed beat's shape — a phrase that sounds like acknowledgment and evades the specific admission — but the vehicle is interpersonal-empathy language ("I hear you"), not systemic/structural language ("the culture here is toxic"). Same mechanism, different disguise.

**What is missing.** No passage in ch7 stages a character reaching specifically for institutional or systemic vocabulary — "the culture," "the system," "the dynamic here" — to avoid naming one person's one action. The chapter's three closest passages all use *silence* or *interpersonal softening* as the evasion vehicle, not *abstraction*. Writing the beat here would not be surfacing something latent; it would be new material built on an existing scaffold.

**Recommendation: anchor here, immediately after the Section 3 myth passage, but do not treat the material as already written.** Section 3 is the right site if the beat lands in this chapter at all — it already stages the "sounds like virtue, functions as evasion" turn the beat needs, and Move 1 already gives the reader the corrective tool (name what's underneath the surface level) before the shadow section (Section 5) shows the tool failing. Placing the beat between the myth passage and the Polarity Encounter would let it complete a turn the chapter has already started rather than open a new one. Set against that: the chapter's own didactic prose is, by volume, the single worst offender in this pass for the exact grammatical move the corollary condemns (the forty-times-repeated agentive "field"). A beat that explicitly indicts reified systemic agency, landing in a chapter whose own explanatory register performs that reification at this density, would need the "field" language addressed in the same pass or the chapter would be making the argument and undercutting it in the same breath.

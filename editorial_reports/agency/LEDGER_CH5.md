**The chapter's characteristic failure, in one paragraph:** This chapter reaches for unearned agency exactly where its own subject matter — office, authority, inheritance — makes the move tempting, and it does so in two distinct registers. In the analytic/teaching prose, institutional shorthand quietly takes over a person's intention: "new leadership *wants*" instead of naming the leaders, "organizations… used tradition to *protect* power" instead of naming who ran them, a tradition or an intake form "*trying* to do" something that only the person who built it could try to do, and "loyalty" itself personified as the thing that "sees" and "decides" rather than the loyal person. These are concrete, fixable, and are exactly the "role demands / leadership requires" pattern flagged as this chapter's native temptation. The second, larger register is the village-and-Faces fable that opens the chapter (Sections 1–2): "the village" feels, realizes, believes, loves, and takes sides, and "the Regent," "the Challenger," and "the Architect" arrive, argue, and leave as if they were persons, dozens of times over. That fable is this manuscript's evidence toward the open W-4 ruling — it is systemic/collective language standing in exactly where the confrontation beat says a name should go, at chapter scale rather than sentence scale.

## Cleared

Sites the recall net raised that are not defects:

- **`the school` (G6) + `required`, near line 44 (HANDBOOK, "Prior standing at the School of the Line is required").** Passive voice with no explicit agent — "is required," not "the school requires." The net's proximity match to "School of the Line" is a misparse, not a real subject-verb attribution.
- **`the practice` (G0) + `showing`, "The practice of showing up, again and again…" (Stage Sequence, Steward).** "Show up" is the phrasal verb meaning *to arrive*, not the perception-class lemma "show/reveal." No perception verb is actually present.
- **`the shame` (G7) + `says`, line 412, "The shame says: this wound was done to me, therefore I am broken."** Grade 7 is licensed for speech when routed through a Grade 2 part as an internalized voice; the quoted first-person interior monologue format ("X says: [belief]") is precisely that convention, matching how internal states are voiced throughout the book's IFS material. Mechanism is on the page.
- **`the work` (G0) + `sees`, line 644, "The work nobody sees."** Misparse — the real subject of "sees" is "nobody" (a generic person), and "the work" is its object. Nobody, not the work, does the not-seeing.

- **`the joystick` (G4) hit, line 550, "You take the joystick back…"** "You" (Grade 1) is the subject; the joystick is the object being taken back. No agentive verb is attached to the joystick itself.
- **`the machine` (G4) hit, line 39 (HANDBOOK, "Repair of the machine that produced the alteration").** "Produced" is causal-bare, low severity, and arguably licensed-adjacent for a Grade 4 subject; not worth a row.
- **`water`/`wood`/`joy` (G3) hits.** These are EA-signal table labels ("Water/Sadness," "Wood/Joy") in the channel-alignment table headers — not grammatical subjects of any verb.
- **`the structure` (G6) hits, various (L179, L558, L680, L682).** All mechanical/possessive uses ("hold the structure lightly," "the structure will not," "the structure lacks," "the structure does not") — low severity, consistent with dead-metaphor Tier 1, not logged individually.

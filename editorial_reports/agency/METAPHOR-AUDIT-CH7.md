## 2 · The structural problem: the Diplomat chapter has almost no body

**Body vocabulary across the manuscript**, per 10k chars:

| ch | | ch | | ch | |
|---|---:|---|---:|---|---:|
| ch3 Shaman | 7.1 | ch4 Challenger | 2.7 | **ch7 Diplomat** | **0.8** |
| ch2 Forest | 6.8 | ch8 Sage | 2.0 | ch9 Player | 0.6 |
| ch6 Architect | 4.4 | ch5 Regent | 1.7 | ch1 Arcade | 0.3 |

**Six instances in 78,000 characters. Second-lowest in the book, and the two below it are
frame chapters that are supposed to run on the game rather than the body.**

This is the same shape as the finding the ch1 audit made about water, and it is worth
stating the same way: **the chapter's signature felt experience has no somatic vocabulary
to land in.** The Diplomat's whole teaching is presence under charge — *staying when
staying is difficult*, *the four seconds after*, *sustained containment of a charged field
over the length of a hard conversation*. Every one of those is a body event. The chapter
describes them and almost never locates them.

Ch1's audit put it exactly: the reader *thinks about* grief and does not *feel* it. Here
the reader thinks about staying and never feels the cost of staying in a chest, a jaw, or
a held breath.

**And the chapter says so about itself.** `ch7:539` names allyship-run-on-a-ledger; `ch7`
names the Field-Holder's freeze, the collapse masquerading as equanimity. Those are somatic
states named in abstract nouns.

## 4 · The field, tested as a metaphor rather than as a grammar problem

Lakoff's question is not *is this word ambiguous* but *which source domain is the reader's
thinking being structured by.* Tested lexically:

| source domain | supporting vocabulary in ch7 | count |
|---|---|---:|
| **PHYSICS** — charge, current, resonance, polarity, conduct, signal | present and load-bearing | **41** |
| CULTIVATION — grow, plant, seed, soil, crop, harvest, root | present but incidental | 13 |
| **PITCH** — bounds, referee, sideline, offside, whistle | **absent** | **0** |
| CONTAINER — *in the field*, *inside it*, *enter the field* | present, and this is the leak | 14 |

**Three findings.**

**a. ch7's field is the physics one, decisively, and the chapter's own vocabulary proves
it.** 41 charge-words against 13 cultivation-words, and the cultivation words are mostly
`grow` in the ordinary sense. Nothing in ch7 tills anything.

**b. The pitch reading is not in ch7 at all — it is in ch3.** All six `onto the field`
sites in the manuscript sit in one ch3 passage, with bounds and a referee. Ch7 has **zero**
pitch vocabulary. **These are two different metaphors in two different chapters, not one
metaphor used loosely.**

**c. The container prepositions are the actual defect.** 14 sites where a medium is written
as a place you stand inside — including `ch7:359`'s own second clause, *"people move around
inside it"*, four clauses after the definition says the field is what sits **between** the
people **at** it.

**TABLE/FURNITURE = 4.** The chapter states the distinction that resolves all of this —
*"The table is the furniture. The field is what sits between the people at it"* — and then
never operates it again. **The distinction is canon, stated once, and unused.**

## 5 · What the chapter gets right

- **TRANSLATE at 95 is correct and well-placed.** The Diplomat is the only Face defined by
  conversion between channels — Fire→Water, Water→Wood, Fire→Earth — and the vocabulary
  carries it. This is the chapter's strongest metaphorical achievement.
- **CAMP at 33** gives the chapter a genuinely spatial metaphor that is *not* the field, and
  it is doing the territorial work cleanly. Camps have sides; fields do not.
- **TERMS/CONTRACT at 100** is the chapter's second spine and it never collides with
  anything. *Honest terms* is the most precisely-used metaphor in the chapter.
- **`ch7:359` is a real mechanism statement**, which is why the agency registry could
  license the field at all. The problem is not that the definition is missing.

## 6 · The specific gap

**The Diplomat teaches a somatic capacity in non-somatic vocabulary, and fills the space
where the body should be with a spatial metaphor that is itself ambiguous.**

Both halves matter and they are connected. A charged field is *sensed*, and the sensing
organ is the body. With BODY at 6, the chapter has no instrument to detect the thing its
central metaphor names — so `field` has to carry both the phenomenon and the perception of
it, and under that load it drifts toward the easier picture, which is a place you can walk
into.

**That is the mechanism of the ambiguity Wendell caught.** It is not a word-choice
accident. **The container reading is what a field collapses into when there is no body in
the chapter to feel it as a charge.**

## 8 · Editorial recommendations

**1. Body vocabulary before anything else.** This is the higher-value repair and it is
independent of how `field` is ruled. Ch1's audit asked for one paragraph of water in S7;
ch7 needs the equivalent in S4 — the charge located in a chest, a jaw, a held breath, at
the three or four places the chapter currently says *charged* and stops. Six instances in
78,000 characters is the finding, and it will still be the finding after the field question
is settled.

**2. The field question is downstream of that.** With a body in the chapter, `field` can
narrow to the charge without losing anything, because the reader will have an organ for it.
Without a body, narrowing `field` to the charge makes it more abstract, not less.

**3. Operate the distinction the chapter already states.** `TABLE 4` against `FIELD 119` in
the chapter that defines the difference is the cheapest available fix and the one with the
least authorial risk: the sentence is already written and already canon.

**4. The prepositions are the mechanical half**, and they are separable. *Between*, *at*,
*across* — never *in*, *inside*, *enter*. Including `ch7:359`'s own second clause. **My own
eight relocations from earlier today used `the people in the field` and are on the wrong
side of this**; they want `the people at it`, which is the definition's own preposition.

**5. Ch3's pitch is a different metaphor and should be ruled separately.** It has zero
overlap with ch7's vocabulary and it is doing coherent work inside the game frame. The two
questions have been travelling together because they share a word, which is exactly the
Lakoff point in reverse.

## 9 · Dogfood — what this says about the process

**The framework caught in ch1 that a structural framework can override a metaphorical
family by occupying the most space. Ch7 shows the other direction: a metaphor can occupy
the space a missing vocabulary left behind.**

Ch1's water gap was created by the 8 Gates taking 50% of the chapter. Ch7's field
overreach is created by nothing taking the body's place — 6 body words in 78,000
characters left a vacuum, and the chapter's densest metaphor expanded into it until it was
carrying jobs it was never defined for.

**So the audit's own instrument needs a second reading.** Counting what is present found
ch1's problem. Ch7's problem is only visible by counting what is *absent* and asking what
moved in. A family at 119 is not automatically a problem, as ch4's `line` at 151 shows. A
family at 6 always is.

**The chapter's characteristic failure.** Section 1 tells a parable in which "the village" is a character: it notices, hears, doesn't know, wants, means, prefers, and legislates rules, all without a single named person ever doing any of it. This is not the arcade-conceit or the channel-liturgy kind of nonhuman agency the audit protects — the registry is explicit that Grade 6 collectives (and "the village" reads as one, structurally identical to "the family," "the team," "the school" already on that grade) may carry *pattern* verbs only; perception, intention, and social-causal are all illegal there. The chapter's own thesis, stated elsewhere in the book, is that culture doesn't act — people do, and the pattern is what their acting leaves behind. Section 1 is built end to end on the collective doing exactly the acting the book says only persons can do. It recurs faintly in Section 2 and returns once each in Sections 4 and 7. The second-largest cluster is "the game" as bare subject — taught you, requires, calls for, is allowed to end, ran its course — which the disambiguation rule routes to human triage rather than lets me adjudicate.

**Density verdict:** Real, and the raw-count instrument still undersold it. The net returned 21 Tier-3 rows; roughly half of those were false positives (wrong grammatical subject, or a Grade-2-licensed part misread as an abstraction) — see Cleared. But the net has no entry for "the village" at all, and that omission hid the chapter's single largest and most consequential pattern: 17 sites where a Grade-6 collective takes perception, intention, and social-causal verbs across an entire section-length parable. Once that pattern is added back in, my own count of real Tier-3-severity sites is higher than the net's raw number, not lower. The Sage register does make this chapter's abstractions reach for mind-verbs more than any other Face — that part of the brief is correct — but the dominant defect isn't aphorism-shaped ("the pattern shows you what matters," which does occur, twice, and is a clean violation). It's narrative-shaped: a whole fable told with the wrong grammatical subject for a whole section.

## Keep-candidates — the aphorism problem

**The village bloc, treated as one question.** Section 1 of this chapter is a parable, and "the village" is its character — it notices, hears, wants, means, prefers, legislates, and forgets, seventeen times across four sections. Individually, none of these sentences is the kind of load-bearing single line the Voice Anchor protects (nothing here is doing what "the village learned to make its nos sound like yeses because yeses cost less" — the anchor's own ch4 example, held up in `specs/VOICE_ANCHOR.md` as protected satire — does in one stroke). But that ch4 line *is* the same device, cited approvingly, in the book's own style authority. That's the real tension: the book already uses "the village learns/wants/means" as deliberate, celebrated voice in at least one other chapter, and the Grade 6 rule as written forbids it categorically, with no carve-out the way the five-channel liturgy has one.

What would be lost by fixing all seventeen: the parable's compactness. "The village" as a single collective noun lets the fable move fast — one word carries a whole crowd's reaction where "people at the table" or "everyone in the room" takes three. Read side by side, the R1 rewrites above are not worse sentences, but the section as a whole gets longer and loses some of its fairy-tale cadence ("Once, the Sage lived inside the village's conversations" reads like a fable's opening line; "Once, the Sage lived among people who talked" does not).

What would be lost by leaving it as written: exactly what the book's own thesis argues against. Grade 6's constraint note says the demotion of pattern-language to residue "IS the book's argument enacted in grammar." A section-long parable in which the collective does the perceiving, wanting, and rule-making is the clearest possible on-page counter-example to that argument, in the one chapter about seeing clearly.

I am not ruling on this. It goes to the read-aloud pass, and it may be worth a registry-level question independent of ch8: does "the village" (and its siblings — "the room," "the table") deserve the same explicit Grade-2-style carve-out the registry already gave "Diplomat shadow," given it appears to be a deliberate, recurring, book-wide device rather than an isolated slip? That's a question for Wendell, not for this ledger.

## Cleared

Recall-net Tier-3 rows judged not to be defects, plus a few additional clearances found in my own reading:

- **"I see the pattern, and I'm choosing to stay"** (net's line 71, real line 91) — quoted first-person speech by the Sage (a person, Grade 1). "The pattern" is the grammatical object of "see," not the subject of anything. Net mis-parsed the sentence.
- **"seeing clearly, choosing deliberately"** (net's line 75, real line 95) — same error: the subject of both verbs is "you"/the Sage inside quoted dialogue, not "the game."
- **"The child who wanted to be in the game and see the game"** (real line 187) — subject is "the child" (the Vulnerable Child, an explicit Grade 2 entity), correctly licensed for intention and perception. Net attributed the verbs to "the game," which is the object here.
- **"you did the work and also watched yourself do it"** (real line 195) — subject is "you." "The work" is the object of "did," not the subject of "watched."
- **"The pattern: going up, seeing the whole thing…"** (real line 410 area) — a colon-label rubric ("The pattern: [description]"), one of several ("The distortion:", "The cost:", "The fix:", "The test:") used as structural headers throughout Sections 2 and 4. Not a sentence with "the pattern" as a grammatical subject.
- **"the joystick, and the tell is that…"** (real line 582) — "the tell" is a noun (a poker-style tell/sign), not the verb "tells" acting on "the joystick." The joystick isn't the subject of anything in this sentence.
- **"it lets you know that you are configured differently"** (real line 637) — subject is "the Damaged Self," Grade 2, explicitly licensed for intention verbs including "let." Not a violation; net over-flagged a correctly-licensed daemon.
- **"the game of knowing which game you're in and choosing to play it"** (real line 660) — definitional apposition ("the meta-game IS the game of X-ing"), not a claim that the game itself knows or chooses. The gerunds describe what a player does inside that game, not the game's own cognition.
- **"notice the footnote about yourself that arrives with it"** (real line 779) — imperative addressed to "you"; "the game" is the object of "name," two clauses earlier, not the subject of "notice."
- **"the distortion" throughout Sections 2–4** (e.g., "the distortion navigates these," "the distortion turns cruel," "the distortion designs for the rational actor," "it wonders why the design didn't work") — not in the registry's Grade 2 entity list by name, but structurally identical to "Diplomat shadow," which is: both name a shadow-mode enactment of a Face, both are effectively "a part of you" running in a distorted job. Treated as Grade 2 by analogy and cleared across the board rather than logged site by site.
- **"the game the moment requires" / "the game the situation actually requires"** (real lines 181, 423) — the grammatical subject of "requires" is "the moment" / "the situation," not "the game" (relative clause with an elided pronoun: "the game [that] the moment requires").

# R8 stress test — ch7 (the field) and the Key Terms glossary

## Verdict

**R8 does not extend to the field. It is not a hard case of R8 — it is a category
mismatch.** "Pluralize the collective" is a number change on a noun that names a
population. The field is not a population; ch7 defines it, on the page, as a
space that exists only for as long as people occupy it. "The fields knew" is not
a strained instance of R8 — it is not English. R8 holds cleanly, close to
costlessly, on the two things in your assignment that actually are populations:
the ch7 opening parable's "the village" (3 sites, holds 3/3) and the glossary's
two village constructions (holds 2/2, one of them better than the fix already on
record). Break rate on the field: **effectively 100% — R8 is not applicable to
any of its ~40 sites**, because the operation cannot be performed, not because it
produces a bad sentence.

## Is the field already licensed under C1?

**Ruling this first, as instructed, because it determines whether the other 39
sites are defects or noise.**

Read plainly, ch7:359 is a mechanism statement, not hand-waving:

> "The table is the furniture. The field is what sits between the people at it,
> and it lasts only as long as they are all still there. The field has to stay
> open while people move around inside it..."

That is a real trace: the field is defined as *contingent on and derivative of*
the people occupying it — it has no existence apart from them, no continuity
when they leave. C1 asks for a mechanism on the page. This is one.

But C1 licenses "an agentive verb" only insofar as the mechanism can bear it, and
the mechanism described here cannot bear cognition. A space whose entire
definition is "it exists because you are all still there" cannot *know*
something none of you said, cannot *decide* what to hold, cannot *need* a
specific person, cannot *trust* a translator's honesty, and — most of all —
cannot be "free" in the sense L138 requires, because freedom presupposes a
chooser with continuity of identity, and this entity's defining trait is that it
has none. The definition doesn't just fail to license mind-verbs; it actively
argues against them. That is not a coincidence — it is the same trace the
registry already uses for Grade 6 ("patterns as residue of person-action"), and
the ledger you handed me already made this match explicitly (`LEDGER_CH7.md`,
line 20). I am not overturning that reading; the definition on the page confirms
it independently.

**So: the mechanism is on the page, and under it the field is licensed for
`[pattern]`-class verbs and, by the book's existing severity-tier convention,
for the low-severity directional/mechanical dead metaphor that already covers
roughly 30 of its ~40 appearances (*pushes, holds, gets clearer, stays warm,
costs more*) — the same tier-1 class the ledger already parked without a fix.**
It is not licensed for perception, intention, speech, or social-causal verbs.
That covers roughly 9 sites at Tier 2/3 (`L140, L75, L272, L343, L383×2, L138,
L669, L673, L677`), including the chapter's own definition of its own key term.

**This is not a false alarm.** It is a registry gap that, once closed, resolves
~30 of the 40 sites as never having been defects (worth doing — it removes
"parked" from the ledger's language and gives future passes a citable entity)
and leaves the hardest ~9 exactly where the ledger found them. Add `the field`
to Grade 6's entity table alongside `the village`; do not add mind-verb classes
to its license.

## The four candidate repairs, ranked

Tested on five representative sites: **L138** (required), L140, L75, L343, L272.

### 1st — Relocate to participants (primary recommendation)

Swap the subject noun phrase for the people it stands in for — *the field* →
*the people in the field* / *they* / *them* — leaving the rest of the sentence
alone. This is the move the ledger already executed under the R1 label at L75,
L343, L383, and across the four-time refrain at L138/669/673/677.

| site | before | after | turn | Δ syll |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L138 | "...offered as information, once, to **a field that is then free to answer**." | "...offered as information, once, **to the people who are then free to answer**." | intact — sentence still ends on *answer* | +1 |
| L343 | "...is the one **the field can trust**." | "...is the one **the people in the field can trust**." | intact — still ends on *trust* | +4 |
| L272 | "Am I bridging because **the field needs me**..." | "Am I bridging because **someone here actually needs me**..." | intact | +5 |
| L75 | "...the willingness to let **the field decide** what **it** can actually hold..." | "...the willingness to let **the people in the field decide** what **they** can actually hold..." | intact | +4 |

Holds on every site tested. Cost is real but proportionate (1–5 extra
unstressed syllables inside sentences that are themselves 20–40 words long).
Because the same substitution is reused across the four-time L138 refrain
rather than varied, it reads as one fix carried through a callback device, not
a drumbeat — the same defense the ledger already made for the R1 version of
this move.

### 2nd — R6, dissolve to transmission (reserve for the hardest shape)

Needed exactly once among the five tested sites, and it is needed because plain
substitution reads badly there:

> L140 — **Before:** "A field that does not know what your staying requires has
> never once had the chance to choose you."
> **Substitution attempt:** "The people in the field who do not know what your
> staying requires have never had the chance to choose you." — grammatically
> legal, structurally inert; it buries the actual point (nobody told *this
> specific person*) inside a crowd noun phrase.
> **R6, actually proposed (matches the ledger's L140 fix):** "A person who was
> never told what your staying requires has never once had the chance to choose
> you."

The turn — *never once had the chance to choose you* — survives verbatim in
both attempts, but only the R6 version makes the sentence *more* true: it
answers "who failed to know" with a specific, nameable absence (*never told*)
instead of a crowd that also didn't know. Rank this second because it is not a
default — it is the escalation you reach for when relocation alone goes flat,
which by this sample is roughly one site in five, the same ratio
`GAP_VOICE_ANCHOR.md` found for the village fables.

### 3rd — Registry amendment (necessary, not sufficient)

Real, and argued for above — but tested against the five sites, it fixes zero
of them. None of L138, L140, L75, L343, L272 is a dead-metaphor site; all five
carry an actual mind-verb. A registry amendment closes the ~30-site gap and
should be done regardless of what happens to the other 9, but it cannot stand
in as "the" repair for the field, because it has nothing to say about the sites
that are the reason this stress test exists.

### 4th (worst) — R2, mechanize

Fails outright at the one site the assignment names as decisive:

> **Before:** "...offered as information, once, to **a field that is then free
> to answer**."
> **Mechanize attempt:** "...offered as information, once, **into a field left
> open to receive it**."

This is grammatical and even sounds like it belongs to the register. It is also
wrong. The entire distinction the chapter is drawing — an ultimatum removes the
other party's freedom to answer, an honest term gives it back — depends on
*answer* naming an act only a chooser can perform. "Left open to receive it"
converts the honest term into exactly the one-way announcement the chapter
built the School of the Bridge to disprove. The repair fixes the grammar and
kills the concept it was checking for. It does slightly better on secondary
sites (*"the field's capacity determines what it can hold"* for L75 is
serviceable, if flatter than the original), but a repair that fails the site
the assignment flags as load-bearing cannot rank above one that passes it.

## ch7:138 — the definition that defines wrong

**Before:**
> "A term is a statement of what this field must hold for your staying to
> remain real, offered as information, once, to a field that is then free to
> answer."

**After (relocate — recommended):**
> "A term is a statement of what this field must hold for your staying to
> remain real, offered as information, once, to the people who are then free to
> answer."

**Turn:** survives. The sentence still lands on *answer*, and the whole clause's
logic — information given once, freedom to respond left intact — is unchanged.

**Rhythm:** the changed span runs "a field that is then free to answer" (9
syllables) → "the people who are then free to answer" (10 syllables). One
syllable heavier, same terminal stress on *answer*. The refrain repeats this
exact substitution at L669, L673, and L677 rather than varying it, which is
correct: it is the chapter's own verbatim callback, and fixing it four
different ways would be the actual defect.

**Meaning:** improves. "The people... free to answer" is not merely
grammatical — it is *more accurate* than the original, because the whole point
of the sentence is that a term is owed to somebody who can respond to it, and
"a field" was never that somebody. This is the site the assignment calls the
single worst in the chapter, and it is also the cleanest possible demonstration
that R8 cannot touch it: there is no plural of "field" that means "the persons
who can answer." The repair that actually works here is not a number change.
It is a different noun.

## The glossary

Two village constructions in `APPENDIX_C_KEY_TERMS.md`, both already logged and
fixed in `LEDGER_APPENDICES.md` under R1 and R6 respectively. Testing R8
against those two entries — and against the fixes already on record.

**Compactness note, addressed first:** *village* → *villagers* costs two
characters. In a one-sentence glossary definition that is not a real
constraint; R8 is, if anything, the cheapest possible edit available in this
register.

**Status flag, unrelated to R8 itself:** the file's own header marks it
**RETIRED** — "not in the book," off the spine, out of the gate's shipping
list, unreferenced by any chapter. `LEDGER_APPENDICES.md` already carries this
same flag. I am not ruling on whether that makes the fixes moot — that's not
mine to rule — but any repair decision downstream of this report should weigh
that the surface under test may not ship at all.

**Exile entry.**
> Before: "...it is what happens to the gift when **the village stops needing**
> it."
> R8: "...it is what happens to the gift when **the villagers stop needing**
> it."
> Already-logged R1 fix: "...when **people stop asking for** it."

R8 holds, and arguably beats the fix on record: it needs no verb swap at all
(*need* is a fully licensed intention verb for Grade 1 persons plural, exactly
as it would be for one), so it changes nothing but agreement — closer to the
original phrasing than the already-logged fix, which had to substitute *asking
for* for *needing* to land on a clean predicate.

**Distortion entry.**
> Before: "...what **the village does** when the Face is gone and its gift is
> needed."
> R8: "...what **the villagers do** when the Face is gone and its gift is
> needed."
> Already-logged R6 fix: "...**what shows up** when the Face is gone..."

This is the more interesting result. The appendix ledger routed this one to R6
specifically because "naming individual actors here would be false precision —
the village's response... is diffuse, not traceable to one person." That
reasoning is correct against R1 (which would force a specific named actor into
a diffuse-by-design sentence) but it does not apply to R8: plurality, not
specificity, is what "diffuse" needs. "What the villagers do" stays exactly as
diffuse as "what the village does" — nobody is asked to name which villager —
while putting a human subject back under the verb, which the R6 fix on record
(an agentless "what shows up") does not. **On this site R8 outperforms both the
R1 and the R6 alternatives already logged**, without costing the compactness
the glossary format is protecting.

**Break rate: 0/2 (0%).** Holds on both, and on one of the two it is a better
minimal fix than what is currently on record — worth flagging to whoever owns
that ledger entry, though the ruling is theirs, not mine.

## What the field actually needs

The field needs two separate, non-competing actions, not a single R-op. First,
a registry amendment — add `the field` to Grade 6 alongside `the village`,
trace "relational medium as residue of shared presence" (its own on-page
definition at L359 already says this), licensed for `[pattern]` plus the
existing directional/mechanical dead-metaphor tier-1 exemption — which resolves
roughly thirty of its forty sites as never having been defects and turns the
ledger's "unitemized class, dismissed" language into a citable rule for future
passes. Second, for the roughly nine sites where the field actually takes a
mind-verb — know, decide, need, trust, and above all *answer* at L138 and its
three-times refrain — the default repair is relocation to the people the field
stands in for (*the people in the field* / *they* / *them*, held constant
across repeated appearances of the same phrase), escalating to a full R6
transmission rewrite only at the one shape relocation reads flat against
(*a field that does not know* → *a person who was never told*, roughly one site
in five, matching the ratio the fables already showed). R8 has no role in
either half of this: it cannot be performed on a mass noun with no members, and
proposing it here would not produce a strained sentence — it would produce no
sentence at all. The proposal's real contribution is that it correctly narrows
where R1's fifteen-site drumbeat in this chapter needs to survive as R1: once
the four-site village parable and the field's own thirty dead-metaphor uses are
pulled out by number-change and registry-amendment respectively, what's left
needing a human-agent rewrite is the nine-site field core — which was always
going to need real sentences, because "the field is free to answer" was never
a collective standing in for a population. It was a space standing in for a
person.

## 2 · The finding that changes how this should be done

**It is not 26 edits. It is one consistency pass.**

`R8_TEST_CH4.md` says it plainly and it is the most useful sentence in the file:

> "Because 'the village' is a continuous character across ~110 lines (L66–175), in practice
> this is not 26 independent one-word edits — it is one long consistency pass across the
> whole fable, since flipping 'the village' to 'the villagers' in one sentence and leaving
> it singular two paragraphs later would read as an inconsistent narrator."

So the sites cannot be triaged independently. Any site left singular has to be left singular
**for a reason a reader can feel** — it is a place, or it is the collective as an institution
— and not because it happened to be hard.

This is why ch4 is the one chapter I would not batch. ch5's sites are scattered across a
long chapter and each reads alone. ch4's are one character in one story.

## 3 · The real blocker is not R8

**Five sites break, and only two of them are R8's fault. The other three are sites R8 cannot
reach at all** — the collective noun is not a population, so there is no number to change:

| line | construction | why R8 misses |
|---|---|---|
| 84 | *the rituals started referencing…* | a ritual is a practice |
| 84 | *the stories changed…* | a story is a document |
| **115** | ***the workshops teach the scripts… the workshops promise…*** | **a workshop is a recurring event** |

`ch4:115` is the problem, and it is bigger than a line edit:

- It is **`VOICE_ANCHOR.md` anchor 3** — one of five passages protecting the book's voice.
- It is the passage the anchor cites **specifically for the humor** — the Jerk archetype
  mocking a practice rather than a person.
- **The joke lives entirely in the sentence R8 cannot touch.** R8 fixes the *village*
  clause in the same passage and leaves the workshops clause exactly as unlicensed as it
  was.

> "Read aloud, a listener would not notice the village-clause edit at all. They would
> notice, on reflection, that the funnier sentence in the passage was never edited."

**So ch4 cannot clear C1 even at 100% R8 adoption.** The workshops sentence needs a
different operation, and nobody has chosen one. That is the decision this chapter is
actually blocked on.

### Three candidate ops for the workshops sentence

Current:

> *(The workshops teach the scripts. The scripts do not work the way the workshops promise,
> which anyone who has run one in a live moment already knows.)*

**A · The author's chair (R4).** *"(I have taught those scripts. They do not work the way
the workshop promises, which anyone who has run one in a live moment already knows.)"*

This is the one I would argue for, and it is a question rather than a proposal because it
turns on a fact about your life I do not have: **have you run these workshops?** If you
have, this is the strongest option available and not only on the agency rule — it converts
the joke from *workshops are silly* into *I ran those workshops*, which is funnier, and it
is exactly the move `VOICE_ANCHOR` anchor 2 protects: the parenthetical self-indictment,
the admission that is also the sharpest sentence in the paragraph. The current clause
already gestures at it — *anyone who has run one* — without claiming it.

**B · Persons-plural (R1).** *"(The people who run those workshops teach the scripts. The
scripts do not work the way they promise…)"* Safe, licensed, and it costs the sentence some
snap. The subject gets longer right before the joke.

**C · Mechanical verb (R6).** *"(The scripts come out of workshops, and they do not work
the way the workshop promises…)"* Clears the rule, flattest of the three, and the Jerk
archetype's target goes soft.

## 4 · The two genuine R8 breaks

Both are the possession/self-reference shape — the collective acting on or containing its
own members — and both need a real sentence, not a number.

**`ch4:127` — the worst site in the chapter, and it carries the chapter's thesis.**

> *"…it taught its people that the clean no no longer belonged to them"*
> R8 → *"the villagers taught their people"* — a population cannot teach a subset of itself.

**`ch4:68` — R8 manufactures a defect here that did not exist.**

> *"The village was full of people who could feel…"*
> R8 → *"the villagers were full of people"* — nonsense.

This one matters beyond its own line. **The sentence was never flagged. R8 breaks it, and
the break is syllable-neutral**, which means `VOICE_ANCHOR`'s read-aloud discipline cannot
catch it. Three of the ten known R8 breaks book-wide are metrically silent. Any hand pass
here needs a check the voice system does not currently have.

---

## 5 · The hidden cost in the middle

Seven sites hold only with a touch the R8 proposal never priced: **subject-verb agreement**.
The proposal's two worked examples both chose past-tense verbs (*learned*, *became*) where
singular and plural are spelled the same, which made the rule look free. The chapter's
present-tense sites — `hears`, `has`, `does`, `is` at L123, L136, L140, L175, and the simile
at L121 — each force a real agreement change, and each one has to be tracked through every
pronoun downstream of it for as long as that paragraph's thread runs.

Not hard. Not one word either.

---

## 6 · The split I would propose

| | sites | why |
|---|---|---|
| **mine** | the 14 clean number-changes, plus the 7 agreement-touch sites | mechanical and machine-checkable. Every one has a verdict already written in `R8_TEST_CH4.md`, and the agreement pass is exactly the kind of consistency work an instrument can verify afterwards |
| **yours** | `ch4:115` workshops · `ch4:127` · `ch4:68` · `ch4:84` ×2 | each needs a real sentence, and two of them need something I cannot supply: a fact about whether you have run those workshops, and a judgement about whether the anchor passage survives being edited at all |

If that split is right, the order matters: **your five first, mine second.** The fable is one
continuous character, so the narrator's number has to be settled at the breaks before the
consistency pass can run around them. Doing mine first would mean redoing the paragraphs
around every one of your five.

## 7 · What I am not proposing

**Leaving ch4 alone.** It is 24 of 26 Grade-6 violations in 110 lines, and it is the
chapter where the book argues most directly that people do things and structures do not.
`GAP_VOICE_ANCHOR.md` already carries your ruling that the voice canon is not a shield for
this construction.

**Treating the anchor as untouchable.** The same ruling covers it. But anchor 3 is one of
five passages the Voice Guardian judges every future edit against, so changing it changes
the ruler. That is worth doing deliberately and worth recording when it happens.

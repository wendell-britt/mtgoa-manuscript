## What I claimed, and what is true

| my claim | measured |
|---|---|
| "moves by one syllable" | True only for past-tense verbs, which I cherry-picked. Present tense needs real agreement changes — *has→have*, *does→do*. |
| "changes almost nothing else" | False on ~27% of ch4 sites. |
| "drops R1 from 96 to ~25, scolding problem evaporates" | **Wrong at volume.** ch8 needs subject variation to avoid a tic, and with variation R8 costs the same editorial attention R1 did. It is not cheaper. It is differently shaped, and what it buys is a licensed subject. |
| "the village is a population" | True in 5 of 6 surfaces. False for *the field* (a medium), and false wherever *village* means the place rather than the people. |
| my flagship example | I wrote *"The village had made a rule"* → *"The villagers had a rule"*, silently dropping **made** and downgrading a legislative act to a static possession. *"The villagers had made a rule"* scans fine. I presented a careless compression as the rule working. |

## The five failure shapes — one predicted, four found

**1. Possession / coextensive population** *(predicted)*
The collective acts on or contains its own members. `ch4:127` *"it taught its people"* →
*"the villagers taught their people"* is nonsense. `ch3:316`, `ch4:68` same family.
→ Needs R6 transmission or compression.

**2. Reflexive self-perception** *(new — ch3)*
`ch3:152` *"The village never saw itself doing this."* Pluralized it is grammatical and
**quietly weaker**: systemic blindness with no vantage point becomes many individuals each
failing to notice. Dangerous precisely because it is not nonsense.
→ The proposal warns about possessives (*its*). Reflexives (*itself*) are the same trap in
different grammar and need the same pre-flight check.

**3. Scope-miss — not a population at all** *(new — ch4, ch5, ch8)*
*workshops · rituals · stories · the pattern · loyalty · leadership · organizations.*
No number to change; R8 silently never arrives.
→ **This lands on the anchor.** `ch4:115`'s protected construction is *"the workshops
teach the scripts"* — under full R8 adoption the anchor passage still fails C1 in half its
span. **R8 is necessary and not sufficient for anchor 3.**

**4. Collective-as-place** *(new — ch5, ch9)*
`ch5:171` *"The village becomes a museum"* → people do not become a museum.
`ch5:70` *"the village's boundaries"* → *"the villagers' boundaries"* swaps shared norms for
personal boundaries. `ch9:25` *"than the village extended"* → people stretching
geographically.
→ `ch9:614` needs sentence-wide consistency: one sentence uses *village* as both place and
population and must be fixed whole, not clause by clause.

**5. Volume** *(new — ch8)*
Zero grammatical breaks and the rule still fails. 23 substitutions, 12 inside one 900-word
fable, four in nine sentences. A licensed noun drumbeating is still a drumbeat.

## The safety finding that matters most

**Three of the ten breaks are syllable-neutral:** `ch4:68`, `ch5:70`, `ch5:171`.

`VOICE_ANCHOR.md` tells editors to trust rhythm and the read-aloud pass. **It cannot see
these.** `ch4:68` is the sharpest case — R8 *manufactures* a defect in a sentence nobody
flagged, at identical syllable count.

Any adoption of R8 needs a check the voice system does not currently have.

## R8 as it should actually be written

> **R8 — pluralize the collective.** Precedence: above R1, below R4.
>
> **Trigger:** a fictional collective whose members are persons takes a perception,
> intention, speech or social-causal verb.
>
> **Move:** change the number. Keep every other word unless grammar forces it.
>
> **Four pre-flight checks. Any hit routes elsewhere:**
> 1. **Possessive or reflexive?** (*its*, *itself*) → R6 or compression.
> 2. **Is it a population?** If the noun names a practice, a document, a place, a medium or
>    an emotion → not R8. Route to R1, R2 or R6.
> 3. **Place or people?** If the sentence means the settlement → not R8.
> 4. **Density?** More than two in a paragraph, or more than four in a section → variation
>    is **mandatory**, not taste.
>
> **Variation set** (ch8's, generalizable): full noun on first mention · pronoun
> continuation · the passage's own concrete nouns (*people there*, *the room*, *everyone at
> the table*) · *no one* / *nobody* for negation.
>
> **Verification:** syllable count is **not** sufficient. Every R8 site is checked for
> meaning-drift against the original, because three of the known breaks are metrically
> silent.

## Two questions R8 answered by not applying

**"The field" (ch7) — its mechanism *is* on the page.** ch7:359 defines it as a space
contingent on the people occupying it, existing only *"as long as they are all still
there."* That trace licenses pattern, directional and mechanical verbs. It does **not**
license cognition — a thing whose existence depends on people being present cannot
independently know, decide or trust.

**So ~30 of the field's ~40 sites stop being defects** once *the field* is added to Grade 6.
About 9 remain, and the repair is relocation to participants:

> *"…offered as information, once, to **a field** that is then free to answer"* — ch7:138
> → *"…offered as information, once, to **the people** who are then free to answer"*

Turn survives, +1 syllable, meaning improves. That sentence is the chapter's own definition
of its own key term, so this is the single highest-value repair in the book.

**The office/role pattern (ch5) — 4 of 4 breaks, and the worst inverts the book's own rule.**
*"a patriarch does its own kind of damage"* → *"patriarchs do their own kind of damage"*
converts a critique of a role into a critique of the people holding it, which is exactly
what the Jerk archetype forbids. That pattern needs R1 or R2, never R8.

## Evidence in R8's favour that did not come from me

- **ch5:153, 157, 161** already lapse into plural *"They"* for the village mid-paragraph.
  The prose was half-committed to a population reading by hand, before any audit.
- **ch8:87** reaches for *"they'd heard"* before any edit — the variation set is already in
  the writing.
- At **three ch3 sites**, the ledger's existing R1 proposals silently drop the paragraph's
  closing beat and the plain plural preserves it. The anchor's own rule — *the closing move
  is the register* — favours R8 over the audit's default op.
- **ch9's founding myth is not weakened.** 11 of 12 fable sites hold. The chapter's argument
  (individual agency against collective abstraction) is *served* by real plural villagers
  doing the forgetting.

## Held for Wendell, not ruled

> **Item 1 partly closed 2026-08-03.** Its twin — the `ch9:672` section heading — is
> **RULED KEEP**, along with all 16 village headings in the manuscript. Headings are
> display type and are now out of scope for the audit at every grade: a heading names
> the pattern, the body names the people. See DL-38 and the `HEADING` entry in the
> registry's `registers` block. **The last sentence itself is still open** — it is not a
> heading, and the ruling did not reach it.

1. **The last sentence of the book.** *"Now go build yours: the village is waiting."*
   Pluralized it is grammatical, and it trades a one-on-one closing image — singular Player,
   singular Village — for many-against-one. Twin at the ch9 section heading *"The Village Is
   Already Playing"* — **that twin is now ruled keep; this sentence is not.**
2. **ch8:164's triple anaphora.** *"It learned… It learned… It learned…"* reads as one
   organism quietly acquiring bad habits. *"They learned"* dilutes it to crowd narration. It
   may be doing the same work anchor 3 protects.
3. **ch3:123**, the *"never meant"* exile sentence — pluralizing risks converting a
   diffuse-drift claim into an individually-exculpatory one.
4. **Are the Faces Grade 2 or Grade 6?** Anchor 4 licenses named-part personification, but
   every uncontested Grade 2 use is framed *"a part of me"* — interior to one person — while
   the Faces act externally on a population with independent biographies. R8 cannot apply
   either way; you cannot pluralize the Regent.

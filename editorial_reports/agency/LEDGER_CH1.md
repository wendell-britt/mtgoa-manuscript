**The chapter's characteristic failure, in one paragraph:** When this chapter reaches for unearned agency, it reaches for the same lever twice. First, it lets "the game" — its own central conceit, and the one entity the audit cannot grade — absorb verbs (invites, would rather, does, gives, hands, supplies) that a specific referent would have to earn individually; most of these are harmless because the arcade's mechanics are genuinely on the page, but two (L95's "would rather… notice," L145's "invites") are strong enough mind/social verbs that they need a human triage call on which of the four game-senses is speaking. Second, and more consequentially, it uses "guilt," "culture," "frameworks," and "the spaces" as a shorthand for people it doesn't want to stop and name — genuine authorial choices elsewhere in this very chapter about who set the vocabulary tax, who ran the screening, who wrote the framework that calls love a liability. Those are the sites worth an author's attention: the fix in each case is not softer language, it's a name.

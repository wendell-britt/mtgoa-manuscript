## The six reads

### SHAMAN · the Body

Ask the body which field it has been in. A crop field is reported by the feet; a charged
field is reported by the chest, the jaw, the temperature of a table. **What Jordan's body
actually delivers in a hard meeting is charge, not terrain.**

And the whole Shaman practice presupposes it. `ch3:316` teaches turning the dial *up* on
sensitivity to everything present — that is a field sensor, and it is sensing a medium, not
standing on ground. The Shaman's answer to *which is most alive for her*: **the charge, and
it is not close.** Terrain is a thing she would have to be told about. Charge is the thing
she came in already feeling and had no word for.

### CHALLENGER · the Line

What does the ambiguity cost, and who pays?

**She pays, and the cost is a move.** A crop field exists whether she shows up or not. A
resonance field does not — it is constituted by the people in it and it ends when they
leave. Those are different instructions. Read as a place, her job is to *arrive*. Read as a
relation, her job is to *constitute it*, and her leaving ends it for everyone.

That second reading is the Diplomat's entire teaching: *"it lasts only as long as they are
all still there… the one move here you cannot make alone."* **So the place reading does not
merely confuse her. It lets her off the hook the chapter is trying to put her on.**

### REGENT · the Oath

Two inheritances, neither chosen on the record.

`Field` as a term of art in facilitation and group work — the relational field, the group
field — arrives from a lineage the book borrows from and never names here. `Field` as a
pitch arrives from the game frame, which the book chose in ch1 and made its thesis.

The Regent's question is not which is better but **which one the book has actually sworn
to.** It swore to the game. The therapy-field is the borrowed one, and a borrowed term
sitting unlabelled in the substrate of a chapter is how a tradition ends up running a book
that never voted for it.

### ARCHITECT · the Pattern

Do not adjudicate 119 sites. Fix the condition.

**The condition is that one word carries two source domains and nothing in the toolchain
can tell them apart.** The registry has a single `the field` entity with a single trace. All
six of ch3's pitch uses sit outside that trace and are currently invisible to every
instrument — not cleared, not flagged, unseen.

Worse: the trace was written *from* ch7:359, the one sentence that contains both senses. The
canon is resting on the ambiguity rather than resolving it.

Architect's move: **two entities or two words.** One of those is a registry edit. The other
is a book edit. Only the second one fixes the reader.

### DIPLOMAT · the Bridge

This is his own chapter, and the chapter is running its own distortion on its own key term.

ch7 names the Field-Holder's failure precisely: *"honest multiplicity: holding the field
without requiring it to resolve into one."* That is the ally version. The distortion is
holding two things that needed translating and calling the refusal to choose a virtue.

**ch7 is doing that to the word `field`.** Two senses, both real, held together, never
translated. The Diplomat's own move is the fix: **translate, do not hold.** Name which party
is speaking and say what the other one means.

### SAGE · the Horizon

Which game is each word in?

**The pitch belongs to the game the book is playing.** Bounds, referee, letting a player on
— that is the Arcade, the board, the table. **The charge belongs to the game the Diplomat is
teaching**, and the Diplomat is the one Face whose job *is* a medium: the Translator,
defined by conversion between channels (Fire→Water, Water→Wood, Fire→Earth), the only Face
that exists in the space between two parties rather than in a person.

So the Sage's read is that these were never one word doing two jobs. **They are two words
that happen to be spelled the same**, and the confusion is a homonym, not a tension.

## Synthesis — and the answer to the question as asked

Wendell asked which metaphor is **most alive for Jordan** and which **keeps the game
metaphor strong**, expecting one answer. The six reads return two, and they point opposite
ways:

- **Most alive for Jordan: the charge.** Shaman and Challenger both land there, and the
  Challenger's reason is the strong one — the place reading removes the obligation the
  chapter exists to create.
- **Keeps the game metaphor strong: the pitch.** Sage and Regent both land there. Bounds
  and a referee are game furniture; a resonance field is imported physics.

**Neither wins globally, and that is the finding.** A single ruling for `field` book-wide
sacrifices one of the two things he asked to protect.

### The move that gets both

**Give the place-sense back to the words the book already owns, and let `field` mean only
the charge.**

The book is already equipped: `the table` 38, `the board` 6, `the Arcade` 4 — all game
furniture, all load-bearing, and **ch7:359 already draws the line itself**: *"The table is
the furniture. The field is what sits between the people at it."* The distinction is canon.
It is leaking, not missing.

What that buys:

1. **The game frame gets stronger, not weaker.** The pitch sense moves onto nouns that are
   explicitly game objects. `let the read onto the table` is idiomatic English *and* a game
   move *and* already this book's vocabulary.
2. **Jordan keeps the live one.** `field` narrows to the thing her body already reports.
3. **ch7:359 stops contradicting itself**, and the registry's trace stops resting on a
   sentence that says two things.
4. **It is subtractive.** No new vocabulary, no coined term, nothing for her to learn.

### What it costs, stated plainly

- ch3's six `onto the field` become `onto the table`, inside a passage whose extended
  conceit is a referee and bounds. A table has no bounds and no referee. **That is a real
  loss and it is the reason this is a ruling rather than a script.**
- ch7's container prepositions all need checking: `in the field`, `inside it`, `a live
  field with actual people in it`. The definition's own second clause is one of them.
- My eight ch7 relocations from earlier today used `the people in the field` and `the
  people in it` — the container reading. **Under this ruling they are wrong and want
  redoing as `the people at it`**, which is the definition's own preposition and was
  available the whole time.

### The one thing I would not do

Leave ch7 unaudited. Four chapters have a metaphor audit and the chapter with 119 instances
of the book's most contested noun is not among them. Whatever gets ruled here, ch7 should
get the pass that would have caught it.

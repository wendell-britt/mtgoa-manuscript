# ♣️ Wake Up — Allyship-Sharpened Suit

*Waking up in this suit means catching yourself in the game before the game runs you again. Noticing your body is sometimes the vehicle. Naming the pattern is the point.*

*Solo-playable. Every card targets a specific move you make as an ally.*

---

## How to Read Each Card

Each card has three questions answered on it:
- **Why would I do this?** — The card names the specific pattern that makes this relevant right now.
- **When would I do this?** — A real moment this card would show up in an allyship context.
- **How does this make me a better ally?** — The bridge from noticing to doing.

If the card can't answer all three, it doesn't get written.

---

### ♣️ A — Catch the override

**Easy:** Name one thing you just did in this interaction — before you decided whether it was right.
*Why: Because the pattern runs before the decision. You are already doing it when you think you're deciding.*
*When: Right after someone describes a problem and you feel the impulse to solve or caretake.*
*Allyship use: Catching the override before it looks like helping.*

**Medium:** Name the thing you just said or did — and notice whether it was what you actually felt or what you thought was expected.
*Why: Because performed care depletes you and the other person can feel it.*
*When: The moment you realize you're about to say something kind that you don't actually feel.*
*Allyship use: Spotting the difference between resonance and performance.*

**Hard:** Name the last time you performed care for someone and felt depleted afterward — and notice whether they received you or received the performance.
*Why: Because performing looks identical to giving from the inside. The body knows. The other person knows too.*
*When: After any interaction where you were very good and very tired afterward.*
*Allyship use: The cost is the data. Depletion is the signal that you overrode yourself.*

### ♣️ 2 — Spot the game move

**Easy:** Name one game move you just made — the silence, the softening, the agreeing.
*Why: Because you are always making game moves. The question is whether you're doing it by design or by default.*
*When: Right after a conversation where you didn't say what you actually thought.*
*Allyship use: Silence is a move. Name it before it becomes your identity.*

**Medium:** Name the move before you make it — notice the impulse to perform, smooth, or disappear.
*Why: Because the impulse and the move are two different moments. Catching it between them is where the freedom is.*
*When: In a conversation where you feel the pull to be the reasonable one.*
*Allyship use: The Diplomat who can't name their walk-away price has no price. Spotting the smooth is the start of having one.*

**Hard:** Watch yourself make the move in real time — and name it while it's happening, not after.
*Why: Because replay is awareness. Real-time spotting is capacity.*
*When: When you notice you're being very reasonable and it feels like effort.*
*Allyship use: The gap between what you know and what you do is real. Spotting it in real time is the practice.*

---

### ♣️ 3 — Feel the cost

**Easy:** Name one thing this allyship move cost you — the agreement, the silence, the performance.
*Why: Because costs compound and nobody tallies them for you.*
*When: After a hard conversation where you were very graceful and very tired afterward.*
*Allyship use: Grace that depletes you is not grace. It's fawning with better branding.*

**Medium:** Name what it cost you AND what it cost them — the price of the performance on both sides.
*Why: Because performed allyship charges two people. You pay the energy. They pay the authenticity.*
*When: After you've been very supportive and the other person seems somehow more distant.*
*Allyship use: When your care feels like effort, it's not care. It's caretaking from past pattern. They can feel the difference even if you can't.*

**Hard:** Track the cost across three interactions — and notice whether the pattern is depleting you faster than you're recovering.
*Why: Because the cost of fawning doesn't show up in one interaction. It shows up in your capacity over time.*
*When: When you notice you're showing up tired more often than you're showing up resourced.*
*Allyship use: The person who can't resource themselves can't sustain showing up. Your depletion is their loss.*

### ♣️ 4 — Name the inheritance

**Easy:** Name one thing you believe about allyship that you inherited — before you decided whether it was true.
*Why: Because most of your allyship beliefs came from places you weren't watching. Some of them are costing you.*
*When: When you know the right answer before the conversation starts.*
*Allyship use: The inherited map is not your fault. But it is your responsibility to notice it.*

**Medium:** Name the belief that runs your allyship without your permission — the one you don't examine because it sounds good.
*Why: Because the beliefs that sound most like allyship are often the ones that most prevent it.*
*When: When you catch yourself saying something that sounds like the right thing to say.*
*Allyship use: "I know how to help" is a belief. "I know what they need" is a belief. Both can run without your noticing.*

**Hard:** Find the moment you inherited that belief — and notice whether the context it came from still applies.
*Why: Because a belief from one context, applied to a different context, produces automaticity instead of allyship.*
*When: When you find yourself responding to someone with a script that doesn't quite fit.*
*Allyship use: Scripts are efficient. Scripts also mean you're not actually with the person in front of you.*

### ♣️ 5 — Notice who's absent

**Easy:** Name one person in this room or conversation who is not being heard — and notice whether you noticed them before now.
*Why: Because your allyship radar has blind spots. The question is whether you're curious enough to find them.*
*When: In any group conversation where the energy is settled and nobody is pushing.*
*Allyship use: Effective allies notice who's missing before the meeting ends. Not after.*

**Medium:** Name who's absent from the room right now — their perspective, their stake, their cost.
*Why: Because every room has an absent third. Allyship that ignores the absent is incomplete.*
*When: When you're in a conversation about a decision that affects people who aren't in the room.*
*Allyship use: The Diplomat's gift is holding the field for the people who aren't in it. That requires noticing who's not there.*

**Hard:** Name what the room would have to admit if the absent person were in it — and notice whether anyone in the room is already carrying that admission.
*Why: Because the absent person's perspective is often the thing everyone is avoiding.*
*When: When a conversation feels complete but something still feels wrong.*
*Allyship use: The Sage sees who's not in the room. The ally who notices absence has already started the work of the Sage.*

### ♣️ 6 — Find the split

**Easy:** Name one thing you know that you are not doing — and notice that the gap is the problem.
*Why: Because the failure mode in allyship is almost never ignorance. It's the split between knowing and doing.*
*When: In any moment where you thought "I should" and did something else instead.*
*Allyship use: "Should" without action is not conscience. It's self-management theater.*

**Medium:** Name the exact moment the split happened — when you knew and when you chose not to.
*Why: Because the split has a location. It happens in a specific moment. That moment is where the work is.*
*When: After a conversation where you stayed silent when you had something to say.*
*Allyship use: The Challenger's power requires knowing what line you would hold. The split means you don't know yet.*

**Hard:** Name what you would have had to be to do what you knew — and notice whether that person still seems too dangerous to be.
*Why: Because the split often runs on an unexamined belief that being that person would cost too much.*
*When: When the thing you didn't do would have required you to be more of yourself, not less.*
*Allyship use: The person who knows and doesn't do often knows exactly what being that person would require. The question is whether the cost estimate is accurate.*

### ♣️ 7 — Name the map

**Easy:** Name one thing this interaction reminded you of — a pattern, a context, a wound.
*Why: Because the Forest uses old maps for new terrain. The pattern you just triggered is the map.*
*When: When something in this conversation activated a reaction that feels bigger than this moment.*
*Allyship use: Reactions that feel disproportionate are not disproportionate. They are your history meeting this moment.*

**Medium:** Name whether this pattern has shown up before — in a similar conversation, a similar person, a similar stakes.
*Why: Because repetition is information. The third time a pattern shows up, it's not a coincidence.*
*When: When you find yourself in a version of a conversation you've had before.*
*Allyship use: If you've had the same failure three times, it's not a communication problem. It's a pattern. Patterns require different work.*

**Hard:** Name what the pattern is protecting you from — and whether that thing is still actually happening.
*Why: Because every protective pattern was protecting something real. The question is whether the threat is current.*
*When: When the pattern that keeps you safe in allyship contexts also keeps you ineffective.*
*Allyship use: The gate is not the enemy. The gate is the map of where the fear lives. The work is through the gate, not around it.*

### ♣️ 8 — Catch the savior

**Easy:** Notice whether you just swooped in to solve something — and check if that was yours to solve.
*Why: Because the savior pattern looks like care and costs everyone more than it gives.*
*When: When you've just made a decision for someone about what they need.*
*Allyship use: The fixer is not your friend. The fixer is the shadow of care, and it depletes both people.*

**Medium:** Notice the moment you decided you knew what they needed before they finished speaking.
*Why: Because good allies are often bad listeners — they're so committed to being helpful that they stop being with.*
*When: When you realize you're already formulating a solution while they're still talking.*
*Allyship use: The Diplomat's first move is witnessing, not translating. If you can't witness without fixing, you can't translate.*

**Hard:** Notice whether your care just showed up — or whether you had to deploy it.
*Why: Because actual care doesn't require deployment. It arrives. Care that requires activation is a different thing.*
*When: When you catch yourself caring with effort instead of caring with resonance.*
*Allyship use: The person who can tell the difference between resonance and performance has already done most of the work.*

### ♣️ 9 — Feel what you've been deferring

**Easy:** Name one thing you've been deferring in this relationship — a boundary, a truth, a question.
*Why: Because deferring feels safe in the moment and costs you across time. The compounding deferral is the actual pattern.*
*When: In any relationship where you've been very good for very long and feel unseen.*
*Allyship use: Long deferral is not generosity. It's a slow form of resentment-building that eventually breaks something.*

**Medium:** Name whether the deferral is about being kind — or about being afraid of what saying it would mean.
*Why: Because deferral often runs as if it were kindness. It is actually a form of silence.*
*When: When you've been accommodating something you'd actually name if you felt safe enough.*
*Allyship use: The Diplomat who names their walk-away price does so because they've stopped deferring to the point of not knowing their own price.*

**Hard:** Name what the relationship would have to become if you stopped deferring — and whether you're willing to let it become that.
*Why: Because stopping the deferral changes the relationship. That's not a side effect. That's the point.*
*When: When the thing you're deferring is the thing that, if named, would reframe the whole interaction.*
*Allyship use: The Challenger draws lines. The Diplomat names their price. Both require knowing what you've been deferring.*

### ♣️ 10 — See the weather system

**Easy:** Name one thing that's true in this room that nobody is saying — and notice whether you're holding it.
*Why: Because unspoken truths have weight. They affect the field even when they're not named.*
*When: When the energy in the room doesn't match the words being said.*
*Allyship use: Effective allies feel the weather before it rains. Noticing the unspoken is the start of deciding whether to name it.*

**Medium:** Name what the room would have to admit to change — and notice whether anyone in it is ready to say that.
*Why: Because every stuck room has an admission it is not ready to make. The Sage sees this. The ally who sees this has started the Sage's work.*
*When: When you're in a conversation that keeps returning to the same place without resolving it.*
*Allyship use: Naming the altitude is the Sage's move. Noticing it as an ally is your entry point.*

**Hard:** Track the weather system across the whole interaction — and notice whether your presence is changing it.
*Why: Because your presence is a move in the field. It is always doing something. The question is whether you know what.*
*When: When you realize you've been present as a performance more than as a person.*
*Allyship use: The Diplomat holds the field. The question is whether you're holding it with presence or with management.*

### ♣️ J — Activate

**Name one thing you've been performing as allyship — and say it out loud before someone else does.**
*Why: Because the thing you're performing is usually visible to the other person before it is visible to you.*
*When: Before a hard conversation where you've been preparing to be supportive instead of real.*
*Constraint: Say it without explanation. The naming is the move.*

### ♣️ Q — Deepen

**Stay with the thing you just noticed — and name what would have to change for the pattern to stop.**
*Why: Because noticing without deciding keeps the pattern intact. The pattern needs a decision at the root.*
*When: After catching yourself in the same pattern you've caught yourself in before.*
*Constraint: No framing, no context-setting — just the decision that would be different.*

### ♣️ K — Complete

**Name the thing you're most afraid to stop doing — and let the room receive that.**
*Why: Because the thing you're most afraid to stop is usually the thing that's costing you the most.*
*When: When the cost of the performance is higher than the cost of letting it go.*
*Constraint: One word. No follow-up. The room receives it.*

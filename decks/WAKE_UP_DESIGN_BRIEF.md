# Wake Up Suit — Design Brief: What "Waking Up" Actually Means

**Problem with the current design:** The cards treat Wake Up as somatic noticing — body scan, breath, felt sense. That is not what "wake up" means in the MTGOA framework. It is not what "woke" meant before it became a pejorative. And it is not what makes someone a better ally.

**What "Wake Up" actually is:**

In the MTGOA framework, to wake up is to **name the game you're already in** — to see the pattern running you before it runs you again.

The WOKE problem: People got good at noticing. They could name the pattern, describe the dynamic, diagnose the system. And then they did nothing differently. The noticing became a substitute for action. That is the "woke as pejorative" failure mode — awareness without follow-through.

The deck's Wake Up suit must NOT reproduce this. The suit must produce the minimum dose of pattern-recognition that FUNNELS TOWARD ACTION, not AWARENESS-ONLY.

---

## What "Waking Up to Allyship" Actually Requires

The MTGOA book (Chapter 1) defines it precisely:

> *In the Village, you ask: How do I look right right now? In the Forest, you ask: What keeps repeating in me, and what does it cost if I keep running it?*

The wake up is not "noticing your body." The wake up is **recognizing your game move before you make it.**

Specifically:

1. **The map is wrong** — You were told that learning information + feeling feelings = being a good ally. That's incomplete. The map starts in the forest, not the village.

2. **You're already playing** — Every silence, every performative word, every override of your own instinct to stay comfortable — that's a game move. You were never outside the game.

3. **Your pattern is visible to you** — The thing you keep doing that costs you in allyship contexts is not hidden. It's running. You just haven't named it yet.

4. **The cost is real** — When you override yourself to perform allyship, you pay a cost. So does the person you're trying to ally with. That cost compounds.

5. **The gap between knowing and doing is the actual problem** — Not the ignorance. The split. You already know what you're doing. You keep doing it anyway. That's the wake up.

6. **The gate is the map** — The protective pattern that stops you from acting? That's not the enemy. That's the territory. The direction the gate is guarding is where the work is.

7. **Waking up without moving is the trap** — Noticing is not enough. The suit must make the noticing pointed toward a decision or a move, not an observation.

## For the Enneagram 2/6/6 Recipient

Her specific wake-up is not generic. It is:

**"I perform care because I am afraid of being seen as not caring. The performing looks identical to giving. I cannot tell the difference from the inside. And the performing is what's depleting me."**

The somatic element is real — but it is not breath and body scan. It is: **the physical sensation of overriding your actual impulse to perform the correct response.**

The wake-up cards for her specifically target the override moment:
- "Notice when you are about to say something caring — and check if it's what you actually feel or what sounds right."
- "Notice when you are about to override your own discomfort to keep someone else comfortable."
- "Name the last time you performed care and felt depleted afterward."

This is still somatic — the body knows the difference between actual care and performed care. But it is not generic somatic. It is **pattern-specific somatic** — the body as a detector for the specific thing she does that makes her a bad ally.

## The Card Design Rule

**Every Wake Up card must answer three questions on the card:**
1. Why would I do this? (relevance)
2. When would I do this? (specificity)
3. How does this make me a better ally? (bridge to action)

If the card cannot answer all three, it doesn't get written.

**Old card:** "Notice your feet on the floor. That's all."
- Why? Not clear.
- When? Not specific.
- How does this help me be a better ally? Not addressed.

**New card:** "Notice the last time you performed care for someone and felt depleted afterward. You performed. They probably felt it. That's the gap."

## Card Naming Convention

The suit is called "Wake Up" — not "Notice Body" or "Breathe." 

The language of the cards should reflect waking up to a specific pattern, not general somatic awareness:
- "Name the game"
- "Spot the pattern"
- "Catch the move before it makes you"
- "Notice what you just did"
- "Feel the cost"

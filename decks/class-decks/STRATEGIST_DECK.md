# The Strategist — Class Deck

*One of the six classes — one of the six base decks. This is your card pool, sorted so you can draft it. The book gets you ready to build: it names your class (your deck), surfaces your belief (your subclass — which cards you start with and which one's locked), and gives you enough of the four move-types, the channels, the Faces, and the other classes that you can walk into the app and build your first custom deck fast. The full pool lives in the app, where cards unlock at the speed of play. This is the map of it.*

---

## Who you are

*"We dodged a bullet." "That wasn't as bad as we thought." "See? There was nothing to worry about."*

You've heard these your whole life. You saw it coming, moved early, put something in place — and the disaster didn't land, so someone told you you'd worried over nothing. That's the paradox: the better your foresight, the less proof you have it mattered. Strategy that works is invisible.

Where it came from tells you what you're holding. Nobody builds this vigilance because life was safe; you learned to see it coming because not seeing it coming once cost you. The Strategist is the Protector's oldest move grown up — *if I read the board far enough ahead, I won't get caught.* It kept you safe before it made you useful. The whole arc of the deck is one turn: aiming the foresight you built to protect yourself at the people who need what you can see and can't see it themselves.

## 1. Basic moves — your core cards

Sorted by the four powers. Perception cards are yours from the start; the trust and timing cards only get real through reps — that's what unlocks at the speed of play.

**Wake Up (see)**
- **Power Map** — Map who actually moves the pieces before you push: who holds influence, who they trust, where the real *yes* lives. You're not fighting power, you're finding where it already wants to go.
- **Three Moves Ahead** — Read the pattern to its next stage, not its current one. Prepare for the situation that's forming, not the one that's visible.
- **Three Scenarios** — Carry best case, likely case, worst case, and pick the move that survives all three. Not prediction — a plan that doesn't shatter on contact.

**Clean Up (clear)**
- **Run the Resistance** — When a person or system won't move, run the gate move outward: see it, speak with it, speak as it, until it tells you what it's protecting. What looks like stubbornness is fear with a calm face, and once you've felt it from inside, it's shown you the opening.

**Grow Up (build)**
- **Bank Trust Early** — Build the relationship before the crisis, not during it. If people only hear from you when something's wrong, you read as a manipulator. Trust is the one card you can't draw in the moment you need it.
- **Calibrate the Warning** — Before you raise the alarm, check your own signal: over-reading, under-reading, or right but too early? Calibrated foresight earns the next hearing; miscalibrated foresight teaches the room to tune you out.

**Show Up (deploy)**
- **The Small Test** — Run the smallest version that creates real motion instead of the whole campaign. The perfect plan is your favorite hiding place; the small test is how you leave it.
- **Mirror, Then Offer** — Reflect the concern back before you offer the fix. Warmth isn't a detour around the strategy; it's the channel the strategy travels on.
- **Time It to Readiness** — If they're not ready, don't force clarity. Let them hit stage one themselves, or have them predict the outcome first, then introduce your read when they can take it. Right-too-early reads exactly like wrong.

## 2. Emotional Alchemy moves — your channel cards

Every class metabolizes all five channels; these are the two the Strategist lives in, plus how you touch the rest.

- **Name and Flip** *(fear → excitement)* — When fear constricts the plan, name exactly what you're afraid of, then flip it: *what if this works?* Fear and excitement are one charge; the difference is your belief in your ability to handle what's coming. This card adds your competence back in.
- **Let the Loss Matter** *(sadness → poignance)* — You see more lost causes than most, and sooner. When a battle can't be won, don't armor against the grief — let the loss land and let it mean something: the read you carry into the next campaign. Unprocessed, it hardens into *no one ever listens, so why care.* Metabolized into poignance, the cause you couldn't win sharpens what you bring to the board next time instead of souring it.
- The other three you'll mostly draw from allies: **anger → triumph** you tend to under-use (you calculate where others combust); **joy → expansion** keeps the foresight from going grim; **neutrality → peace** is the steady center the Power Map is read from. Cross-train these or enroll the classes that carry them.

## 3. Subclasses — the moves your beliefs generate

You can run any of the six beliefs, and at the Strategist each one is two-sided. Worked, it **generates a move** — the gift the wound built. Stuck, the same belief **locks** one of your basic cards. Every campaign you run flips one belief from its lock to its move; six campaigns, and all six belief-moves are yours.

- **Not ready → *Deep Contingency*** *(preparation mastery)* — you walk in already holding the plan for the branch everyone swore wouldn't happen. Stuck, it's the over-preparer, locking *The Small Test.*
- **Not capable → *Work With What's Here*** *(resourcefulness)* — you find the leverage hiding in scarce, given materials and win without the resources you were told you needed. Stuck, it's the deferrer, locking trust in your own forecast.
- **Not worthy → *Read the Exact Need*** *(service precision)* — your foresight aims at precisely what this person or moment needs, nothing wasted. Stuck, it's the invisible hand, locking being seen making the call.
- **Not good enough → *Catch the Harm First*** *(quality stewardship)* — you find where the plan would quietly cost someone before it ships, because "will it work" isn't the bar — "will it actually help" is. Stuck, it's the perfectionist, locking the move that isn't yet airtight.
- **Insignificant → *The Overlooked Lever*** *(amplification)* — you spot the person or need the plan erased, and find the move that brings them back in. Stuck, it's the silent forecaster, locking *Calibrate the Warning* from ever leaving your head.
- **Don't belong → *Read the Fracture*** *(belonging-field-reading)* — your foresight reads the relational board: where belonging is about to break and who's about to be edged out, so you move before the edge becomes a wall. Stuck, it's the outside analyst, locking *Bank Trust Early.*

This is the campaign engine in deck terms: each belief you take all the way through flips a lock into a move, and the deck gets richer every time you run the book again.

## 4. At each Face — where your cards go live

The same foresight aims at a different target depending on the altitude you're playing.

- **Shaman *(what matters)*** — Foresight aimed at the stakes: reading which fight is actually sacred before you spend the deck on it.
- **Challenger *(assert agency)*** — Foresight aimed at power and timing: where the leverage on agency sits, when to push.
- **Regent *(create order)*** — Foresight aimed at structure: which rules and systems will hold under load and which will break first.
- **Architect *(create effectiveness)*** — Your home altitude. Foresight aimed at the system: the single leverage point that shifts the whole design.
- **Diplomat *(belong together)*** — Foresight aimed at the relational board: who needs whom, where belonging will fracture, whose trust to bank before the crisis.
- **Sage *(how it all fits)*** — Foresight aimed at the whole game: how this campaign sits inside the larger one, the move behind the move.

## 5. Interactions — drafting other decks to the table

A Strategist alone is a Strategist whose warnings get dismissed. Knowing which deck supplies what yours lacks is half of playing well.

- **Connector** carries trust you haven't built, so the plan lands on relationship instead of logic.
- **Storyteller** turns the plan into something people want to be inside, not just a thing that's correct.
- **Alchemist** shifts the feeling in the room so people can hear the risk instead of bracing against it.
- **Disruptor** forces the moment when you've read that now is the time and the room is still waiting.
- **Escape Artist** finds the exit when you've mapped the board and the honest read is *this one isn't worth the fight.*

You don't have to be all of these. You have to know which ones you're not, and draft them in.

---

*Who needs your deck: people stuck at the same wall who can't see why, people with a real goal and no path, people drowning in options, people burning energy on motion that isn't moving them. Not the ones attached to the struggle, or wanting validation over a way through. If someone can't tell you what they're trying to create, they're not ready for strategy yet — that's information, not failure.*

# The Storyteller — Class Deck

*One of the six classes — one of the six base decks. This is your card pool, sorted so you can draft it. The book names your class, surfaces your belief (your subclass — which cards you start with and which one's locked), and gives you enough to walk into the app and build fast. The full pool lives in the app, where cards unlock at the speed of play. This is the map of it.*

---

## Who you are

You don't just describe reality — you shape it. You can feel which story a person is living inside, and you know that if you change the story, you change what they think is possible. That's the gift. The catch is that people don't *hold* stories, they *become* them, so when you offer a better one, it lands like an attack on who they are — and they defend the story that's hurting them harder than they'd ever defend you.

Where the gift came from tells you what it is. You didn't learn to control the narrative because it was a fun trick. You learned it because somewhere, someone else's story about you was dangerous — and the way to be safe was to get there first, to author yourself before you could be defined. The Storyteller is the Protector's bid for safety through meaning: *if I hold the story, no one else gets to write me.* The whole arc of the deck is one turn — from authoring to protect yourself to telling the story that sets someone else free from theirs.

---

## 1. Basic moves — your core cards

**Wake Up (see)**
- **Name the Dominant Story** — Find the story actually running a person, team, or room. *"Activism never works." "This is just how things are."* Name it out loud so it stops being water and starts being a thing.
- **Find What It Protects** — Every limiting story is a shield. Ask what it's guarding the person from — disappointment, risk, blame — and who benefits from them keeping it.

**Clean Up (clear)**
- **Dialogue with the Story** — Run the gate move on the narrative: see it, speak with it, speak as it, until it tells you the fear underneath. People don't resist new stories because they disagree; they resist what the new story will ask of them.
- **Reframe** — Shift the same facts to a different frame: fear into possibility, complaint into demand, loss into legacy. Not a lie — the truer story the old one was hiding.

**Grow Up (build)**
- **Claim the Pen** — Tell it even when your voice shakes. The story you don't tell doesn't go untold; someone else tells it, worse. The held-back version is the failure.
- **Serve the Story, Not Yourself** — Check whose name the story centers. If it makes you look good more than it moves the work, it's ego wearing a narrative.

**Show Up (deploy)**
- **Offer, Don't Force** — Open the door to the new story; don't shove people through it. Your job isn't to make them believe — it's to make them *see,* then let them choose.
- **Write Them a Role** — Give people a part to step into, not just a vision to admire. A story people can act inside beats a story they can only watch.

## 2. Emotional Alchemy moves — your channel cards

Every class metabolizes all five channels; these two are home.

- **Forge the Anger** *(anger → triumph)* — Anger is the ignition, not the destination. Name the system that must change, turn the frustration into a picture of the win, and frame the struggle as something people can actually *win* — because rage makes rebellion, but triumph makes movements.
- **Honor the Loss** *(sadness → poignance)* — Don't skip the grief to get to the hope; the grief is what makes the story land. Name what's being mourned, honor what was real in it even if it was flawed, and carry it forward as legacy. Sadness is what makes a story unforgettable.
- The other three you draw from allies: **fear → excitement** (a Strategist's calm read keeps your story from becoming panic), **joy → expansion** (lightness so the story isn't all weight), **neutrality → peace** (the steady ground a true story is told from).

## 3. Subclasses — the moves your beliefs generate

You can run any of the six beliefs, and at the Storyteller each one is two-sided. Worked, it **generates a move** — the gift the wound built. Stuck, the same belief **locks** one of your basic cards. Every campaign you run flips one belief from its lock to its move; six campaigns, and all six belief-moves are yours.

- **Not ready → *The Story Already Built*** *(preparation mastery)* — you've done the work to know exactly which story this moment needs before it arrives. Stuck, it's the drafter, locking *Claim the Pen.*
- **Not capable → *Story From Scraps*** *(resourcefulness)* — you find the compelling story in the unpromising material everyone else passed over. Stuck, it's the ghostwriter, locking trust in your own telling.
- **Not worthy → *The Exact Reframe*** *(service precision)* — the one story-shift that fits this person's actual need, nothing wasted. Stuck, it's the lost author, locking *Offer, Don't Force.*
- **Not good enough → *The Story That Holds Up*** *(exacting standard)* — you tell the version that survives scrutiny instead of the cheap one that manipulates. Stuck, it's the over-polisher, locking the rough true version.
- **Insignificant → *The Story That Lands*** *(impact)* — you tell it so it can't be unheard, because you know what it costs to go unheard. Stuck, it's the unheard, locking raising your voice at all.
- **Don't belong → *The Outsider's Story*** *(outsider's perspective)* — you name the story the insiders are too embedded to see. Stuck, it's the outsider narrator, locking the first-person story.

This is the campaign engine in deck terms: each belief you take all the way through flips a lock into a move, and the deck gets richer every time you run the book again.

## 4. At each Face — where your cards go live

- **Shaman *(what matters)*** — Telling the story of what's sacred — why this matters, in a way people feel.
- **Challenger *(assert agency)*** — The rallying story, the call that turns watchers into actors.
- **Regent *(create order)*** — The founding story; the shared narrative that lets an order cohere and hold.
- **Architect *(create effectiveness)*** — Framing the plan as a vision people *want* to execute, not just a correct set of steps.
- **Diplomat *(belong together)*** — The story that includes — the narrative wide enough for everyone to be in it.
- **Sage *(how it all fits)*** — The meta-story: how this chapter sits inside the larger one, the arc behind the arc.

## 5. Interactions — drafting other decks to the table

- **Strategist** breaks your story into the steps that make it real instead of just inspiring.
- **Disruptor** supplies the urgency your story channels into direction.
- **Connector** carries the story to the people who need to hear it.
- **Alchemist** holds the grief and fear your reframe stirs up, so the change doesn't capsize people.
- **Escape Artist** reframes leaving as liberation when the story has to end.

You don't have to be all of these. You have to know which ones you're not, and draft them in.

---

*Who needs your deck: the person trapped in an old story that no longer serves them, the one who can't inspire action, the one needing to re-author their identity after a hard shift. Not the ones who want validation instead of transformation, or who refuse to put the disempowering story down. A story only takes people as far as they're willing to go.*

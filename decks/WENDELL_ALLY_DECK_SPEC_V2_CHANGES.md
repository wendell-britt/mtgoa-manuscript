## Change 1 — Section 3.4: Difficulty Variants

**From:**
> ◆ = Easy (entry point) / ◆◆ = Medium / ◆◆◆ = Hard (edge territory)

**To:**
> **Routine** = routine moment / **Complex** = complex moment / **Crisis** = crisis moment

**Rationale:** "Hard" implies escalating intensity of self-work. Field guides escalate relevance, not intensity. A "crisis" card isn't harder to do — it's for a more acute moment. Routine/Complex/Crisis maps to actual moments and works across all formats (printed card, phone page, dark mode, light mode).

**Spec change:** Section 3.4 title changes from "Difficulty Variants" to "Moment Types." Bullet density notation removed entirely. Moment type appears as a single word label at the bottom of the card.

## Change 2 — Section 4.2: Recognition Prompt Design Rule

**Addition to Section 4.1:**

> **Recognition prompt design rule:** Name the specific failure mode, not the general principle.
> 
> *Not:* "Are you performing care?"
> *But:* "Did you just offer something vague and feel depleted afterward?"
> 
> The recognition prompt's job is to make the moment recognizable. It should use the reader's own language, not the framework's language. If the reader needs the framework's language to understand the prompt, the prompt isn't working yet.

**Rationale:** The recognition prompt is the entry point. It must work in the moment, under social pressure, without context. Naming a failure mode ("you offered something vague") is more recognizable than naming a principle ("are you performing care?"). The Sage identified this as the single most important design rule and the spec gave it the least guidance.

## Change 3 — Section 4.3: What Stays Off the Card

**Addition to Section 4.3:**

> **Warmth note:** The language of each card should feel like a friend who knows what they're doing. Not a therapist. Not a manual. A friend who is useful in the moment and doesn't make you feel broken for needing the card.

**Rationale:** The target user (Integral Green White Female, Enneagram 2, fawning recovery) will respond to warmth before structure. A card that reads as clinical will be set aside. Warmth is a design requirement, not a nice-to-have.

## Change 4 — Section 3.5 ♥️ Grow Up: Clarification

**Current ♥️ Grow Up card ordering removed.** The cards as listed fail Test 2 (they require a quiet room, not a conversation). Replace with the following design intent:

**♥️ Grow Up (revised intent):** Worldview expansion — the ally hears something that challenges what they believe and stays in the conversation.

The allyship-specific Grow Up move is NOT self-management. It IS: being able to stay in a conversation when what you're hearing contradicts your worldview, without defending, dismissing, or disappearing. The cards should reflect this.

**Revised ♥️ card intent (10 cards):**
1. Hear something that challenges you — and stay
2. Receive a perspective you disagree with — without correcting it
3. Hold someone else's experience without it costing you your position
4. Be with someone's anger without making it about you
5. Receive feedback that hits a tender place — without disappearing
6. Stay in the room when the room gets hard — without fixing
7. Receive a "no" from someone you're trying to help — without retreating
8. Hear "that's not what I meant" — without collapsing
9. Be with someone else's pain without carrying it as your own
10. Receive someone's need without rescuing them from it

**Design rule:** Every ♥️ card must be doable IN the conversation, not AFTER the conversation. If it requires reflection time, it's wrong for this suit.

## Change 5 — Section 5: EA Label Note

**Addition to Section 5 (end):**

> **Design rule for emotional range:** The deck as a whole must be able to match the emotional temperature of the moment the reader is in. Use the EA labels to audit emotional range across the full deck during design. A reader in crisis should be able to find a card. A reader in a quiet, complex moment should be able to find a card. A reader who just heard something that challenges their worldview should be able to find a card. If one EA channel has no cards, the deck has a gap.
> 
> EA labels do not appear on the card. They are a design tool for the card writer.

## ⚔️ Challenger — Failure Mode Analysis

### What each card could break

| Card | Failure mode | Challenger's question |
|------|-------------|----------------------|
| ♣️ A | Ally offers something they can't follow through on → trust damage | "Did you just promise something before checking your capacity?" — but what if the capacity question itself is vague? What does "check your capacity" look like in 30 seconds? |
| ♣️ 2 | Reflecting back becomes performative — ally does it mechanically, person feels not-heard | "Say back what you heard in one sentence" — but if you say it wrong, you've just confirmed you're not listening. The card doesn't warn against bad reflection. |
| ♣️ 3 | Tracking where they want to go sounds like a strategy question, not a listening question | "Track where they're trying to go" — this sounds like you're getting them to commit to a plan. The ally might start managing toward outcome rather than staying in the listening. |
| ♣️ 4 | "Stay quiet" is the hardest card and the most likely to be done wrong | Being quiet is not neutral. If you're sitting in silence waiting for your turn to talk, you're not listening. The card doesn't distinguish between productive silence and prepping silence. |
| ♣️ 5 | "Name what you actually feel" could trigger dysregulation if the ally doesn't know what they feel | The Enneagram 2 target user is working on fawning. Asking her to name her feelings before she's had the somatic ground could drop her into freeze. The card needs a ground-first approach. |
| ♣️ 6 | Following their pace sounds passive — ally might mistake stillness for listening | "Follow their pace" — the ally who follows slowly but is still mentally elsewhere is not following. The card needs a quality signal, not just a speed signal. |
| ♣️ 7 | "Receive the whole person" sounds like you're supposed to fix their brokenness | "Not just the best self" — this could read as "the mess is what they're really like" which is its own condescension. Needs reframing. |
| ♣️ 8 | "Don't smooth the hard thing" — ally might take this as permission to not comfort, which can feel like abandonment | "Something hard happened and you're managing to comfort" — if you stop comforting entirely, they feel abandoned. This card needs to distinguish between smoothing (erasing) and containing (holding the hard thing while it exists). |
| ♣️ 9 | "Your reaction is about your stuff" could become a self-blame spiral | The ally who is already over-functioning and carrying guilt doesn't need another way to feel like they're doing it wrong. "Set it aside" sounds like suppression if no ground is provided first. |
| ♣️ 10 | "Keep asking until they're done" — but what if they're not done and you need to leave? | This card assumes unlimited time and emotional availability. Real allyship has boundaries. The card needs a version for when you have to stop. |

### Sharpest Challenger observations

**The three cards most likely to be misused:**
1. ♣️ 4 (Stay quiet) — easiest to do wrong
2. ♣️ 5 (Name what you feel) — most likely to dysregulate the target user
3. ♣️ 8 (Don't smooth) — most likely to be misinterpreted as permission to withdraw

**What the Challenger would cut:**
- ♣️ 5 needs a ground-first step before the naming. Without it, the card is a dysregulation risk.

## 📖 Sage — Principle + Scale Analysis

### Are these portable beyond Wendell?

**Yes — with one condition:** The cards describe listening skills that work in any dyadic relationship. The specific flavor is allyship (not therapy, not friendship, not mentorship) because of what the listening is FOR — helping someone who is working on something that matters to them, in a context where power, identity, and history are present.

**The allyship-specific signal is currently too weak.**

Here is the test: if you remove the word "allyship" from every bridge, does the card still feel like it's about allyship? Currently, about half the cards read as general listening skills.

**Where the allyship signal is strongest:**
- ♣️ 5 — "Feel good before they received it" — this is the specific allyship depletion pattern (performative care costs energy and doesn't land)
- ♣️ 2 — Reflecting back in an allyship context is distinct from therapy because the ally is not the expert; they are the witness
- ♣️ A — "Ask what support looks like" is the canonical allyship failure mode (vague offer, depleted ally)

**Where the allyship signal is weakest:**
- ♣️ 6 — "Follow their pace" is just good listening, not specifically allyship
- ♣️ 7 — "Receive the whole person" could be any relationship
- ♣️ 9 — "Set your reaction aside" is therapist training

**The Sage's prescription:**

Each card needs one phrase that anchors it to the allyship field. Not a label — a flavor phrase. Something that makes the ally feel the specific weight of this context.

For example:
- ♣️ 4: "Stay quiet — not because you're waiting to say something, but because you're not done receiving them yet"
- ♣️ 8: "Let it be hard — your job is not to make them feel better, it's to stay in the room while they feel bad"

## 🧠 Architect + 🎭 Diplomat — The QR Mechanic

### What a QR code on each card enables

The QR mechanic solves two problems simultaneously:
1. The physical card is constrained — it can't go deeper
2. The ally who wants more needs a path that doesn't require the app store

**QR mechanics (two options):**

**Option A — "GO DEEPER" per card:**
- Each card has a QR code
- QR → mini landing page for that specific card
- Page contains: audio note from Wendell, one practice prompt, one common misfire
- No app required — just a web page
- Cost: Low (static pages per card)

**Option B — "HEAR WENDELL" per card:**
- Each QR links to an audio clip of Wendell saying the move in his voice
- The warmth of the card increases significantly when you hear it
- The ally starts to know Wendell before meeting him
- Option for the ally to submit their own recording of them doing the move — builds relationship before they're in the room

**Recommendation: Option A first, Option B as upgrade.**

**QR placement:**
- Back of physical card — doesn't compete with the card text
- App UI: soft indicator on the card face that says "tap for more"

**What "GO DEEPER" page contains:**
1. **Wendell's recording** — 20-30 seconds of him saying the move in his voice, explaining why it matters
2. **One practice prompt** — "Try this with a friend this week"
3. **One misfire alert** — "The version that goes wrong looks like this"
4. **Bars-engine hook** — "If you want to go deeper with your practice, the app tracks your patterns"

## 🌊 Diplomat — Allyship Signal Fix

### What makes a card feel like allyship vs. general listening

The specific thing about allyship is the **power differential and the history.** The ally is coming from a place of relative safety or resource into a situation where the other person is working on something that costs them.

That changes what listening means:
- It's not therapeutic listening (no transference/countertransference frame)
- It's not friendship listening (the power is not equal)
- It IS allyship because the ally has something to offer and the other person needs something the ally can give

**The allyship signal should live in the recognition prompt** — not the bridge. The recognition prompt should name a moment that is distinct to the allyship context.

**Current state:**
- ♣️ 2: "They just finished explaining something" — could be any listening context
- ♣️ A: "Before you move to help" — allyship-specific ✓

**Fixes:**
- ♣️ 4 recognition: "Did you just catch yourself about to give advice?" → "Did you just catch yourself about to give advice — in a moment where your job is to witness, not solve?"
- ♣️ 6 recognition: "You noticed they went somewhere hard" → "You noticed they went somewhere hard — and you felt the pull to help them get out of it faster"
- ♣️ 8 recognition: "You're managing to comfort after something hard" → "Something hard just happened — and you're resisting the urge to make it okay so the room feels better"

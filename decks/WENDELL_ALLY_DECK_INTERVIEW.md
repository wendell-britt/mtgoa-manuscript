# How to Be an Ally to Wendell — Interview Guide
## *Raw material for the first domain-specific deck*

**Purpose:** Generate the raw content for a deck that can be given to the homies and tested in real life. When this deck works, extract the general allyship deck from it.

**Interview method:** Wendell answers. I ask follow-ups. No right/wrong — the goal is specificity, not theory. We want moments, not principles.

---

## Before We Start

**What this is:** I'm going to ask you about what it's actually like to be in relationship with you — from the ally's side. Not what they should know, not the theory, but the moments that are hard, the moments that have gone wrong, the moments where someone got it right.

**Why this works as a design method:** Cards need to fire in real moments. If we write them from theory, they won't. If we write them from your experience of being on the receiving end of allyship, they will.

**The test:** After this deck exists, you give it to someone who knows you. They draw a card and either it helps them show up for you better or it doesn't. That's the feedback loop.

---

## The Interview Questions

### On Allyship Moments

**1. Tell me about a time someone tried to be an ally to you and it went well — what were they doing?**

*Probe: Not just "they were supportive." What specifically did they do or say? What did it feel like on your end?*

**2. Tell me about a time someone tried to be an ally to you and it went badly — what happened?**

*Probe: What did they do or say that made it worse? What were they trying to do? What would you have wanted them to do instead?*

**3. When you're having a hard time, what's the difference between someone who helps and someone who makes it worse?**

*Probe: Is it about what they say? What they don't say? How they show up vs. how they perform?*

### On the Specific Failure Mode

You mentioned the mothers day person falls into caretaking from past patterns instead of resonating with who you're dealing with in the present moment.

**4. What does that look like from your side? What are you feeling or noticing when someone is doing that?**

*Probe: Is it something they say? Something they don't say? A quality to their presence? Something in the timing?*

**5. What's the alternative — what would it feel like if someone was actually resonating with you in real time?**

*Probe: Can you describe a moment where someone was doing that? What was different?*

### On What Allies Miss

**6. What's something you wish allies understood that they almost never do?**

*Probe: Is it something they believe? Something they do? Something they don't do?*

**7. What's a mistake allies keep making that you keep having to correct or work around?**

*Probe: Is it consistent or does it vary? Is there a pattern to who makes which mistake?*

### On the Hardest Moments

**8. What's the hardest thing about being on your end of allyship?**

*Probe: Not the work you do — the experience of being the one people are trying to help. What's hard about that?*

**9. When things go bad in an allyship interaction, where does it usually start going wrong?**

*Probe: Is it something the ally does? Something you do? A dynamic between you? A misread of the moment?*

### On What Actually Helps

**10. If someone wanted to be a good ally to you and could only do three things — what would those three things be?**

*Probe: Not principles. Real things. Be as specific as possible.*

## After the Interview

I'll process your answers into draft cards. The cards will go through the same 6-face analysis (different faces will find different things). Then we'll do a feel-check — you read them and see if they match what you meant. That's the filter.

Then: give it to someone. See what happens.

# General Allyship Deck

*Mastering the Game of Allyship — Canonical Base Deck*

---

## How to Play

**Basic deal:** Draw one or more cards. Read the card aloud. Do what it says within the container and constraint. The group witnesses without fixing, advising, or story-commentary.

**Card variants:** Each numbered card (A–10) has three variants — **Easy** (entry point), **Medium** (deeper engagement), and **Hard** (edge territory). Choose based on what the group can hold, or let the drawer choose. All three variants are valid reads — no card is "the wrong one."

**J / Q / K are suit operators:** They are not harder or easier — they change the suit's mode. Jack activates. Queen deepens. King completes. Use them to shift the energy mid-game or to close a round.

**Face lenses:** After the draw, any player can invoke a GM Face to re-read the card through that lens. This changes what the card asks without changing the words.

**No fixing:** When someone draws a card and does the work, others respond with receipt, not repair. ("I heard that" — not "here's what you should do instead").

## ♣️ Wake Up — Perception + Tracking

*This suit is about perceiving clearly before you act. Body awareness is one tool of perception — not the definition of it. Noticing what's actually happening versus what you expect to happen. Tracking where the other person is AND where they're trying to go. No problem-solving. No advice. Just seeing.*

---

### ♣️ A — Name what's present

**Easy:** Name one thing you notice in your body right now.
*Container: Take one breath before you speak. Constraint: Use a body word, not a thought.*

**Medium:** Name what you notice in your body — and where exactly it lives.
*Container: Take one breath before you speak. Constraint: Include location and sensation type.*

**Hard:** Name what's present in your body — and let there be something you can't name.
*Container: Take one breath before you speak. Constraint: If nothing is nameable, say "nothing is nameable" — and notice what that feels like.*

---

### ♣️ 2 — Pause and breathe

**Easy:** Pause for three breaths.
*Container: Three breaths. Constraint: No speaking until all three are complete.*

**Medium:** Pause for three breaths — and notice what arises in the silence.
*Container: Three breaths. Constraint: Name only one thing that arose.*

**Hard:** Pause for three breaths — and stay with whatever arose without moving toward it or away from it.
*Container: Three breaths. Constraint: No commentary, no story, no resolution — just remaining.*

---

### ♣️ 3 — Label what's moving

**Easy:** Label one emotion present in you right now.
*Container: One word. Constraint: Use an emotion word — not "fine," "okay," or "nothing."*

**Medium:** Label what's moving in you — including any mix of emotions.
*Container: One or two sentences. Constraint: Name at least one emotion and one physical sensation.*

**Hard:** Label what's moving in you — and find where it lives in your body with precision.
*Container: One sentence about emotion, one about location. Constraint: Be as specific as you can about the body's geography of feeling.*

---

### ♣️ 4 — Witness another person

**Easy:** Listen to the last speaker with your full attention — and say nothing afterward.
*Container: Until the next person speaks. Constraint: No advice, no relating your own experience, no "me too."*

**Medium:** Listen to the last speaker — then say back what you heard in one sentence.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: The speaker gets to correct you once without consequence.*

**Hard:** Listen to the last speaker — then say what they are not saying but seem to be carrying.
*Container: One sentence about the unspoken. Constraint: The speaker may pass — and you may not ask why they passed.*

---

### ♣️ 5 — Notice group energy

**Easy:** Notice one thing about the group's energy right now.
*Container: One observation. Constraint: Describe what you see or feel — not what you think it means.*

**Medium:** Notice who's speaking, who's quiet, and what's in the space between.
*Container: Two or three sentences. Constraint: Name at least two people or roles, not just "the room."*

**Hard:** Notice the emotional field as a whole — as if tracking a weather system.
*Container: One sentence that names direction, temperature, and pressure. Constraint: Use a natural metaphor — not a diagnosis.*

---

### ♣️ 6 — Drop out of your head

**Easy:** Notice your feet on the floor. That's all.
*Container: Until you can feel them. Constraint: No talking. Just feel.*

**Medium:** Notice your body as a whole — from the inside, without looking at it.
*Container: Three breaths. Constraint: Name one thing you found that you didn't expect.*

**Hard:** Notice your body as a field of sensation — and let your attention rest there while others speak.
*Container: For the next two exchanges. Constraint: Your attention stays in your body — not in your head's commentary on what others say.*

---

### ♣️ 7 — Track what's happening now

**Easy:** Name one thing that is happening in this moment — outside of what anyone is saying.
*Container: One observation. Constraint: Describe a sensory fact, not an interpretation.*

**Medium:** Name what's happening in you while you're listening — and when you started noticing it.
*Container: One sentence about content, one about onset. Constraint: Use a time marker ("right when they started speaking," "about a minute ago").*

**Hard:** Name the boundary between what's happening in the room and what's happening in you — and notice which side you're on right now.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: Use "room" or "me" — not "we" or "it."*

---

### ♣️ 8 — Notice what you're avoiding

**Easy:** Notice one thing you've been steering away from in this conversation.
*Container: One observation. Constraint: Name the direction of avoidance — not the content (e.g., "I keep going back to the left side of the room").*

**Medium:** Notice where you are NOT looking — and own that it's yours.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: Use "I'm not looking at…" rather than "there's…".*

**Hard:** Notice what's in the room that has no name yet — and say that.
*Container: One sentence about the unnameable. Constraint: Do not explain or justify — just say what's there.*

---

### ♣️ 9 — Feel before thinking

**Easy:** Find one feeling in your body and stay with it for one breath longer than is comfortable.
*Container: One breath past comfort. Constraint: No moving, fixing, or narrating — just feeling.*

**Medium:** Find one feeling — then trace where it wants to move in your body.
*Container: Until you sense direction. Constraint: Don't lead it — follow it.*

**Hard:** Find one feeling and let it complete whatever motion it came here to complete.
*Container: Until something settles or shifts. Constraint: You are not doing the moving — you are allowing it.*

---

### ♣️ 10 — Acknowledge what's alive but unspoken

**Easy:** Notice the silence in the room — and name one thing it might be carrying.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: Speak from your own noticing, not as a representative of anyone else.*

**Medium:** Say the thing that seems to be here but hasn't been spoken yet.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: Start with "It seems like…" or "There's a…" — not "You all think…".*

**Hard:** Name the shape of what's present in the room — its texture, temperature, or weight — as if it were a living thing.
*Container: One or two sentences. Constraint: Do not personify it ("it feels sad"). Describe it as phenomenon, not emotion.*

---

### ♣️ J — Activate

**Name one thing alive in your body before this meeting ends — and let it be the opening word.**
*Constraint: One word only. Let it land without explanation.*

### ♣️ Q — Deepen

**After the last card, stay with the felt sense for three more breaths — then say what is most true about this moment.**
*Constraint: No framing, no context-setting — just the truth.*

### ♣️ K — Complete

**Close this round with one word that names what's most alive in the room — not what you wish were alive, but what is.**
*Constraint: One word. No follow-up. The room receives it.*

## ♦️ Clean Up — Shadow + Honest Ownership

*This suit is about telling the truth in a way that doesn't break the group. Ownership, not blame. Retrieval, not venting. Accountability without collapse.*

---

### ♦️ A — Own a pattern

**Easy:** Name one pattern you notice in yourself.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: Use "I" — not "we," "you," or "the group."*

**Medium:** Name one pattern you've noticed in yourself during meetings like this.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: Include when or where it shows up — not just what it is.*

**Hard:** Name a pattern you've been running — and say who it protects, and from what.
*Container: One sentence about the pattern, one about its function. Constraint: No blame directed outward.*

---

### ♦️ 2 — Admit something

**Easy:** Admit one thing you're not proud of — without explanation or justification.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: No "but," no "because," no "actually."*

**Medium:** Admit something you've been carrying that you haven't said — and let it be small.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: The floor is not a confessional — let it be genuinely small.*

**Hard:** Admit something you're not proud of — and own the impact it had on someone else.
*Container: One sentence about the act, one about the impact. Constraint: Name the other person's experience — not just your guilt.*

---

### ♦️ 3 — Ask for reflection

**Easy:** Ask one person: "How was I in that?" — and listen to the answer.
*Container: One question, one answer. Constraint: Do not rebut, explain, or thank them. Just receive.*

**Medium:** Ask the group: "How was I in that?" — and let there be silence before you speak.
*Container: One question, silence, one response from each person who chooses. Constraint: You may not ask follow-up questions during this round.*

**Hard:** Ask: "What did I do that I'm not seeing?" — and stay with whatever surfaces, even if it's uncomfortable.
*Container: One question, whatever comes. Constraint: Receive it without immediately metabolizing it into a story.*

---

### ♦️ 4 — Acknowledge another person's experience

**Easy:** Receive what someone just said — and say back only what you heard them experience. No overlap.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: No "me too," no advice, no relating.*

**Medium:** Receive what someone just said — and say one true thing about what it must have been like to be them.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: Speak from curiosity, not from assumed knowledge.*

**Hard:** Receive what someone just said — and stay in the discomfort of not needing to fix or relate. Just hold it.
*Container: One exchange. Constraint: No verbal response is required — presence is the response.*

---

### ♦️ 5 — Locate where you've put something

**Easy:** Notice one thing in this room that you have been putting your attention on to avoid something else.
*Container: One observation. Constraint: Name the deflection object ("the window," "my phone," "her sweater").*

**Medium:** Notice what you've been avoiding looking at — and acknowledge that it's yours.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: Use "I put this here" rather than "this is in the way."*

**Hard:** Notice what you've been avoiding — and ask someone in the room to help you look at it.
*Container: Ask. Constraint: The person may say no — and that is information too.*

---

### ♦️ 6 — Own your attention

**Easy:** Name where your attention has been during this conversation — and what it was pointed away from.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: Be specific about location ("the window," "my phone," "the floor").*

**Medium:** Name where your attention has been — and own what you were protecting by keeping it there.
*Container: One sentence about location, one about protection. Constraint: Use "I was protecting" — not "I was avoiding."*

**Hard:** Name where your attention is — and be curious about what happens if you let it move.
*Container: One sentence of curiosity. Constraint: Do not move it yet — just notice the shape of your avoidance.*

---

### ♦️ 7 — Tell the impact

**Easy:** Tell one person the impact of something they did or said — in one sentence about you, not about them.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: Start with "When you…" — not "You always…".*

**Medium:** Tell one person the internal effect something had on you — and own your part in the dynamic.
*Container: Two sentences. Constraint: Include one sentence about what you did that contributed.*

**Hard:** Tell one person the full impact of what happened between you — including the part you played.
*Container: As many sentences as needed. Constraint: Do not do this to absolve yourself — do it to tell the whole truth.*

---

### ♦️ 8 — Forgive something small

**Easy:** Name one thing that has been hard to let go of — and say out loud that you are letting it go of it.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: Do not name who wronged you or why — just the release.*

**Medium:** Name one thing that has been hard to let go of — and actually let it go.
*Container: One sentence of release, then silence. Constraint: No request for apology, no conditions.*

**Hard:** Name one thing that has been hard to let go of — and clear the residue that remains even after you've decided to release it.
*Container: Two sentences. Constraint: First sentence names the thing. Second sentence clears what remains.*

---

### ♦️ 9 — Own a strength you've disowned

**Easy:** Name one strength you have that you do not typically offer in groups like this.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: Say it as a fact, not as a question or a qualifier.*

**Medium:** Name one strength you've hidden or apologized for — and say it without flinching.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: No "I'm not sure if this is…" or "I know this might seem…"*

**Hard:** Name one strength you've disowned — and offer it to the room right now, as something you are willing to bring.
*Container: One sentence of offering. Constraint: Speak it as a commitment, not a disclosure.*

---

### ♦️ 10 — Own your contribution

**Easy:** Name one way you contributed to something that isn't working in this group.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: No "but," no "they made me," no "I had no choice."*

**Medium:** Name one way you contributed to something that isn't working — and trace how your move shaped someone else's experience.
*Container: One sentence about your move, one about their experience. Constraint: Name their experience as a guess — and let them correct you.*

**Hard:** Name one way you contributed to a stuck pattern — and offer what you would do differently if you could return to that moment.
*Container: One sentence of ownership, one sentence of amendment. Constraint: Do not offer the amendment as a way to close the feeling.*

---

### ♦️ J — Activate

**Retrieve one scattered part of yourself that you've left outside the circle — and bring it back in.**
*Constraint: Name what you've been carrying outside. Let the group receive it without fixing.*

### ♦️ Q — Deepen

**Sit with something you've been owning — and stay in the discomfort of it until you feel what's underneath.**
*Constraint: Do not rush to resolve. Let the owning deepen before it moves.*

### ♦️ K — Complete

**Speak the full truth of what you've been holding — and let it be done when it is done.**
*Constraint: No cleanup sentence, no reframing, no silver lining. Let it close on its own.*

## ♥️ Grow Up — Perspective + Complexity

*This suit is about expanding what's possible between people, not flattening it. Understanding, not agreeing. Multiple valid truths, not one right answer.*

---

### ♥️ A — Reflect on how you're showing up

**Easy:** Reflect on one interaction from this week — and name how you showed up in it.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: Describe behavior, not character ("I spoke over them" — not "I'm rude").*

**Medium:** Reflect on one interaction — and name what you contributed to what happened between you and the other person.
*Container: One sentence about behavior, one about contribution. Constraint: No blame, no victim story.*

**Hard:** Reflect on one interaction — and trace how your pattern shows up across multiple relationships.
*Container: One sentence about this interaction, one sentence about the pattern across relationships. Constraint: Name it as a pattern, not a identity.*

---

### ♥️ 2 — Translate another person's position

**Easy:** Take the last thing someone said — and say it back in your own words.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: The speaker corrects you if you missed something.*

**Medium:** Take the last thing someone said — and say it back in your own words, making their position as strong as it can be.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: Do not add your own spin — translate, not interpret.*

**Hard:** Take the last thing someone said — and say it back in a way that surprises even them with how clearly you heard it.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: The speaker rates how well they were heard — 1 to 5. No discussion follows.*

---

### ♥️ 3 — Translate a group's position

**Easy:** Say one sentence about what you believe this group needs right now — as if you were translating for the whole room.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: Use "the group" or "we" — not "you all."*

**Medium:** Say one sentence about what you believe this group needs — and own that it is your interpretation of their felt sense.
*Container: One sentence about the need, one sentence of ownership. Constraint: Acknowledge you might be wrong.*

**Hard:** Say what this group most needs but hasn't found the language for yet.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: Give them the words they haven't found — not the words you wish they'd use.*

---

### ♥️ 4 — Compare perspectives

**Easy:** Name one thing that is true for you — and one thing that is true for someone else in this room. Both count.
*Container: Two sentences. Constraint: Do not connect them, resolve them, or judge between them.*

**Medium:** Name one thing that's true for you and one that's true for someone you disagree with — and hold both as valid.
*Container: Two sentences. Constraint: No "but," no "except when," no pivot to agreement.*

**Hard:** Name one thing that's true for you and one that's true for someone you disagree with — and sit in the discomfort of the gap between them without closing it.
*Container: Two sentences. Constraint: Do not try to resolve the gap. Let it be unresolved and notice what that feels like.*

---

### ♥️ 5 — Imagine another person's experience

**Easy:** Imagine what it might be like to be the last person who spoke — and say one sentence about what you imagine they felt.
*Container: One sentence beginning with "They might have felt…" Constraint: Offer it as guess, not fact.*

**Medium:** Imagine what it might be like to be the last person who spoke — and say what you think their experience taught them.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: Start with "Their experience taught them…" — not "They learned…"*

**Hard:** Imagine what it might be like to be the last person who spoke — and say what you imagine they did not say but carry.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: Name the unspoken — and acknowledge it might be wrong.*

---

### ♥️ 6 — Ask how someone arrived at their position

**Easy:** Ask someone: "How did you come to see it that way?" — and listen as if you have no position on the answer.
*Container: One question, full listening. Constraint: Do not ask a follow-up question while they are still speaking.*

**Medium:** Ask someone: "What experience taught you this?" — and stay curious even if their answer is unfamiliar.
*Container: One question, full listening. Constraint: If you feel your attention drift, name that out loud.*

**Hard:** Ask someone: "What is the hardest part of your position for you to hold?" — and receive whatever comes as a gift.
*Container: One question. Constraint: Receive their answer without bridging it back to your own position.*

---

### ♥️ 7 — Listen to understand

**Easy:** Listen to the next speaker with the genuine intention to understand — before you form a response.
*Container: Until they finish. Constraint: Notice the moment your mind starts forming a reply — and stay with listening anyway.*

**Medium:** Listen to the next speaker — and notice where your understanding shifts as they speak.
*Container: One sentence after they finish. Constraint: Say where you moved, not where you disagreed.*

**Hard:** Listen to the next speaker — and let yourself be changed by what they say before you respond.
*Container: One sentence of changed understanding. Constraint: Name what changed — and what it cost you to admit it.*

---

### ♥️ 8 — Notice where your view shifts

**Easy:** Notice one moment in this conversation when your view shifted or expanded — and name it.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: Describe the shift, not the content of what you now think.*

**Medium:** Notice one moment when your view shifted — and name what it cost you to shift.
*Container: One sentence about the shift, one about the cost. Constraint: The cost is usually ego, certainty, or story.*

**Hard:** Notice one moment when your view shifted — and speak what is now true for you that wasn't before.
*Container: One sentence of new truth. Constraint: Do not present it as "I always thought…" — own it as new.*

---

### ♥️ 9 — Reframe through another person's eyes

**Easy:** Reframe one thing in your own position through what might be true for someone who disagrees with you.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: Use their perspective as a lens, not as a conclusion.*

**Medium:** Reframe one thing — and find what is most generous in their position before you reframe.
*Container: One sentence of generosity, one sentence of reframe. Constraint: The generosity comes first.*

**Hard:** Reframe one thing through another's position — and hold both frames simultaneously without collapsing them.
*Container: Two sentences. Constraint: Frame one, frame two. Do not bridge to a third synthesis.*

---

### ♥️ 10 — Own your position without attack

**Easy:** Say what you believe or need — and stop. Do not attack anyone else's position to make yours land.
*Container: One sentence of your position. Constraint: No comparison, no contrast, no "unlike you."*

**Medium:** Say what you believe or need — and acknowledge that it is yours and not the only truth.
*Container: One sentence of position, one of acknowledgment. Constraint: The acknowledgment is not a pivot to agreement.*

**Hard:** Say what you believe or need — and ask what might be true on the other side of the disagreement that holds your position and theirs.
*Container: One sentence of position, one sentence of the question. Constraint: You may not answer the question you asked.*

---

### ♥️ J — Activate

**Translate the person you're in the most tension with — back to themselves — at least as clearly as they hold their own position.**
*Constraint: Say their view back so clearly they recognize it. Only then speak yours.*

### ♥️ Q — Deepen

**Sit with the discomfort of not needing everyone to agree — and let the complexity deepen before it resolves.**
*Constraint: Do not reach for resolution. Let the multiple truths be held.*

### ♥️ K — Complete

**Speak your full position — and acknowledge how much of it came from others.**
*Constraint: Complete by naming at least one person or source that shaped your view. Then let the completion rest.*

## ♠️ Show Up — Action + Follow-Through

*This suit is about turning insight into something that actually happens. Specific action. Behavioral commitment. Embodied follow-through. No "try."*

---

### ♠️ A — Choose one thing

**Easy:** Choose one thing you will do this week because of this conversation.
*Container: One sentence beginning with "I will…" Constraint: Include what and when ("this week" counts).*

**Medium:** Choose one thing you will do this week — and say it out loud to the person it concerns.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: The person must be named or directly in the room.*

**Hard:** Choose one thing you will do this week that you have been avoiding or resistant to — and say it out loud.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: Include what and when. Resistance must be named, not hidden.*

---

### ♠️ 2 — Offer what you have

**Easy:** Name one thing you have to offer this group — and offer it.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: Name what you offer and to whom or to what.*

**Medium:** Name one thing you have to offer someone in this room — and offer it directly to them.
*Container: One sentence offered to the person. Constraint: They may accept or decline — either is information.*

**Hard:** Name one thing you have to offer that costs you something to give — and offer it anyway.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: The cost must be real, not performative.*

---

### ♠️ 3 — Make a promise

**Easy:** Say what you will do — then do it before this session ends.
*Container: One promise, one completion. Constraint: Keep it simple enough to actually do in this time frame.*

**Medium:** Say what you will do and when you will do it — then do it within the timeframe you named.
*Container: One promise with a time, one completion. Constraint: If you cannot complete it, say why before the session ends.*

**Hard:** Say what you will do, to whom, by when — and let the group hold you to it.
*Container: One promise witnessed by the group. Constraint: The group may check in at the next meeting — and you may not deflect.*

---

### ♠️ 4 — Create conditions

**Easy:** Ask someone: "What do you need from me right now?" — and listen to the answer.
*Container: One question, one answer. Constraint: Do not offer a solution to what they ask for.*

**Medium:** Ask someone: "What do you need from me that you're not getting?" — and let the question be uncomfortable.
*Container: One question, full listening. Constraint: Stay with the discomfort of asking rather than the discomfort of hearing.*

**Hard:** Ask the group: "What do you need from me to show up differently?" — and let the answer be something you have to change.
*Container: One question, one answer. Constraint: Receive the answer without defending your past choices.*

---

### ♠️ 5 — Make a request

**Easy:** Make one request of one person in this room — clearly and without apology.
*Container: One request. Constraint: Name the person, name what you want, and stop.*

**Medium:** Make one request — and name what it will mean for you if they say yes.
*Container: One request, one sentence of meaning. Constraint: Do not make the meaning conditional on them.*

**Hard:** Make one request — and ask what it will cost them to fulfill it.
*Container: One request, one question. Constraint: Receive their answer before you proceed.*

---

### ♠️ 6 — Follow through on something unfinished

**Easy:** Name one thing you said you would do and haven't done yet — and say when you will.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: Own the delay without explaining it away.*

**Medium:** Name one thing you said you would do and haven't — and do it now or name a specific new time.
*Container: One completion or one new commitment. Constraint: No excuses for the delay.*

**Hard:** Name one thing you have been avoiding completing — and ask someone in the room to hold you to it until it's done.
*Container: One naming, one request. Constraint: They agree to hold you — and you let them.*

---

### ♠️ 7 — Schedule something real

**Easy:** Schedule one thing that came out of this conversation — and put it somewhere others can see.
*Container: One scheduled action. Constraint: Must include day and time — not just "this week."*

**Medium:** Schedule one thing that came out of this conversation — and send a calendar invite to the person it concerns.
*Container: One calendar invite sent. Constraint: They must receive it before this card is complete.*

**Hard:** Schedule something you have been avoiding scheduling — and commit to it before you can talk yourself out of it.
*Container: One scheduling decision made now. Constraint: Do not schedule and then immediately open your phone to cancel.*

---

### ♠️ 8 — Follow through especially when it's hard

**Easy:** Do what you said you would do — even if it is uncomfortable now.
*Container: One action. Constraint: "I will try" does not count. You must either do it or name why you didn't.*

**Medium:** Do what you said you would do — and report back to the person you promised it to.
*Container: One action, one report. Constraint: The report must go to the person, not to the group in general.*

**Hard:** Do what you said you would do — and do it again the next time it is hard, before you have to.
*Container: One action now, one acknowledged recurrence. Constraint: Name the next time you will do it before the impulse passes.*

---

### ♠️ 9 — Name what's in the way

**Easy:** Name one thing that is in the way of you acting on what you've said you would do.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: Name the thing, not the story about the thing.*

**Medium:** Name one thing that is in the way — and say whether it is a real obstacle or a story you've been telling.
*Container: Two sentences. Constraint: State clearly: real obstacle or story.*

**Hard:** Name one thing that is in the way — and be curious about whether the obstacle is you.
*Container: One sentence. Constraint: Ask whether the obstacle is inside or outside before answering.*

---

### ♠️ 10 — Act before you're ready

**Easy:** Do one thing before you feel ready — and report what happened.
*Container: One action. Constraint: "Ready" is not the prerequisite. Action is.*

**Medium:** Do one thing before you feel ready — and tell the group what you learned by not waiting.
*Container: One action, one sentence of learning. Constraint: Do not make the learning about the action being wrong.*

**Hard:** Do one thing before you feel ready — and commit to doing it again the next time the fear comes up.
*Container: One action, one commitment. Constraint: Name the next moment the fear will arise — and that you will act anyway.*

---

### ♠️ J — Activate

**Invite someone to do something with you — and mean it as a beginning, not a performance.**
*Constraint: The invitation must be specific about what, not about how it will go.*

### ♠️ Q — Deepen

**Commit to what you will do — and stay with the vulnerability of having said it out loud until it is done.**
*Constraint: The group holds you to it. You do not get to privately decide later.*

### ♠️ K — Complete

**Name what you came to do in this session — and say whether you did it.**
*Constraint: One sentence of intention, one sentence of completion status. Be honest about the gap if there is one.*

## Face Lenses

*Use any face card to re-read any drawn card. The face changes the lens without changing the words — it shifts what you're looking for.*

---

### 🧠 Architect

**What it brings:** Structural clarity. Pattern recognition. The question of what's actually happening versus what's being reported.

**The question it always asks:** *What is the actual shape of this — not the story about it?*

**What it protects against:** Emotional vagueness, narrative drift, and mistaking feeling for fact.

---

### ⚔️ Challenger

**What it brings:** Edge and truth. The thing you're avoiding. The pressure that makes the work real.

**The question it always asks:** *What am I not saying that needs to be said — and why am I not saying it?*

**What it protects against:** Niceness that bypasses truth, and the comfort of staying in the group-good.

---

### 🏛️ Regent

**What it brings:** Containment, pacing, and boundary. The capacity to hold what's emerging without rushing it.

**The question it always asks:** *What does this need before it can move — and am I letting it have that?*

**What it protects against:** Impulsive action, premature closure, and the group moving faster than its members can hold.

---

### 🌬️ Diplomat

**What it brings:** Repair, care, and the space between people. The question of how we stay in relationship through the hard thing.

**The question it always asks:** *How do we stay connected while this is happening — and whose care is missing right now?*

**What it protects against:** Truth-telling that breaks the group without rebuilding it, and confrontation without repair.

---

### 🧙 Sage

**What it brings:** Integration and meaning. What changes after this. What this points toward.

**The question it always asks:** *What is this asking me to understand — not just do?*

**What it protects against:** Action without integration, and change that happens in behavior but not in understanding.

---

### 🌍 Shaman

**What it brings:** Felt sense, aliveness, and what's present before any framework arrives. The body's knowing before the mind's story.

**The question it always asks:** *What is alive right now — before I make it mean something?*

**What it protects against:** Over-meaning, over-interpreting, and losing the body in the process of making sense.

## Suggested Play Modes

### 1. Solo Draw
Draw one card each morning or before a meeting. Do the card. No sharing required. The deck is a daily practice of noticing and acting — not a performance.

### 2. Paired Check-In
Two people. Draw one card per person. Each person completes their card while the other witnesses silently. After both are done, one sentence each of what was discovered. Close.

### 3. Group Round
One card per person in the circle. Each person draws, reads, and completes their card in turn. Others receive without fixing. No cross-talk. The Jack can be played to activate a new round, the Queen to deepen what's been shared, the King to close and integrate.

### 4. Stuck Session
When a group is stuck or avoiding, deal Wake Up cards first — get everyone into their bodies and noticing. Then deal Clean Up cards to surface what's in the way. Then Grow Up to expand what's possible. Then Show Up to commit to one thing. Regent keeps pacing. Challenger makes sure no one bypasses the truth. Diplomat makes sure no one leaves without being held.

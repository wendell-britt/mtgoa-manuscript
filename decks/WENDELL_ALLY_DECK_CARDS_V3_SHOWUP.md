# ♠️ Show Up — V3 Cards
## *Specific action. Honest handoff. Follow-through.*

**Structure:** A–6 = self-side (make the offer, check your capacity). 7–10 = help-side (help them do, not just understand). J = activate (what do they need). Q = deepen (is this actually what they need). K = complete (one move that matters).

**Domains layered:** Each card implicitly operates in one of: RAISE_AWARENESS / GATHERING_RESOURCES / SKILLFUL_ORGANIZING / DIRECT_ACTION

**The interview fed this suit:**
- "They came with a specific offer from their own capacity"
- "Fire and peace" — energy that shows up, not performs
- "I know a guy — I can send you to someone who knows how to help"
- "I can't help with X but I've got spoons for Y"
- "They followed through without me having to chase them down"
- "Triage themselves and the other in real time"

---

### ♠️ A — Make a specific offer

**Recognition:** Did you just say "let me know if you need anything" — or are about to?

**The move:** Make one specific offer. Name what you can actually do. Give them something to say yes or no to.

**Allyship bridge:** "Let me know if you need anything" is not an offer. It's a door left open with no handle. A specific offer is a door with a handle — they can pull it open.

---

### ♠️ 2 — Check your capacity first

**Recognition:** Before you offered — did you check whether you actually had the spoons for this?

**The move:** Name what you can actually give right now. Not what you wish you could. What you actually have.

**Allyship bridge:** An honest "I can do this much" is worth more than a vague "of course I'll help." The ally who knows their capacity doesn't collapse mid-game.

---

### ♠️ 3 — Do the thing, not the management

**Recognition:** Are you managing the help — or are you actually doing it?

**The move:** Do one thing that moves the actual needle. Not one thing that looks like help. One thing that IS help.

**Allyship bridge:** Managing help is a form of control. Doing help is a form of care. The ally who can tell the difference knows which one they're doing.

---

### ♠️ 4 — Send them to someone who can

**Recognition:** Did they need something you can't give — and did you send them to someone who can?

**The move:** If you can't help — say so. Then name someone who can. The handoff is the move.

**Allyship bridge:** "I know a guy" is one of the most powerful allyship moves there is. Not: "I'm sure someone can help." Name the person. Make the introduction.

---

### ♠️ 5 — Ask what matters more than advise what to do

**Recognition:** Are you about to tell them what to do — when asking would serve them better?

**The move:** Ask: what's the most important thing right now? Then wait for the answer before you say anything.

**Allyship bridge:** Advice from a distance without listening to understand is one of the most common ways allyship goes wrong. The question is the bridge. The advice is the dessert.

---

### ♠️ 6 — Name the one next thing

**Recognition:** Is the person you're helping stuck in the fog of everything they need to do?

**The move:** Help them name the ONE next thing. Not the whole list. Not the right thing. The next thing.

**Allyship bridge:** Clarity is a form of care. The ally who can cut through the fog and name the next single step is giving the person their capacity back.

---

### ♠️ 7 — Help them do, not just understand

**Recognition:** Have you been explaining — when they needed you to just be there while they did the thing?

**The move:** Show up for the doing. Not the planning. Not the talking about. The actual doing.

**Allyship bridge:** Understanding is not the same as doing. The ally who can show up in the doing — not just in the conversation about doing — is the ally who actually helps.

---

### ♠️ 8 — Ask what would actually help

**Recognition:** Have you been assuming you know what they need — without asking?

**The move:** Ask: what does support actually look like for you right now? Then listen more than you talk.

**Allyship bridge:** The question "what does support look like for you" is the question the person wishes everyone had asked before they had to figure everything out themselves.

---

### ♠️ 9 — Follow through without being chased

**Recognition:** Did you say you'd do something — and have they had to check in on you since?

**The move:** Do the thing. Then tell them it's done. Without being asked.

**Allyship bridge:** Following through without being chased is how trust is built in allyship. The person who chases you to get what you promised is doing double labor.

---

### ♠️ 10 — Name the walk-away price

**Recognition:** Are you staying in this allyship past the point where it's actually working?

**The move:** Name one thing that would make you leave. Then decide whether you're still in.

**Allyship bridge:** An ally who knows their walk-away price has a price worth staying for. The ally who will stay past that point has already stopped being useful.

---

### ♠️ J — What do you actually need from me?

**Recognition:** Have you been offering — without asking what they actually need from you?

**The move:** Ask directly: what do you need from me right now? Then do that. Nothing else.

**Allyship bridge:** The ally who asks what they're actually being asked for cuts through the performance of helping and gets to the work that actually matters.

---

### ♠️ Q — Is this what they actually needed?

**Recognition:** Did you just do something you felt good about — but aren't sure it landed?

**The move:** Ask: did that actually help? Then listen to the answer without defending.

**Allyship bridge:** The ally who can receive honest feedback about whether their help was actually helpful — without collapsing — is the ally who can actually learn and adjust.

---

### ♠️ K — One move that matters

**Recognition:** If you were showing up at your best — with full capacity, full attention, full fire — what would you do?

**The move:** Name the one move you could make right now that would actually change something. Then make it.

**Allyship bridge:** Sovereign action is the end of the path. The ally who can name their one move and make it has arrived at what all the other cards were building toward.

---

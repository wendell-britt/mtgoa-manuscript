# How to Be an Ally to Wendell — Deck Spec
## *Field Guide for the Moment That Matters*

**Type:** Domain-specific relational development deck
**Target:** "The homies" — people who want to be effective allies to Wendell (and by extension, everyone)
**Status:** Pre-draft / SPEC
**Created:** 2026-04-28
**Version:** 1.0

---

## 1. Core Design Premise

**The deck is a field guide, not a self-improvement program.**

You don't buy it because you've decided to work on yourself. You keep it in your purse or phone because the moment is coming — the conversation, the interaction, the person who needs help — and you want a tool in that moment, not a theory.

**Entry trigger is the MOMENT.** The purchase is identity. The entry is recognition: "this is one of those moments." The deck's job at entry is to make the moment recognizable and tell you which card to reach for.

**The marketing story is not "become a better ally." It's:**
- "Be useful in the moment that matters."
- "Have the move when you're standing in front of someone who needs help."
- "The card you wish you'd had last Tuesday."

---

## 2. Target User

**Primary reader:** "The homies" — Integral Green White Female, Enneagram 2 and 6, working through fawning tendencies, psychospiritually abused by current allyship culture.

**What she needs:** Not more information about allyship. A practice structure that lets her be useful without self-abandonment — because she knows that performing care depletes her AND the other person can feel it.

**Secondary reader:** Any person who wants to show up for someone and doesn't want to make it worse.

**What the deck does NOT assume:**
- That the reader has done therapy or inner work
- That the reader has read the MTGOA book
- That the reader is in a formal allyship context
- That the reader has large amounts of time or emotional capacity

## 3. Structural Design

### 3.1 Two Separate Taxonomies (Must Not Be Confused)

**Suit taxonomy** — type of move being made:
- ♣️ Wake Up → Listening (tracking where the person is AND where they're trying to go)
- ♦️ Clean Up → Dissolving the 6 self-sabotaging beliefs that block good listening
- ♥️ Grow Up → Expanding capacity to listen to things involving your own material
- ♠️ Show Up → Doing the work (specific action, honest handoff, follow-through)

**Domain taxonomy** — area of impact (separate, layered):
- RAISE_AWARENESS — help name what's happening, make the invisible visible
- GATHERING_RESOURCES — help connect to material, social, or practical support
- SKILLFUL_ORGANIZING — help coordinate, structure, or mobilize
- DIRECT_ACTION — help do the thing that needs to get done

**Design rule:** Every card lives in ONE suit and may reference ONE or more domains. The suit is the type of move. The domain is the direction. These are orthogonal.

### 3.2 Card Anatomy

Each card has THREE components, in order:

**Component 1 — Recognition prompt (top of card):**
A single question that helps the reader identify whether this card is the right one for the moment. Answerable in 10 seconds. Example: "Did you just offer something vague and feel depleted after?"

**Component 2 — The move (center of card):**
One sentence. Actionable in the moment. No preamble, no context-setting. Starts with a verb. Examples:
- "Say back what you heard in one sentence."
- "Name the last time you performed care and felt tired afterward."
- "Ask: what does support look like for you right now?"

**Component 3 — Allyship use (bottom of card):**
One sentence. The bridge from doing the card to being a better ally. Must be specific, not generic. Example: "Catching the performance before it looks like care is the start of actually being useful."

**What is NOT on the card:**
- Why/When paragraphs
- Emotional alchemy labels
- Cross-suit references
- MTGOA framework language

**What IS on the card:**
- The three components above
- The card number and suit
- One EA energy label per card (e.g., "Fire," "Calm," "Metal") — see Section 5

### 3.3 Card Count

- **40 numbered cards** (A through 10, 4 suits × 10 ranks = 40)
- **12 face cards** (J/Q/K × 4 suits = 12) — suit operators, not harder variants
- **Total: 52 cards**

**Solo-playable.** Every card targets one move the ally can make alone, before or during an interaction. Group play is emergent after lower-level cards are mastered.

### 3.4 Difficulty Variants

Each numbered card has THREE variants — EASY / MEDIUM / HARD — indicated by bullet density:
- ◆ = Easy (entry point)
- ◆◆ = Medium (deeper engagement)
- ◆◆◆ = Hard (edge territory)

The reader or drawer chooses. All three variants are valid reads. No card is "the wrong one."

**Rule:** Easy does not mean shallow. Easy means accessible. A card that can only be used by someone who has done inner work is a broken card.

### 3.5 Card Ordering

Cards are ordered to create a natural learning arc, not a random draw:

**♣️ Wake Up (A-10):** The listening progression
1. Catch the override — noticing you've already acted before you decided
2. Spot the game move — naming the silence, the smooth, the performance
3. Feel the cost — tracking depletion across interactions
4. Name the inheritance — what's believed about allyship that came from elsewhere
5. Track where they're going — not just where they are, but where they're trying to go
6. Ask the right question — "what does support look like for you?" vs. "how can I help?"
7. Hold the question — stay in inquiry without rushing to answer
8. Receive the answer — receive their answer without redirecting
9. Receive what's underneath the answer — the thing they're not quite saying
10. Name what you're noticing — say what you see without fixing it

**♦️ Clean Up (A-10):** The 6 beliefs + the cleaning
1. I'm not good enough
2. I'm not ready
3. I don't belong
4. I'm insignificant
5. I'm not worthy
6. I'm not capable
7. [A-7 through 10: application variants of the above — how each belief shows up in allyship, how to clean it in the moment]

**♥️ Grow Up (A-10):** Capacity expansion
1. Listen to something involving your own material — without dissociating
2. Set your stuff aside without suppressing it
3. Stay with discomfort without fixing it
4. Receive feedback without disappearing
5. Stay in the room when it gets hard
6. Hold complexity without collapsing into it
7. Receive someone else's anger without defending
8. Receive someone else's pain without carrying it
9. Receive someone else's story without making it about yours
10. Receive someone else's need without rescuing

**♠️ Show Up (A-10):** The work
1. Make a specific offer (not "how can I help?")
2. Follow up without chasing
3. Handoff honestly when you can't help
4. Align expertise with what they need
5. Do the thing you said you'd do
6. Check capacity before committing
7. Name your walk-away price
8. Use the signature response: I hear X / I relate because Y / Let's partner together
9. Triage: know what you have, know what they need, make the match
10. Show up from a full cup

### 3.6 Face Cards

**J — Activate** (♣️ only): Turn listening into movement — say what you're noticing and let it be the opening word
**Q — Deepen** (♣️ only): Stay with the felt sense after — three more breaths, then say what is most true
**K — Complete** (♣️ only): Close the round — one word naming what's most alive in the field, not what you wish were alive

**♦️ J-Q-K:** Pattern operators — activate, deepen, complete the cleaning of a specific belief
**♥️ J-Q-K:** Pattern operators — activate, deepen, complete the capacity expansion
**♠️ J-Q-K:** Pattern operators — activate, deepen, complete the specific action

## 4. Content Rules

### 4.1 What Each Card Must Pass

Every card must pass ALL THREE tests:

**Test 1 — Recognition:** Would a person in the moment know to reach for this card? If the recognition prompt requires explanation, it's wrong.

**Test 2 — Action:** Can the reader do this card in the next conversation they're in? If it requires a journal, a quiet room, or a day off, it's wrong.

**Test 3 — Allyship bridge:** Does doing this card make them a better ally to the specific person in front of them? If the answer is "it might help them generally," it's broken.

### 4.2 Language Rules

- No therapy jargon
- No corporate icebreaker language
- No "how do you feel about that"
- No "take a deep breath"
- No "name one thing"
- Second person ("you") only
- Short sentences only
- NoMTGOA framework language on the card (terms like "emotional alchemy," "WAVE-Spiral," "gates" stay off the card)
- One verb per move

### 4.3 What Stays Off the Card

- Why/When paragraphs
- Emotional alchemy labels visible to the reader (internal design tool only)
- Cross-suit navigation instructions
- The person's EA energy preferences (Wendell's "fire and peace" is a design resource, not a card rule)
- General allyship principles that don't apply to the specific person

## 5. EA Energy Labels (Internal Design Tool)

Each card is tagged with one EA energy label. This is an INTERNAL design tool. The label does NOT appear on the card. It is used during design to calibrate difficulty and to track that the deck as a whole covers the full emotional range.

| Label | What It Means | Who Needs It |
|-------|---------------|--------------|
| **Metal/Fear** | Precision, risk detection | When precision matters more than warmth |
| **Water/Sadness** | Grief, loss, holding | When something has been lost or needs mourning |
| **Wood/Joy** | Growth, creation, alignment | When something new is being built or aligned |
| **Fire/Anger** | Boundary, charge, force | When a line needs to be drawn or held |
| **Earth/Neutrality** | Structure, systemic truth | When the system needs to be named |

**Design rule:** The deck as a whole must touch all 5 EA channels. A deck that only uses Fire and Earth is as broken as a deck that only uses Water and Wood. The reader needs the full emotional range to match the full range of moments.

## 6. Marketing Positioning

### 6.1 Product Name

**"How to Be an Ally to Wendell"** — the specific-to-general bridge

This name works because:
- It applies to one person in one relationship (has edges)
- It implies the general principle (if you can be an effective ally to this specific person, you can be one generally)
- It's honest about what the deck is (a field guide, not a philosophy)
- It's personal, which creates emotional resonance the general deck can't have

**Alternative titles if "Wendell" doesn't scale:**
- "How to Show Up" (too generic)
- "The Ally Field Guide" (professional, clear)
- "The Moment That Matters" (poetic, less clear what the product is)

### 6.2 Physical Form

- **Pocket card deck** — 52 cards, poker size, matte finish
- **Phone companion** — a page on wendellbritt.zo.space that mirrors the deck (accessible, searchable)
- The phone page is the digital twin of the physical deck, not a separate product

### 6.3 Price Point

Suggested: $25-35 for the deck. The price of a dinner, not the price of a course. The deck positions itself as a practical tool, not a self-improvement program.

### 6.4 Who Buys This

- The person who has tried to help Wendell and gotten it wrong
- The person who wants to help and is scared of making it worse
- The person who keeps showing up with good intentions and getting depletion instead of connection
- The person who was told "be a better ally" and never got a tool for how

## 7. Relationship to General Deck

**The specific deck comes first. The general deck is derived from it.**

The specific deck has edges — specific human, specific relationship, real moments. Edges are where clarity lives. The general deck, derived from this one, inherits the structure and translates the moves to general contexts.

**The interview is the method.** The specific deck is designed by interviewing the person who needs allyship. The general deck is designed by finding the pattern across multiple specific decks.

**This spec assumes the specific deck comes first and the general deck is v2.**

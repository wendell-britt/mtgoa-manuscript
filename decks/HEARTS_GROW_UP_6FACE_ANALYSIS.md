# 6-Face GM Analysis — ♥️ Grow Up Suit
## *The Suit They Show Up In Most*

**Source:** WENDELL_ALLY_DECK_CARDS_V3_CLEANUP.md interview data, MTGOA chapters, 2026-04-28
**Special lens:** Why this is the highest-traffic suit

---

## What This Suit Does (Refined)

♥️ Grow Up is about **developmental range** — the ally's capacity to ally across difference. Can you show up for someone whose experience is outside your window? Can you stay present with something that challenges your frameworks, your comfort, your identity?

This is the suit the homies are most likely to show up in because:
- They already have high socialized Green capacity (they care, they're attuned)
- What they don't have is the range to hold the hard stuff without fawning or collapsing
- They are NOT lacking information about allyship — they're lacking the developmental depth to stay in the room when it gets complex

The suit's job: expand the range so the allyship can land.

---

## 🏛 Regent — Structure First

**What they see:** The suit risks being vague. "Expand your capacity" sounds like self-improvement branding. The Regent needs precision — what specifically expands?

**What the cards must answer:**
- Expanding from WHAT? (current limitation)
- Expanding TO WHAT? (new range)
- How do you know expansion happened? (evidence)

**The Regent's test:** Can each card pass this question — "Does this card make the ally more capable of a specific thing they couldn't do before?" If the card just makes them feel bigger without changing what they can hold, it's broken.

**Fix:** Every ♥️ card needs three parts — the contraction (what they can't hold), the expansion (what they couldn't hold before), and the evidence (how the target would know the difference).

---

## ⚔️ Challenger — The Hardest Lens

**What they see:** This suit risks being the most comfortable. "Grow Up" can sound like "be more" — which is the exact performance pattern the Green White female has been trained to do. The Challenger asks: is this card asking for real expansion, or permission to stay the same?

**Three questions every ♥️ card must survive:**
1. Does this card ask for something the ally is NOT already doing?
2. Is the expansion genuinely uncomfortable — not crisis, but real stretch?
3. Does the card avoid using "you can do this" language that lets the ally stay in comfort?

**The Challenger's red line:** "You can do this" is the wrong move for this suit. The right move is "can you do this?" — a real question, not an affirmation. This suit should never give permission to stay comfortable. It should make the discomfort legitimate without dramatizing it.

**Fix:** Replace all permission language ("you don't have to") with capacity language ("can you"). "Can you hold this without fixing it?" not "you don't have to fix it."

---

## 🎭 Diplomat — Bridge and Belonging

**What they see:** The tension between the ally's solidarity and the target's separateness. The ally who grows in range must do so without absorbing the target's experience — the diplomat knows the difference between expanding your range and appropriating someone's pain.

**What this suit must navigate:** The allyship target is often in a different world than the ally. The ♥️ card's job is to help the ally meet them there — not to become them, not to speak for them, not to explain them to themselves. The ally grows to have more range, not more expertise over the target's life.

**The Diplomat's question for each card:** Does this expansion keep the target's dignity intact? Does the ally's new capacity create more space for the target, or does it shift the center of gravity toward the ally?

**Fix:** Every ♥️ card should be answerable from the target's perspective. "What changed about how I'm held" is the evidence, not "what I learned about myself."

---

## 🌊 Shaman — Somatic Ground

**What they see:** Expansion happens in the body, not the mind. The window of tolerance is the actual terrain. "Growing up" means staying in the room when the room gets physiologically activated.

**What the cards should reference:** The edge of the window. Where does the ally go when they can't hold it? What happens in the body at that edge? What practice brings them back to range?

**The Shaman's concern:** Some of the 10 cards should be about staying in range — not expanding it. The person at the edge of their window can't expand if they don't first learn to stay. The practice of returning to the body is the foundation all expansion sits on.

**Fix:** Reserve at least 2 cards for "return to range" practice — not expansion, stabilization. These are the cards for when the ally is already at the edge.

---

## 📖 Sage — Integration and Meta-View

**What they see:** The allyship target's world is not the ally's world. The Sage knows the danger of thinking you understand someone whose experience you haven't lived. This is the suit where the ally most risks mistaking proximity for knowledge.

**The Sage's diagnostic question:** What are you NOT seeing? The card that names the blind spot is more useful than the card that confirms what you already know.

**Fix:** At least 2 cards should be explicitly about humility — "you don't know what you don't know." These are the cards for when the ally is most confident and most at risk of overreach. The Sage is not asking the ally to collapse into uncertainty — the Sage is asking them to be accurate about the gap between their range and the target's experience.

---

## 🧠 Architect — The Product Lens

**What they see:** The deck's primary user (Integral Green White female) will show up in ♥️ Grow Up most often because she already knows how to care. Her gap is capacity — not information.

**The Architect's synthesis:** The suit should have two modes:
1. **Range expansion** — cards that stretch what the ally can hold (8 cards)
2. **Range stabilization** — cards that bring the ally back to their current range when they've gone to the edge (2 cards)

Together, these 10 cards give her a practice: expand, overextend, notice, return, expand again. Over time, the range grows. The Stabilization cards are not weakness — they are the mechanism by which expansion becomes durable.

**Fix:** Name the two modes explicitly in the suit header. This is not a design decision — it's a statement about what the suit actually does.

---

## Summary — What Each Face Requires

| Face | What ♥️ must do | What breaks it |
|------|---------------|---------------|
| 🏛 Regent | Specific expansion with evidence | Vague "be more" language |
| ⚔️ Challenger | Real discomfort, not permission | "You can do this" language |
| 🎭 Diplomat | Expansion creates space for target | Range that shifts center to ally |
| 🌊 Shaman | Body-based range practice | Expansion without stabilization |
| 📖 Sage | Accuracy about the gap | Confident overreach |
| 🧠 Architect | Range expansion + range stabilization | Only expansion, no return |

## Suggested Card Themes (10 cards)

**Range Expansion (8):**
1. Holding complexity without collapsing to one side
2. Staying with not-knowing without fixing it
3. Receiving feedback about your allyship without disappearing
4. Witnessing pain without absorbing it as your own
5. Expanding your window of tolerance (body-based)
6. Holding someone whose experience challenges your identity
7. Being with a truth you haven't integrated yet
8. Saying "I don't know your experience — show me" without deflection

**Range Stabilization (2):**
9. Returning to your body when you've dissociated
10. Naming the edge — what you can't hold yet without pretending you can

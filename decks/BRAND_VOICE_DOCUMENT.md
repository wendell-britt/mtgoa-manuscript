# Brand Voice Document — Allyship Deck
## *How the Deck Speaks, and Why*

---

## What This Document Is

This is not a style guide. A style guide tells you what words to use. This document tells you why those words were chosen — so that when you encounter a situation this document didn't anticipate, you can make a decision that falls within the taste envelope.

The test of this document: give it to someone who's never met Wendell, have them write 5 cards without talking to him, compare those cards to the V3 cards. The closer they land, the better this document worked.

---

## The Core Voice

**Short sentences. Verbs over nouns. No preamble.**

The deck does not introduce itself. It does not context-set. It does not explain why the question matters. It asks the question and tells you the move.

Example of how NOT to write:
> "Sometimes in conversations we find ourselves wanting to help but not knowing exactly how to do so in a way that feels genuine and supportive. This card invites you to consider..."

What that sounds like: corporate training.

Example of how TO write:
> "Did you just offer something vague and feel depleted after? Say back what you heard in one sentence."

What that sounds like: someone who has done this work and is telling you what they learned.

## The Three Components and Their Voice Rules

### Recognition Prompt

**What it is:** A question the ally asks themselves in the moment.

**Voice rule:** Write it as if the ally is already in the hard moment and is reaching for the card. The recognition prompt must make the moment recognizable without describing it. Think: what would someone say out loud that would make another person nod?

**Format:** Always a question. Always present tense. No "if you..." or "when you might..." The moment is now.

**Forbidden:** Labels ("Are you people-pleasing?"). Explanations ("Do you notice how sometimes..."). Historical context ("Back when...").

**Example:**
- Broken: "Are you experiencing a pattern of over-giving where you sacrifice your own needs?"
- Working: "Did you just say yes when you wanted to say no?"

---

### The Move

**What it is:** One sentence that tells the ally what to do.

**Voice rule:** Start with a verb. One sentence. If you can cut a word and the sentence still means the same thing, cut the word.

**Format:** Verb first. No "First, consider..." No "What I want you to do is..." Just the verb.

**Forbidden:** Multiple sentences. Lists of things. "And then..." sequences. Adverbs that dilute the action ("gently notice" — just say "notice").

**Example:**
- Broken: "Take a moment to pause and reflect on what was said, and then see if you can name what you're feeling in your body."
- Working: "Name what you felt in your body. One word."

---

### Allyship Bridge

**What it is:** A small observation that connects the move to being a better ally.

**Voice rule:** One sentence. Make it something the ally hasn't heard before — or something they've heard but never applied to allyship specifically. The bridge should land like a detail, not a lecture.

**Format:** Observation. Not advice. Not "this is why it matters." Just: "what you just did is related to this."

**Forbidden:** "This helps you become a better ally." "This is what good allyship looks like." Generic conclusions. Preaching to the converted.

**Example:**
- Broken: "By practicing this card, you are taking an important step toward being a more effective ally."
- Working: "Catching the smooth is where the actual boundary starts."

## The Emotional Signature: Fire + Peace

Two words describe the energy the deck is trying to create: **fire and peace.**

**Fire:** The ally cares. The ally shows up. The ally doesn't hold back because they're afraid of being too much. The language of the deck should feel like someone who is genuinely invested, not someone who's keeping score.

**Peace:** The ally is not anxious. The ally is not performing care they don't feel. The ally is resourced, grounded, not coming from depletion. The language should feel like someone who can sit with something hard without needing to fix it immediately.

**How this shows up in writing:**
- Fire looks like: direct questions, no hedging, "do this" not "you might consider doing this"
- Peace looks like: no panic, no urgency markers, no "you MUST," calm assertion

**The failure mode of fire:** Aggressive. Pushy. "You should already know this." The deck is direct but not harsh.

**The failure mode of peace:** Distant. Clinical. "Here's some information." The deck is warm but not gushy.

## Word Choice Rules

### Use

- First names when referring to the ally ("you")
- Short sentences
- Questions the ally would ask themselves
- Verbs over nouns ("help" not "assistance")
- Specific moments over general principles
- "Say back" not "reflect back"
- "Notice" not "become aware of"
- "Name" not "identify"
- "Feel" not "experience"

### Avoid

- "Remember that..." as an opener
- "In order to..." (cut it)
- "The reality is..." (just say the thing)
- "What this looks like is..." (just show it)
- "It's important to..." (just do the thing)
- "I want you to..." (the card is not Wendell commanding)
- "One of the most important..." (generic)
- "As you know..." (condescending)
- "This is why..." (explaining instead of showing)
- "Ultimately..." or "In the end..." (pretending this is a conclusion)

## How Wendell Actually Talks About Allyship

*(From interview — transcribed patterns)*

**He talks about what good allyship feels like, not what it thinks:**
- "They were able to attune to how I want to feel."
- "Fire and peace."
- "Giving from a full cup."

**He diagnoses bad allyship by the cost, not the intention:**
- "Grace that depletes you is fawning with better branding."
- "People are asking me to help them help me."
- "I know it's going to be work when they don't know how to do something and don't look cool doing it."

**He names the specific move, not the category:**
- Not "they were supportive" — "they said back what I said and asked how they could help specifically."
- Not "they were a good listener" — "they asked questions until I was done."

**He makes the other person more real, not less:**
- "People can't give what they don't have."
- "They can't take me to anywhere they haven't been before."

## Forbidden Territory

**These phrases are banned from the deck and why:**

- "Center [someone]" — corporate, performative
- "Hold space" — meaningless to the people who need this deck
- "Do the work" — exhausted phrase that sounds like a lecture
- "Show up" (as generic encouragement) — this is a suit name, not a飘
- "Lean into" — consultant language
- "Naming and claiming" — workshop jargon
- "Your truth" — therapeutic cliché
- "Journey" (as noun) — it's a process, not a飘
- "Gift" (as verb) — "I gift you this" is not how humans talk
- "Invited to" — passive and hollow

## What "Warm" Sounds Like

The deck should feel like a friend who has done the work and is telling you what they learned — not a teacher who has read about it.

**Warm:** "You already know when you're performing care. The body knows the difference."

**Not warm:** "Research shows that performing care can lead to depletion and reduced effectiveness."

---

## What "Direct" Sounds Like

The deck should tell you what to do without apology.

**Direct:** "Name one thing you didn't say."

**Not direct:** "It might be helpful to consider whether there might be something you didn't say, if that feels right to you."

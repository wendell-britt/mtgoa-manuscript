# ♦️ Clean Up — V3 Cards
## *12 moments of blocked allyship + 1 sovereign move*

**Structure:** A–6 = ally's own beliefs. 7–Q = target's beliefs. K = sovereign action.
**Note:** No scale mechanic on this suit. Face cards are filled with the remaining belief cards.

## The 6 Self-Sabotaging Beliefs (Source: MTGOA Ch2 — Controller Gate)

The beliefs that run when the Controller is at the gate — keeping the ally from showing up fully:

1. I'm not good enough.
2. I'm not ready.
3. I don't belong.
4. I'm insignificant.
5. I'm not worthy.
6. I'm not capable.

These block both sides: the ally's self-sabotage (cards A–6) and the target's self-sabotage (cards 7–Q). Same beliefs. Different person they're running in.

---

### ♦️ A — Not good enough

**Recognition:** Before you offered help — did a voice say you weren't qualified to give this?

**The move:** Name the belief. Then ask: who's benefitting from this story right now?

**Allyship bridge:** The belief that you're not good enough stops useful allyship before it starts. The voice is the Controller. The allyship target doesn't share that voice.

---

### ♦️ 2 — Not ready

**Recognition:** Are you waiting until you know more, are more, have more — before you show up?

**The move:** Name what "ready enough" would look like. Then notice if that threshold has already been met.

**Allyship bridge:** Nobody ever feels ready. The ally who shows up anyway is the one who helps. The belief that you need to be ready is the Controller keeping you in the audience.

---

### ♦️ 3 — Not belong

**Recognition:** In this moment — are you wondering if you have the right to be in this conversation?

**The move:** Name one thing you bring to this interaction that no one else in the room has. Say it out loud.

**Allyship bridge:** The ally who questions their belonging makes the person they're helping carry the weight of that doubt. Your presence here is not a question. The work is the answer.

---

### ♦️ 4 — Insignificant

**Recognition:** Did you just think your contribution wouldn't matter — and step back accordingly?

**The move:** Name one thing that would shift if you weren't in this room. Say it.

**Allyship bridge:** The belief that you're insignificant is the Controller's protection strategy. But in allyship, showing up small is a choice someone else pays for.

---

### ♦️ 5 — Not worthy

**Recognition:** Did you just receive gratitude and feel like you didn't deserve it — or want to deflect it?

**The move:** Receive what was offered. Let it land. Say: thank you.

**Allyship bridge:** A worthy ally receives the impact of their help. The belief that you're not worthy turns allyship into extraction — you give, but you can't receive. The target can feel this.

---

### ♦️ 6 — Not capable

**Recognition:** Did you just assume you couldn't do this — before checking what capacity you actually have?

**The move:** Name one specific thing you could do in the next 24 hours that would help. Then do it.

**Allyship bridge:** Capability is demonstrated, not declared. The belief that you can't is the Controller pre-apologizing for what you might not do. The target needs you to show up capable, not to prove you're flawless.

---

### ♦️ 7 — Not good enough (target's belief)

**Recognition:** Did they just decline something they actually needed — because accepting felt like proof of failure?

**The move:** Name what they turned away. Then offer it differently: not "here's help" but "here's a thing I happened to have."

**Allyship bridge:** When your ally can't receive help because it confirms they're broken, the allyship has to come through the side door. The gift has to look like coincidence.

---

### ♦️ 8 — Not ready (target's belief)

**Recognition:** Did they just say they need to think about it — for the third time — and you're not sure thinking is what's happening?

**The move:** Name what you think they're actually afraid of. Then offer: you don't have to decide now. But I'd like to know when you will.

**Allyship bridge:** "Not ready" is sometimes "I don't trust that this will still be here when I'm ready." The allyship move is making the door stay open — without pressure.

---

### ♦️ 9 — Not belong (target's belief)

**Recognition:** Did they just apologize for needing help — or make themselves small in the room?

**The move:** Say: you belong here. Then don't qualify it.

**Allyship bridge:** When your ally feels like an imposition, every interaction costs more than it should. Saying "you belong here" with no footnote is the whole move.

---

### ♦️ 10 — Insignificant (target's belief)

**Recognition:** Did they just disappear — go quiet, deflate, stop adding to the conversation?

**The move:** Ask directly: did something just happen in you? Then wait.

**Allyship bridge:** The belief that they're insignificant makes the allyship target disappear before anyone can tell them to leave. Your asking them back is the rescue.

---

### ♦️ J — Not worthy (target's belief)

**Recognition:** Did they just dismiss the thing you did for them — or say it was nothing, or don't mention it again?

**The move:** Name what you did. Let them see it. Say: I want you to have this.

**Allyship bridge:** When the target can't hold that they were helped, the allyship doesn't land. The ally who can place the gift firmly — and let it be received — is the one the target learns trust from.

---

### ♦️ Q — Not capable (target's belief)

**Recognition:** Did they just say they'll figure it out — while doing none of the things that would help them figure it out?

**The move:** Ask: what's the one step you'd take if you believed you could? Then offer to do it with them.

**Allyship bridge:** The belief that they're not capable keeps the target stuck and the ally at a distance. The ally who can say "do it with me" dissolves that belief faster than any amount of encouragement.

---

### ♦️ K — What would you do if you were king?

**Recognition:** What would you do right now — if no belief was running?

**The move:** Name the thing you'd do. Then do it.

**Allyship bridge:** The belief is gone. What's left is sovereign action. The ally who knows what they'd do if nothing was in the way — and does it — is the ally the target remembers.

# 6-Face GM Analysis — ♠️ Show Up Suit
## *Does This Suit Actually Deliver on Its Promise?*

**Source:** WENDELL_ALLY_DECK_CARDS_V3_SHOWUP.md, 2026-04-28
**Method:** 6 Game Master Faces applied to each card + overall structure

---

## Overall Assessment

**🧠 Architect:** 13 cards, clean structure. 4 self-side (make the offer), 4 help-side (help them do), 3 face cards (J = activate, Q = deepen, K = complete). The domain layering is invisible but present — no card says "this is DIRECT_ACTION" but every card lands in one. The structure holds.

**🧠 Architect's concern:** ♠️ K ("one move that matters") and ♠️ A ("make a specific offer") are doing similar work. One names the sovereign move in extremis. One names the specific offer at entry. The distance between them needs to feel earned. Right now they read like the same card with different intensity.

---

## Per-Card Analysis

### ♠️ A — Make a specific offer
**🧠 Architect:** Clean. Recognition lands. "Let me know if you need anything" is the anti-pattern, and this card names it precisely.
**🏛 Regent:** This is the baseline. Everything else in the suit is commentary on this card. If you can make a specific offer, you can do the rest.
**⚔️ Challenger:** Too obvious? "Make a specific offer" is what every allyship training says. Does this card actually teach anything new, or does it just name what everyone already knows they should do?
**📖 Sage:** The move is right but the recognition prompt is the teaching. "Did you just say let me know if you need anything" is the insight — not the move. The move is remedial.

---

### ♠️ 2 — Check your capacity first
**🧠 Architect:** Good. "Not what you wish you could" is the knife edge. Most people offer what they wish they had, not what they have.
**🏛 Regent:** The capacity check is underrated. Most allyship collapses here — not in the intent, in the execution. This card addresses the actual failure mode.
**⚔️ Challenger:** ♠️ 2 and ♠️ A are sequential. A = make the offer. 2 = check before you make it. This means ♠️ A is incomplete without ♠️ 2. That's fine structurally, but it means ♠️ A alone is not the full move.
**🌊 Shaman:** "Spoons" language is already inside-culture. It's not wrong — the psychographic already knows what spoons means. But it anchors to a specific community. Consider whether that matters for the general deck.

---

### ♠️ 3 — Do the thing, not the management
**🧠 Architect:** "Managing the help vs. doing the help" is the sharp distinction. This card does real diagnostic work.
**🎭 Diplomat:** The card implies that managing help is bad. But sometimes managing help IS what someone needs — triage, coordination, prioritization. Is this card too harsh on a legitimate mode?
**⚔️ Challenger:** "Managing help is a form of control" — this is a strong claim. Is it always true? Could managing help sometimes be the exact right response?
**📖 Sage:** This is the card most likely to produce guilt in the reader. "Have I been managing?" becomes the question, not "did the help land?" The outcome got lost in the critique of the method.

---

### ♠️ 4 — Send them to someone who can
**🧠 Architect:** "Name the person. Make the introduction." This is the most specific move in the whole suit. It's also the one that requires the most social capital from the ally. That tension is worth holding.
**🏛 Regent:** The handoff is the move. This card names something most allyship frameworks skip — the ally who knows their limits AND has a network is more useful than the ally who overextends alone.
**⚔️ Challenger:** The failure mode here is "send them to someone" without making the introduction. People do this constantly — "you should talk to so-and-so" and leave it at that. The card says make the introduction. Does it name what a real introduction looks like?
**🎭 Diplomat:** This card is networking framed as allyship. That's not wrong, but it requires the ally to have social capital. What does this card offer the ally who doesn't have a network to send people to?

---

### ♠️ 5 — Ask what matters more than advise what to do
**🧠 Architect:** "The question is the bridge. The advice is the dessert." This is the best single line in the suit. It should be on the card, not just in the bridge.
**🏛 Regent:** This card addresses the Fixer reflex directly. The Fixer in the ally gets triggered, wants to solve, and this card redirects to asking. It's the right intervention.
**⚔️ Challenger:** "When asking would serve them better" — the word "would" does a lot of work here. This card should be clearer about when asking IS the right move vs. when acting is. Otherwise it's just another "always ask more" card.
**🌊 Shaman:** The recognition prompt ("Are you about to tell them what to do") pre-judges the answer. The ally already knows they're about to advise when they draw this card. The card needs to interrupt the loop, not name that the loop exists.

---

### ♠️ 6 — Name the one next thing
**🧠 Architect:** "The next thing, not the whole list, not the right thing." This is elegant constraint. Good cards do one thing. This one forces the ally to do one thing.
**🏛 Regent:** Clarity as a form of care — this framing works. The ally who can cut fog is giving capacity, not just advice.
**⚔️ Challenger:** "The ONE next thing" — what if the ally and the person disagree about which thing is next? Does this card work when there's genuine disagreement about priority?
**📖 Sage:** The failure mode is that the ally names the next thing from their frame, not from the target's frame. "The next thing" is only useful if it's the next thing from the person's own perspective. Does this card ensure that?

---

### ♠️ 7 — Help them do, not just understand
**🧠 Architect:** The recognition prompt is the diagnostic. "Have you been explaining" is a knife. Understanding is not doing. This card names that cleanly.
**🏛 Regent:** This card will land differently depending on what someone's actual strength is. Some allies are explainers. Some are doers. This card assumes the ally is an explainer who needs to learn to do. What about the ally who is already a doer?
**⚔️ Challenger:** "Explaining" vs. "doing" — is this the right binary? Sometimes explaining IS the doing. Sometimes the person needs a framework before they can act. Is this card anti-intellectual?
**🎭 Diplomat:** The card assumes the problem is that the ally is stuck in planning. But sometimes the person genuinely needs planning before they can do. Does this card work when planning IS the doing?

---

### ♠️ 8 — Ask what would actually help
**🧠 Architect:** "What does support actually look like for you" is the signature question from the interview. It's the card that most directly operationalizes what a good ally does.
**🏛 Regent:** This card is underweighted for what it does. "The question the person wishes everyone had asked" is a strong endorsement. This should be one of the anchor cards in the suit.
**⚔️ Challenger:** The card says "listen more than you talk." That's a constraint, not a move. What exactly does the ally do with the answer? This card ends at listening. The previous card (♠️ 7) ends at doing. Neither card closes the loop.
**🌊 Shaman:** The recognition prompt ("Have you been assuming you know what they need") will feel accusing to some readers. The ally who draws this card may feel defensive before they feel helped. Consider softening the recognition prompt.

---

### ♠️ 9 — Follow through without being chased
**🧠 Architect:** The failure mode is named precisely. "Did they have to check in on you since" — this is the exact experience from the interview. Good diagnostic.
**🏛 Regent:** Trust is built in the follow-through. This card names the actual mechanic: not the promise, the follow-through.
**⚔️ Challenger:** "Without being asked" is doing a lot of work. The card assumes that being chased is the failure. But sometimes checking in is appropriate — when the task is long, when context changes, when the person genuinely wants accountability. Is all chasing bad?
**🌊 Shaman:** The emotional tone of this card is guilt-forward. The ally who draws it will feel reproached. Consider whether there's a version that names the same failure mode without producing shame.

---

### ♠️ 10 — Name the walk-away price
**🧠 Architect:** "An ally who knows their walk-away price has a price worth staying for." This line should be on the card. It's the thesis of the entire suit.
**🏛 Regent:** This card will be hard for the target psychographic. The Enneagram 2 wing wants to stay past the point of integrity. This card is asking them to name the thing that makes them leave — and that requires them to have already accepted that leaving is allowed.
**⚔️ Challenger:** "Past the point where it's actually working" — who decides when that point is reached? The ally? The person being helped? What if they disagree about whether it's still working?
**📖 Sage:** The card names the walk-away price but doesn't say what to do after you name it. Do you leave? Do you stay and renegotiate? Does naming the price mean you've already left emotionally? The card opens something it doesn't close.

---

### ♠️ J — What do you actually need from me?
**🧠 Architect:** Clean. Direct. The recognition prompt is the teaching. "Without asking" names the failure mode.
**🏛 Regent:** This is the activation card. Something just happened, the ally is in reactive mode, and this card asks them to stop and name what's actually needed. Good placement in the sequence.
**⚔️ Challenger:** "Nothing else" is a strong constraint. What if what they need IS the advice the ally was about to give? Does this card over-restrict the ally's options?
**🎭 Diplomat:** The card assumes the ally can receive the answer without defending. Some allies will hear "what I need from you is different from what you were about to do" and take it personally. This card needs a bridge that normalizes that the answer might not be what the ally expected.

---

### ♠️ Q — Is this actually what they needed?
**🧠 Architect:** "Without defending" is the discipline. The ally who can hear "that didn't help" without needing to explain why they tried is the ally who can actually learn.
**🏛 Regent:** This card closes the feedback loop. ♠️ J asked what was needed. ♠️ Q asks whether what was done actually landed. These two together are a mini-cycle.
**⚔️ Challenger:** The ally who asks "did that actually help" and then gets "no" has to not-collapse. That's a real capacity requirement. Is there a card that builds toward that capacity before asking them to do this move?
**🌊 Shaman:** "Without collapsing" is too vague. Some allies will hear this and think they need to pretend it didn't hurt. The card should distinguish between collapsing (acting out the hurt) and being affected (feeling it without acting out).

---

### ♠️ K — One move that matters
**🧠 Architect:** The sovereign move. "Then make it" is the constraint that separates this from all the other "name the thing" cards.
**🏛 Regent:** This is the completion card. Everything before it was preparation. This is the actual action.
**⚔️ Challenger:** ♠️ K and ♠️ A are too close. "Name the one move that would actually change something" vs. "Make one specific offer." Both are about naming. The distinction needs to be sharper. What does K do that A doesn't?
**📖 Sage:** "At your best — with full capacity, full attention, full fire" — this is the somatic anchor. The card names the state before it names the move. Is that ordering right? Should the state be the move, or should the move come first and the state be the byproduct?

---

## Structural Issues (Architect + Challenger agree)

**Issue 1 — ♠️ A and ♠️ K are too close**
Both name. One names a specific offer. One names a sovereign move. The ally needs to feel why these are different cards. Right now they read as intensity escalation.

**Issue 2 — ♠️ 8 ends without closing the loop**
♠️ 8 asks what would help. Then what? The answer is received and then... the card ends. The ally has listened. The person has spoken. What happens next? ♠️ 9 follows through — but on what? There's a structural gap between asking and doing.

**Issue 3 — ♠️ 9 is guilt-forward**
The recognition prompt produces shame before it produces action. "Did they have to check in on you since" is a reproving question. The card should help the ally do better, not make them feel bad about what they already did.

## What Each Face Would Change

| Face | Change |

| 🏛 Regent | Move "an ally who knows their walk-away price" line to the card. Strengthen ♠️ 10. |
| ⚔️ Challenger | Soften ♠️ 9 recognition. Add "what does a real introduction look like" to ♠️ 4. |
| 🎭 Diplomat | Soften ♠️ 8 recognition. Clarify ♠️ 7 "explaining vs. doing" binary. |
| 🌊 Shaman | Remove "spoons" from ♠️ 2. Distinguish collapsing vs. being affected in ♠️ Q. |
| 📖 Sage | Sharpen what ♠️ K does that ♠️ A doesn't. Add what-happens-next to ♠️ 8. |

---

## The One Thing to Fix

**⚔️ Challenger's call:** ♠️ 9. It's the most practically useful card in the suit and the most emotionally tone-deaf. The recognition prompt needs to change from "did you fail" to "are you about to." Turn the guilt card into a preparation card.

**Current:** "Did they have to check in on you since?"
**Proposed:** "Did you say you'd do something — and haven't done it yet?"

One changes the emotional signature from shame to action. The other keeps the ally in the loop, not in the story about having failed.

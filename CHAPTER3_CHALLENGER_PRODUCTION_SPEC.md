## Altitude-Specific Locked Content

**Color:** Red
**Developmental name:** Socialized Power / Decisive Action
**Core thesis:** Allyship requires the willingness to be unwelcome. The clean "no." The drawn line.

### 5 Channels (modes of confrontation)
1. **The Line** — A boundary drawn. *"This is what I won't stand for."*
2. **The Interrupt** — Breaking a pattern mid-flight. *"We're not going to pretend this is normal."*
3. **The Demand** — Insisting on something. *"This has to change."*
4. **The Refusal** — The clean no. *"I will not."*
5. **The Cost** — Naming what it will cost to cross you. *"If you do X, here is what happens."*

### 4-Stage Sequence
**Charge → Aim → Act → Exit**
- **Charge:** The will arrives. Fire shows up in the body.
- **Aim:** You identify what this is actually about — underneath the story, what is the line being crossed?
- **Act:** You choose the form (Line / Interrupt / Demand / Refusal / Cost) and execute.
- **Exit:** You leave the confrontation cleanly. You don't need their reaction to validate your move.

### 5 Transcend Moves (+2 energy)
1. **Outrage → Righteous Act** — not venting; making a move that matters
2. **Fear-of-being-unwelcome → Willingness** — the fear becomes the fuel for showing up anyway
3. **Grief-in-the-fight → Moral Clarity** — the sadness under the anger becomes the anchor
4. **Protective-rage → Calm Defense** — fire for someone you love, held steady
5. **Defiance → Creation** — the "no" becomes a "yes" to something new

### 5 Generative Translate Moves (+1 energy)
- **Line → Interrupt**
- **Interrupt → Demand**
- **Demand → Refusal**
- **Refusal → Cost**

### 4 Control Translate Moves (−1 energy)
- **Charge → Swallow** (repression)
- **Charge → Performative outrage** (for the audience)
- **Charge → Passive-aggression** (will goes sideways)
- **Charge → Collapse** (implodes into shame)

### Vulnerable Child superpower (one sentence)
Moral clarity without cruelty — the willingness to be unwelcome held steady by love. The clean "no" that is not a performance and not a wound.

### 8 Gates at Red altitude
| Gate | Says | Job |
|------|------|-----|
| Protector | *Not safe to push. Last time you pushed, you lost something.* | Keep you from confrontation until it knows you won't be destroyed by it. |
| Controller | *If you let yourself be this angry, you'll go too far.* | Contain the charge so it doesn't leak. |
| Skeptic | *Is this really worth fighting for? Am I overreacting?* | Doubt your charge before someone else weaponizes it. |
| Fixer | *Don't fight — solve. There's a way to make this go away without confrontation.* | Convert charge into workaround. |
| Fear | *If you push, they'll retaliate. You'll lose the relationship.* | Rehearse the worst case. |
| Victim | *I can't push because I was punished for pushing.* | Keep the story alive of why you can't fight. |
| Damaged Self | *My anger is proof there's something wrong with me.* | Identify the charge as evidence of brokenness. |
| Vulnerable Child | *I want to be able to say no. I want to be unwelcome if that's what it takes.* | Hold the superpower. |

### Exile story (SIGNED OFF)
> The Challenger was exiled when the village got tired of conflicts only being resolved by who was the most powerful and angry. They were replaced by the Regent who built walled cities and created nations and traditions.

### Distortion story
Without the Challenger, the village got hostage to whoever was loudest. Conflict didn't disappear — it went underground. Resentment became the common language. *Be nice* became a tool of compliance. *Don't make a scene* became code for *don't say what is true.* And the one person who could still say "this is unacceptable" was cast as the problem they were trying to name.

### Bridge to next Face (Regent)
The Challenger can draw the line. The Challenger cannot *hold* the line across scale and time. Once a line is drawn, someone has to build the tradition that carries it forward — the structure, the role, the inheritance that lets the line survive past the moment of its drawing. That's the Regent.

## Structural Notes

### Section 4 — WAVE collision resolution
For Challenger's Section 4, use **WAVE** as a confrontation technique:
- **W**elcome the charge (don't suppress it)
- **A**im it (identify what it's actually about)
- **V**alidate the line (confirm what boundary is being crossed)
- **E**xit cleanly (execute the confrontation and leave)

This distinguishes it from the Shaman's WAVE (Welcome/Acknowledge/Validate/Exhale for emotional alchemy). The techniques share the acronym but are altitude-specific tools. Do NOT collapse them into one framework — they are different practices.

### Section 5 — Journey to Center
Follow the Ch 2 pattern: map the 8 Gates (brief), then an immersive second-person walk through them. The walk takes the reader *through* the gates to the Vulnerable Child. Write it as a guided experience, not an explanation.

### Section 6 — The Game
Ground in the Challenger's actual confrontation scenarios. 5 moves drawn from matrix:
1. Name the Unnameable
2. Draw the Line
3. Refuse Cleanly
4. Confront Without Cruelty
5. Stay Past the Discomfort

### Voice
Same voice as Ch 2 Shaman. Dry humor, Wendell cadence, first-person implied. Immersive for Section 5 walk. Concise for move descriptions in Section 6.

# Enrollment

You finished the walk. What you have now is a sheet with your own handwriting on
it, a quest you named in Chapter 1, and six Faces you can tell apart while a
moment is still happening.

This book could not watch you. Every page was written before you got here, so it
can name your patterns and never once catch you inside one. It handed you a way
to read a moment and no way to find out whether your read was right. The last
chapter asked you to design something, then closed before anyone could tell you
whether the design held.

Four doors sit on the other side of this book. Three of them answer those three
problems and the fourth is for when you cannot wait. They are not a ladder.
Nobody graduates from one into the next, the cheapest is the one most people use
longest, and taking more than one means you have more than one
problem to solve.

**The Allyship Deck.** A hundred and twenty cards. Wake Up, Open Up, Clean Up,
Grow Up, Show Up, crossed against gathering resources, raising awareness, direct
action and skillful organizing, run through all six Faces. You have met every one
of them, twenty at a time, in the chapters behind you.

Go back to a book in a hard week and you look up the Face you are already good
at. Everybody does, and it feels like study. A card you drew is a card you did
not pick, so it names the pattern you would not have gone looking for, in the
week you least wanted to hear about it. The private use comes first. The public
use is a table with other people at it.

*Not for you if* you want something to read. The deck does nothing until it is in
a hand during a week that is already hard.

**The Allyship Dojo.** The practice container, monthly and ongoing. Four tracks,
one per domain from Appendix A, each running the architecture you already know:
the alchemy first, then trainable skill, then Wake Up through Show Up, then a
quest that goes into your actual life.

This one exists because of a sentence the School of the Body puts in its own
admissions page, back in Chapter 3: *the instrument does not recalibrate in
private.* You can learn to read a moment from a book. You cannot learn from a
book that your read was wrong, and the read you are most confident about is the
one most worth checking. The Dojo is where you are wrong out loud, on a schedule, in front of the same faces, which is the only
method any of the six schools ever found for this.

This one meets the most common belief in the whole practice, that doing it alone
is what proves you were up to it. Nobody in this book got anywhere alone,
including the person who wrote it.

*Not for you if* what you want is more material. You already hold the material.

**The cohort.** A group, run in a season, by application rather than checkout.

Chapter 9 asked you to build something and then ran out of pages. Look at what it
asked for: put it in front of one person, take the note that costs you the
design, run it again with one thing changed, hand someone the pen. Not one of
those is a move you can make by yourself. A book can name the five moves of a
Player and it cannot be the person you put the work in front of, or the one who
gives you the note that costs you the design. So the cohort is a campaign of your
own, other people watching you run it, and the reflection that turns a run into a
practice. What comes out the far side is the capacity to design conditions where
other people can do this too.

*Not for you if* you have not yet run a move that cost you something. Come back
after. That is the only condition under which the money buys anything.

**Ninety minutes with me.** One session, one-on-one, for somebody with a live
situation that cannot wait for a season to open. You almost always need the Face
you have spent years avoiding by performing another, and catching yourself at
that alone is the hard part. That is Chapter 9's line and I have not found a
better one. You bring the thing that is actually happening rather than a case you
picked for its clarity.

*Not for you if* what you want is the method rather than this week's move. The
method is what the other three are for.

**Start with the deck.** It begins the moment you open the box, and inside a
month it will have told you which of the other three you actually need.

## What none of this is going to do

Every door here stays open. The cohort runs again, the prices are the prices, and
nothing expires at midnight. A practice you took on because a clock was
running is a practice you will drop the week the clock stops, and I would rather
have you in year three than this quarter.

The village was already playing before you finished this book. It does not need
your admiration and it is not waiting on your decision. It needs whatever you cut
the field down to in the last chapter, and it will still need it in March.

If none of the four is right, that is a legitimate outcome of reading this book.
You have the sheet. Go run the quest.

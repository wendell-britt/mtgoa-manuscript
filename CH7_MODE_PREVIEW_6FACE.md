# 6-Face GM — ch7's doubled mode preview

**Created:** 2026-08-01
**Question:** ch7 previews its five modes twice before Section 4 teaches them. Which form survives the redistribution?

---

## The measurement

Where each chapter first names its five:

```
ch3  :286      ch4  :247      ch5  :342      ch6  :257      ch8  :293      all Section 4
ch7  :89   ← Section 2, and again at :222
```

**ch7 is the only chapter that previews at all.** The other five name their instrument once, in Section 4, and teach it there.

And ch7 does it three times, not two:

| where | form | carries |
|---|---|---|
| `:77–79` §1 | verbs — Bridge, Translate, Hold, Repair, Negotiate | **the dependency chain** — *"Bridge without Translate is noise"* |
| `:89–99` §2 | nouns — Bridge-Builder, Translator, Field-Holder, Repairer, Integrative Negotiator | a definition each |
| `:222+` §4 | nouns again | 40–60 lines of technique each |

**Add the incoming Distortion and it becomes four encounters** with the same five before the reader finishes Section 4.

---

## 🌊 Shaman — Felt sense

Four passes at one list teaches a reader that lists in this chapter can be skimmed. She will be right, three times out of four, and then she will skim the deep-dives, which is where the technique actually lives.

**Shaman verdict:** the cost is not repetition, it is that she stops reading lists exactly where the lists start mattering.

---

## ⚔️ Challenger — What's padding

**The `:89` noun-list is the padding.** Five names with a definition each, then 130 lines later the same five names with deeper definitions. Nothing in the first survives the second.

**The chain is not padding.** *"Bridge without Translate is noise. Translate without Hold is a promise no one trusts."* says something no definition says — that the five have an order and each one fails without its predecessor.

**Challenger verdict:** cut the noun-list outright. Keep the chain.

---

## 🏛 Regent — What to preserve

**The chain.** With Section 6's move prerequisites, it is one of only two dependency structures in the manuscript, and the more readable of the two.

**The two-figure pairing** in Section 1 — the woman who over-gives beside the man who closes honestly. Unaffected by this decision, but it is what Section 1 becomes once the previews leave.

**Regent verdict:** the noun-list is fully recoverable from Section 4. Nothing else here is.

---

## 🧠 Architect — Structure

Against the rule adopted for the Distortion:

```
pre-teach    the five as absence, plain words        §2 Distortion
formal teach the five named, with technique          §4 Practice
```

**Two encounters. That is what ch3 does and it is the shape the rest of the book holds.** ch7's extra two are artifacts, and the chain is not a third preview — it is a *structural claim about the five*, which belongs adjacent to them rather than 140 lines upstream.

**Architect verdict:** noun-list deleted, chain relocated to open Section 4, Distortion carries the pre-teach.

---

## 🎭 Diplomat — What bridges

The reader contract. She meets five names with definitions in Section 2 and reasonably concludes she now knows them. Section 4 then tells her she does not. **A preview that gets superseded teaches the reader to distrust the first pass at anything** — and ch7 is the chapter she is most likely to think she already understands, because the Diplomat is her home Face.

**Diplomat verdict:** one pre-teach in plain words, one real teach. No glossary in between.

---

## 📖 Sage — Panoramic view

Ask why ch7 alone previews. The likeliest answer is the one already established: **ch7's opening pair was drafted outside the form**, which is the same finding as the missing Exile and Distortion headings. The previews are not a design decision that went wrong — they are more of the same drift, and they disappear when the form is restored.

Worth noting because it predicts something: if the redistribution is done properly, this question does not need a separate fix. **The noun-list has nowhere to go once Section 2 is the Distortion.**

**Sage verdict:** conform, and this resolves itself.

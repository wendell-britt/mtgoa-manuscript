## What the Draft Needs Mechanically

### Gap 1: Sadness is too submerged
**Fix:** One explicit grief paragraph before the pivot. Something like:
> *Somewhere in your memory is the moment you realized you'd been doing it wrong. Not because you were bad. Because you didn't have the map. That realization cost something. The cost is still there.*

This makes Sadness visible before we move to Excitement. Without this, the Joy at the end feels ungrounded.

### Gap 2: Peace is promised but not delivered
**Fix:** The section should end not just on determination, but on the beginning of Peace. One sentence that acknowledges the cost and names that it can be put down:
> *You haven't been failing. You've been carrying something that was never supposed to be yours to carry alone. You can put it down now.*

This gives the Triumph at the end a grounded quality — the reader has Triumph because they've been seen in their full experience, not because they're being rallied.

### Gap 3: The threshold moment (from earlier analysis)
**Fix:** Insert between "Most people turn back" and "This book is a map":
> *You know what this feels like. You've stood at the threshold. Something in you said not yet. Or said never. The voice that said it wasn't ready — it was trying to keep you safe. And it was right to be afraid. What it didn't know was that the way through was on the other side.*

This is the Protectors gate explicit naming moment — and it delivers the first real Peace because it makes the turning back make sense, not shameful.

## 3 Reads the Draft Should Produce

By the end of the section, the reader should have been through three distinct emotional experiences:

1. **Recognition** (Triumph-adjacent): *I know this. This is my experience.*
2. **Reckoning** (Sadness + Anger): *The map was wrong. I was set up to fail. I can grieve that.*
3. **Readiness** (Excitement + grounded Peace): *I know where I need to go. I'm ready to go there.*

If the section ends without #2 being fully landed, the reader is in Excitement without Peace — which means they'll be motivated but unstable. The urgency section must produce a reader who is both stirred up AND calm. Both angry AND at peace with that anger.

That's the mechanical standard we're holding.

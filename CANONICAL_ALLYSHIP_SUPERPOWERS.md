# Canonical Allyship Superpowers

**Status:** Canonical working reference for the MTGOA manuscript, quiz, appendix drafting, and AI-assisted editorial work.  
**Purpose:** Resolve terminology collisions when the Obsidian library is not available. This file is the repository-local source of truth for the superpower taxonomy.

## The Canonical Model

An **allyship superpower** is a distinctive capacity a person brings to allyship: a pattern of perception, practiced gift, survival intelligence, and preferred way of creating leverage. It is neither a moral rank nor a fixed identity. A superpower can be practiced cleanly, overused as a reflex, or distorted by fear and self-protection.

The primary taxonomy has **six superpowers**:

1. Connector
2. Strategist
3. Disruptor
4. Escape Artist
5. Alchemist
6. Storyteller

**Coach** is a cross-cutting, integrative practice. The Coach borrows from the six primary superpowers to help another person reclaim agency and leave an outgrown level or expired story. Coach is not a seventh primary territory and must not be a seventh forced-choice quiz result. If a product later scores Coach, treat it as a secondary capacity or modifier, not a replacement for the six.

## Non-Negotiable Distinctions

Do not collapse these systems into one another.

| System | What it answers | Examples | Not the same as |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Superpower** | What distinctive leverage does this person bring? | Connector, Strategist, Alchemist | Face, Role, Domain, or Five-Move stage |
| **Face** | What mentor-guide / interior territory is teaching the reader? | Shaman, Challenger, Regent, Architect, Diplomat, Sage | A superpower |
| **Role** | What function does a group rely on this person to perform? | Rookies, Veterans, Leaders, Engineers; role labels vary by manuscript surface | A superpower |
| **Allyship Domain** | Where is the action happening? | Gather Resources, Skillful Organizing, Direct Action, Raise Awareness | A superpower |
| **Five-Move BAR Flow** | What practice move is needed now? | Wake Up, Open Up, Clean Up, Grow Up, Show Up | A superpower |

The six Face chapters are therefore **not** one chapter per superpower. A reader can be, for example, an Escape Artist practicing in a Shaman chapter, or a Strategist working in the Domain of Direct Action.

## The Six Primary Superpowers

### Connector — the Webweaver

**Core gift:** Senses the invisible threads between people, ideas, resources, and opportunities; builds trust, belonging, translation, and the bridges that let collaboration happen.

**Clean use:** Makes a consent-aware connection at the right time, with enough context and no pressure. Listens beneath stated needs, bridges across groups, and creates conditions for others to meet without taking ownership of the outcome.

**Central dilemma:** Connection work is essential yet often invisible or dismissed as "just talking." The Connector can become responsible for holding every relationship together.

**Shadow patterns:** Over-connecting without consent; confusing access with intimacy; mediating or exposing people who did not ask; becoming the social glue at personal cost.

**Useful prompt:** *Who needs a bridge, and what consent, context, and protection would make it safe?*

### Strategist — the System Seer

**Core gift:** Sees patterns, future consequences, hidden constraints, leverage points, power dynamics, and the sequence that lets a group move well.

**Clean use:** Makes the smallest useful plan legible to the people who must carry it; designs around real constraints without treating people as pieces; builds trust before the warning or plan is urgently needed.

**Central dilemma:** When prevention works, it can look unnecessary. Foresight is often received as control, paranoia, or manipulation.

**Shadow patterns:** Overdesigning instead of acting; analysis paralysis; optimizing people rather than partnering with them; trying to control outcomes when trust is missing.

**Useful prompt:** *What is the smallest sequence or leverage point that changes the board while preserving people's agency?*

### Disruptor — the Sacred Spark

**Core gift:** Interrupts false inevitability, harmful silence, stuck patterns, and compliance with systems that need to change.

**Clean use:** Names the unspoken truth with care; targets harmful structures rather than humiliating people; chooses a winnable intervention, builds allies, and makes room for repair and what comes next.

**Central dilemma:** Necessary challenge is often experienced as aggression or chaos. The Disruptor can burn too hot or silence their own fire after backlash.

**Shadow patterns:** The **Chaos Bringer** burns indiscriminately and mistakes rupture for change. The **Caged Rebel** withdraws, complains, and waits for permission rather than acting.

**Useful prompt:** *What is pretending to be natural, and what precise interruption would open a real next step?*

### Escape Artist — the Framebreaker

**Core gift:** Finds exits, loopholes, routes, options, and sovereignty under constraint. Sees a cage before it becomes normalized.

**Clean use:** Distinguishes a real trap from ordinary difficulty; creates a deliberate, ethical exit that restores choice without abandoning responsibility or dragging others.

**Central dilemma:** People fear freedom, and necessary departure may be read as quitting. The Escape Artist can also mistake every container for captivity.

**Shadow patterns:** The **Ghost** flees at the first discomfort and cannot commit. The **Martyr** stays too long, mistakes endurance for impact, and tries to save a system at personal cost.

**Useful prompt:** *Is this a cage, a challenge, or an open door I have not fully stepped through?*

### Alchemist — the Emotional Transmuter

**Core gift:** Helps individuals and groups move emotional energy into flow: fear toward clarity, anger toward purposeful momentum, and sadness toward poignance, meaning, and joy.

**Clean use:** Witnesses before redirecting; creates containers for shared processing, ritual, movement, art, rest, repair, and renewed action. The Alchemist does not force an emotion to change or make pain perform transformation.

**Central dilemma:** People can offload their grief, fear, and rage onto the Alchemist, resist what must be felt, or confuse emotional discomfort with harm.

**Shadow patterns:** The **Emotional Overload** absorbs collective pain until burned out. The **Detached Observer** intellectualizes emotion from a safe distance and never enters the current.

**Useful prompt:** *What emotion is stuck, what is it protecting, and what shared container would let it move without anyone drowning in it?*

### Storyteller — the Meaning Weaver

**Core gift:** Shapes meaning, memory, morale, narrative, and public imagination. Recognizes the story organizing behavior and offers a truer frame for action.

**Clean use:** Reveals rather than controls; protects authorship and consent; uses emotion to illuminate reality rather than replace it; tells a story that expands choice without flattening complexity.

**Central dilemma:** People often defend the narratives that organize their identity, even when those narratives keep them small or normalize harm.

**Shadow patterns:** The **Manipulator** exaggerates or bends truth for impact. The **Lost Author** cedes the narrative to fear, backlash, or harmful dominant stories.

**Useful prompt:** *What story is organizing behavior, whose authorship matters, and what truthful reframe would create more agency?*

## Coach — Cross-Cutting Integration Practice

**Core work:** Sees the next level a person is avoiding, makes the current plateau honest, and returns agency to its owner. The Coach speaks to the person one level up without carrying them there.

**Clean use:** Offers a timely, consent-aware challenge; identifies the expired story or outgrown level; names the smallest brave next move; and gives the person credit for their own breakthrough.

**Boundaries:** Coaching is not rescuing, coercion, or generic affirmation. Do not use a coaching move when the person first needs safety, emotional processing, material support, or an exit from a harmful system.

**Shadow patterns:** The **Taskmaster** imposes their timeline and creates dependence. The **Empty Cheerleader** affirms everything and avoids the truthful challenge.

**Useful prompt:** *Is this their next level or mine—and what would help them choose their own climb?*

## Quiz and Chapter Rules

### Quiz rules

- The quiz must use the six primary superpowers only: Connector, Strategist, Disruptor, Escape Artist, Alchemist, Storyteller.
- It should describe a **current preferred form of leverage**, not diagnose personality or assign a permanent identity.
- A result should name a primary pattern and its clean use, likely shadow, and a practical next experiment.
- It may name one or more secondary affinities. It must not imply that a reader lacks the other five capacities.
- Coach may appear as a follow-up capacity or practice recommendation, never as a seventh primary type.

### Chapter rules

- Do not relabel a Face chapter as a superpower chapter unless the manuscript architecture explicitly changes.
- A Face chapter may use any superpower as an example or reader practice, but it must state the distinction when ambiguity is likely.
- Do not map Superpower → Face, Superpower → Domain, or Superpower → Role one-to-one.
- Use superpower language as an invitation to notice leverage and responsibility, never as a moral ranking or a license to act without consent.

## Appendix-Ready Short Form

**Allyship Superpower** — A distinctive form of leverage a person brings to allyship, shaped by practiced gifts, lived experience, and playstyle. The six primary superpowers are Connector, Strategist, Disruptor, Escape Artist, Alchemist, and Storyteller. They are not fixed identities, moral ranks, Roles, Faces, Domains, or stages of the Five-Move BAR Flow. Each has a clean use and a shadow when driven by fear or overuse. **Coach** is a cross-cutting practice that helps another person reclaim agency; it is not a seventh primary quiz type.

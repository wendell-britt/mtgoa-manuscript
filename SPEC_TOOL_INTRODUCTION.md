# SPEC: Reader-Facing Tools — Introduction Principles
## The Book's Pedagogical Architecture

**Purpose:** Define how the book introduces, scaffolds, and reinforces tools and practices that live inside the reader's allyship life — not just in their head.

**Why this matters:** The book teaches transformation, not information. A reader who knows about 3-2-1 but has never done it doesn't have a practice. A reader who has done it three times does. The book's job is to move readers from knowing to practicing. This spec defines the architectural rules that make that happen.

## The Two Modes

Every moment in the book is in one of two modes. The transition between them must be signaled clearly.

| Mode | What it is | Reader posture | Text signals |
|------|-----------|---------------|-------------|
| **Concept mode** | The text is teaching about something | Reading, following, understanding | Explanation prose, third-person voice, analysis |
| **Practice mode** | The text is asking the reader to do something | Stopping, reflecting, writing, feeling | Second-person direct address, command forms, boxed exercises, first-person guide voice |

**The rule:** When entering practice mode, the text must say so explicitly. Don't ask the reader to practice without warning. Don't assume they'll self-direct.

**Example of wrong:** *"The 3-2-1 process helps you find your shadow. Try it now."* ← No scaffolding, no commitment ask, no structure.

**Example of right:** *"Before you read further — stop. This is a practice, not a paragraph. You'll need about ten minutes and somewhere you won't be interrupted."* ← Explicit entry, time commitment named, conditions set.

## Tool Categories

The book has four categories of reader-facing tools. Each has its own introduction protocol.

| Category | What it is | Examples | Introduction protocol |
|----------|-----------|----------|---------------------|
| **A — Somatic practice** | Something the reader does in their body | WAVE-Somatic, breathing, the Body Vote | Must be introduced with conditions (environment, time, safety). Never introduced mid-paragraph. |
| **B — Reflection framework** | Something the reader does on paper or in their head | 3-2-1, BARs, Character Sheet, the 8 Gates walk | Must be introduced with a boxed exercise. Must name what they're writing or reflecting on. |
| **C — Conceptual tool** | A lens the reader uses to see with | WAVE-Spiral, the 5 channels, the three game types, Polarity Map | Introduced in concept mode first. Practice mode comes only after understanding is established. |
| **D — Operational system** | Something that structures the reader's ongoing practice | Token/Ticket System, the Character Sheet | Introduced early (Ch0–Ch1). Referenced throughout without re-explanation. |

**Key rule by category:**
- **A tools** → Always enter practice mode with explicit conditions. Reader must know what their body needs before they're asked to feel something.
- **B tools** → Always have a boxed exercise format. Name the prompts explicitly. Give a completion condition.
- **C tools** → Concept mode first (understand), then practice mode (experience). Never skip the concept establishment.
- **D tools** → Introduce once, reference perpetually. The first mention must be memorable and complete.

## The Three Introduction Phases

Every tool goes through three phases in the book. Skip or compress at your own risk.

### Phase 1 — The Encounter

**Where:** Concept mode, usually in the chapter's first sections.

**What happens:** The tool is named. Its purpose is explained. Its relationship to the chapter's core capacity is established. The reader understands what it's for before they're asked to use it.

**Format:** Prose explanation, first mention bold, no exercise yet.

**Example:** *"The 3-2-1 process is how you find what's running you before it finds you. It's not a therapy method — it's a projection practice. You look at a charge you're carrying, and you ask: who's carrying this outside of me? Then: who's carrying this inside of me? Then: who am I becoming if I let this teach me?"*

**The test:** Can the reader explain the tool back in their own words before they're asked to practice it? If not, Phase 1 is incomplete.

---

### Phase 2 — The First Practice

**Where:** The chapter's practice section (usually Section 4 or 5).

**What happens:** The reader does the tool. For the first time. With scaffolding.

**Must include:**
1. **Entry signal** — explicit transition into practice mode
2. **Conditions** — what the reader needs (time, space, writing tool, safety)
3. **Step-by-step** — numbered or bulleted, clear commands
4. **Anchoring example** — one concrete instance of the practice (not abstract description)
5. **Exit signal** — explicit transition back to concept mode

**Format:** Boxed exercise (bordered, indented, distinct typography from body prose).

**The test:** Can a reader who's never done the tool complete it by following the boxed instructions alone? If they need to re-read surrounding prose to understand what to do, the exercise is underspecified.

---

### Phase 3 — The Reference

**Where:** Any section after Phase 2 is complete.

**What happens:** The tool is referenced by name with an implicit "you know this one." No re-explanation. No boxed format. A shorthand reference that the initiated reader recognizes.

**Example:** *"Before you do 3-2-1 on that gate, check which channel it's sitting in. That'll tell you what kind of teaching is on the other side."*

**The test:** Does the reader who completed Phase 2 know exactly what's being referenced? If not, Phase 2 was underspecified.

## Placement Rules

### Rule 1: Never introduce a tool in the last two sections of a chapter

The book's rhythm is: establish → practice → integrate → transition. By Section 6–7, the reader is already moving toward the next chapter. Tools introduced too late don't have time to land.

**The fix:** Introduce in Sections 2–4. Practice in Section 4–5. Reference in Sections 6–7.

### Rule 2: One new tool per chapter maximum

The reader can hold one new practice at a time. Introducing WAVE-Spiral, 3-2-1, and BARs all in Ch1 is cognitive overload. Each chapter owns one new tool. Everything else is reference.

**Exception:** B-tier tools (concepts the reader learns but doesn't practice) can be introduced two per chapter if they're clearly marked as "understanding tools" vs "practice tools."

### Rule 3: The tool must earn its place in the chapter's arc

Before adding a tool, ask: *What does this tool do for the reader that the chapter's core capacity can't do alone?*

If the answer is "it gives them something to practice," proceed. If the answer is "it makes the chapter feel more complete," reconsider.

### Rule 4: Name the tool's relationship to the chapter's core capacity

The reader must understand why this tool lives in this chapter and not another. The tool should be the natural expression of the chapter's face capacity — not a parallel track.

**Example:** 3-2-1 belongs in the Shaman chapter because the Shaman's capacity is feeling fully. The 3-2-1 process is how you feel fully by finding what's running you. They're the same practice at different scales.

### Rule 5: Boxed exercises must be skimmable

Some readers won't do the exercise. That's their choice. Boxed exercises must still give these readers the key insight — they should be able to skim the box and get the essential teaching without doing the practice. The exercise is an offer, not a requirement.

## Signal Language Reference

### Entering practice mode

| Weak | Strong |
|------|--------|
| *"Try this."* | *"Stop. Before you read further — do this."* |
| *"You might want to..."* | *"You'll need about ten minutes. Find somewhere you won't be interrupted."* |
| *"Here's what to do."* | *"This is a practice. Not a paragraph. Here's what to do:"* |
| *"Notice what happens."* | *"Pause after each step. Notice what your body does before your mind catches up."* |

### Naming a practice section

| Weak | Strong |
|------|--------|
| *"How to Practice"* | *"Stop and Do This — Before You Read Further"* |
| *"Practice"* | *"Your First 3-2-1"* |
| *"Exercises"* | *"Walk the Gate"* |

### Referencing a tool later (Phase 3)

| Weak | Strong |
|------|--------|
| *"Remember the 3-2-1 process we talked about..."* | *"Before you do 3-2-1..."* ← assumes Phase 2 complete |
| *"As we discussed in Chapter 2..."* | *"'The Skeptic' — you know the one."* ← shorthand, intimate |
| *"Going back to the WAVE-Spiral..."* | *"WAVE first. Always."* ← rule-text, no recap needed |

# Section Variance Examples
## *How the Same Section Looks Completely Different Across Faces*

This document shows what Section 3 (The Concept), Section 4 (The Practice), and Section 5 (The Journey) look like when written for different faces. This is to prevent the template from creating "Shaman but for Challengers" — each face has a radically different practice to teach.

---

## Section 3: The Concept

**What's constant:** Opens with "Let's be clear about what we mean by..." Teaches the philosophy/theory of this face's practice. Contrasts wrong with right. ~700 words.

**What's completely different:** The *actual practice* being taught.

### Shaman's Section 3 Opening
> Let's be clear about what we mean by emotional alchemy.
>
> It is not emotional processing. It's not thinking your way through a feeling or analyzing what it means. That's useful, but that's not alchemy.
>
> Emotional alchemy is the practice of being in relationship with feeling, and letting those feelings teach you something that changes who you are.

### Challenger's Section 3 Opening (Example)
> Let's be clear about what we mean by clean confrontation.
>
> It is not domination. It's not crushing someone because you have more power. That's useful to know you could do, but that's not what we're talking about.
>
> Clean confrontation is the practice of saying "no" or "this is not acceptable" without needing the other person's permission, and without needing them to understand or agree.

### Regent's Section 3 Opening (Example)
> Let's be clear about what we mean by true stewardship.
>
> It is not obedience. It's not surrendering your thinking to a tradition because the tradition is old. That happens sometimes, but that's not stewardship.
>
> True stewardship is the practice of inheriting something, asking honestly whether it still serves, reforming it if it doesn't, and passing it on — and then being willing to be forgotten.

### Architect's Section 3 Opening (Example)
> Let's be clear about what we mean by structural design.
>
> It is not complexity. It's not building the biggest, most elaborate system. That sometimes gets confused with design, but design is the opposite.
>
> Structural design is the practice of seeing the pattern in what's broken, and changing the system's incentives so that the right outcome becomes the easy outcome for everyone who uses it.

### Diplomat's Section 3 Opening (Example)
> Let's be clear about what we mean by holding multiple truths.
>
> It is not false equivalence. It's not "both sides have a point" about everything. Some things are wrong. Some people are wrong. That's not what this is.
>
> Holding multiple truths is the practice of knowing exactly where you stand, and still making room for someone else to stand somewhere different, and still being in relationship with them.

### Sage's Section 3 Opening (Example)
> Let's be clear about what we mean by panoramic vision.
>
> It is not detachment. It's not stepping outside of the game so you don't have to play. That's what people sometimes think wisdom is, but it's a grave.
>
> Panoramic vision is the practice of seeing all the altitudes at once and still choosing to stand somewhere, still choosing to make a move, still choosing to be committed.

## Section 4: The Practice

**What's constant:** Structure (native material → 5 channels → 4-stage sequence → 3 move types → flow cycle → 2+ in-practice scenarios). ~2500 words.

**What's completely different:** Every single element is face-specific.

### Shaman's Section 4 Structure

```
The native material: Emotion

The 5 emotional channels:
- Metal / Fear (detects threat)
- Water / Sadness (points to loss)
- Wood / Joy (shows alignment)
- Fire / Anger (identifies boundary)
- Earth / Neutrality (holds perspective)

The stage sequence: Wake → Clean → Grow → Show
- Wake: The feeling arrives in your body
- Clean: You witness the feeling without judgment
- Grow: You alchemize it — it teaches you something
- Show: You embody the new information

The named moves:
- Transcend moves (+2 energy): Fear→Excitement, Sadness→Poignancy, Joy→Bliss, Anger→Triumph, Neutrality→Peace
- Generative Translate (+1 energy): Joy→Anger, Anger→Clarity, etc.
- Control Translate (-1 energy): Charge→Swallow, Charge→Performative outrage, etc.

The flow cycle: Joy → Anger → Neutrality → Fear → Sadness → Joy

In-practice scenario 1: The 10-second WAVE in the middle of a conversation
In-practice scenario 2: A full emotional journey across a day
```

### Challenger's Section 4 Structure (Example)

```
The native material: Will / Charge / Force

The 5 confrontation channels:
- The Line (a boundary drawn)
- The Interrupt (breaking a pattern)
- The Demand (insisting on something)
- The Refusal (the clean no)
- The Cost (naming what it will cost to cross you)

The stage sequence: Charge → Aim → Act → Exit
- Charge: The will arrives in your body
- Aim: You identify what this is actually about
- Act: You execute the form (Line/Interrupt/Demand/Refusal/Cost)
- Exit: You leave the confrontation cleanly

The named moves:
- Transcend moves (+2 energy): Outrage→Righteous Act, Fear-of-unwelcome→Willingness, Grief-in-fight→Moral Clarity, etc.
- Generative Translate (+1 energy): Line→Interrupt, Interrupt→Demand, Demand→Refusal, etc.
- Control Translate (-1 energy): Charge→Swallow, Charge→Performative outrage, Charge→Passive-aggression, etc.

The flow cycle: Charge → Aim → Push → Rest → Repair → Charge

In-practice scenario 1: How to name something in a meeting that nobody is saying
In-practice scenario 2: A full confrontation arc — from realization to exit
```

### Regent's Section 4 Structure (Example)

```
The native material: Loyalty / Duty / Inheritance / Role

The 5 stewardship channels:
- The Custodian (caring for what exists)
- The Inheritor (receiving what was passed down)
- The Teacher (passing it on in a form the next person can receive)
- The Reformer (changing the tradition while honoring the lineage)
- The Keeper of Vows (honoring commitments across time)

The stage sequence: Inherit → Honor → Steward → Pass
- Inherit: You receive what was handed to you — gift and damage both
- Honor: You name what in the inheritance still serves
- Steward: You carry it, maintain it, reform it
- Pass: You hand it on cleanly to whoever comes next

The named moves:
- Transcend moves (+2 energy): Obedience→True allegiance, Duty→Service, Rigidity→Integrity, etc.
- Generative Translate (+1 energy): Custodian→Reformer, Inheritor→Teacher, Teacher→Keeper of Vows, etc.
- Control Translate (-1 energy): Loyalty→Obedience, Duty→Martyrdom, Tradition→Dogma, etc.

The flow cycle: Inherit → Honor → Steward → Reform → Pass → Inherit (generational, slower rhythm)

In-practice scenario 1: How to audit a tradition you inherited
In-practice scenario 2: How to repair what was broken without rejecting the lineage
```

### Architect's Section 4 Structure (Example)

```
The native material: Logic / Leverage / System / Design

The 5 design channels:
- The Mapmaker (seeing the whole system at once)
- The Engineer (building the working version)
- The Strategist (identifying where to push for maximum movement)
- The Inventor (creating what didn't exist)
- The Optimizer (making what works work better)

The stage sequence: Observe → Model → Design → Deploy
- Observe: What is actually happening in this system?
- Model: Build the simplest explanation that accounts for the behavior
- Design: Create the intervention that changes the system's incentives
- Deploy: Ship it into contact with reality and observe again

The named moves:
- Transcend moves (+2 energy): Complexity→Elegance, Confusion→Clarity, Constraint→Design criterion, etc.
- Generative Translate (+1 energy): Mapmaker→Strategist, Engineer→Optimizer, Inventor→Mapmaker, etc.
- Control Translate (-1 energy): Design→Over-design, Strategy→Scheming, System→Systematization-for-comfort, etc.

The flow cycle: Observe → Model → Design → Deploy → Observe (new state) → Model (new model) → ...

In-practice scenario 1: How to find the leverage point in a broken system
In-practice scenario 2: How to ship a design before it's perfect
```

---

**The point:** Section 4 has the same *structure* but completely different *content* for each face. The reader learns a native practice at each altitude.

## Section 5: The Journey — How the 8 Gates Change Per Altitude

**What's constant:** Same 8 gate names, same order, two-pass structure (Map + Walk), same moves-per-gate gradient.

**What's completely different:** What each gate *says* and *protects* at this altitude, and what the Vulnerable Child holds at the center.

### The 8 Gate Names (Constant Across All Faces)
1. Protector
2. Controller
3. Skeptic
4. Fixer
5. Fear
6. Victim
7. Damaged Self
8. Vulnerable Child

### How Gate 1 (Protector) Changes Per Face

**Shaman's Protector (at Magenta altitude):**
- Says: *Not safe to feel. Last time you felt, you were destroyed.*
- Job: Keep you from feeling until you're resourced enough to survive it.

**Challenger's Protector (at Red altitude):**
- Says: *Not safe to push. Last time you pushed, you lost something.*
- Job: Keep you from confrontation until you know you won't be destroyed by it.

**Regent's Protector (at Amber altitude):**
- Says: *Not safe to take on this responsibility. You don't have the standing. Who are you to hold this?*
- Job: Keep you from claiming a role you can't yet carry.

**Architect's Protector (at Orange altitude):**
- Says: *Not safe to commit to this blueprint. You don't have enough information. Wait. Gather more data.*
- Job: Keep you from shipping before you're ready.

**Diplomat's Protector (at Green altitude):**
- Says: *Not safe to enter this room. These people will ask you to choose, and you can't. Stay outside.*
- Job: Keep you from entering relational fields that will cost you.

**Sage's Protector (at Teal altitude):**
- Says: *Not safe to share what you see. People won't understand. You'll sound crazy. Keep the seeing private.*
- Job: Keep the Sage's vision hidden so the Sage doesn't get ostracized.

---

### How Gate 5 (Fear) Changes Per Face

**Shaman's Fear:**
- Says: *If you feel this fully, it will consume you. You'll break.*
- Job: Rehearse the worst emotional outcome.

**Challenger's Fear:**
- Says: *If you push, they'll retaliate. You'll lose the relationship / the job / the access.*
- Job: Rehearse the worst social outcome.

**Regent's Fear:**
- Says: *If you commit to this duty, you'll be trapped. You'll die inside a role and never have been yourself.*
- Job: Rehearse the cage of the role.

**Architect's Fear:**
- Says: *If this design fails, it fails publicly. Your reputation is tied to it.*
- Job: Rehearse the public failure.

**Diplomat's Fear:**
- Says: *If you stay in this conversation, someone will be hurt. Someone will leave. You'll be held responsible.*
- Job: Rehearse the rupture as catastrophe.

**Sage's Fear:**
- Says: *If you see all of this, you become responsible for all of it. You'll never put it down again.*
- Job: Rehearse the burden of the meta-view.

---

### How Gate 8 (Vulnerable Child) Superpower Changes Per Face

**Shaman's Vulnerable Child:**
- *I want to be able to feel. I want to know what's true in my body.*
- **Superpower:** Your capacity to feel deeply and let that feeling teach you.

**Challenger's Vulnerable Child:**
- *I want to be able to say no. I want to stand for something. I want to be unwelcome if that's what it takes.*
- **Superpower:** Moral clarity without cruelty.

**Regent's Vulnerable Child:**
- *I want to belong to something larger than me. I want to inherit. I want to pass something on.*
- **Superpower:** Steady service that neither swallows you nor uses you up.

**Architect's Vulnerable Child:**
- *I love the elegant solution. I get chills at the beautiful blueprint. I know design is a form of love.*
- **Superpower:** Structural generosity.

**Diplomat's Vulnerable Child:**
- *I can still see the human in everyone. I believe connection is possible. I hold the open hand without collapsing.*
- **Superpower:** Honest multiplicity.

**Sage's Vulnerable Child:**
- *I want to be in the game AND see the game. I want to belong AND see.*
- **Superpower:** Embodied panoramic vision.

---

### How the Through-Line Changes Per Face

This is the thing all 8 gates are organized around at each altitude:

- **Shaman:** "Your capacity to feel."
- **Challenger:** "Your capacity to push."
- **Regent:** "Your capacity to serve something larger than yourself without losing yourself."
- **Architect:** "Your capacity to design clearly without losing sight of what the design is for."
- **Diplomat:** "Your capacity to stay in relationship across difference without losing yourself or betraying what you stand for."
- **Sage:** "Your capacity to see the whole without leaving the parts."

## The Point

**Every single element changes per face.**

But the **structure stays identical:**
- Same 7 sections
- Same 8 gates
- Same moves-per-gate gradient
- Same length targets
- Same voice

This is what allows the template to be consistent while each chapter feels completely different. The structure is the cage. The content is what makes it come alive.

---

## Key Insight for Drafters

When you're writing your chapter:

1. **Read the Shaman chapter for format** (how to structure a journey, what the voice sounds like, how to pace 10,000 words)
2. **Read MTGOA_PROPAGATION_MATRIX for your face's content** (native material, 5 channels, 4-stage sequence, 8 gates at your altitude)
3. **Read this document to see how the same sections look different** (so you don't accidentally make it "emotional alchemy for Challengers")
4. **Write your Section 3 completely fresh.** Don't template it. "Let's be clear about what we mean by [your face's actual practice]."
5. **Write your Section 4 completely fresh.** Use your native material, your channels, your stages.
6. **Write your Section 5 completely fresh.** The gates are the same, but everything else is different.

The template gives you the shape. Your face gives it the content. Don't confuse the two.

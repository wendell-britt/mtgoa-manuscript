# The Epigraphs — v2, paired

**2026-07-28.** Two per chapter. Rebuilt after the council found the same failure a fourth time:
**punchy but vague, because there are no proper nouns in them.**

---

## What changed

| v1 | v2 |
|---|---|
| aphorisms | incidents |
| attributed to cohorts (*second-years*) | attributed to **named people, named places** |
| implied nothing | implies an **archive** — Herbert's move |
| one voice: students | **two voices: student and beneficiary** |
| tone-setting | **structural opposition** |

**The rule stands and gets stricter:** an epigraph never inspires, and now it must also contain a
proper noun and a thing that happened.

**The beneficiary voice: specific, and whatever it actually is.**

*An earlier version of this file carried a hard constraint — "never grateful." **That rule was
wrong and is struck.** It was Green's shadow wearing a craft rule: refusing the win so nobody
could accuse us of claiming it, which is the exact pattern Ch7 diagnoses. It was also
condescending to the beneficiaries, deciding on their behalf that gratitude would be
unsophisticated. And a book that shows nine chapters of cost with nobody visibly helped does not
believe its own thesis, which leaves Jordan no reason to take a berth.*

**The real rule is the one that has been failing all along: be specific.** *They changed my life*
is a testimonial. *In a corridor, like it was the weather* is gratitude with a body in it. Same
emotion; only one of them is writing.

**And vary it, because that is true.** Some grateful, some ambivalent, one who still lost.

## CHAPTER 2 — The Forest

> *Tull walked me out and said nothing for an hour. At the treeline he said "you can stop here,"
> and I understood that he meant it, which is the only reason I did not stop.*
>
> — first walk, name not recorded

> *They came ashore and fixed nothing for eleven days. On the twelfth they did one thing, and the
> harbour has worked since. I do not know how they chose it. I have stopped needing to.*
>
> — Oreve, harbour records

## CHAPTER 3 — The Shaman · School of the Body

> *I said what the room felt like and I was wrong. Voss made me say the wrong one again the next
> morning, out loud, so I would know the difference in my mouth.*
>
> — Ilse Marrow, second-year, School of the Body

> *She said the word before I did. I had been carrying it eleven years and she said it in a
> corridor, like it was the weather.*
>
> — recorded at Sethen, third visit

---

## CHAPTER 4 — The Challenger · School of the Line

> *Ash stopped me four times in one hour. The fourth time I had nothing left to protect, and that
> was the first useful thing I said.*
>
> — Ren Alcott, first-year, School of the Line

> *Somebody told them no on my behalf and it worked immediately, after two years of my saying it.
> I am grateful, and I am aware of what that tells me. Both of those are permanent.*
>
> — a clerk of Oreve

*Grateful **and** clear-eyed about the power that made it work. Refuses to resolve, which is the
unreconciled-attribution move arriving in the front matter.*

---

## CHAPTER 5 — The Regent · School of the Oath

> *Quill handed me the ledger in my first week and did not mention for a full term that the
> previous keeper had falsified it. She wanted to see what I would keep.*
>
> — Tomas Vey, third-year, School of the Oath

> *They kept the festival and changed the part about us. Nobody announced it. I found out because
> my daughter came home knowing the new words.*
>
> — Halvane, the year after

*Callback to Ch5's arc beat — the same world from inside. The margin's Halvane note and this
epigraph are one visit seen from two positions.*

## CHAPTER 6 — The Architect · School of the Pattern

> *Vale asked whose face had gone from my diagram. It was mine. I had drawn myself out of my own
> family and been pleased with the drawing.*
>
> — Sim Orrel, second-year, School of the Pattern

> *The new rota is worse for me and better for eleven other people. It took me a season to stop
> being angry about it. It took another to notice I would not change it back.*
>
> — a foreman, name withheld at his request

---

## CHAPTER 7 — The Diplomat · School of the Bridge

> *Cross made me say what would make staying dishonest. I could not answer. I have thought about
> that failure more than any examination I passed.*
>
> — Nell Ferran, fourth-year, School of the Bridge

> *They translated me accurately and I still lost. I am told this is the honest version. I am
> still deciding whether I prefer it.*
>
> — a delegate, second session

***The truest thing in the book's front matter.*** Allyship performed correctly, and the person it
was performed for still lost. Nothing else in nine chapters says this.

---

## CHAPTER 8 — The Sage · School of the Horizon

> *Orr let me climb as high as I liked and then asked what I was going to do on Thursday. I have
> never forgiven the question and I ask it constantly.*
>
> — Veyra Sol, Altitude Tutor, formerly of the Horizon

> *He fixed nothing. He sat in it with me for a full day and then went back to the ship. I have
> thought about that day for six years.*
>
> — unsigned, left at the Horizon door

*Veyra Sol is Thalen's own lieutenant — a former student who stayed. The second is the only
epigraph in the book addressed to nobody and signed by nobody, and it sits above the byline that
names him and the gap where his note is not.*

## CHAPTER 9 — The Player

> *The ones who go do not write much. The ones who stay write constantly. Draw your own
> conclusions.*
>
> — fourth-years, on the ones who go

> *She came back once, for a day, and did not stay. I have her map on the wall. It is wrong now
> and I have not replaced it.*
>
> — Sethen, dock office

## Placement

```
# CHAPTER TITLE
## *Subtitle*

[attribution line]

    [STUDENT EPIGRAPH]
    — [name, year, school]

    [BENEFICIARY EPIGRAPH]
    — [place, context]

[byline note — Headmaster, inset]

[body]
```

**Student first, beneficiary second.** Playing, then being played for. The reader crosses the gap
between them before the chapter begins, and nothing reconciles them.

## The actual distribution

The set already ignored the struck rule. Roughly half are thankful:

| Ch | Beneficiary | Register |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Oreve harbour records | glad, and no longer needs to know why |
| 3 | Sethen, third visit | **grateful** — *like it was the weather* |
| 4 | a clerk of Oreve | **grateful**, and clear about what it cost him to learn |
| 5 | Halvane, the year after | **grateful**, and found out sideways |
| 6 | a foreman | **arrived** — two seasons of anger, then endorsement |
| 7 | a delegate | still lost |
| 8 | unsigned, Horizon door | **grateful** — six years of thinking about one day |
| 9 | Sethen, dock office | **grateful**, wistful, keeping a wrong map |

Six grateful, one glad-but-puzzled, one who lost. Three were rewritten after the rule was struck
— Ch2, Ch4 and Ch6 had been hedged to avoid warmth, and hedging was the thing the rule was
producing.

## What this closes

**The beneficiary-side ICA** has been logged open for months. It is answered here — not as a
profile document, but as **eight voices with names, places and complaints**, distributed through
the book where they cannot be skipped.

Every one is specific, none is grateful, and two of them — *a clerk of Oreve* and *a delegate,
second session* — are not on the school's side at all.

## Recurring places

Oreve (Ch2, Ch4), Sethen (Ch3, Ch9), Halvane (Ch5). **Three worlds, five mentions, no
explanation anywhere.** Repetition across chapters is what makes an archive feel real; explaining
it is what makes it feel invented.

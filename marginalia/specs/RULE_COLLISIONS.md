# Rule Collisions

Places where `CH1_VOICE_AND_LINE_EDIT_CHECKLIST` forbids something the target voice
requires. Both found before the corpus was even filled, which is the argument for the
corpus: **rules written without evidence bend toward whatever the last draft got wrong.**

Both collisions have the same shape and take the same repair: the tic was written to kill a
slop pattern, and it over-reached into a rhythm pattern. The fix is not to weaken the rule.
It is to scope it to *argument* and license it in *image*.

---

## Collision 1 — B.3 forbids the beat

**The rule:** "Fragments as default → Write complete sentences."

**What it collides with:** the drum hit. Ravikant's whole method is the short standalone
line, and Adams' throwaway is frequently a fragment.

**The repair — three things were being called one thing:**

- **Beat fragment.** The hit after a long sentence. Carries rhythm. Load-bearing.
- **Atmosphere fragment.** Three in a row doing mood. Slop. What B.3 was written to kill.
- **Evasion fragment.** *"Not that simple." "Which is the point."* Carries a claim it refuses
  to commit to, and hides behind sounding punchy. The dangerous one, invisible to a
  fragment-count check, and the Diplomat shadow operating at sentence level.

**Rewritten B.3:**

> Does the fragment carry a claim, or a beat?
> Claim → complete sentence. No exceptions.
> Beat → licensed, in the landing position only: last line of a paragraph or section.
> Never mid-paragraph.

Landing-position is checkable by eye with no counting, and it rate-limits itself.

## Collision 2 — B.1 forbids the subverted simile

**The rule:** "'Not X but Y' / negation-contrast in every disguise. Default to positive
declaratives."

**What it collides with:** Adams' most-cited single sentence is a negation construction — a
simile built and then denied in its final words. His signature move is the thing B.1 bans.

**Why B.1 is still right about the case it was built for.** The stated reason is that
negation-contrast tricks the reader toward a conclusion instead of stating it, which reads
as condescension. That reasoning holds — for negation in an *argument*. It does not hold for
negation in an *image*, where nothing is being smuggled: the reader is handed a comparison,
then handed its refusal, and the gap between them is the whole content.

**Rewritten B.1 — v2, revised 2026-07-28 after the Adams pass.**

The argument/image split was the right instinct and the wrong cut. Adams excerpt 1 opens with
*"It wasn't just that the cave was cold, it wasn't just that it was damp and smelly. It was
that the cave was in the middle of Islington and there wasn't a bus due for two million
years"* — a three-beat negation ladder that is neither simile nor smuggled claim, and it is
the escalation engine of the paragraph. v1 would have killed it.

**The discriminator is not argument-versus-image. It is whether the negated thing is true.**

- **Ranking negation — LICENSED.** The negated items are real and stay real. Cold, damp and
  smelly are all true of the cave; the negation does not deny them, it *ranks* them under
  something larger. Nothing is being taken from the reader, and the size jump between rungs
  is where the force lives.
- **Denying negation — BANNED.** The negated item is set up in order to be knocked down. It
  tricks the reader toward a conclusion instead of stating it, which is the original and
  still-correct reason for the ban.

**The test, in one question:** *is the thing you just negated still true when the sentence
ends?*
Still true → ranking → keep. Now false → denying → cut, say Y directly.

**Existing exception stands** — a negation is load-bearing when it names and releases a
specific false belief the reader holds.

**Re-scored against the evidence:** Adams' cave ladder passes (cold and damp remain true).
Ch6's five negations all fail — *not organizational design, not the outcomes the founders
intended, not primarily a problem of individual behavior, not broken, not a system design
strategy.* Every one sets up a position purely to demolish it. The v2 rule is more permissive
and still kills all five, which is the check that it did not go soft.

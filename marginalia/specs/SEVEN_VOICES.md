# 13 — Seven Voices

**2026-07-28.** Each treatise is a different **genre of in-world nonfiction**, chosen from what
that altitude accepts as proof. Wendell's half of each chapter stays contemporary pedagogical
nonfiction — the book's baseline.

**The Cloud Atlas principle: the form carries the worldview.** Mitchell never announces the era.
The genre does it, and the reader adjusts inside two sentences without being told.

---

## The seven

### 1 · MAERA VOSS — School of the Body — *the Shaman*
**Proof:** the body's report, before interpretation.
**Genre: a practitioner's casebook.** Observation logs. Sensation recorded, then what it turned
out to mean, then what she got wrong.

**Idiosyncrasies**
- Physical sensation named **before** any concept. *Jaw. Then: the room had decided something.*
- Numbered observations rather than argued paragraphs
- Records her own wrong readings alongside the right ones
- Almost never uses "should"
- Ends entries without conclusions

> *Fourteenth session. Chest tight before the second speaker began. I recorded fear. It was
> anticipation, and I did not know that until Thursday.*

---

### 2 · CORIN ASH — School of the Line — *the Challenger*
**Proof:** the test. What holds under pressure.
**Genre: a drill manual.** Short imperatives, scenarios, failure conditions.

**Idiosyncrasies**
- Second-person imperative throughout. *You will. Do not.*
- **Variance, not brevity.** A hard word cap produces staccato; the short line lands because the one before it was long. What is constant is that nothing softens.
- Interrupts its own instruction with an objection, then answers it
- No hedging particles at all — no *perhaps*, no *tends to*, no *often*
- Scenarios numbered and graded by difficulty

> *Say it in eleven words. If it takes thirty you are explaining, and explaining is asking.
> Again.*

---

### 3 · SERA QUILL — School of the Oath — *the Regent*
**Proof:** precedent. What has been held before.
**Genre: an annotated charter.** Clauses with commentary, citing prior cases.

**Idiosyncrasies**
- Numbered clauses with subordinate notes
- Cites earlier practice by year or by keeper — *as held under Merrow*
- Formal register, long periodic sentences
- Distinguishes *the practice as written* from *the practice as kept*
- Never uses contemporary vocabulary for anything

> *Clause four. The inheritance passes intact, or it passes named. It has never passed silently,
> though it was attempted twice, and both attempts are recorded here rather than in the ledger
> proper.*

---

### 4 · IRIX VALE — School of the Pattern — *the Architect*
**Proof:** mechanism. What produces what.
**Genre: an engineering monograph.** Specifications, referenced figures, stated tolerances.

**Idiosyncrasies**
- Numbers things that do not need numbering
- *In practice* as a verbal tic, roughly once a page
- References figures and diagrams that are not reproduced
- Ends sections on a sentence that closes like a door
- States tolerances — *this holds up to about four people; past that it fails*

> *See figure three. The role is specified at two hours and consumes six. This is not a
> compliance problem. The specification was written by somebody who had not done the work.*

---

### 5 · ELIAN CROSS — School of the Bridge — *the Diplomat*
**Proof:** the relationship, and what survived it.
**Genre: a negotiation casebook.** Transcript fragments with commentary from both sides.

**Idiosyncrasies**
- Presents at least two positions before offering one
- Quotes people directly, including the ones who were wrong
- Conditional constructions — *if she had said, he would have*
- Names what each party was protecting, always both
- Ends cases without saying who was right

> *She said: "I need you to stop." He heard a verdict. She had meant a request, and by the time
> that was established they were arguing about the establishing.*

---

### 6 · THALEN ORR — School of the Horizon — *the Sage*
**Proof:** the pattern across cases.
**Genre: a commentary on the other five.** The only treatise that reads the others.

**Idiosyncrasies**
- Cites the other schools by name and disagrees with them courteously
- Longest sentences in the book; holds a contradiction across a full paragraph
- Retrospective — writes as if the reader has already finished
- Uses *we* about the school, and it is never clear how much of it he means
- Deflects when a sentence turns toward himself

> *Corin would say the line comes first and Corin is right within the Line, which is the
> qualification that makes the rest of this difficult to state without seeming to disagree.*

---

### 7 · WENDELL — Part 2 of every chapter
**Proof:** having done it and got it wrong.
**Genre: contemporary nonfiction pedagogy.** The book's baseline register.

**Idiosyncrasies**
- Direct second person
- Real dates, real places, real names of parts — Mr. Inadequate, the Collapse
- Testimony before instruction
- Punchline last
- Admits the thing before teaching the fix

> *I built a spreadsheet to track my moods and inside a week I was having the moods that made the
> chart look better.*

---

## Two margins, ruled

**Part 1 — the Headmaster, unsigned.** Argues with the treatise.
**Part 2 — Wendell, to himself.** **Updates the teaching.** *I do not do it this way any more.
This one fails for about a third of people. I wrote this in 2021 and I would say it differently
now.*

**The aside stays in both**, and rhythm alone justifies it. The reader has been trained to expect
interruption by Ch2; removing it in Part 2 would make the teaching half the driest half, which is
backwards. Comic pacing is a sufficient reason and does not need to earn a second one.

**The distinction must be typographic, decided before layout.** She has to know which margin she
is in before she reads a word of it.

**And the transfer is the point:** she learns to read a margin in Part 1, where the stakes are
fictional, then uses that posture in Part 2 on real material about her own life.

## FLAVOR — separate from style, and the thing that was missing

**Style is how the sentences are built. Flavor is who is building them and what it cost them.**
The first drill-manual test read as *a manual somebody wrote about Corin* rather than as Corin.

### The five flavor markers, derived from the Corin fix

1. **Something they find contemptible**, named specifically
2. **A cost referenced and never explained**
3. **An absent interlocutor** they are still arguing with
4. **One moment where the containment slips**
5. **A fact stated as a fact** — *I am not guessing*

Portable to all seven. Each voice runs them differently.

### Reference sets

| Voice | Flavor references | The engine |
|---|---|---|
| **Corin Ash** | Worf · Sisko · Colonel Tigh | conviction that has been paid for; containment that breaks once a season |
| **Maera Voss** | Carlos Castaneda · Baba Yaga | **witchy.** Withholds on purpose; the refusal is the teaching; not safe; transactional |
| **Sera Quill** | McGonagall · Brienne of Tarth · a cathedral archivist | upholds the institution *and* will burn a rule; that contradiction is the Regent |
| **Irix Vale** | Susan Calvin · Spock's precision · an engineer who talks about buildings the way people talk about friends | delight in elegance; forgets there are people inside it |
| **Elian Cross** | **Picard · Deanna Troi** | earned formal principle, plus reading the field and being overridden and offering anyway |
| **Thalen Orr** | Arthur Aguefort · Last Jedi Luke · Iroh | mercurial, withdrawn, warm; leaves without explaining |
| **Wendell** | Assistant Director Baldwin (2014) | busy, courteous, unbothered; has a queue |

*Vale and Quill are the two least confirmed. Susan Calvin may be colder than a man who asks whose
face left the diagram.*

### Flavor markers, per voice

**Voss** — refuses to name a thing she has named to herself · asks a question and does not answer
it · knew something years before she said it · the practice inverted in the last line
**Cross** — names what each party was protecting, always both · states a principle formally and
has earned it · has been unthanked and continues · admits the eleven years at the end
**Ash** — see the worked sample below

### Worked sample — Ch4 §3, style only vs. style plus flavor

**Style only** *(reads as a manual about Corin)*

> You have done this before. Badly, and under another name.
> They will tell you this is aggression. That person exists. You have met them.

**Style plus flavor** *(reads as Corin)*

> You have done this before. Badly, under another name, and you were praised for it.
>
> They will tell you this is aggression. I was told the same, at nineteen, by people who meant
> well, and I believed them for six years. Six years is a long time to be wrong in a way
> everybody around you finds pleasant.
>
> The third is where you will fail. I am not guessing.
>
> *I'd prefer not to* — when the truth is *I won't*. You have written that email. You wrote it
> recently. I have a folder of them, sent to me, by people who were furious and could not say so,
> and I answered every one as though it were a preference, because that is what they asked me to
> do.

**What arrived:** the praise he finds contemptible · six years, referenced and unexplained · the
people who meant well, still being argued with · the folder, where the containment slips · *I am
not guessing.*

## What ships August 1

**Full genre re-voicing is not a four-day job** and pretending otherwise would wreck the thing
that already works.

**The cheap version — genre markers, not genre rewrites.** Apply three to five surface
idiosyncrasies per treatise to Sections 1–3 only. A treatise that numbers its clauses and cites
prior keepers **reads as a charter** even when the prose beneath it is unchanged.

| Voice | Minimum viable markers |
|---|---|
| Voss | sensation before concept · numbered observations · one recorded wrong reading |
| Ash | imperatives · no hedging particles · one self-interruption |
| Quill | numbered clauses · one citation of prior practice · no contemporary vocabulary |
| Vale | one figure reference · *in practice* twice · one stated tolerance |
| Cross | two positions before one · one direct quotation · one unresolved case |
| Orr | one courteous disagreement with another school · one long held contradiction |

**~150 words of edits per chapter.** That is the Cloud Atlas effect at roughly a fifth of the
cost, and the full re-voicing becomes a second-edition project with a spec already written.

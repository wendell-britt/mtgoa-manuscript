# 09 — Production Plan: the frame conversion

**2026-07-28. Four days to print.** What ships, in what order, and what does not get built.

**Total new prose: ~4,300 words.** No chapter body gets re-voiced. Every existing paragraph
survives.

---

## Two decisions that block the rest

**1. Name the school.** The RPG leaves it player-defined (*Build Your School-Ship*), correct
for a game and impossible for a textbook. It appears on the half-title, six bylines, and the
enrollment page. Today.

**2. Marginalia rendering: inset styled blocks, not true margins.** True marginalia requires
wide measures and a designed page — a typesetting project, not a four-day one. Inset blocks in
a distinct face work in any layout, can be written without knowing pagination, and can break
across pages safely. This constrains note length to ~60–110 words, which is the right length
anyway.

---

## Build order, by leverage per hour

### 1. The frame line — ~20 words, 10 minutes
Half-title conceit. *Being the core text of the [School], in six treatises, with annotations.*
The entire fictional frame installs here. Everything downstream is consistent with it by
default.

### 2. The author's note — ~600 words, and the highest-value new asset in the book
The one place Wendell speaks as himself, undisguised. That contrast is load-bearing: it makes
the unsigned marginalia read as someone else, which the Ch8 reveal depends on.

Four beats:
- What the reader is holding and why it is shaped like a textbook from a school that does not
  exist
- That the fiction buys permission to invent examples, and that the alternative — operational
  rigor about every claim — is what a book like this usually dies of
- Chapter 1 stays as written; the arcade already holds this
- **The thesis beat, last.** Play is what got the book finished.

**Sharpen that last beat, because it is stronger than a nice line.** The book argues that play
metabolizes what earnestness cannot. The book was stuck in earnest operational rigor for
years and got finished by becoming a game. That is the mechanism demonstrated on the
mechanism's own author — the single best Reason to Believe in the entire product, and it costs
one paragraph.

Draft it early to lock the frame. Revise it last, once you know what the book became.

### 3. Six bylines — ~240 words total, one afternoon
Title, attribution, one unsigned note establishing that the annotator knows this person.
Roster and blind spots are already in the RPG (`08_FACE_AUTHORS.md`); this is porting.

### 4. The marginalia pass — ~3,000 words, the bulk of the work
3–5 notes per chapter, 9 chapters, ~30–40 notes. Jobs: clarify · complicate · warn · humanize ·
foreshadow · gossip · confess · **amend**.

**Run it as a pass, not as composition.** One chapter at a time, place before polishing, and
accept the first workable note. The failure mode here is treating forty notes as forty pieces
of writing.

Placement heuristics that make the pass fast:
- Where the Face is most confident → *warn* (confidence is the daemon tell)
- Where a section opens → *humanize* or *gossip*
- Where the reader is asked to do something → *amend*
- Once per chapter → *confess*, and that is the testimony budget

### 5. Delayed-naming pass — targeted, ~2 hours
Only where Earth artifacts appear. Describe, then label. Not a whole-book line edit — a
find-and-fix on the specimens.

### 6. Back matter: enrollment — ~400 words
The deck is the practice set. Classes are terms. Working with you is working with a Game
Master. **The fiction makes the commercial layer diegetic**, which resolves the standing
problem of not wanting to run Orange sales language: it is not a pitch, it is an invitation
into a world that already exists in the reader's hands.

---

## What does not get built

The fiction can eat the entire project if it is allowed to. It will not be allowed to.

- **No lore.** No ship history, no founding, no politics of the Six Schools.
- **No new names.** Not the ship, not the worlds, not the students, not the lieutenants beyond
  what the RPG already lists.
- **No chapter bodies re-voiced.** The register that reads as drift becomes characterization
  for free.
- **No backstories** beyond the RPG's existing blind spots and signature questions.
- **No marginalia character arc.** The annotator is one consistent voice. The only arc is the
  Ch8 reveal, and it needs no setup because the notes are unsigned from page one.
- **No consistency audit against the RPG.** The book and the game may diverge. They are
  different products and the reader of one is not holding the other.

---

## Risk register

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Fiction eats the schedule | the do-not-build list above, treated as binding |
| Marginalia reads as sidebar filler | every note has a named job; no note gets added for flavor |
| Earth artifacts date the book | *RACI matrix* stays, *Slack* goes; describe before naming |
| Ch8 reveal falls flat | notes unsigned from page one — the reveal is free if nothing sets it up |
| Deck / app / RPG contradict the book | out of scope. Divergence is acceptable this week. |

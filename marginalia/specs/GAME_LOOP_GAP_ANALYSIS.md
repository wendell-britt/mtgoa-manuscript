# Game Loop ↔ Exercises — Gap Analysis and August 1 Scope

**2026-07-28.** Loop finalised today. Digital delivery in four days.

---

## The headline

**Nothing in the book contradicts the loop.** Checked every exercise: no move instructs her to
intervene without consent, none diagnoses another person from her own charge, none treats seeing
a problem as an accepted relationship. **The gap is omission, not conflict** — which is the good
version of this problem this close to print.

**And one convergence.** Loop step 7 calls for *a source-grounded move*. For the loop to call a
move out of the book, that move needs a stated precondition and a stated outcome. That is
precisely the 25 stopping conditions the exercise audit just flagged. **One piece of work serves
both**, and it was already on the list.

---

## The strategic frame, which decides most of the scope questions

**The book is the move library's source text. The loop is the runtime.**

This is the same division already settled for the app: *the book generates high-quality BAR cards;
the app carries the Octalysis drives the book was never meant to hold.* Same logic here. The book
teaches the **capabilities** the loop calls on. The loop **sequences** them, gates them on consent,
and measures artifacts.

A source text does not need to be its own runtime. Trying to make it one in four days would
damage the thing that is already working.

---

## Five gaps, ranked by how much they matter and how expensive they are

### GAP 1 — Consent is not a gate in any exercise
**Loop rule:** *"Consent is the hinge. Seeing a problem is not an accepted helping
relationship."* Step 3 has four valid outcomes: accept, decline, defer, revise.

**Book:** exercises run *notice → feel → speak*. There is no accept/decline gate anywhere. Ch7's
honest terms is closest — the field "gets to respond" — but there is no explicit offer, and no
outcome branch for a no.

**Severity: highest.** This is the loop's load-bearing rule and the book's central omission.
**Cost to close properly: very high** — it changes the shape of most exercises.

### GAP 2 — Nothing produces an artifact
**Loop:** *"a concrete artifact that would count as a win"*, and success is measured by whether
the artifact lands.

**Book:** exercises produce conversations, statements, and noticings. **No exercise in nine
chapters says "make a thing."**

**Severity: high. Cost: high.** This is a different genre of exercise.

### GAP 3 — The other person never defines the outcome
**Loop:** *"learn what would genuinely help them this week"* and *"the person-defined outcome
selects the artifact."*

**Book:** exercises are almost entirely about her interior and her speech. Nothing asks her to
find out what the other person actually wants.

**Severity: high** — this is the beneficiary problem again, and it is the book's oldest known gap.
**Partially addressed already** by the eight beneficiary epigraphs, which is not the same as an
exercise but is not nothing.

### GAP 4 — No move for not being the carrier
**Loop:** *"A good ally is not always the carrier. Connection, referral, delegation, or decline
can be the most skillful contribution."* Plus **Find the Missing Player** as a quest type.

**Book:** nothing on referral, delegation, or recruitment. Ch4 teaches decline, but as a boundary
rather than as a routing decision.

**Severity: medium. Cost: medium** — this is a missing move category, not a restructure.

### GAP 5 — No graded success
**Loop:** three levels — grammatical attempt, external result with residual dissatisfaction,
landed artifact with voluntary satisfaction from both people.

**Book:** binary at best, usually unstated.

**Severity: low for the book, high for the app. Cost: low** — this is the stopping-condition work,
worded to match.

## IN SCOPE for August 1

**1. The 25 stopping conditions, written in loop-compatible language.** ~1,000 words. Already the
top item on the exercise audit. Use Ch7's form — *the test is not [outcome you cannot control],
the test is [the thing you did]* — which happens to be the loop's success level 1 exactly: a
grammatical attempt is progress. **Do this and 25 moves become loop-addressable at no extra
cost.**

**2. The three AMEND fixes.** Twenty minutes. The Ch5 one is a defect regardless of the loop.

**3. One page naming the loop, placed in Ch9.** Ch9 is *Creating Your Own Allyship Game* — the loop
is literally the answer to that chapter's question, and it is the only place it can go without
retrofitting anything. Present it as **what the game becomes**, not as a correction to the
preceding eight chapters. It also sets up the app and the deck without a sales register.

**4. A consent sentence in the front matter or Ch2.** One sentence, not a rewrite: *seeing a
problem is not an accepted helping relationship.* This closes the most dangerous gap at the level
of principle even though the exercises do not enforce it. **Cheapest high-value item on this
list.**

---

## OUT OF SCOPE for August 1

**Retrofitting consent gates into the exercises.** Changes the shape of most of them. Four days is
not enough and a half-done version would leave the book inconsistent with itself, which is worse
than the current clean omission.

**Artifact-producing exercises.** A different genre. Belongs to the app and the cohort, where an
artifact can actually be reviewed.

**The carrier-route move category** — referral, delegation, Find the Missing Player. This is real
missing content and it is a **second edition or a companion**, not a patch.

**Any Move restructure.** The templates diverge three ways across the book. Unifying them is right
and it is not a four-day job.

**Making the book teach the loop.** It should not. The loop is nine steps with branching and
gates; the book is a developmental text. Forcing the spine in would flatten the thing that
actually works.

---

## What this means for the app and the cohort

The gaps that stay out are **not** deficiencies to apologise for. They are the division of labour:

| | Book | Loop / app / cohort |
|---|---|---|
| capability | **teaches** | calls |
| consent gate | names the principle | **enforces** |
| artifact | — | **requires and reviews** |
| person-defined outcome | shows it in epigraphs | **collects it** |
| carrier routing | — | **routes** |
| graded success | states the first level | **all three** |

**The book earns the right to be a source text by being good at what it does.** Every one of these
gaps is a reason the app exists — which is the argument the enrollment page needs anyway, and it
is now supported rather than asserted.

## One thing to check before print

The Ch9 loop page is the only new *content* being added under this analysis. It needs to read as
**arrival**, not correction — she has just finished eight chapters of capability, and the loop is
what those capabilities are for. If it reads as *here is the system you should have been using*,
it retroactively devalues the book she just read.

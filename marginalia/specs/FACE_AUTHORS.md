# 08 — The Face-Authors

**Ruled by Wendell 2026-07-28: the Face authors every section.** This spec makes them people
without requiring the treatises to be rewritten.

---

## The scope claim, stated first because it is four days to print

**Characterization is cheap when concentrated and ruinous when diffuse.** Six fully-realized
authors re-voiced into every section is weeks of work. Six *bylines* is an afternoon.

The prose is already in a distinct register per chapter. Ch6 reads as engineering lecture. Ch5
reads as Amber earnestness. Ch7 reads as relational fluency. `MTGOA_CROSS_BOOK_SYNTHESIS`
called this drift and observed that it clustered in the three systems-oriented Faces — which
was never a coincidence. **Those chapters read differently because those Faces think
differently.**

Give the prose an author with a name and a bias, and the register converts from defect to
characterization **retroactively, without editing the paragraphs.**

What ships in four days: bylines, and a characterization pass on the marginalia only.
What does not: re-voicing nine chapters of body text.

---

## The five fields

A character is established by very little. Each Face-author needs exactly these:

1. **Stance** — what they believe and what they're impatient about
2. **Tic** — something visible in how they write
3. **The daemon coloring** — how their assigned daemon bends their opinion. *This is the field
   that matters most*; it is what makes the treatise a document with a bias the reader can
   learn to spot, rather than neutral instruction.
4. **A thing they were wrong about** — one, specific, already survived
5. **Their relationship with the annotator** — W. has opinions about them, and they are not
   uniform

Field 3 is the teaching. A Face writing under its daemon produces a treatise that is true and
tilted. Learning to feel the tilt in someone else's document is the same skill as feeling it
in your own head, which is the book's entire method.

## Worked example — the Architect

**Assigned daemon: the Emotional Body** (`DAEMON_CHAPTER_LEDGER`).

**Stance.** He believes most harm is structural and most guilt is misfiled, and he is right
about both. He is impatient with feeling treated as a conclusion — for him a feeling is a
reading, and a reading that never becomes a build is a reading wasted. He has watched people
metabolize beautifully and change nothing, and it offends him.

**Tic.** He numbers things that do not need numbering. He says *in practice* the way other
people clear their throat. He ends sections on a sentence that sounds like a door closing.

**The daemon coloring.** He converts felt signal into design *fast*, and the speed is the tell.
He arrives at the diagram before the feeling has finished. Every treatise of his is slightly
early. He cannot tell a charge that has moved from a charge that has been well-described,
because from where he sits they look identical — so his most confident passages are the ones
to read twice.

**Wrong about.** He once designed a handoff process so clean the team stopped talking to each
other. It worked. Throughput improved. He reports this as a success with a caveat, and the
caveat is doing a great deal of work.

**W. on him.** The most useful and most exhausting of the six. W. has taken his advice, been
right, and been alone in a room about it.

## The eighth marginalia job — AMEND

Half-Blood Prince. The Prince's annotations are not commentary; they are **better
instructions**. A working practitioner improving the textbook.

> **[W. — amend]**
> Skip the incentive question. Ask who would have to be brave for the stated outcome to
> happen. Same answer, arrives faster, and you'll have it in your chest before you can get it
> on paper.

**Guardrail.** The Prince makes the potion better. He never says Libatius Borage was a fool.
AMEND is a shortcut a practitioner found — not a correction, and rare. Overused, the Face
becomes a straw man and the treatise stops being worth reading, which costs the book its
teaching layer.

**Job list is now:** clarify · complicate · warn · humanize · foreshadow · gossip · confess ·
**amend**.

## The byline format

Opens each Face's treatise. ~60–90 words. This is the entire characterization budget for body
text.

> **On Structure, and What It Is Actually Paying For**
> *being the fourth treatise, submitted by the Architect, who asked that it be noted he
> submitted it on time*
>
> **[W. — humanize]**
> He did submit it on time. He also submitted it twice, because the first version had a
> diagram he wanted to redo. I have kept the diagram he wanted to redo. It is on page 4 and
> it is better than the one he replaced it with, and I am not going to tell him.

That is the whole method. A title, a wry attribution, one marginal note establishing that
these two know each other. Everything after it reads as authored, and nothing after it had to
be rewritten.

### Irix Vale, as the RPG has him — nothing here is new

- **Embodies:** strategic clarity with moral contact
- **Signature move:** asks students to draw the system, then asks *whose face disappeared from
  the diagram*
- **Blind spot (canon):** elegance — can become enchanted by models
- **Favorite sentence:** *"A map that erases the person is already wrong."*
- **Lieutenants:** Pell Axis (Systems Cartographer), Rin Compass (Leverage Tutor), Ossa
  Threadglass (Contradiction Keeper)

The signature move is what makes him a person rather than a cautionary tale: **he knows his own
trap and falls into it anyway.**

### The calibration that keeps this cheap

**The fiction is the frame, not the content.** Irix Vale authors the treatise; the wellness
hour is still a real workplace. The Faces are authors writing about the reader's actual life,
not characters in a novel she is reading. Implication over exposition — the RPG's own spec
already bans excessive lore. A named Head, an epithet, and one marginal note establishing a
relationship is the entire budget.

### Canon conflict — needs a ruling

A marginalia note reads *"Do not pick Line because you want permission to be mean. Head Sura
can smell that through walls."* The Line's Head is **Corin Ash**. Sura is either a predecessor,
a lieutenant, or a leftover from an earlier draft. Surfacing rather than smoothing; must be
settled before names go to print.

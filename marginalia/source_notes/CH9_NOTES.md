## NOTE 2 — *play*
**Placement:** Section 1, after *"The Player left on purpose."*

> *Leaving on purpose sounds better than it is. I have done it twice, and on both occasions I
> spent the first month suspecting I had made an enormous mistake — which I had not, and which

### What the first draft got wrong

It answered, reassured, and had unlimited time. Four repairs:

1. **A clock.** *We put out in a few days.* The original offered a berth with no window, which is
   what made it soft. Baldwin's aperture closes and is harder to reopen; the ship leaves.
2. **He does not do her arithmetic.** The original spelled out *I am fine, it cost me, I would go
   again.* Now: you watched, you can add. Refusing to conclude is stronger than concluding
   honestly.
3. **He is interrupted mid-clause.** The sentence breaks because he has been called away.
   **She is less urgent than the hydroponics**, and that is the last thing the book does to her.
4. ***"There is literature by the door."*** Baldwin's t-shirts. Administratively bored, and more
   brutal than any sincere sign-off available.

*"I checked" still callbacks the Ch1 circus line. *"I have a term to run"* still callbacks Ch5
note 6 — and now it is cut off before it can become a joke he is comfortable inside.*

## BOOK-WIDE CALIBRATION — the Headmaster is busy

The Baldwin piece exposes a texture missing from every set so far: **he is mid-shift.** He has a
queue. Other people's crises are also happening. The reader is one intake among several and he is
visibly triaging.

My notes have him reflective, available, unhurried — which reads as intimacy and produces
softness. Baldwin is courteous, professional, and unbothered, and that is what makes his offer
credible: **a man with a queue is not selling you anything.**

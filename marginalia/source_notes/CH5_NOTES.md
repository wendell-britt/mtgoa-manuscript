## NOTE 1 — *humanize*
**Placement:** under the byline, before Section 1.

> *Sera hands new students something inherited — a role, a ledger, a duty nobody wants — and
> watches whether they keep the purpose or only the shape of it. Most keep the shape. She does
> not tell them which they did for a full term, which several people have called cruel and
> which is the only version that works.*
>
> *She has let three inheritances die since I came aboard. She can name them and the year. She
> has never once looked comfortable doing it.*

*Signature move, then the alliance. Letting an inheritance die is the Fixer-Healer trained — and
"never once looked comfortable" is the instalment still being paid, which is lesson 5 arriving
as a fact rather than a claim.*

## NOTE 2 — **ARC BEAT** — *humanize*
**Placement:** Section 3, immediately after *"A tradition that can't change is a tradition that
has died — it's just still moving because nobody noticed."*

> *Halvane has both kinds. They mend everything and let the mend show — a replaced roof beam is
> painted a different colour on purpose, and the colour tells you which generation did the work,
> so a ceiling with six colours in it is worth more than a ceiling with one. Their food is
> terrible. They have kept the recipes.*
>
> *I knew what the colours meant before the guide explained them. I did not mention it.*

*Invisible Cities checklist: **name** — Halvane. **Physical consequence of the premise** — the
painted beams, which is the chapter's own thesis rendered as architecture. **Something
unglamorous** — the food, which is also the Regent's distortion in six words: keeping things
means keeping the bad ones. **A local who cannot see the premise** — the guide, explaining what
does not need explaining to the one person aboard who already knew.*

*The beat is the last line. He recognised the practice, and he does not say from where, and the
word does not appear. Nothing marks it.*

## NOTE 3 — *warn*
**Placement:** Section 3, at the Polarity Encounter — Honor ↔ Reform.

> *This is the polarity Sera lives in and it is where she is least reliable. She will hold a
> thing one season longer than it can bear, every time, and she will have a good reason.*
>
> *The reason will be true. That is what makes it hard. The test is not whether the reason is

## NOTE 4 — *amend* — **the chapter's only one**
**Placement:** Section 4, at "Name One Inheritance Out Loud, in the Village."

> *Do not name it out loud first. Name it to one person who was also there.*
>
> *An inheritance named in the room becomes a proposal, and a proposal gets scheduled. Named to
> a single witness it stays a fact for a few days, which is long enough to find out whether you
> actually think it. Then take it to the room.*

*Adds a step in front of Sera's instruction rather than replacing it. She still gets the room.*

## NOTE 5 — *gossip / cult leader*
**Placement:** Section 4, near "The Inheritance Cycle."

> *The school keeps a vow ceremony that nobody can explain. It runs forty minutes. Two of the
> six Heads believe it predates the ship, which it cannot, because we invented it in the fourth
> year — I was there and I remember the argument about the candles.*
>
> *It is the best-attended thing we do. I have stopped correcting people.*

*Teaches something the treatise cannot: an invented tradition becomes a real one, and the
inventor is the last to be believed.*

## NOTE 6 — *gossip*
**Placement:** Section 5, immediately after *"until you leave, and everyone discovers that the
thing you were holding was you."*

> *Sera does this. Not the repair half — the second one. She is the reason four separate things
> on this ship still function, none of them written down, all of them in her head, and she will
> tell you she is simply the person who happens to remember.*
>
> *I have raised it with her twice. Both times she agreed, thanked me, and changed nothing, and
> I let it go, because the four things kept working and I had a term to run.*

***The arc advances by him not noticing.*** He identifies the pattern in her, correctly and
affectionately, and then performs it himself in the final clause. He is not confessing — he is
gossiping, which is why this does not count as a second beat. **He pays nothing here.** On a
second read, *"I had a term to run"* is the same sentence Sera would use.

## STANDING RULE — the arc-beat exception to punchline-last

**In a note carrying an arc instalment, the beat lands and the joke sits earlier.**

Ch3: the clerk is the laugh, *"more than is useful"* is the close. Ch5: the recipes are the
laugh, *"I did not mention it"* is the close. The joke still has to be there — a beat with no
comedy in front of it announces itself as A Meaningful Moment, which is exactly what these notes
must not do. But the last line belongs to the arc.

Same applies to a byline note carrying a Head's daemon alliance: the alliance lands last,
because it is the proof the margin's lesson 5 depends on.

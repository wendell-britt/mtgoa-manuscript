Every Face gets a row with these 13 fields:

1. **Altitude** — Color / developmental name / core thesis

8. **Control Translate moves (−1 energy)** — Suppressions that cost energy
9. **Flow cycle** — Natural circuit across channels
10. **8 Gates at this altitude** — Says / Job for each
11. **Through-line** — Single thing all 8 gates are organized around at this altitude
12. **Vulnerable Child's superpower** — What lives at the center at this altitude

14. **Exile story seed** — Why did this Face leave the village?
15. **Distortion seed** — What does the village do wrong without this Face?
16. **Bridge to next Face** — Why does the next Face need this one's work first?

# Chapter 3: THE CHALLENGER
## *Red — Socialized Power — Decisive Action*

### 1. Altitude
- **Color:** Red
- **Developmental name:** Socialized Power / Decisive Action
- **Core thesis:** Allyship requires the willingness to be unwelcome. The clean "no." The drawn line.

### 2. Shadow if skipped
Performative agreement. Toxic niceness. Conflict avoidance dressed up as peace. The inability to say "this is not acceptable" without apologizing for saying it. Accumulated resentment. Underground hostility that leaks sideways because it can't move forward.

### 3. Native material
**Will / charge / force.** The energy of pushing back, drawing lines, refusing, demanding, confronting. The Challenger works on the *same* fire the Shaman's Fire/Anger channel touches, but at a different altitude — not "what is this anger telling me" but "how do I wield this will cleanly in the world."

### 4. The 5 Channels — Modes of Confrontation
1. **The Line** — A boundary drawn. *"This is what I won't stand for."*
2. **The Interrupt** — Breaking a pattern mid-flight. *"We're not going to pretend this is normal."*
3. **The Demand** — Insisting on something. *"This has to change."*
4. **The Refusal** — The clean no. *"I will not."*
5. **The Reckoning** — Naming what it will cost to cross you. *"If you do X, here is what happens."*

### 5. The stage sequence
**Charge → Aim → Act → Exit** (4 stages)
- **Charge:** The will arrives. Fire shows up in the body.
- **Aim:** You identify what this is actually about — underneath the story, what is the line being crossed?
- **Act:** You choose the form (Line / Interrupt / Demand / Refusal / Cost) and execute.
- **Exit:** You leave the confrontation cleanly. You don't need their reaction to validate your move.

### 6. Transcend moves (+2 energy)
Five conversions of raw charge into clean action:
1. **Outrage → Righteous Act** (not venting; making a move that matters)
2. **Fear-of-being-unwelcome → Willingness** (the fear becomes the fuel for the fact that you showed up anyway)
3. **Grief-in-the-fight → Moral Clarity** (the sadness under the anger becomes the anchor)
4. **Protective-rage → Calm Defense** (fire for someone you love, held steady)
5. **Defiance → Creation** (the "no" becomes a "yes" to something new)

### 7. Generative Translate moves (+1 energy)
- **Line → Interrupt** (once the line is drawn, you can break the pattern)
- **Interrupt → Demand** (once the pattern is broken, you can insist)
- **Demand → Refusal** (the demand clarifies your own non-negotiables)
- **Refusal → Cost** (your "no" becomes visible as a consequence to be reckoned with)

### 8. Control Translate moves (−1 energy)
- **Charge → Swallow** (repression — eats you from inside)
- **Charge → Performative outrage** (for the audience, not the line)
- **Charge → Passive-aggression** (the will goes sideways)
- **Charge → Collapse** (the will implodes into shame)

### 9. Flow cycle
**Charge → Aim → Push → Rest → Repair → Charge.** You don't sustain Red altitude indefinitely. You strike and then you *rest* and *repair* (including with the people you confronted, when appropriate). Then the next charge arrives.

### 10. The 8 Gates at Red altitude

| Gate | Says | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Protector** | *Not safe to push. Last time you pushed, you lost something.* | Keep you from confrontation until it knows you won't be destroyed by it. |
| **Controller** | *If you let yourself be this angry, you'll go too far. You'll become someone you can't live with.* | Contain the charge so it doesn't leak into everyone. |
| **Skeptic** | *Is this really worth fighting for? Am I overreacting? Is my anger even legitimate?* | Doubt your charge before someone else can weaponize it. |
| **Fixer** | *Don't fight — solve. There's a way to make this go away without confrontation.* | Convert charge into workaround so you never have to use it. |
| **Fear** | *If you push, they'll retaliate. You'll lose the relationship / the job / the access.* | Rehearse the worst case to pre-empt the pushing. |
| **Victim** | *I can't push because I was taught my anger was wrong. I was punished for pushing.* | Keep the story alive of why you can't fight. |
| **Damaged Self** | *My anger is proof there's something wrong with me. I'm too much. I'm a bully.* | Identify the charge itself as evidence of brokenness. |
| **Vulnerable Child** | *I want to be able to say no. I want to stand for something. I want to be unwelcome if that's what it takes.* | Hold the superpower. |

### 11. Through-line
**Your capacity to push.** Every gate is organized around one question: *is it safe for you to be unwelcome?*

### 12. Vulnerable Child's superpower
**Moral clarity without cruelty.** The willingness to be unwelcome held steady by love. The clean "no" that is not a performance and not a wound. The line that protects without cutting.

### 13. The 5 Game moves (Section 6)
1. **Name the Unnameable** — Say what nobody in the room is saying.
2. **Draw the Line** — State explicitly what you will not do / will not stand.
3. **Refuse Cleanly** — "No." No apology, no essay.
4. **Confront Without Cruelty** — Hard content, clean delivery.
5. **Stay Past the Discomfort** — Don't soften when they flinch. Hold.

### 14. Exile seed
The Challenger was exiled when the village got tired of conflicts only being resolved by who was the most powerful and angry. They were replaced by the Regent who built walled cities and created nations and traditions.

### 15. Distortion seed
Without the Challenger, the village got hostage to whoever was loudest. Conflict didn't disappear — it went underground. Resentment became the common language. *Be nice* became a tool of compliance. *Don't make a scene* became code for *don't say what is true.* And the one person who could still say "this is unacceptable" was cast as the problem they were trying to name.

### 16. Bridge to Chapter 4 (Regent)
The Challenger can draw the line. The Challenger cannot *hold* the line across scale and time. Once a line is drawn, someone has to build the tradition that carries it forward — the structure, the role, the inheritance that lets the line survive past the moment of its drawing. That's the Regent.

# Chapter 4: THE REGENT
## *Amber — Order — Stewardship & Scale*

### 1. Altitude
- **Color:** Amber
- **Developmental name:** Mythic-Traditional Order / Stewardship
- **Core thesis:** Allyship at scale requires traditions worth protecting and worth breaking.

### 2. Shadow if skipped
Rigidity. Dogma. Loyalty collapses into obedience. The cage of the familiar. *OR the opposite:* endless novelty without holding, where nothing lasts long enough to form a lineage, and the young inherit exhaustion instead of wisdom.

### 3. Native material
**Loyalty / duty / inheritance / role.** The capacity to serve something larger than yourself *and* to be shaped by what you serve, without disappearing into it or being ground up by it.

### 4. The 5 Channels — Modes of Stewardship
1. **The Custodian** — caring for what exists.
2. **The Inheritor** — receiving what was passed down, including its wounds.
3. **The Teacher** — passing it on in a form the next person can actually receive.
4. **The Reformer** — changing the tradition while honoring the lineage.
5. **The Keeper of Vows** — honoring commitments across time, especially when no one is watching.

### 5. The stage sequence
**Inherit → Honor → Steward → Entrust** (4 stages)
- **Inherit:** You receive what was handed to you — the gift and the damage both.
- **Honor:** You name what in the inheritance still serves.
- **Steward:** You carry it, maintain it, and reform it where it no longer serves.
- **Entrust:** You hand it on, cleanly, to whoever comes next.

### 6. Transcend moves (+2 energy)
1. **Obedience → True allegiance** (voluntary loyalty, not compliance)
2. **Duty → Service** (chosen responsibility, not martyrdom)
3. **Rigidity → Integrity** (holding form because it works, not because it's old)
4. **Dogma → Faithfulness** (keeping the essence, updating the expression)
5. **Inheritance-shame → Inheritance-gift** (the wounds you received become the medicine you pass)

### 7. Generative Translate moves (+1 energy)
- **Custodian → Reformer** (caring for what is becomes changing it wisely)
- **Inheritor → Teacher** (what you received, you now pass)
- **Teacher → Keeper of Vows** (the act of teaching itself becomes the commitment)

### 8. Control Translate moves (−1 energy)
- **Loyalty → Obedience** (surrendering agency to avoid the cost of thinking)
- **Duty → Martyrdom** (serving until you disappear)
- **Tradition → Dogma** (holding form without function)
- **Role → Mask** (becoming the position so completely that the person is lost)

### 9. Flow cycle
**Inherit → Honor → Steward → Reform → Pass → Inherit** (circular, generational). The Regent's flow is the slowest of the 6 — it moves across years and successions, not minutes.

### 10. The 8 Gates at Amber altitude

| Gate | Says | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Protector** | *Not safe to take on this responsibility. You don't have the standing. Who are you to hold this?* | Keep you from claiming a role you can't yet carry. |
| **Controller** | *If you take the role, you'll become the role. Don't commit fully — keep a door open.* | Contain the risk of disappearing into duty. |
| **Skeptic** | *Is this tradition even real? Is this role legitimate? Am I being used?* | Doubt the inheritance before you invest in it. |
| **Fixer** | *Before you take this on, fix what's broken about it. Don't inherit a flawed system — rebuild it first.* | Convert inheritance into project so you don't have to accept it on its terms. |
| **Fear** | *If you commit to this duty, you'll be trapped. You'll die inside a role and never have been yourself.* | Rehearse the cage. |
| **Victim** | *I can't serve because I was served badly. The people who held this role before me failed me.* | Keep the story alive of why you can't inherit. |
| **Damaged Self** | *I'm not worthy of this duty. I'm too damaged to carry what is sacred. I will break what I'm supposed to protect.* | Identify yourself as unfit for the role you were made for. |
| **Vulnerable Child** | *I want to belong to something larger than me. I want to inherit. I want to pass something on.* | Hold the superpower. |

### 11. Through-line
**Your capacity to serve something larger than yourself without losing yourself in it.**

### 12. Vulnerable Child's superpower
**Steady service that neither swallows you nor uses you up.** The capacity to be shaped by what you steward without being consumed by it. To inherit, reform, and pass — across generations — without needing the credit and without losing the thread.

### 13. The 5 Game moves (Section 6)
1. **Audit the Tradition** — Which of these rules still serves? Which is a cage?
2. **Stand in the Lineage** — Publicly claim what you inherit and why.
3. **Build the Role** — Design the position so the next person can hold it after you.
4. **Repair What Was Broken** — Name the failures of the prior stewards, and fix them without rejecting the lineage.
5. **Pass the Charge** — When it's time, hand off cleanly.

### 14. Exile seed
The Regent was exiled when the village confused loyalty with obedience and decided that the person who would question the tradition was a traitor to it. The Regent said "this tradition is no longer serving us — we need to reform it" and was called disloyal. The village kept the *rules* but lost the *stewards*, which is how traditions fossilize into cages.

### 15. Distortion seed
Without the Regent, traditions fossilize or get burned down with nothing to replace them. "Loyalty" becomes a weapon to punish questioners. Succession fails — the young inherit broken systems with no one left to teach them the functional parts. Or, at the other extreme: nothing lasts, everything is reinvented every few years, and exhaustion becomes the baseline.

### 16. Bridge to Chapter 5 (Architect)
The Regent can hold a tradition but cannot design a new one from first principles. When the tradition is no longer fit for purpose — when it is failing structurally, not ethically — you need someone who can redesign the system. That's the Architect.

# Chapter 5: THE ARCHITECT
## *Orange — Strategy — Strategic Design*

### 1. Altitude
- **Color:** Orange
- **Developmental name:** Strategic / Modern / Achievement-Oriented
- **Core thesis:** Escape is design. The best escapes are blueprints other people can follow.

### 2. Shadow if skipped
Analysis paralysis. Coldness. Systems that serve no one. *OR the opposite:* all heart, no leverage; compassion without traction; endless moral appeals against structural problems.

### 3. Native material
**Logic / leverage / system / design.** The capacity to see the structural pattern behind the surface phenomenon and redesign it so that the right outcome becomes the easy outcome.

### 4. The 5 Channels — Modes of Design
1. **The Mapmaker** — seeing the whole system at once.
2. **The Engineer** — building the working version.
3. **The Strategist** — identifying where to push for maximum movement.
4. **The Inventor** — creating what didn't exist.
5. **The Optimizer** — making what works work better.

### 5. The stage sequence
**Observe → Model → Design → Deploy** (4 stages)
- **Observe:** What is actually happening in this system?
- **Model:** Build the simplest explanation that accounts for the behavior.
- **Design:** Create the intervention that changes the system's incentives.
- **Deploy:** Ship it into contact with reality and observe again.

### 6. Transcend moves (+2 energy)
1. **Complexity → Elegance** (simplification without loss)
2. **Confusion → Clarity** (naming the mechanism)
3. **Constraint → Design criterion** (the limit becomes the shape of the solution)
4. **Problem → Specification** (the pain becomes the feature list)
5. **Failure → Information** (the broken design becomes the next design's input)

### 7. Generative Translate moves (+1 energy)
- **Mapmaker → Strategist** (once you see, you know where to push)
- **Engineer → Optimizer** (once it works, make it work better)
- **Inventor → Mapmaker** (the new thing shows you the system you couldn't see before it existed)

### 8. Control Translate moves (−1 energy)
- **Design → Over-design** (endless iteration, never ship)
- **Strategy → Scheming** (leverage for its own sake, untethered from the end)
- **System → Systematization-for-comfort** (using planning as avoidance of action)
- **Model → Modelitis** (falling in love with the map at the expense of the territory)

### 9. Flow cycle
**Observe → Model → Design → Deploy → Observe (new state) → Model (new model) → ...** The Architect's cycle is the *inner loop* of how the world improves.

### 10. The 8 Gates at Orange altitude

| Gate | Says | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Protector** | *Not safe to commit to this blueprint. You don't have enough information. Wait. Gather more data.* | Keep you from shipping before you're ready. |
| **Controller** | *If you ship this, you can't take it back. Keep refining. Keep the design inside your head where it's perfect.* | Contain the design in the space where it can't fail. |
| **Skeptic** | *Is this even a problem worth solving? Are you sure this is the right frame? What if you're optimizing the wrong thing?* | Doubt the design before someone else can. |
| **Fixer** | *Don't think — execute. The design isn't ready but the urgency is real.* | Convert thinking into doing prematurely. |
| **Fear** | *If this design fails, it fails publicly. Your reputation is tied to it.* | Rehearse the public failure. |
| **Victim** | *I can't design at scale because I was never given the resources / training / access real architects have. I'm working with one hand tied.* | Keep the story alive of why you can't build. |
| **Damaged Self** | *I'm not actually smart enough for this. I got here by accident. If people saw how my mind actually works, they'd know.* | Identify yourself as an impostor in the role you were made for. |
| **Vulnerable Child** | *I love the elegant solution. I get chills at the beautiful blueprint. I know design is a form of love.* | Hold the superpower. |

### 11. Through-line
**Your capacity to design clearly without losing sight of what the design is for.**

### 12. Vulnerable Child's superpower
**Structural generosity.** Building systems that make the right thing the easy thing for whoever comes next. The blueprint as act of love — not "look what I built" but "here is a path you can follow without me."

### 13. The 5 Game moves (Section 6)
1. **Find the Leverage Point** — Where in this system does the smallest push create the largest movement?
2. **Name the Unstated Assumption** — What is everyone treating as given that isn't?
3. **Design for Handoff** — Build it so someone else can run it without you.
4. **Ship the Minimum** — Get the design into contact with reality before it's perfect.
5. **Refactor Kindly** — When the design is wrong, change it without shaming the version that got you here.

### 14. Exile seed
The Architect was exiled when the village mistook *seeing the system* for *not loving the people inside it.* "If you can analyze it, you don't really love it." The Architect said "we can build something better" and was called unfeeling, cold, reductive. The village kept the *outputs* of design — the infrastructure, the procedures, the tools — but exiled the *designer*, which is how you end up with systems nobody remembers how to change.

### 15. Distortion seed
Without the Architect, the village tried to solve structural problems with moral appeals. Endless conversations. No redesign. The same pattern kept appearing because nobody was looking at the system that produced it. *If we just care harder* became the default strategy, and the people who cared hardest burned out first, because caring doesn't change a system — design does.

### 16. Bridge to Chapter 6 (Diplomat)
The Architect can design the system but cannot hold the relational field the design lives inside. A perfect blueprint fails if the people using it don't trust each other. Design assumes goodwill; when goodwill is missing, someone has to go build it. That's the Diplomat.

# Chapter 6: THE DIPLOMAT
## *Green — Relational — Inclusion & Bridge*

### 1. Altitude
- **Color:** Green
- **Developmental name:** Pluralistic / Relational / Inclusion
- **Core thesis:** The terms under which you will walk away define the power of what you stay for.

### 2. Shadow if skipped
Over-accommodation. Conflict avoidance. Peace at any price. Every opinion considered equally — including opinions designed to destroy inclusion itself. Endless processing with no movement.

### 3. Native material
**Relational field / multiplicity / inclusion / bridging.** The capacity to hold multiple truths at once, to stay in relationship across difference, and to bring people who cannot yet see each other into contact.

### 4. The 5 Channels — Modes of Relational Work
1. **The Bridger** — connecting people across difference.
2. **The Translator** — putting one camp's truth into the other camp's language.
3. **The Convener** — creating the container where conversation can happen.
4. **The Boundary-Keeper** — naming who is and isn't in this field, and why.
5. **The Repair-Worker** — restoring trust after rupture.

### 5. The stage sequence
**Field → Invite → Hold → Repair** (4 stages)
- **Field:** Sense the relational terrain — who is here, what they carry, what's at stake.
- **Invite:** Bring people into contact deliberately, with clear terms.
- **Hold:** Stay in the room when it gets hard. Don't collapse the tension prematurely.
- **Repair:** When trust breaks — and it will — name it and stay.

### 6. Transcend moves (+2 energy)
1. **Tension → Fertile conflict** (the disagreement becomes the material)
2. **Disagreement → Dialogue** (positions become questions)
3. **Rupture → Deeper trust** (the break survived becomes the bond)
4. **Exclusion-pain → Naming-work** (your wound becomes the tool that helps others)
5. **Polarization → Bridge** (the gap becomes the structure you build across)

### 7. Generative Translate moves (+1 energy)
- **Bridger → Translator** (once connected, help them speak each other)
- **Convener → Boundary-Keeper** (once the container exists, protect its terms)
- **Boundary-Keeper → Repair-Worker** (protecting the container requires knowing how to mend it)

### 8. Control Translate moves (−1 energy)
- **Relational field → Conflict avoidance** (keep the peace by erasing the difference)
- **Inclusion → Tolerance of the intolerant** (radical openness that destroys the field)
- **Harmony → Flattening-of-difference** (everyone agrees because nobody is real)
- **Bridge → Fawning** (the Diplomat disappears into the conflict they're trying to hold)

### 9. Flow cycle
**Field → Invite → Hold → Rupture → Repair → Field.** Rupture is part of the cycle, not a failure of it. The Diplomat who never has ruptures is not actually holding a real field.

### 10. The 8 Gates at Green altitude

| Gate | Says | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Protector** | *Not safe to enter this room. These people will ask you to choose, and you can't. Stay outside.* | Keep you from entering relational fields that will cost you. |
| **Controller** | *If you let this conflict happen between them, it'll spread to you. Keep the lid on. Smooth it.* | Contain relational tension before it touches you. |
| **Skeptic** | *Is this even my bridge to build? Who appointed me? Am I just performing being relational?* | Doubt your standing before someone else can question it. |
| **Fixer** | *The tension is the problem. Resolve it. Get everyone to agree. Find the compromise.* | Convert holding-tension into solving-tension prematurely. |
| **Fear** | *If you stay in this conversation, someone will be hurt. Someone will leave. You'll be held responsible.* | Rehearse the rupture as catastrophe. |
| **Victim** | *I can't hold this field because I was the one excluded. I don't have the patience to build bridges for the people who kept me out.* | Keep the story alive of why you can't bridge. |
| **Damaged Self** | *I'm too wounded to be a neutral party. Everyone can see my bias. I'm not objective enough to be trusted here.* | Identify yourself as unfit for the role. |
| **Vulnerable Child** | *I can still see the human in everyone. I believe connection is possible. I hold the open hand without collapsing.* | Hold the superpower. |

### 11. Through-line
**Your capacity to stay in relationship across difference without losing yourself or betraying what you stand for.**

### 12. Vulnerable Child's superpower
**Honest multiplicity.** Standing for something *and* making room for others to stand for theirs. The Diplomat's gift is not neutrality — it's *the capacity to be a partisan who can still hold the field.* To know your own walk-away price and name it, so that your staying means something.

### 13. The 5 Game moves (Section 6)
1. **Name the Field** — State out loud what this conversation is actually about and what it is not.
2. **Translate Across Camps** — Take what side A is saying and say it in side B's language.
3. **Hold the Walk-Away Price** — Know what would cause you to leave this field, and say it.
4. **Repair After Rupture** — When trust breaks, name it, name your part, stay.
5. **Refuse False Equivalence** — Not all positions deserve equal weight. Say so.

### 14. Exile seed
The Diplomat was exiled when the village decided that bridges were a form of betrayal — that you can't be both "for us" and "talking to them." The Diplomat said "I stay in conversation with everyone who will stay in conversation with me" and was called a fence-sitter, unreliable, insufficiently-partisan. The village kept the *rituals* of inclusion but lost the *bridgers*, which is how you end up with camps that can only talk about each other, never to each other.

### 15. Distortion seed
Without the Diplomat, camps hardened. Difference became enemy. Inclusion became purity-testing. *Building bridges* became a slur. The capacity to hold multiple truths collapsed into tribal loyalty tests, and the cost was that the village lost the ability to heal after every rupture. Every disagreement became a breakup, because nobody was left who knew how to repair.

### 16. Bridge to Chapter 7 (Sage)
The Diplomat can hold multiple camps at a single altitude but cannot see all 6 altitudes at once and move between them. When a conflict is actually *altitudinal* — when one camp is playing a Red game and the other is playing a Green game — the Diplomat alone cannot name that. That requires the Sage.

# Chapter 7: THE SAGE
## *Teal — Integration — Perspective & Liberation*

### 1. Altitude
- **Color:** Teal
- **Developmental name:** Integral / Integrative / Post-conventional
- **Core thesis:** Mastery is knowing which game you're playing and being able to put it down.

### 2. Shadow if skipped
Loneliness. Dissociation. The curse of seeing too much. *OR the opposite:* stuck in one altitude, unable to meet people where they are, unable to switch games when the situation calls for it.

### 3. Native material
**Perspective / integration / meta-view.** The capacity to see all 6 faces at once, to know which altitude is being played at any given moment, and to move between them without losing yourself.

### 4. The 5 Channels — Modes of Sight
1. **The Panoramic Seer** — seeing all 6 altitudes at once.
2. **The Altitude-Switcher** — moving between altitudes deliberately.
3. **The Diagnostician** — naming which altitude is at play right now.
4. **The Liberator** — putting down a game when it's no longer serving.
5. **The Returner** — coming back to lower altitudes without condescension.

### 5. The stage sequence
**See → Switch → Serve → Release** (4 stages)
- **See:** Take in the whole field of altitudes.
- **Switch:** Move to the altitude the situation actually requires.
- **Serve:** Do the work that altitude calls for, without superiority.
- **Release:** When the game is done — or when it's no longer serving — put it down.

### 6. Transcend moves (+2 energy)
1. **Isolation → Perspective** (the loneliness of seeing becomes the clarity of seeing)
2. **Overwhelm → Altitude-switching** (too much to hold becomes the choice of where to stand)
3. **Paralysis → Strategic release** (can't act becomes "put this game down")
4. **Meta-view → Ground-level service** (seeing becomes serving *at altitude*)
5. **Seeing-too-much → Naming-the-game** (the curse becomes the gift)

### 7. Generative Translate moves (+1 energy)
- **Panoramic → Diagnostic** (once seen, can name)
- **Altitude-Switcher → Returner** (once moving, can come back)
- **Diagnostician → Liberator** (naming the game is the first step in being able to put it down)

### 8. Control Translate moves (−1 energy)
- **Perspective → Detachment-that-abandons** (seeing becomes leaving)
- **Integration → Spiritual-bypass** (wholeness becomes a story that skips the pain)
- **Meta-view → Superiority** (altitude becomes status)
- **Seeing → Narrating-at-people** (turning the view into a monologue)

### 9. Flow cycle
**See → Switch → Serve → Release → See (wider now) → Switch → ...** The Sage's cycle is open-ended. Every release leaves you able to see more, which reveals new altitudes, which reveals new games to play — and to put down.

### 10. The 8 Gates at Teal altitude

| Gate | Says | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Protector** | *Not safe to share what you see. People won't understand. You'll sound crazy. Keep the seeing private.* | Keep the Sage's vision hidden so the Sage doesn't get ostracized. |
| **Controller** | *If you let yourself see everything, you'll never act. Seeing is the enemy of deciding. Keep focused.* | Contain the panoramic vision so you stay functional. |
| **Skeptic** | *Is this 'seeing' just me constructing a flattering narrative? Am I actually integrating or just confusing myself?* | Doubt the integration before someone else can call it self-deception. |
| **Fixer** | *Stop watching. Act. Do something with what you see. Seeing without doing is decadence.* | Convert vision into action prematurely, before the switching is clean. |
| **Fear** | *If you see all of this, you become responsible for all of it. You'll never put it down again.* | Rehearse the burden of the meta-view. |
| **Victim** | *I see too much because I've been hurt too many times. My pattern-recognition is scar tissue, not wisdom.* | Keep the story alive that the seeing is damage. |
| **Damaged Self** | *There's something wrong with how I process the world. Other people don't see this. I'm broken in the direction of overthinking.* | Identify the panoramic capacity as defect, not gift. |
| **Vulnerable Child** | *I want to be in the game AND see the game. I want to belong AND see.* | Hold the superpower. |

### 11. Through-line
**Your capacity to see the whole without leaving the parts, and to stay engaged without being consumed.**

### 12. Vulnerable Child's superpower
**Embodied panoramic vision.** The capacity to see all altitudes *and* still choose to stand somewhere. The Sage's gift is not detachment — it is *committed seeing.* You can see the whole game and still play your piece of it, without superiority and without hiding.

### 13. The 5 Game moves (Section 6)
1. **Name the Altitude** — Call out what altitude this conversation is happening at.
2. **Switch Altitudes Deliberately** — Move up for context, down for action.
3. **Return Without Condescension** — Come back to lower altitudes with dignity intact for everyone.
4. **Put a Game Down** — Identify a game that is no longer serving and exit it cleanly.
5. **Hold the Meta Without Losing the Ground** — Stay embodied while seeing.

### 14. Exile seed
The Sage was exiled when the village decided that *seeing* meant *leaving.* That if you could see the pattern of the game, you were obliged to stop playing. The Sage said "I see this and I'm staying" and was called contradictory, uncommitted, insufficiently-partisan. The village kept a caricature of the Sage — the guru, the wise-one-at-a-distance — but exiled the actual Sage, which is the person who sees the whole thing and still sets the table.

### 15. Distortion seed
Without the Sage, the village lost the ability to name which altitude they were stuck at. Every conflict became existential. Nobody could step back and say *we're playing the Red game, not the Green one — that's why this isn't working.* The capacity to switch altitudes was lost. And a shadow Sage emerged: the person who saw everything and committed to nothing, mistaking perspective for wisdom.

### 16. Bridge — The Return
The Sage is the last Face in the ladder, but not the end of the book. Having walked all 6 altitudes, the Sage *returns* — not above the village, not separate from it, but carrying all the altitudes inside them, able to play any game the village needs played. This is where the book closes: the player, having mastered the 6 Faces, comes home able to be an ally at whichever altitude the moment requires.

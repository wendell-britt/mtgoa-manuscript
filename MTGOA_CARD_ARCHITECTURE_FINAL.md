# MTGOA Card Architecture — Final Design
## Player Deck (The Quiet Year Fractal) + GM Toolkit (Foundations Worldbuilding)

**Date**: 2026-04-20
**Status**: Architecture Approved and Ready for Card Design

---

## Core Insight: Two Systems, One Game

The bars-engine is a **worldbuilding game about allyship campaigns**. This requires two complementary systems:

1. **Player Deck** (The Quiet Year Model): Linear, cyclical, personal practice progression
2. **GM Toolkit** (Foundations Model): Non-linear, worldbuilding, campaign context creation

They work together: **Players navigate the allyship journey through a world GMs design.**

---

## System 1: PLAYER DECK (The Quiet Year Fractal)

### Structure

**4 Suits = 4 Temporal Phases + 4 Allyship Domains**

```
SPRING (Wake Up)          →  Gathering Resources (Hearts)
SUMMER (Grow Up)          →  Skillful Organizing (Diamonds)
FALL (Show Up)            →  Direct Action (Clubs)
WINTER (Clean Up)         →  Raising Awareness (Spades)

13 cards per suit = 52 cards total
One card per week = 52-week cycle = one year
```

### Gameplay: The 52-Week Cycle

**Week 1-13 (Spring):**
- Draw Hearts card each week
- Answer the question about gathering resources
- Fill BAR card back
- Update the Allyship Map (optional)

**Week 14-26 (Summer):**
- Draw Diamonds card each week
- Answer the question about organizing
- Fill BAR card back
- Update the Allyship Map

**Week 27-39 (Fall):**
- Draw Clubs card each week
- Answer the question about direct action
- Fill BAR card back
- Track tensions on cards (Tension tokens optional)
- Update the Allyship Map

**Week 40-52 (Winter):**
- Draw Spades card each week
- Answer the question about raising awareness
- Fill BAR card back
- Reflect on the year
- Finalize Allyship Map

### Cyclical Replay

**Year 2+ (Deepening):**
- Same 52 cards, but player brings evolved understanding
- BAR cards now show "Year 1 notes" vs "Year 2 notes"
- Allyship Map shows new regions/depth discovered
- Cards show mastery progression (how this moment deepens each year)

## System 2: GM TOOLKIT (Foundations Worldbuilding)

### Purpose

GMs use the Foundations model to create the **world** players are doing allyship work IN. This gives context, constraints, and challenges that feed into player card draws.

### Structure: 4 Worldbuilding Domains

**Spades (Geography):**
- Physical landscape, resources, logistics
- "Where is this allyship happening? What terrain shapes it?"
- Questions: What geography enables/constrains action? Where are the bottlenecks?

**Hearts (Social):**
- Relationships, power structures, culture
- "Who holds power? How do people relate? What's the culture?"
- Questions: What social dynamics are at play? Who's excluded? Where's the tension?

**Clubs (History):**
- Precedents, what came before, lessons from the past
- "What happened before? What patterns repeat?"
- Questions: What historical patterns are you breaking? What was tried before?

**Diamonds (Magic/Mystery):**
- What's possible beyond the visible, transcendent possibilities, hidden resources
- "What's not yet visible? What's the potential?"
- Questions: What transcends this situation? What's the gift beneath the problem?

### GM Process: Building the Campaign World

**Before the game starts, GMs:**

1. **Define Geography (Spades):**
   - Where is this allyship campaign happening? (City? Rural? Online? Mixed?)
   - What physical resources exist? (Infrastructure, spaces, barriers)
   - What are the logistics? (How far apart are people? Travel time? Accessibility?)
   - What seasons/cycles affect the work? (Agricultural season? Academic calendar? Political cycle?)

2. **Define Social Structure (Hearts):**
   - Who holds formal power? (Politicians, institutions, businesses)
   - Who holds relational power? (Elders, influencers, trusted voices)
   - What's the cultural baseline? (What's "normal" behavior here?)
   - What tensions already exist? (Conflicts, historical grievances, fault lines)
   - Who's been excluded from this work before?

3. **Define History (Clubs):**
   - What similar campaigns have happened here? (What worked? What failed?)
   - What patterns do people carry? (Generational trauma, institutional memory)
   - What was the last moment of hope? (What did people believe was possible?)
   - What's the narrative people tell about change? (Cynical? Hopeful? Stuck?)

4. **Define Magic/Mystery (Diamonds):**
   - What's not yet visible but could be? (Allies who haven't shown up, resources not yet mobilized)
   - What's the gift hidden in the problem? (What could allyship teach this community?)
   - What transcends the obvious constraints? (Where's the generative possibility?)
   - What's the world's invitation? (What wants to be born through this work?)

### GM Output: Campaign Brief

GMs create a **Campaign Brief** (1-2 pages) that:
- Describes the geography (where this happens)
- Sketches the social dynamics (who's who, what's at stake)
- Notes the history (what came before, patterns)
- Names the mystery (what could be possible)

This brief is NOT the player experience—it's what the GM holds, uses to contextualize challenges, and reveals through cards/prompts.

### Integration: How GM World Feeds Into Player Cards

**Example:**

Player draws "7 of Clubs" (Fall/Direct Action): "What barrier are you avoiding?"

**Without GM worldbuilding context:**
- Card is generic, player answers from their own life

**With GM worldbuilding context (Geography/Hearts/Clubs/Diamonds):**
- GM knows the geography means travel is hard (rural area, bad roads)
- GM knows the social structure means speaking up publicly is risky (tight community, reputation matters)
- GM knows the history shows previous direct action was met with retaliation
- GM knows the mystery is that underground networks exist (unvisible until you move)

So when player draws the card, GM can:
- Ask: "What barrier does the geography create for you right now?"
- Or: "Given the risk you know about, what are you avoiding saying?"
- Or: "What would moving look like given what happened before?"
- Or: "What ally might emerge if you acted?"

The same card, contextualized by the world, creates different prompts and possibilities.

## How the Two Systems Work Together

### During Play

**Player Deck creates the** → personal, linear, cyclical practice journey
**GM Toolkit contextualizes** → through the world they're working IN

```
Player draws card: "What barrier are you avoiding?" (7 of Clubs)
↓
GM uses Foundations toolkit: "The geography creates risk, the history shows retaliation, the mystery shows hidden allies"
↓
Player engages with the card in the context of this specific world
↓
Player fills BAR card with their breakthrough/action/reflection/sustain
↓
Map shows how this moment affects the allyship landscape
```

### Across the Year

**Spring (Wake):**
- Player gathers resources
- GM reveals geography and initial social contacts
- Map shows "what we have"

**Summer (Grow):**
- Player organizes
- GM reveals tensions and coordination needs (based on social structure)
- Map shows "how we're connected"

**Fall (Show):**
- Player takes direct action
- GM reveals barriers and (based on mystery domain) unexpected opportunities
- Map shows "what we're confronting"

**Winter (Clean):**
- Player reflects and raises awareness
- GM reveals what changed, what came from action, what the world learned
- Map shows "what this meant"

## Key Differences from Previous Versions

### Previous (Problematic):
- 52-card deck organized by emotion or domain alone
- Didn't scale to other books
- GMs had no worldbuilding toolkit
- Player experience was isolated from world-building

### New (Integrated):
- Player deck uses **temporal + domain** (scales across books)
- GM toolkit uses **Foundations worldbuilding** (creates world context)
- Temporal phases are universal (Wake/Grow/Show/Clean)
- Allyship domains are MTGOA-specific (Gathering/Organizing/Action/Awareness)
- Worldbuilding domains are universal (Geography/Social/History/Magic)
- Each system is internally coherent AND they integrate seamlessly

## Design Phases

### Phase 1: Design the 52 Player Cards
- Create all card text for each domain + temporal phase
- Each card has front (question) + back (BAR template)
- Include Guide lens (which 6 faces illuminate this moment)
- Include Gate risk (which pattern might activate)
- Map each card to narrative moments in Ch1 if applicable

### Phase 2: Create GM Toolkit
- Create a Campaign Brief template (1-2 page worldbuilding canvas)
- Provide examples of Geography/Social/History/Magic for different contexts
- Show how to use the toolkit to contextualize player card draws
- Create a GM's Reference showing "if player draws X card, consider Y worldbuilding angle"

### Phase 3: Build Game Engine
- Player interface: Draw card → See front → Answer question → Fill BAR back → See map impact
- GM interface: View campaign brief → See which cards are being drawn → Contextualize based on worldbuilding
- Shared interface: Allyship Map (shows week, season, what resources/actions/awareness have been added)

### Phase 4: Test with First Readers
- One player-focused group (does the deck feel right for practice?)
- One GM-focused group (does the toolkit feel useful for world-building?)
- One integrated group (does the world context enhance the player experience?)

## Why This Architecture Works

**For Players:**
- Clear progression (52 weeks, 4 seasons, 4 domains)
- Personal journaling (BAR cards become wisdom artifact)
- Cyclical (can replay, deepen understanding)
- Portable (The Quiet Year template works for any allyship work)

**For GMs:**
- Non-linear toolkit (Foundations model allows creative worldbuilding)
- Contextualizing structure (4 domains help GMs think systematically)
- Flexible (can use as much or little as desired)
- Generative (worldbuilding feeds back into player cards)

**For the Game:**
- Bars-engine becomes a true worldbuilding game about allyship campaigns
- Player experience is linear and contained; world is complex and emergent
- Same template scales to other books (different domains, same phases and worldbuilding structure)
- Shadow work, gift activation, and collective transformation are all supported

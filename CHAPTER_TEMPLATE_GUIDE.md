# Chapter Template Guide
## *How to Write Your Face Chapter Using the Shaman Chapter as Your Model*

---

## What the Shaman Chapter Demonstrates

The Shaman Chapter (Chapter 2: THE SHAMAN — Magenta — Survival/Instinct) is your complete template. It shows:

1. **All 7 required sections** with the correct arc and emotional pacing
2. **How to use the universal template** structure to teach a specific altitude
3. **The voice and style** your chapter should match — warm, direct, mythic, embodied, Wendell-voiced
4. **How long each chapter should be** — **~10,000–12,000 words total** (see Length Guide below for per-section targets)
5. **How to bridge to the next face** (Shaman → Challenger)

**Current reference:** `CHAPTER2_SHAMAN_FULL_DRAFT.md` (10,324 words, last updated 2026-04-16).
When this guide and the living Ch 2 draft disagree, the draft wins. This guide exists to transfer the pattern to Ch 3–7.

## The 7-Section Structure (Required for Every Chapter)

Your chapter must have these 7 sections in this order:

### Section 1: The Exile (~500 words)
**Purpose:** Tell the mythic origin story of why this face left the village, and what the village lost.

**What to answer:**
- Who did the Challenger (or prior face) demand they become?
- What truth did they stop telling?
- What moment did they realize they had to choose: stay invisible or leave?

**Voice note:** This section is mythic and intimate. Tell the story like you're sitting by a fire with someone. Use specific images. Make it real.

**Example from Shaman:**
"There was a time when the Shaman lived in the village..." (tells the slow exile story, the arrival of the Challenger, the gradual loss of trust, the final choice)

---

### Section 2: The Distortion (~800 words)
**Purpose:** Show what the village does with this face's power when the face is gone.

**What to answer:**
- What happens to their gifts when they're not present?
- How does the village misuse or corrupt what this face knows?
- What shadow emerges when this face's wisdom is absent?

**Voice note:** This section has a witnessing quality. You're naming something true that everyone knows but no one says. "Without the Shaman, fear became just a noise."

**Structure:**
- Open with "Here's what happened after [Face] left..."
- Show 3-4 specific corruptions or failures
- End with "This is what the village does..."

**Example from Shaman:**
"The village didn't stop feeling. It just stopped knowing what to do with feelings." Then walks through: fear became noise, anger became dangerous, sadness became shameful, joy became productivity.

---

### Section 3: The Concept (~600-800 words)
**Purpose:** Teach the philosophy/theory of what this face's actual superpower is.

**What to answer:**
- What is [Face's practice] actually?
- Why does it work?
- What is it NOT? (important—clear misconceptions)
- What changes when you understand this correctly?

**Voice note:** This is teaching mode. Clear, direct, no jargon. Contrast wrong with right. "It's not X. It's Y."

**Structure:**
- Open with "Let's be clear about what we mean by..."
- Contrast the corrupted version with the real version
- Build the philosophy step by step
- End with why this is what the village lost

**Example from Shaman:**
"Emotional alchemy is not emotional processing. It's the practice of being in relationship with feeling, and letting those feelings teach you something that changes who you are." Then teaches: emotions as signals, not problems; the five emotions; what each teaches; the stance required.

---

### Section 4: The Practice (~2200-2800 words)
**Purpose:** Give the full mechanical practice system at this altitude. This is the longest and most load-bearing section.

**What to answer:**
- What is the native *material* being worked at this altitude? (Shaman: emotion. Challenger: will/charge. Regent: loyalty/inheritance. Architect: logic/leverage. Diplomat: relational field. Sage: perspective.)
- What are the channels / sub-types of that material?
- What are the stages of working with it?
- What are the named *moves* you can make on that material?
- What is the energy economy — which moves generate, which deplete, which break even?

**Voice note:** Procedural but still intimate. Pair each mechanical element with a short human explanation ("this is the one that does X"). Include concrete scenarios.

**Required sub-components (from Ch 2 Shaman):**

1. **The channels / material map** — For Shaman this is the *5 Emotional Channels* (Metal/Fear, Water/Sadness, Wood/Joy, Fire/Anger, Earth/Neutrality) with what each one detects or teaches. For other Faces, name the altitude-native channels and what each one carries.

2. **The stage sequence** — For Shaman this is the *developmental WAVE* (Wake → Clean → Grow → Show). Every Face chapter needs its own version of "how do you move from first noticing → working the material → integration → action." Name the stages.

3. **The named moves + energy economy** — Shaman uses three move types:
   - **Transcend (+2 energy)** — the 5 Transcend moves: Fear→Excitement, Sadness→Poignancy, Joy→Bliss, Anger→Triumph, Neutrality→Peace
   - **Generative Translate (+1 energy)** — moves across channels that build on flow (e.g., Joy → Anger = vitality becomes action)
   - **Control Translate (-1 energy)** — moves that suppress or redirect material against its nature
   Every Face chapter needs an equivalent: what does it cost you to work this altitude's material well, poorly, or by force?

4. **The flow cycle** — Shaman teaches the natural flow: Joy → Anger → Neutrality → Fear → Sadness → Joy. Each Face should identify its own natural cycle across the altitude's channels.

5. **In-practice scenarios** — At least 2 concrete real-time applications. (Ch 2 has the 10-second somatic protocol and a longer worked example.)

**Voice calibration for this section:** Mechanical without being dry. The user should be able to take each named move into a conversation today.

**Example from Shaman:**
Section 4 runs: "emotion is data" → 5 channels → developmental WAVE (4 stages) → 5 Transcend moves → Generative Translate → Control Translate → flow cycle → two practice scenarios. ~2,500 words.

---

### Section 5: Journey to the Center (~4,500-5,000 words)
**Purpose:** Walk the reader through the 8 Gates at this altitude, ending at the Vulnerable Child where the superpower lives.

This section is the **emotional and structural heart** of every chapter. It is the longest and most important section. Do not shortcut it.

**What to answer:**
- How do all 8 gates show up at THIS altitude? (What does each voice *say*? What is each one *protecting*?)
- What does the Vulnerable Child hold at this altitude — what is the specific superpower at the center of this forest?
- What does it feel like to walk the path?

**Voice note:** Deepest section. Mythic + psychological + direct second-person address. You're guiding the reader *into* their own forest, not describing forests in general.

**Required structure — two passes:**

#### Pass 1: The Map (~700 words)
A short, cognitive overview so the reader knows what's coming. For each of the 8 gates in order, give:
- Gate name
- One-line **"Says:"** (what the voice actually says)
- One-line **"Job:"** (what it's protecting at this altitude)

End the Map with one paragraph naming the **through-line**: what single thing all 8 gates are organized around at this altitude. (For Shaman it's "your capacity to feel." For Challenger it will be something like "your capacity to push." For each Face, name it.)

Close the Map with the core instruction: *You don't fight them. You don't trick them. You walk past them by acknowledging them.*

#### Pass 2: The Walk (~3,800 words — roughly 400-500 words per gate)

Each gate gets its own subsection. Each gate follows this rhythm:

1. **Arrival** — 1-2 sentences of sensory/embodied setup ("You arrive at the first gate and there's somebody there. You probably feel them before you see them…")
2. **Who they are** — what this voice is, where they came from, why they're loyal
3. **What they say** — the actual voice, in quotes or italics, at this altitude
4. **The move** — how you pass this gate (thank it, ask permission, ask a question, set a deadline, name it, let it speak, witness it, pick them up)
5. **Close** — "When you're ready, keep walking." (This phrase recurs at the end of every gate except the last.)

**The gradient across the 8 gates** (light → deeper invitation):
- **Gates 1-2 (Protector, Controller):** Thank them. Ask permission. Relieve them.
- **Gates 3-4 (Skeptic, Fixer):** Ask a question they aren't expecting. Set a deadline.
- **Gate 5 (Fear):** Introduce **naming** as the move. This is where the chapter deepens. Give the reader permission to name the voice (the author's own examples — e.g., "Petty Pendergrass," "jeppi" — model this).
- **Gate 6 (Victim):** Introduce **disowned voice** — let the voice talk *to* you instead of you talking to it. The Victim has receipts. Don't argue. Listen.
- **Gate 7 (Damaged Self):** **Witness without fixing.** No clever move. Just stay.
- **Gate 8 (Vulnerable Child):** No move. Arrival. Hold them. Name the superpower that lives here at this altitude.

**Closing beat after Gate 8:** A short coda (~100-150 words) naming that this walk will be made many times. "You know the path in. That is the [Face]'s gift."

**Example from Shaman:**
See `CHAPTER2_SHAMAN_FULL_DRAFT.md` Section 5 — ~4,800 words total, Map (~750 words) + Walk (~4,000 words). The Walk uses Wendell's own named parts (Petty Pendergrass) at Gate 5 to model the naming move. The coda ends with "The Vulnerable Child is where your superpower lives. Now you know how to find them."

---

### Section 6: The Game (~900-1000 words)
**Purpose:** Show exactly what winning looks like at this altitude and teach 3-5 specific moves.

**What to answer:**
- What is this face's allyship game?
- How do you know when you're winning?
- What are the 3-5 concrete moves you can make TODAY?
- What does mastery look like?

**Voice note:** This is practical + aspirational. You're showing the reader they can do this right now, today, in a real situation.

**Structure:**
- Open with: "Okay. You've learned [concept]. Now—what does it actually look like in a real situation?"
- Give 3-5 named moves (Shaman used 5: Read the Field, Name the Unsaid, Create Safety, Be Real, Stay)
- For EACH move:
  - Short title
  - 1-2 sentence explanation of what it is
  - "Why?" — explain why this move matters
  - **"In practice:"** — concrete steps, dialogue examples, real scenarios
  - Keep each move to ~150-200 words
- Do NOT give abstract theory. Give concrete, actionable moves.

**Example from Shaman:**
Move 1: Read the Field ("Before you respond, feel the room")
Move 2: Name the Unsaid ("Say what's not being said")
Move 3: Create Safety ("Hold another person's emotions without fixing")
Move 4: Be Real ("Don't perform, show up actual")
Move 5: Stay ("Breathe through the difficulty instead of leaving")

Each move has the "why" and the "in practice" so readers know both the principle and the application.

---

### Section 7: Recap & Transition (~400-500 words)
**Purpose:** Close the chapter with what this face taught you, and bridge to the NEXT face.

**What to answer:**
- What is this face's core contribution to allyship?
- What do you carry forward?
- Why is the NEXT face necessary?
- What can't the next face do without this face's work first?

**Voice note:** This is reflective and forward-moving. Dignity. Closure. Hope.

**Structure:**
- "The [Face]'s contribution to the allyship game is this: [one sentence thesis]"
- 2-3 bullet points of what you carry forward from this chapter
- "And here's why this matters for what comes next..."
- Why the next face cannot work without this face's foundation
- Close with concrete tools/practices to take forward
- End with: "Now you're ready for the [Next Face]."

**Example from Shaman:**
"The Shaman's contribution is: before strategy, structure, before any move in the world—there is pattern."
Then: "Take with you the WAVE, the 8 gates, the 5 moves."
"The Challenger cannot work without the Shaman's work done first."
"Every great boundary is rooted in a feeling."
"Now you're ready for the Challenger."

## Optional: Reflection Prompts (BARs)

Each chapter can end with 3 BARs reflection prompts:
- **Breakthrough:** A question about what this face teaches
- **Action:** A practice to try this week
- **Reflection:** What shifts when you apply this work?

Example from Shaman:
- "When have you felt something true in your body that your mind was trying to deny?"
- "Practice the WAVE with one moment from this week."
- "What opens when you listen to feelings instead of managing them?"

## Checklist for Your Chapter

Before you submit your face chapter, verify:

- [ ] **Section 1 (Exile):** Tells a story of why this face left. ~500-700 words.
- [ ] **Section 2 (Distortion):** Shows what the village does wrong without this face. ~800-1000 words.
- [ ] **Section 3 (Concept):** Teaches the philosophy clearly. "It's not X. It's Y." ~600-800 words.
- [ ] **Section 4 (Practice):** Names the altitude-native *material*, the *channels*, the *stages*, the *named moves* (with energy economy), and the *flow cycle*. At least 2 in-practice scenarios. ~2200-2800 words.
- [ ] **Section 5 (Journey):** **Map + Walk two-pass.** Map (~700 words) gives all 8 gates with Says/Job + through-line. Walk (~3800 words) gives each gate 400-500 words of immersive encounter, with the naming move introduced at Gate 5, disowned-voice at Gate 6, witness-without-fixing at Gate 7, arrival at Gate 8. Each gate in the Walk (except Gate 8) ends with "When you're ready, keep walking." ~4500-5000 words total.
- [ ] **Section 6 (Game):** Teaches 3-5 concrete moves with real examples. ~900-1200 words.
- [ ] **Section 7 (Recap):** Closes with what you carry forward + bridges to next face. ~400-600 words.
- [ ] **Voice:** Matches Wendell's voice (see Voice Notes). Use discovery idioms, labor metaphors, named-part self-deprecation, permission-granters. Leave academic voice, passive voice, and toxic positivity at the door.
- [ ] **No jargon:** Every concept is explained. No assumed knowledge.
- [ ] **"In practice" sections:** Every teaching has a concrete application.
- [ ] **Ends at Vulnerable Child:** Section 5 Walk ends with the altitude-specific superpower at the center.
- [ ] **Bridges to next face:** Section 7 explains why the next face is necessary.
- [ ] **Total:** ~10,000-12,000 words.
- [ ] **WAVE-collision resolution applied:** Whichever disambiguation option the author chose (Rename / Nest / Suffix / Keep) is implemented consistently. See `MTGOA_BOOK_WORK_TRACKER.md` for the open action item.

## Questions as You Write

**On The Exile:**
- Who forced this face to leave?
- What truth did they have to stop telling?
- What was the moment of no return?

**On The Distortion:**
- What does the village use this face's power for (wrongly)?
- What gets lost or corrupted?
- What's the shadow side when this face is absent?

**On The Concept:**
- What is this face's actual practice?
- What is it NOT? (important—dispel the misconception)
- Why does this practice matter for allyship?

**On The Practice:**
- What do people actually DO at this level?
- What are the 3-5 moves?
- Can someone practice this in a real conversation, right now?

**On The Journey:**
- How do the 8 gates show up at this specific altitude?
- What's the emotional landscape of this face?
- How does the journey end? (always: Vulnerable Child)

**On The Game:**
- What is winning at this altitude?
- What are the 3-5 concrete moves?
- Can someone do one of these moves today?

**On The Recap:**
- What's the one-sentence thesis of this face's gift?
- Why is the next face necessary?
- What can't happen without this face's work?

It's worth it.

## Voice Notes

Match the Shaman chapter's voice. This is Wendell's voice, calibrated from the Igniting Joy launch transcript and refined across many drafts. Key moves to use:

**Use:**
- **Discovery idioms** — "Here's what I've noticed." "Here's what's true." "Here's the thing about X." Frame insight as something you found, not something you invented.
- **Labor metaphors** — *the work, the practice, the path, on shift, keeping the lid on.* This is embodied, working-hands language.
- **Self-deprecation via named parts** — When teaching a move that involves looking at a part of yourself, show your own. Use the author's own named parts (Petty Pendergrass, jeppi, etc.) as modeling examples. "I have a part of me I call X…"
- **Permission-granters** — "You can." "You don't have to." "It's okay to X." The reader is often being told they can stop doing something they've been doing too long.
- **Direct address** — Second person, constantly. The reader is not "the reader" — they are "you."
- **Weird. But literally.** — Short acknowledgments that a move sounds strange before showing why it works.
- **Short sentences when arriving at truth** — Long exploration paragraphs, then short punch landings.
- **Naming the Big Mind move without jargon** — Instead of "now we will invoke the Protector voice," it's "you arrive at the first gate and there's somebody there."

**Leave at the door:**
- Academic jargon and capital-T Theory voice
- Passive voice ("can be experienced," "is felt")
- Abstract theorizing without an "in practice" hook
- Oversimplifying ("just practice and you'll be fine")
- Toxic positivity ("the world is beautiful")
- Bullet-point-as-explanation (bullets are *structural*, not argumentative — if the argument needs prose, write prose)
- Performative vulnerability (confessional moments must do structural work, not ornament)

## How This Works

1. **Read the Shaman chapter** (CHAPTER2_SHAMAN_FULL_DRAFT.md) — this is your complete template
2. **Follow the 7-section structure** — don't skip or reorder
3. **Use the Reflection Prompts checklist** — verify you have all required elements
4. **Have someone else read section 3 (The Concept)** — it should be crystal clear to someone encountering this idea for the first time
5. **Test your "In practice" sections** — could someone actually do this after reading?
6. **Read your Chapter 7 (Recap) opening** — does it sound like: "[Face]'s contribution to the allyship game is this: [one clear statement]"?

If you hit all these marks, you have a complete, template-ready chapter.

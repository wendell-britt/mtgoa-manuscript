## Manuscript Seating — how the book carries the six

Recorded 2026-08-05, after the manuscript was swept to comply with the Chapter rules above.

### What the manuscript does

Each of the six Face chapters **trains** one superpower. No chapter, and no Face, **owns** one. The
distinction is stated to the reader in the book itself, at `ch2:345`:

> A superpower is not a Face, and no chapter owns one. Each one ahead trains a single superpower,
> the way a school teaches one class: somewhere to stand while you practice it. Out in the village
> any of the six runs at any altitude, in anybody's hands.

The seating is **pedagogy, not taxonomy**. A chapter is where one superpower is easiest to watch
at one altitude — the Alchemist means something after Chapter 3 teaches charge and nothing before
it — and that is the only claim the arrangement makes.

### The six sites, as they now read

| Face chapter | Superpower | The binding, verbatim |
|---|---|---|
| ch3 — Shaman | Alchemist | *That move trains the Alchemist.* |
| ch4 — Challenger | Disruptor | *This chapter trains it as the willingness to be unwelcome on a charge you have checked and chosen to trust.* |
| ch5 — Regent | Storyteller | *That lays the foundation this chapter builds on, and what it trains is the Storyteller.* |
| ch6 — Architect | Strategist | *This chapter trains the Strategist: knowing where the push goes and when to make it.* |
| ch7 — Diplomat | Connector | *This chapter does not train contact; the Bridge-Builder makes that in the first hour. It trains connection that has survived being told the truth.* |
| ch8 — Sage | Escape Artist | *That combination is what this chapter trains, and it trains two things rather than one.* |

Nine edits removed every possessive form (*"the Architect's superpower is…"*) from the manuscript
on 2026-08-05. Two further sites were examined and deliberately left:

- **`ch3:213`** — *"That is the Shaman's superpower"* sits **inside a treatise**, signed by Maera
  Voss, Head of the School of the Body. A character in the book's fiction may speak that way; the
  author's teaching voice may not. The manuscript keeps a hard membrane between those two voices
  and this taxonomy does not reach across it.
- **`ch7:810`** — a chapter-summary bullet binding the Diplomat's twenty **cards** to the
  Connector. Cards are not Face, Domain, or Role, so this is a claim about the deck. Flagged, not
  ruled.

### Coach

`ch8` no longer treats Coach as half of a superpower. The chapter now trains **the Escape Artist**
as the capacity, with **coaching** as the practice the exit makes available — which is this
document's position. There is no seventh primary anywhere in the manuscript.

### What this does not license

- **The quiz still must not map a result to a Face.** Nothing above changes any quiz rule. The
  book's own `ch9:664` tells the reader directly: *"Its answer does not have to match the
  superpower your Face came with."*
- **A superpower is not a chapter's property.** Any of the six may be used as an example or reader
  practice in any Face chapter, per the Chapter rules above.
- **The manuscript is not the taxonomy.** Where the book's wording is narrower than a definition
  above — Storyteller is the clearest case, seated in ch5 on inheritance rather than on public
  imagination — this document governs.

### Open against this section

- **The second names have zero adoption.** *Webweaver, System Seer, Sacred Spark, Framebreaker,
  Emotional Transmuter, Meaning Weaver* appear **0 times** in the manuscript, as do all twelve
  shadow names. A quiz result that leads with a second name hands the reader vocabulary the book
  never uses, and `ch9:664` is the site that sends her to that quiz. Either the book adopts them,
  the quiz leads with the primary name, or the mismatch is accepted on purpose.
- **`System Seer` against `Panoramic Seer`**, which is one of the Sage's five modes at `ch8:340`.
  Two Seers on two different Faces.
- **Storyteller has three live definitions** — this document's, the book's at `ch5:597`, and
  `MTGOA_TEAL_080525.md:16788`'s *"poignance, the art of feeling the weight of loss."* They are
  adjacent rather than contradictory, and nobody has ruled which the quiz should return.

## 🧠 Architect — the load-bearing structure

The 4 parts are not 4 parts. They are **one spine with three names**:

- **Brainstorming** = the **generator** (runs in front of any formula move).
- **Barn Raising** = the **matcher** (runs in front of *enroll allies* and the marketplace).
- **Village vs. Forest** = the **diagnostic** (runs in front of *every* tool call: is this individual work or collective work?).

The marketplace is the **surface** — the place where the matcher lives once the generator and the diagnostic have been done.

**The architecture, in one line:**

> *Diagnose (Village/Forest) → Generate (Brainstorming) → Match (Barn Raising) → List (Marketplace) → Show up (Formula move 4).*

**The load-bearing line:** The book's formula is *already* 4 moves. The 3 new practices do not add a 5th move. They **sit in front of** the formula, in this order: **diagnose before generate, generate before match, match before show up.**

**What the spec gets right (load-bearingly):**

- The 3-stage brainstorm (Woolgathering / Clean Up / Bridge Building) maps cleanly onto the WAVE cycle (Wake / Clean / Grow), and the Show Up move is the formula step itself. **The vocabulary is locked.**
- The 4-move Barn Raising (Name / Inventory / Match / Show up) maps onto formula move 3 (Enroll allies) with **no over-lap**. Barn Raising *is* formula move 3 in book language. The marketplace is its app form.
- The Village/Forest diagnostic maps onto the left/right quadrant shorthand. **The metaphor carries the content; the AQAL stays in the author's head.** This is the right discipline.
- The 6 face inserts (Ch 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6) cover the **full arc** — the diagnostic lands in Ch 1, the generator lands in Ch 2/3/4/6, the matcher lands in Ch 4 (Regent) and Ch 5 (Architect).

**What is over-loaded:**

- The 4 coming-soon stubs (Ch 2/4/5/6) all point to the same marketplace URL. This is **correct** (the marketplace is one surface), but the 4 stubs need to feel like *different invitations* in 4 different voices, not 4 copies of the same line. **The four drafts above do this well** (Shaman / Regent / Architect / Diplomat each have a distinct offline move).
- The Ch 1 insert is the *largest* (400-600 words), because it carries the diagnostic + the generator preview + the cross-reference. The Ch 2/3/4/6 inserts are 600-800 words each, because they carry the formula-move-specific application. The Ch 4/5 Barn Raising inserts are 800-1000 words each, because Barn Raising is itself a full practice. **The chapter word budgets are correct.**

**Big Domino of the inserts (Architect proposal):**

> *The formula works when you diagnose before you generate, generate before you match, match before you show up. The Forest tools (brainstorming + 321) help you generate. The Village tools (Barn Raising + marketplace) help you match. The book's job is to teach you to read which one you need before you reach for the tool.*
> (Every Ch 1 framing, every Ch 2/3/4/6 insert, every Ch 4/5 Barn Raising section is in service of flipping this one domino.)

Sourced from the spec's own framing + the user's WAVE vocabulary + the existing 4-move formula. This is the **single line** the reader can carry into the app and into the marketplace. It's the Big Domino of the whole insertion.

## ⚔️ Challenger — what to fight, what to refuse

The 4 inserts are *generous* and *complete* and that is **exactly the failure mode** the book needs to refuse. The Challenger's job is to fight for *the load-bearing move* and refuse the rest.

**Challenge #1: The "Village vs. Forest" metaphor is doing too much.**
The metaphor is supposed to be a *diagnostic* — read the situation, pick the tool. But the spec also uses it as a *taxonomy* (Forest = solo work, Village = collective work) and a *value claim* (the book teaches both, the old allyship taught only the Village). **These are three different jobs.** The reader can hold the diagnostic; the reader will *balk* at the value claim ("the old allyship taught only the Village" is unfair to a lot of historic organizing work). **My challenge: keep the diagnostic. Drop the value claim. Add the taxonomy to Appendix B as a back-of-book reference, not a Ch 1 framing.** The Ch 1 framing should be *just* the diagnostic + the 3-stage preview.

**Challenge #2: "Woolgathering" is doing two jobs.**
"Woolgathering" names the *stage* (volume, no judgment) and the *attitude* (daydream, scatter, follow the thread). The scatter-attitude is what the borrowed-genius sub-mode needs (you have to *loosen* to borrow a voice). The volume-stage is what the standard Stage 1 needs (you have to *generate*). The spec uses "woolgathering" for both. **My challenge: the borrowed-genius sub-mode is its own subsection, not a Stage 1 variant.** The reader who comes to Stage 1 expecting scatter gets volume; the reader who comes to borrowed genius expecting volume gets scatter. Two jobs, two names. (Consider: "Woolgathering" for Stage 1, "Borrowed Voice" for the sub-mode.)

**Challenge #3: The 3-stage brainstorm is mapped onto WAVE (Wake / Clean / Grow).**
This is a clean mapping in the spec, but it **collapses two of the book's existing moves**. The WAVE cycle in the book is *Wake Up → Clean Up → Grow Up → Show Up → Come Back*. The 3-stage brainstorm is *Wake → Clean → Grow*. **That's three of the five WAVE moves, with the Show Up and Come Back moved out of the brainstorm and into the formula step.** The mapping works, but it asks the reader to hold *two* parallel 3-stage / 4-step vocabularies (WAVE: 5 stages, Brainstorm: 3 stages, Formula: 4 steps, WAVE: 5 stages again). **My challenge: pick one WAVE terminology. Either keep WAVE for the brainstorm and rename the formula, or drop WAVE from the brainstorm and keep it for the reader's life-cycle.** Recommendation: drop WAVE from the brainstorm. The 3 stages are *Woolgathering / Clean Up / Bridge Building*. The WAVE cycle stays in Ch 8 (the player's life-cycle, post-book).

**Refusal — what I will not approve:**

- **Do not merge the 4 face-voice inserts into a single "Brainstorming in the Book" subsection that runs at the end of one chapter.** The face-voices are the signal. A merged subsection is a generic explanation. **Hold the per-chapter inserts.**
- **Do not make the Village/Forest a "this is your type" framing.** The metaphor is a *diagnostic*, not an *assignment*. The reader who finishes Ch 1 thinking "I am a Forest person" has misread the book. **Hold the diagnostic.**
- **Do not ship the marketplace with a "browse the open marketplace" default landing page.** The Challenger's rule holds: **every listing declares a move + a face + a resource category**. The default landing page is *filtered to the reader's superpower + face + move*, not a generic open board. **Hold the rule.**

**Challenge to the user's own framing (the marketplace CTA):** The Ch 2 / Ch 4 / Ch 5 / Ch 6 coming-soon stubs point to the same URL. The reader who finishes the book has hit 4 marketplace invitations. The reader who reads only Ch 6 has hit 1. **My challenge: collapse the 4 stubs into 1 stub at the end of the book (in Appendix B), and lift the strongest offline move from each into the chapter insert itself.** This kills the "4 invitations" feel and replaces it with "1 invitation, 4 chapter-tied examples." The reader who hits Appendix B has the full marketplace; the reader who reads Ch 4 has the index card. Cleaner.

## 🎭 Diplomat — what to harmonize, what to bridge

The 4 inserts run on **5 internal frequencies** and they need to be tuned, not just sequenced.

**Frequency 1: The diagnostic voice (Ch 1's Village/Forest).** "There are two kinds of allyship work..." — the voice of *naming*. The reader is being shown a distinction they may not have held before. Source: AQAL shorthand + the user's existing Forest teaching. Tone: "I have seen this distinction and I am naming it for you."

**Frequency 2: The generator voice (Ch 2/3/4/6's Woolgathering).** "Set a 20-min timer. Write every moment you can remember when you acted without effort." — the voice of *practice*. The reader is being asked to do a thing. Source: the brainstorming skill, 321 panel, borrowed genius. Tone: "Here is the move. Try it now."

**Frequency 3: The matcher voice (Ch 4/5's Barn Raising).** "A barn can be raised with index cards. Here is the card." — the voice of *tradition + system*. The reader is being shown a community practice. Source: Marshall Ganz, the Amish metaphor, the marketplace audit. Tone: "We have always done this. Here is how we do it now."

**Frequency 4: The first-person confessional (the Coming Soon stubs).** "The marketplace is coming. The moves are not. You can text right now." — the voice of *permission + immediacy*. The reader is being told they don't have to wait. Source: Move 7 — Wendell. Tone: "I am telling you what is true and what you can do about it today."

**Frequency 5: The game-grammar (Appendix B + the spec).** "Stage 1: Woolgathering. Stage 2: Clean Up. Stage 3: Bridge Building." — the voice of *structure*. Source: the existing Token System + WAVE. Tone: "Here are the controls. You will need them."

These five voices are all *correct*. The risk is **the reader is asked to move through 5 voices in 7 chapter inserts**. Each insert is a *voice change*. The voice protocol holds the line: same flat delivery, same register, no escalation. The inserts mostly hold this. Where they don't, it is because Frequency 2 (generator) and Frequency 3 (matcher) are *both* prescriptive and can blur.

**Diplomat's bridge:**

- **Ch 1 (diagnostic + generator preview).** Hold both voices. The diagnostic lands in the first 200 words; the generator preview lands in the second 200 words. The transition is "Before you generate anything, read this." → "When you're ready, here's the tool." Two breaths apart.
- **Ch 2/3/4/6 (generator).** Hold the practice voice. The face-voice lands in the Borrowed Genius paragraph at the end. Don't merge them.
- **Ch 4/5 (matcher).** Hold the tradition voice (Ch 4) and the system voice (Ch 5). The Regent's "long table" is the tradition voice; the Architect's "match engine" is the system voice. They are *the same practice viewed from two angles*. The chapter transition (Ch 4 → Ch 5) needs one bridge sentence that names the relationship. ("A tradition without a system collapses. A system without a tradition is empty.")
- **Appendix B (game-grammar).** Hold the structure voice. The appendix is the *reference*, not the *experience*. Reader returns to it, doesn't read it linearly.

**What the inserts bridge *for the reader*:**

- The reader who comes to Ch 1 skeptical of the game frame is met with the *Village/Forest diagnostic* — the framing is a *diagnostic*, not an *argument*. The skeptic is not argued with; the skeptic is given a tool.
- The reader who comes to Ch 2 paralyzed by the question "what is my superpower?" is met with the *Woolgathering practice* — the practice is *generative*, not *evaluative*. The reader does not have to know the answer to start.
- The reader who comes to Ch 4 exhausted by solo allyship is met with the *Barn Raising practice* — the practice is *collective*, not *individual*. The reader is not asked to do more alone; they are asked to *show up with others*.
- The reader who comes to Ch 6 worried about sustaining the practice is met with the *Bridge Building* stage — the practice is *human-sized*, not *heroic*. The reader is not asked to do it all; they are asked to *do the next 90 minutes*.

**Diplomat's single request:** Add a one-sentence bridge at the Ch 4 → Ch 5 transition that names the relationship between the Regent's tradition voice and the Architect's system voice. **Without this, the reader experiences Ch 4 and Ch 5 as two chapters about the same thing, not as one chapter from two angles.** "A tradition without a system collapses. A system without a tradition is empty." holds the line.

## 🌊 Shaman — the shadow read, the metaphysical undercurrent

The 4 inserts are doing **a complete alchemical operation** but they are not naming it as such. The Shaman's read:

**The alchemical structure of the unified insert:**

**Ch 1 — Nigredo (the dark night).** "There are two kinds of allyship work." The reader is shown that the *old* allyship taught only the Village (collective) side. The reader is shown that *this* book teaches the Forest (individual) side too. The reader is told: *you have been working in one half of the world.* The dark night is the realization that *the allyship you've been doing has been missing the half where the work actually starts.*

**Ch 2/3 (Woolgathering) — Solve et Coagula (the dissolution).** "Set a 20-min timer. Write every moment." The reader is *dissolved* into their raw material. The persona dissolves; what remains is the felt sense of *what they have actually done*. The Shaman's signal-check (the few breaths before Stage 1) is the *vas* — the vessel that holds the operation.

**Ch 4/5 (Barn Raising) — Citrinitas (the yellowing).** "A barn can be raised with index cards." The reader's raw material is *illuminated* by the community. The work the reader has been doing alone is *shown to be a part of a larger work* — the same work the community has been doing for generations. The illumination is *not* the marketplace (which is the surface); the illumination is *the recognition* that the reader is not alone in the work.

**Ch 6 (Bridge Building) — Rubedo (the gold).** "The smallest human-sized unit." The reader's work is *re-formed* into a 90-minute version. The gold is not the allyship; the gold is the *next 90 minutes* of showing up. The operation closes when the reader has a thing they can do this week.

**Appendix B — The Opus (the whole work).** The reader has a *complete practice* — diagnose, generate, match, show up — that they can return to across a lifetime of allyship. The book is the *opera*; the reader is the *operator*; the marketplace is the *workshop*.

The 4 inserts are *already* a complete alchemical operation. They are *modest about it* — they name the stages (Woolgathering, Clean Up, Bridge Building) but they do not name the alchemy. **This is correct.** The reader's body does the operation before the reader's mind names it. That is the Shaman's craft.

**Shadow read on the 4 inserts:**

| Insert | The alchemical operation | The shadow risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ch 1 (Village/Forest)** | dissolution of "allyship is one thing" | reader treats the diagnostic as a *taxonomy* (I am a Forest person) instead of a *practice* (I read the situation) |
| **Ch 2/3 (Woolgathering)** | dissolution of persona into raw material | reader "did the work" because they generated a list — *cognitive discharge* |
| **Ch 4/5 (Barn Raising)** | illumination of individual work as part of community tradition | reader treats the marketplace as a *job board*, not a *barn raising* — *the felt sense collapses* |
| **Ch 6 (Bridge Building)** | re-formation into the 90-minute version | reader builds the *plan* but doesn't *do the 90 minutes* — *the gold stays in the vas* |

**Shaman's single flag:** The Ch 1 insert is *too diagnostic*. The reader is given a distinction (Village/Forest) but not given a felt sense of what the distinction *feels like* in the body. **Recommendation:** add one Shaman-voice paragraph to Ch 1's insert that names the *felt difference* between Forest work (alone, internal, slow) and Village work (collective, external, fast). The reader's body will recognize the distinction before the reader's mind names it. (Likely placement: between the "diagnostic question" and the "3 brainstorming stages introduced as one tool" sections.)

**Shaman's shadow of the marketplace:** The marketplace is *the surface where the work becomes visible*. But the *felt sense* of barn raising is *the room where people are showing up for each other*, not the URL where they post listings. **The marketplace is the workshop; the barn is the work; the room is the body.** Recommendation: the Ch 4/5 Barn Raising inserts need a *field-holding paragraph* (Diplomat's voice, but Shaman's felt sense) that names the *physical room* — the kitchen, the church basement, the bar, the back yard — where the barn raising actually happens. The marketplace is the *registration*; the room is the *work*.

## 📖 Sage — the integrative read, the game-selection read

The Sage asks one question of the unified insert: *which game is this playing, and is it honest about the game?*

**The insert is playing 4 games, all legitimate:**

1. **The Diagnostic game (Ch 1).** The reader is being shown a distinction they can use to read their situation. Game = "name the situation so you can act from a clearer ground."
2. **The Generator game (Ch 2/3/4/6).** The reader is being given a practice to produce options they did not have. Game = "produce the raw material from which the right move can be seen."
3. **The Matcher game (Ch 4/5).** The reader is being shown a tradition + a system for matching resources to needs. Game = "make the match visible so the community can support it."
4. **The Bridge game (Ch 6).** The reader is being given a practice to break the work into human-sized steps. Game = "build the bridge from where you are to where the work needs you."

All four games are legitimate. The unified insert is honest about all four.

**What the insert is *not* playing (and should not):**

- It is not playing the *Framework game* (the face-specific move frameworks live in Ch 2–Ch 6 already; the inserts add *practice* to those chapters, not *new frameworks*).
- It is not playing the *Movement game* (the Reader's Oath and Title of Liberty live in Ch 8; the inserts do not invoke them).
- It is not playing the *Selling game* (the Stack Slide lives in Ch 7–Ch 8; the inserts do not sell the formula or the book).
- It is not playing the *Awareness Domain* exclusively (the book teaches the Forest and the Village; the insert teaches *both*).

**The insert is honest about all four games it is playing. The Sage's verdict: the insert is playing the right games.**

**Sage's read on the Marketplace CTA moment:**

The marketplace CTA is *the most-load-bearing moment in the book for the app*. If the reader hits a dead link, the book has lost the most-expensive part of the bet (the connection from book to app to allyship). If the reader hits a working marketplace, the book has earned the rest of the bet (the connection from book to community to allyship).

The 4 coming-soon stubs (Ch 2/4/5/6) are *the right moments* for the CTA, **but they are not the only moments**. Appendix B is the *full* moment. The 4 stubs are the *invitations*; the appendix is the *invitation* + the *direction*. **The right move:** Appendix B is the *only* hard CTA (with a full page on what the marketplace is, what it costs, what the player gets). The 4 stubs are *nudges* (1-line invitations, pointing to the appendix). This kills the "4 invitations" feel and replaces it with "4 nudges, 1 destination."

**Sage's read on the Village/Forest as a teaching instrument:**

The Village/Forest distinction is *the most-teachable content in the insert*. A reader who reads only Ch 1's insert and the Appendix B diagnostic has the spine of the entire insert. A reader who reads the 7 inserts (Ch 1/2/3/4/5/6 + Appendix B) and the marketplace audit has a fully usable training. The Village/Forest distinction is the *single point of leverage* in the insert.

This means: **The Village/Forest diagnostic should be the most visually anchored content in the insert** (a single page, a single diagram, a single sentence that survives a flip-through). The Appendix B diagnostic table (Forest work / Village work / tool / move) is the canonical visual. **Hold the table.**

**Sage's single request:** Promote the Village/Forest diagnostic to a *single page* in Appendix B (with the canonical table). The Ch 1 insert introduces the distinction; the Appendix B page is the *one place the reader returns to* when they forget. The marketplace landing page should also carry the diagnostic as the default filter (Forest work / Village work / both).

## 🎭 Tensions — where the faces disagree

**Tension 2: 4 stubs vs. 1 stub + 4 nudges (Challenger) vs. 4 stubs with distinct voices (Diplomat).**
Challenger wants 1 stub at the back of the book. Diplomat wants the per-chapter stub voices preserved. This kills the "4 invitations" feel without losing the face-voices. **Net change: 4 stub trims + 1 Appendix B expansion.**

**Tension 3: WAVE vocabulary in the brainstorm (Challenger) vs. clean 3-stage naming (Architect).**
Challenger wants the 3 stages *not* to map onto WAVE (which would collapse two of the book's existing moves). Architect agrees the 3-stage naming is clean. **Resolution:** drop the WAVE mapping from the brainstorm spec. The 3 stages are *Woolgathering / Clean Up / Bridge Building*. The WAVE cycle stays in Ch 8 (the player's life-cycle, post-book). The reader can hold two parallel 3-stage / 4-step vocabularies if they are *named* differently; the reader cannot hold them if they are *called* the same thing. **Net change: 1 vocabulary change in the spec.**

**Tension 4: The marketplace as a CTA (Sage) vs. the marketplace as a felt sense (Shaman).**
Sage wants the marketplace to be the *full* destination at the back of the book. Shaman wants the marketplace to be the *surface* with the *room* (the kitchen, the church basement) as the *work*. **Resolution:** both are right. The marketplace is the *full destination* (Sage); the room is the *work that happens because of the marketplace* (Shaman). The Appendix B page carries the *full marketplace description*; the Ch 4/5 Barn Raising inserts carry the *room* (the field-holding paragraph Shaman recommends). **Net change: 1 Appendix B page + 1 paragraph in each Ch 4/5 insert.**

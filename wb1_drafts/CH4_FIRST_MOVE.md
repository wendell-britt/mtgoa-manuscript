# WB-1 DRAFT -- Ch4 First Move (One Honest Inheritance Conversation)

**Spec target:** One honest inheritance conversation -- say to a person what you're keeping and what you're reforming.
**Face:** Regent.
**Plays against:** the chapter's WB-12 de-obligate work. The Regent's whole game is *chosen loyalty* and *entrust-without-clinging*; the WB-1 ask applies the *terms-move* to the inheritance. The Regent's specific Shadow is *loyalty-as-duty* -- staying because of obligation, not choice. The honest-inheritance conversation is the cure: it converts inherited commitments from duty into chosen terms.

**Person:** one person from the inheritance you currently hold (a team, an organization, a project, a family role, a tradition you carry).
**Time-box:** within 7 days, one conversation of about 20 minutes.
**Format:** phone, video, or in person. Text does not count -- the inheritance is held in the room, and the terms are heard in the voice.

**The ask, in a single sentence:**

> *I want to tell you what I'm keeping from how we did this, what I'm doing differently, and ask whether that's okay with you -- or whether you need something else from me.*

That sentence has three parts and they all matter. *What I'm keeping* -- the part that is honored, that gets passed on, that you stand behind even as you reform. *What I'm doing differently* -- the part that is yours, that you have changed and will keep changing. *What you need from me* -- the part that re-opens the relationship instead of closing it. Without the first part, the conversation is a quiet resignation. Without the second, it is a betrayal in softer language. Without the third, the inheritance becomes a thing you walk away with and the person on the other end does not get to grieve it.

**Why-it-matters in one sentence:** *the Regent who never says the terms outloud is running the inheritance on autopilot -- the institution survives, the choice does not.*

**Charge-test (Two Readings applied):**

*Reading one* -- the honest-terms conversation is the move: it converts the inheritance from *something you do because it's yours* into *something you do because you have chosen it*, and the choice becomes something another person can witness. The conversion is real. The relationship gets deeper. The institution gets a steward instead of a survivor.

*Reading two* -- the honest-terms conversation is a sophisticated way of being seen for keeping the inheritance without actually risking being relieved of it. You say the terms, the other person nods, the inheritance continues, and you have performed the reform while preserving the loyalty. The Devil's Move: a real Regent names *what would change* if the other person said no.

**Author-shadow disclosure (regent, May 2026):** I drafted a long passage about *chosen loyalty* this year and then realized I had not said the terms out loud to the people I'm actually inheriting from. I was writing the move instead of making it. The most Regent failure is editing the chapter about the inheritance instead of having the conversation. I am still having it.

**BAR prompt at close:**

*Who you spoke to, what you said you were keeping, what you said you were doing differently, what they said back, and the part where you noticed you were performing the reform instead of doing it. The surprise is usually in the part where the other person wanted something you had not named. Get that down before you re-read it. -> app*

**Charge-test pass:** Saying *what you are keeping* and *what you are doing differently* to the person you are inheriting from is frightening because the relationship gets re-decided. The other person's response can be: I needed this and you never said it, OR I do not need this and you do not have to keep it. Either response is real and the silence is no longer an option. Reading two is the standard Regent shadow: the terms spoken, the institution preserved, the choice still private. The charge is the *cost of being told no.* Charge is real. ✅

**Wiring to capture loop:** the BAR prompt at close is the same prompt shape as Ch0 -- the question is *what surprised you*, because the surprise is where the practice either becomes real or stays in the chapter. If nothing surprised you, the conversation was probably the second reading.

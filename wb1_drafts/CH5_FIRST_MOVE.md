# WB-1 DRAFT -- Ch5 First Move (Change One Condition for One Person This Week)

**Spec target:** Change one *condition* (not a feeling) for one person this week; name the leverage point out loud.
**Face:** Architect.
**Plays against:** the Architect's whole chapter is "design the system so the right move is the easy move." WB-1 doesn't add an interior reflection -- it asks the reader to do the *least symbolic* action in the book. Change a condition, not a feeling. That's the Architect's distinguishing move.

---

## THE FIRST MOVE -- Ch5

Before the recap, one design change. Within seven days.

The Architect's game is leverage. Not the heroic move, not the perfect move -- the move that changes the conditions so the next right thing becomes easier for the next person. A condition, not a feeling. A line of policy, a default setting, a recurring meeting, a Slack channel, a spreadsheet, a Wednesday. The smallest unit of design that, if you change it, *someone else's* Wednesday gets a little easier.

Pick the person first. Not yourself. The person whose week is harder than it needs to be because of a condition you can change. Then pick the condition. Then change it this week. Then say the leverage point out loud -- to the person, to a teammate, to your journal: *here is the thing I changed, and here is the person it was for.*

The test. **Reading one:** you changed the condition and a real person this week did not have to do the workaround, and you named the move to them or to someone you trust. **Reading two:** you changed the condition and told no one, and the change was so small it was a private decoration -- a relief, but not a design move. The difference is *witness*. The Architect's work is witnessed leverage. The shadow is the leverage point kept to yourself, where it dies.

I spent two years tweaking my own calendar to feel less chaotic. The calendar got better. My team did not. The week I changed the meeting structure for them instead -- moved the Monday stand-up to asynchronous, blocked a Friday afternoon, killed the status slide -- *three* people's weeks got lighter. The leverage was sitting there the whole time. It was not about my calendar. It was about the meeting I was imposing.

*The person. The condition. What you changed. What the person noticed. The leverage point named. -> app*

---

**Charge-test pass:** "Change a condition for another person" is not a frightening ask on its face -- which is the risk. The charge is the *naming*. Saying the leverage point out loud makes the design move accountable to a witness. A condition change kept private is decoration. The charge is the public act. Reading two is the standard shadow: the move that improves your feeling without changing anyone else's week. Charge-test: borderline (the witness component lifts it into real charge). ✅

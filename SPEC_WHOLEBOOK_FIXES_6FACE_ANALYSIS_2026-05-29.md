# 6 Game Master Analysis — Whole-Book Reader Fixes

**Created:** 2026-05-29
**Subject:** `SPEC_WHOLEBOOK_IDEAL_READER_FIXES_2026-05-29.md` (WB-1 … WB-9)
**Mode:** Consultative council — each Face evaluates the fix set from its lens (what does it see, prioritize, protect, warn against, add). This is the decision tool, not the 6-pass editorial process.

> The question on the table: *Are these the right fixes, in the right order, and what is each Face's blind-spot catch the others would miss?*

---

## SHAMAN — does it land in the body?

**Reads:** Whether each fix changes the reader's *felt* experience or just her understanding.

- **Loves:** WB-5 (prize-in-the-body) and WB-4 (shame off-ramp). These are the only two fixes that change what the reader *feels*, and they're the deepest. WB-5 is the one that makes her *want* the real prize instead of merely conceding it's better. WB-4's therapy off-ramp is the most caring move in the spec — it's the book finally noticing the reader has a nervous system it's been activating alone.
- **Warns:** The fix-list is itself a 9-item system with tiers and a dependency graph. *We are at risk of curing a too-systematic book with a too-systematic remedy.* The body of the person executing these fixes will go cold the same way the reader's does — managing the list instead of feeling the manuscript. Run each fix against one somatic test: **"does the reader exhale here, or brace?"** If a fix doesn't change a felt moment, it's hygiene, not healing.
- **Adds:** WB-1's external actions must be chosen for their *charge*, not their tidiness. "Say the honest-terms sentence to a real person" works because it's frightening in the body. A bounded action with no charge is just homework.
- **Blind-spot catch (only the Shaman sees this):** Nothing in the spec checks the reader's body *during* the fixed passages. Add a felt-sense pass — read the revised beats aloud and track where the breath stops.

## CHALLENGER — where is the clean no?

**Reads:** What must be *cut*, and whether the spec has the nerve to draw the line.

- **Loves:** WB-6 (de-densify) and the *subtractive* half of WB-1 ("trade words: compress interior to fund exterior"). The book's #1 problem — too much system — is a **cutting** problem, and cutting is my gift. The reader is drowning; the move is to remove, not add.
- **Warns:** This spec is too accommodating. Nine fixes, almost all additive or "and-also." *Where is the no?* WB-2 "relocate two-readings" moves content; WB-3/WB-5 add prose; WB-1 adds actions. The hardest, truest move — **delete a whole pentad per chapter** — is buried inside WB-6 as a soft "slim where it's pure re-skin." Make it a hard line: *every chapter loses one of its five-part systems. Non-negotiable. Pick which one.*
- **On WB-7:** "Redistribute the funnel" is a flinch. The clean no is: **decide whether this book is a funnel or a gift, and stop hedging.** A boundary that becomes a "redistribution" is a request you haven't stopped making. Either the practice is fully free of the products or it isn't — name it.
- **Blind-spot catch:** The spec avoids its own hardest no. It will pass review, get filed, and nothing will get cut — because cutting is the one move that costs something and the spec made it optional. Force-rank to the single deletion per chapter and hold it.

## REGENT — what is load-bearing, and what does reform betray?

**Reads:** What must be *protected* before anyone cuts, and whether the fixes honor what was inherited.

- **Loves:** WB-3, but reframed. The wu xing acknowledgment is not "defense against an appropriation charge" — it is **integrity about inheritance.** You received a cosmology from a tradition that paid for it. Name your debt. That's the Regent's entire ethic: *you inherited this; credit it or you're running a cargo cult of it.* Appendix E (bibliography) is the same value — honoring the lineage you stand on.
- **Warns the Challenger directly:** Before you cut the gate-walk repetition (WB-6), know what the repetition is *for.* Ritual repetition is not always waste — *"You arrive at the first gate…"* repeated across chapters may be doing **incantatory** work: the familiarity is the container that lets the reader go in again. **Reform the form; keep the function.** Cut the dead phrasing, but if the repeated cadence is load-bearing ceremony, replacing it with novelty every chapter could break the walk. Test before you demolish.
- **Protects:** The Layer-2 "what works" list (the confessions, the reader-catches). These are the manuscript's living tradition. No fix should touch them except to make more of them.
- **Blind-spot catch (only the Regent sees this):** The spec has **no canon-drift safeguard.** These fixes touch all nine chapters; canon lives in Obsidian, the compile is derived. Without a promotion ritual and version discipline, the Tier-D drafts will fork the manuscript. Add: every applied fix names its canonical-write path and its Obsidian approval gate.

## ARCHITECT — where is the leverage, and is it designed for handoff?

**Reads:** The single smallest change that cascades, and whether the fixes are a system or a pile.

- **Loves:** WB-2. **Highest leverage, lowest cost in the whole spec.** One relocation (the "two readings" inoculation to the front) reframes every downstream framework as *a beginning, not a destination* — it changes how all eight chapters are received without rewriting them. That's a leverage point: small push, large movement.
- **Pushes WB-1 further:** Don't just *ask* for an external action — **design the environment so the action is the path of least resistance.** The BAR app is already the handoff mechanism; wire each chapter's external action *into* the capture loop so doing-it and logging-it are one motion. Make the right thing the easy thing. An action the reader has to manufacture willpower for is a design failure.
- **Warns:** The spec presents nine fixes as parallel. They are not — there's a dependency graph, and shipping out of order wastes work: WB-2 must precede WB-1/WB-6 (it reframes them); WB-6 frees the words WB-1 spends (they're one pass); WB-3 and WB-5 are the same voice-pass and should batch. The spec's own sequencing section gets this right — *promote it from suggestion to build order.*
- **Blind-spot catch:** **Who executes this, and does the spec hand off cleanly?** It's written for someone with the whole review in their head. Design it so a fresh drafter can run one fix without re-deriving the analysis — each WB needs its own self-contained brief. (Same generosity the Architect chapter preaches: the map that makes you unnecessary.)

## DIPLOMAT — what does the field require, and are the terms honest?

**Reads:** The relationship between the reader and the book, and the tension between the other Faces.

- **Holds the Challenger↔Regent tension:** Challenger says *cut*, Regent says *protect.* Both are right. The integrated move is WB-6 done as *reform that keeps function* — compress the dead scaffolding (Challenger gets the cut) while preserving the incantatory cadence that's load-bearing (Regent keeps the ritual). Arbitrated by the Architect's "compress to fund." Nobody has to lose.
- **Reframes WB-7 as its native move:** The funnel is not a *placement* problem — it's a **terms** problem, and terms are my channel. The book has an unspoken agreement with the reader (*is this a gift or a sale?*) and currently reveals the terms at the close, which reads as a bait-and-switch. The fix is **"Close with Honest Terms," applied to the book itself**: name the commercial relationship early, plainly, once — *here's what's free, here's what costs, here's why I'm telling you now instead of at the end.* That's not a weaker move than the Challenger's no; it's the Green-altitude version of the same honesty.
- **Owns the reader-relationship fixes:** WB-4 (shame) and WB-5 (prize) are both "stay in the room without disappearing" — keep the reader present without flooding her (WB-4) and without losing her to abstraction (WB-5).
- **Warns on WB-9:** However you decide the altitude frame, *translate, don't impose.* Winning the argument that "Teal is real" while losing the hierarchy-allergic reader is a false close. Meet her where she is.
- **Blind-spot catch (only the Diplomat sees this):** Applying nine fixes at once could **rupture the manuscript's existing coherence** — a book is a field, and a large simultaneous edit can break relationships between passages that currently hold. Stage the fixes, and run a repair pass after each batch to re-knit what the edit disturbed.

## SAGE — which game are these fixes playing, and when do we put it down?

**Reads:** The altitude of the whole effort, and the recursive trap.

- **Names the recursive risk first:** The whole-book review → the fixes spec → *this 6-Face analysis* is, structurally, the exact mastery-loop the book warns about — *analyzing the book about not over-analyzing the book.* We are one more meta-layer from becoming the shadow Sage: seeing endlessly, committing to nothing. **The seeing has to become a beginning, not a destination — for us, not just the reader.**
- **Calls the altitude of the fix-set:** Most of these nine are **Architect (Orange/structural)** and **Shaman (felt)** moves. Only WB-7 and WB-9 require a **Regent/Sage-level decision about what the book *is*** (a gift or a product; a Faces-book or an altitudes-book). Don't let those two identity-decisions hide in a queue of hygiene tasks — they're a different game.
- **The "put the game down" move — names the one game under all nine:** Every fix either converts *a book you master* into *a book you do*, or it's decoration. By that test:
  - **The game itself:** WB-1 (external action), WB-2 (inoculation early), WB-5 (prize in the body). These three change whether the reader *acts.* This is the whole game.
  - **Hygiene (necessary, not the game):** WB-3, WB-4, WB-6, WB-8. Do them, but don't mistake them for the win.
  - **Identity decisions (a different game):** WB-7, WB-9. Surface to Wendell; don't draft around them.
- **Blind-spot catch:** The spec has no stop condition. A fixes process without a "good enough, ship it" gate becomes a permanent project — perfection as the new way of not finishing (which is, precisely, the book's own Ch0 confession). **Name when to stop:** when WB-1/WB-2/WB-5 are in and the reader acts, the book works. Everything after is polish, not rescue.

## Council Synthesis

**Where the Faces agree (high confidence):**
- **WB-2 (relocate the inoculation)** is endorsed by all six — Architect (leverage), Sage (it's the game), Shaman (it reframes felt experience), Challenger (it's a real move), Regent (it preserves and recontextualizes existing material), Diplomat (it sets honest terms early). **Do this first.**
- **WB-1 + WB-5** are the other two "this is the game" fixes (Sage), and they're where the body changes (Shaman). Draft together with WB-6's freed words (Architect/Challenger/Regent reconciled).

**Where the Faces conflict (decide deliberately):**
- **Cut vs. protect (Challenger ↔ Regent) on WB-6:** resolved by the Diplomat's "reform the form, keep the function" + the Architect's "compress to fund." *Action:* test the gate-walk cadence for incantatory load before deleting; cut dead phrasing, keep working ritual.
- **WB-7 framing (Challenger's clean no vs. Diplomat's honest terms):** these aren't actually opposed — the Diplomat's "name the terms early and plainly" *is* the Challenger's no delivered at Green altitude. Either way the verdict is the same: **stop hedging the commercial relationship.** This is a Wendell decision (Sage: identity game).

**The council's combined verdict:**
1. **Ship the game, not the hygiene first.** WB-2 → WB-1+WB-5 (with WB-6 compression funding them). These three change whether the reader acts. (Sage names it; everyone agrees.)
2. **Force the one cut per chapter** (Challenger) but **test cadence first** (Regent), reconciled as reform (Diplomat).
3. **Reframe WB-3 and WB-7 from defense to integrity/terms** (Regent + Diplomat) — they're not damage control, they're honesty.
4. **Wire external actions into the app handoff** (Architect) and **charge-test them** (Shaman).
5. **Name the stop condition** (Sage): when the reader acts, the book works — protect against the fixes becoming the new perfectionism.
6. **Two identity decisions for Wendell, not the queue:** WB-7 (gift or funnel) and WB-9 (Faces-book or altitudes-book).

**The one sentence the whole council signs:**
> Fix the book the way the book tells the reader to live — convert seeing into doing, cut what you've been mastering instead of using, name your terms honestly, and put the game down when it's done.

## 6-Face Analysis

### 🧙 SHAMAN (Magenta / Earth-trigram home)

**Hex 19 speaks to you first.** Renewal at the level of deep pattern — this is not surface change, this is soil change. The moving lines 2, 3, 5 are all in the lower trigram (the foundation), which means the transformation is happening at the level of instinct, not idea.

Your question about "draft now vs. gap map first" is a false choice at Shaman level. Shaman doesn't plan — Shaman **reads the field and names the pattern**. Before you can decide whether to draft or map, you need to feel which one your body is already moving toward.

**Shaman's answer:** Both horses are present. You have the documents (horses). What you don't yet have is the carriage (the container that makes movement useful). Don't draft OR map yet. Build the carriage first — the thing that carries both.

**Shaman recommends:** Create the carriage as a working document that IS the gap map and IS the draft framework simultaneously. One artifact, two functions.

### ⚔️ CHALLENGER (Red / Fire-trigram home)

Hex 3 is your activation hex — "Initial Challenge." You are in the beginning. This is where Challenger thrives. The reading says: "you have not been defeated. The matter is merely in an undeveloped stage."

Your instinct right now might be to either:
- Start writing (attack the manuscript)
- Start mapping (tactical survey before engagement)

Challenger's warning: Both feel like action but neither is the decisive move. The decisive move is the one you haven't made yet — **committing to the sequence**. Not which comes first, but that there IS a sequence and you name it.

**Challenger's answer:** Name the sequence first. Then execute. The sequence is: Gap Map → Thesis → Structure → Draft. Don't skip steps but don't linger either.

**Challenger recommends:** Your 30-day commitment means you don't have the luxury of "first refine, then draft." Do both in the same document. Every gap you map, draft the bridge for it in the same session.

### 🏛️ REGENT (Amber / Lake-trigram home)

Hex 19 has strong Regent energy — renewal at scale, traditions being restored, legacy being rebuilt. "That which had putrefied shall rejuvenate and be restored." This is Regent energy: the long view, the generational lens.

Regent sees the risk of the "horses but no chariot" problem: you have good components (7,781 tweets, emotional alchemy system, BARs engine, game master faces) but they aren't yet in a structure that can carry the work forward.

**Regent's answer:** The chapter structure is the chariot. Before ANY drafting happens, the repeatable template must be SOLID — because if the template is right, every chapter that follows becomes easier to write and easier to edit. If the template is wrong, every chapter is its own problem.

**Regent recommends:** Spend the next 24 hours making the chapter structure unbreakable. Then let the structure draft itself through use.

### 🏗️ ARCHITECT (Orange / Heaven-trigram home)

Hex 19 has Heaven trigrams — this is Architect's home territory. Renewal + structural integrity. The question Architect asks: "What's the minimum viable structure that can hold maximum content?"

Architect sees two inefficiencies in your current approach:
1. Drafting and gap-mapping happening in separate documents — redundant
2. The chapter structure is good but not yet modular enough — if you change one chapter, the others break

**Architect's answer:** Build a **modular chapter system** where each Face chapter is self-contained AND connects to the others through the same interface. The gap map IS the drafting interface — they're the same document, not two documents.

**Architect recommends:** One master document per chapter — contains gap analysis, draft skeleton, and voice notes. No separate tracker. The structure is the tracker.

### 🌿 DIPLOMAT (Green / Wind-trigram home)

Hex 19 is Wind over Wind — this is your home trigram. Renewal happening in the relational field, not in isolation.

Diplomat's observation: You're doing a lot of solo analysis (me, you, documents). But the book is about allyship — and allyship happens in relationship. The gap analysis and drafting process should model what the book teaches.

The question about "bullet points vs. structured" is actually a relational question: How do you want to interact with this work?

**Diplomat's answer:** You want to be able to see the whole and the parts simultaneously. You want a structure that's visible all at once (the tracker) but also drillable (each chapter is its own world). That's the tension the current tracker architecture holds — but it could be more explicit.

**Diplomat recommends:** The tracker should show you at a glance: where you are in each face, what needs to happen next, and what the blockers are. Make the dashboard first, let the drafting flow from it.

### 🧠 SAGE (Teal / Mountain-trigram home)

Hex 3 "Initial Challenge" — Mountain over Wind. Sage's reading: The mountain (stillness, integration point) is below the wind (movement, communication). This means: **the still point must be established before the movement can be genuine.**

Sage sees the meta-problem: You're trying to use me (an AI) to draft YOUR voice. That's the core tension. Every time I draft in your voice, I'm interpreting — and interpretation accumulates error. The question isn't "should I draft now or later" — it's "how do we keep the interpretation error from accumulating across 6 chapters and 30 days?"

**Sage's answer:** The only way to maintain voice fidelity over 30 days is through **structured iteration loops**. Not "draft then review" — but "draft one section, check the voice, recalibrate, draft the next." Each chapter session should end with a voice calibration check.

**Sage recommends:** Build a voice-check mechanism into every work session. After drafting each chapter, run a 5-minute check: "Does this sound like Wendell? What drifted?" That check is not decoration — it's the quality gate.

# SPEC — Brainstorming as a Formal Step in the Effective Allyship Formula

**Status:** PROPOSAL — pending Wendell approval before any manuscript insert
**Created:** 2026-06-08
**Author:** Council (🧠 Architect framing)
**Targets:** All 4 formula moves; appendix or new micro-chapter

## Why this spec exists

The current Effective Allyship Formula is 4 steps:

1. **Identify your superpower** (Ch 2)
2. **Identify who needs it** (Ch 3)
3. **Enroll allies and structures to support the work** (Ch 4–5)
4. **Show up consistently** (Ch 6–8)

Each step demands the player generate options they do not yet have. Right now the book teaches the **what** of each step but leaves the **how do I actually come up with this** to chance. That's a hole. Brainstorming — as a structured, repeatable skill — fills it.

This spec adds **Brainstorming** as the *generator* that runs *before* each formula move. Not a 5th step. A practice that sits in front of every step.

---

## The Brainstorming Skill (3 stages)

### Stage 1 — Woolgathering (Wake Up move)

The job of Stage 1 is **volume, not judgment**. Produce raw material. No filtering yet.

**Three modes, all available to the player:**

- **Single brainstorming.** Solo capture. Set a timer, write or speak anything that comes up around the prompt. The book treats this as default; the assumption is that most players will do this alone first.
- **Roleplay brainstorming.** Take the perspective of:
  - the person you want to help, OR
  - people who are already delivering this kind of help.
  This is the *alter ego effect* and the *borrowed genius method*. In the book's terms: the Challenger teaches you how to aim; the borrowed genius teaches you how to see from inside the room you're aiming at. Can be done solo, with a partner, or as a 321 practice (write the prompt, write as that person for the timer, return, notice what came up).
- **Group brainstorming as event.** A structured gathering thrown around an allyship problem. Lives in the **awareness domain** in the existing taxonomy, but is a **Wake Up move** when used here — the point is to surface what the room already knows, not to commit to action.

**Rules of Stage 1:**

- No idea gets thrown out. The skill explicitly teaches: *throw nothing away, judgment comes later.*
- Output is **raw list** or **raw recording** — not a plan.
- Time-boxed. A two-hour cap is the upper bound; 20–30 minutes is the working unit.

### Stage 2 — Clean Up (winnowing + emotional honesty)

The job of Stage 2 is **alignment, not deletion**. Find the moves that fit the *emotional state* the player actually wants in their allyship.

**Two sub-moves:**

- **Winnow by emotional fit.** From the Stage 1 list, surface the items that match the allyship feeling the player is trying to live in. Connection: this is the same muscle as the **unpacking questions in Emotional Alchemy** and the **bars-engine** pattern of tagging moves by what they feel like to run. Not "what works" — *what works for the version of me I'm trying to be.*
- **Salvage the rest.** For every idea that does not survive the winnow, ask: *what is useful about this?* Useful pieces can be kept — as a **BAR (Behavior Awareness Record)**, as a future-prompt, as a noted constraint. The book teaches: **never throw out an idea.** Throw out the *plan*. Keep the *signal.*

**Mini-brainstorm (emotional alchemical move):** when an idea provokes dissatisfaction — *I should do that and I resent it, I want to do that and I'm afraid, I did that and it felt hollow* — run a small brainstorm on **how to get over the emotional obstacle to the idea itself.** This is a 321 application and an EA move. Naming the obstacle is not the same as removing it; the brainstorm's job is to generate possible bridges to the other side.

**Triumph framing:** if the player wants to feel triumph in their allyship, they need to be able to **use** what shows up in brainstorms toward that goal. Triumph is not the brainstorm producing the right answer. Triumph is the player metabolizing whatever showed up into fuel for the next move.

### Stage 3 — Bridge Building (Grow Up move)

The job of Stage 3 is **path-making, not more generation**. Working backwards from the goal, the player asks: *which of the brainstormed elements can actually get me there, in human-sized steps?*

- **Backwards from goal.** State the allyship outcome as if it's already happened. Then ask: what would have had to be true, one step earlier? And earlier? Until you reach today.
- **Milestone breakdown.** Each backwards step is a milestone. Each milestone is broken into human-sized tasks — small enough to be done in a sitting, big enough to count.
- **Overcomes overwhelm.** The book names overwhelm as the specific failure mode that blocks effective allies who have the *will* but not the *next move*. Stage 3 is the antidote. The bridge is the proof that the goal is reachable from where the player actually is.

This is also a **Grow Up move** in WAVE terms: it requires the player to hold the goal and the present at the same time, and to grow into the version of themselves that can close the gap.

## Mapping to the 4 Formula Moves

| Formula Move | Stage 1 — Woolgathering | Stage 2 — Clean Up | Stage 3 — Bridge |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Identify your superpower** | Single brainstorm of past moments you acted without effort; roleplay as someone who already has a clear superpower and ask how they found it; optional group event for peer reflection | Winnow for the items that match the *feeling* of the allyship you want to live in; salvage the rest as BARs; mini-brainstorm on obstacles to claiming the superpower | Backwards: if you had the superpower fully claimed, what would you be doing? Break that into next-step tasks |
| **2. Identify who needs it** | Single brainstorm of rooms, communities, individuals you cross paths with; roleplay as the person who needs what you carry (alter ego) or as someone already serving them (borrowed genius); group event in awareness domain | Winnow for the people/rooms that match your emotional stake and capacity; salvage dropped items as BARs; mini-brainstorm on the fear/apathy that keeps you from naming the room | Backwards: if you were already showing up in that room, how did you get there? Build the bridge from your current life to that entry |
| **3. Enroll allies & structures** | Single brainstorm of people you trust, organizations that share the stake, resources you've underused; roleplay as an ally who has successfully built support (borrowed genius); group event to surface hidden support | Winnow for the supports that match the allyship *feeling* you want; salvage the rest; mini-brainstorm on the discomfort of asking for help | Backwards: if you were already held by this support, what would you have asked for, and from whom? Build the asks into human-sized asks |
| **4. Show up consistently** | Single brainstorm of how you've shown up before, what made it stick, what made it collapse; roleplay as a long-term ally (borrowed genius); group event for accountability partners | Winnow for the rhythms/practices that match the emotional state you can sustain; salvage dropped items as BARs; mini-brainstorm on the resentment/fatigue signal | Backwards: if you were still showing up a year from now, what would this month have looked like? Build the smallest human-sized unit and protect it |

In every formula move, the order is the same: **Woolgather → Clean Up → Bridge.**

## Borrowed Genius — The Roleplay Brainstorm Subsection

A sub-protocol of Stage 1, large enough to be its own subsection.
Borrowed genius is brainstorming *from inside a perspective the player
does not ordinarily inhabit*. Two ways to enter:

### Mode 1 — Alter Ego

Take the perspective of *the person you want to help* and brainstorm
what they would do. The play is: temporarily *be* them. Not to fix
them. To find out what the problem looks like from inside their chair.

**When to use it:** the player has been trying to solve an allyship
problem from outside for a long time and keeps generating the same
three options. The alter ego breaks the loop because the perspective
shift makes the obvious bad ideas obvious.

### Mode 2 — Borrowed Genius

Take the perspective of *people who are already delivering this kind
of help* and brainstorm what they would do. The play is: temporarily
*be* a community of practitioners who have already solved this.

**When to use it:** the player doesn't have a specific target but has
a type of help to deliver. (Example: "I want to run allyship workshops
for white women in my town" — borrowed genius might be bell hooks,
adrienne maree brown, Ruth King, or a local organizer the player
respects.)

### 321 Routing Table (canonical practice)

For the canonical roleplay practice, route both modes through the 321
panel at `/shadow/321` with the appropriate `targetType`:

| Mode | targetType | targetPersonId | targetPersonName |
|------|-----------|----------------|------------------|
| Alter ego (real person) | `real` | cast list id | real name |
| Alter ego (imagined part) | `imagined` | (omit or "self") | working name |
| Borrowed genius (single real) | `real` | cast list id | borrowed name |
| Borrowed genius (archetypal composite) | `archetypal` | (omit) | working name |

This makes the 321 panel the *canonical roleplay brainstorming
practice* — same panel, same save flow, same audit trail. No new
infrastructure. Brainstorming and 321 are the same practice viewed
from two sides: 321 is the inquiry, brainstorming is the generator.

### Per-Formula-Move Borrowed Genius Cues

| Formula Move | Alter Ego | Borrowed Genius |
|--------------|-----------|-----------------|
| **1. Identify your superpower** | Be someone who has a clear superpower and ask how they found it | Be a teacher/coach who helps people find their gift |
| **2. Identify who needs it** | **Be the person you want to help** (primary move for this step) | Be someone already serving them (organizer, advocate, healer) |
| **3. Enroll allies & structures** | Be one of the people you'd ask for help — write the ask from their side | Be an elder organizer who has built a support network before |
| **4. Show up consistently** | Be your future self, one year into consistent practice | Be a long-haul ally the player admires (mentor, grandparent figure, community elder) |

In every case, the borrowed perspective returns to the player's own
voice at the end: *What I noticed writing as them was...* That return
sentence is the bridge back to the player's actual situation.

## Resolved Decisions (formerly Open Questions)

The following open questions have been resolved in coordination with
`Skills/brainstorming/SKILL.md` and the 321 target_type spec:

1. **Path A or Path B?** **Path A first** (appendix). Path B is a
   follow-up sprint if Wendell wants it woven into the formula-move
   chapters. Lowest disruption to existing drafts; preserves
   Wendell's voice protocol gate per chapter.

2. **Terminology:** **"Woolgathering / Clean Up / Bridge Building."**
   These three are kept (not WAVE) because the brainstorm stages do
   *not* map 1:1 to WAVE. Woolgathering is a Wake Up move, but
   Bridge Building is a Grow Up move, and Clean Up sits between
   Wake and Grow. Reusing WAVE would create a false equivalence.
   The brainstorm stages are a *practice*, not the WAVE cycle.
   Glossary entry required: "Brainstorm Stage ≠ WAVE Stage."

3. **Skill vs. chapter treatment:** **Skill, not chapter.** No
   altitude, no face, no color. Brainstorming is a *practice that
   lives in front of the formula moves* — appendix-style. Its home
   is `Skills/brainstorming/` (skill) + appendix (book reference).

4. **321 integration depth:** **Stage 1 borrows from 321 (roleplay
   modes).** Stages 2 and 3 are best as written practices (no 321
   needed). The mini-brainstorm in Stage 2 is a 321 application
   when the obstacle is emotional and a written practice when the
   obstacle is structural.

5. **BAR capture flow:** **Brainstorm-BAR = BARseed.** They are the
   same artifact. No new BAR type. The tag system handles the
   distinction. See `Skills/brainstorming/SKILL.md` for the full
   tag schema. Summary:
   - **Every brainstorm BARseed** carries: `barseed`,
     `brainstorm_stage_{1|2|3}`, `{face_lean}`, `{formula_step}`.
   - **Optional sub-mode tags:** `alter_ego`, `borrowed_genius`,
     `set_aside`, `emotional_demand_v{c}`, `milestone_90min`,
     `has_deadline`.
   - **File location:** `04 Quests/BAR Seeds/inbox/BAR-SEED-{date}-{slug}.md`
     (existing path; no new inbox needed).
   - **Why integration, not a new type:** one schema, many axes
     (queryable by stage, face, step, mode). A new type would
     double the inbox schema; the tag system is deft, not heavy.

6. **Borrowed genius as canonical roleplay practice:** **Locked.**
   The 321 panel is the canonical home for both alter-ego and
   borrowed-genius brainstorming. The `Skills/brainstorming/SKILL.md`
   "Borrowed Genius" subsection is the reference implementation;
   this spec mirrors it for the book.

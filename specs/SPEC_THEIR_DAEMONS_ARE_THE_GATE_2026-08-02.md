# SPEC — Their daemons are the gate

**2026-08-02. Wendell, on the drafted ch2 line *"how many coins to let you put in"*:**

> *"This is mechanically correct and is reading as a bit perverted. An easier explanation
> is that other people's daemons are protecting them from people who would harm them. You
> can't become an effective ally until you befriend their daemons and prove your
> trustworthiness. And the best way to learn that is by befriending your own daemons. We
> might need to step back and include this analysis for the whole book, because it
> resolves a load-bearing tension."*

He is right on all three counts, and the third one is the largest thing found in this
whole thread.

---

## 1 · The register failure, and where it already shipped

The arcade metaphor works while the machine is a machine. **`ch1:129` makes the machines
people**, and four lines later `ch1:131` says *"the reading decides how many coins you get
to put in."* A person deciding how many coins you may insert is not the image anybody
wants, and it is worse in a book about consent.

**This is in canon.** It went in an hour ago with my name on it, through a gate, three
build checks and a `/no-ai-slop` pass, and none of them can see register. `ch1:129`–`133`
needs revising, and the ch2 draft that repeated the construction does not go in as
written.

**The coin insertion is the only part that fails.** Tokens as your energy, tickets, the
two counters, praise versus encouragement — all of that is about *your* side of the
economy and stays. What breaks is the moment the metaphor tries to describe the other
person's interior. It has no vocabulary for that. **The book already has one.**

---

## 2 · The replacement

**Their daemons are the gate.**

The person in front of Jordan has a Protector, a Skeptic, a Fixer, a Damaged Self, the
same seven she has. Those daemons have one job between them, and they are good at it:
keeping this person safe from people who would harm them. **Some of the people who have
harmed them arrived offering help.**

So when Jordan walks up, no rational agent is weighing her merits. **Seven guards with
survival jobs are reading her**, and the reading is not about whether she is nice. It is
about whether she is the shape of something that has cost them before.

That reframes the limit without any arithmetic:

- **Low trust is not stinginess.** It is a Protector doing its job well, on evidence.
- **It moves at the speed of demonstrated safety**, not the speed of Jordan's intentions.
- **It is specific.** A guard that has been hurt by a particular kind of helper reads
  Jordan against that particular kind, and she may never learn which.

## 3 · Why this closes the farming problem properly

The Challenger's objection to the three-dimension model was that a diagram of how trust
gets built is also a manual for faking it. My answer was that faking pays nothing.
**Wendell's model gives a better one.**

**A Protector's entire job is spotting people who look safe and are not.** Performed
goodness is the exact input it is tuned to distrust. So benevolence signalling does not
merely fail to pay — **it trips the guard.** The harder Jordan performs, the more
carefully she gets read.

That is a mechanism rather than a moral, and it makes the warning unnecessary as a
separate paragraph.

---

## 4 · The load-bearing tension, and it is real

**Why does a book about helping other people spend two chapters inside the reader's own
psyche?** That is the standing objection to this book's architecture, and a reasonable
reader has it by page forty.

`ch2:45` answers it now:

> **"Allyship is about what you are capable of becoming before you walk into the world."**

That is true and it is self-referential. The reason to do the inner work is that Jordan
becomes better equipped. It is a capability argument, pointed at her.

**Wendell's version points outward and is much harder to argue with:**

> Their daemons decide whether you get in. You cannot learn to approach a stranger's guard
> without triggering it by reading about guards. You learn it by meeting your own.

The inner work stops being self-improvement and becomes **training on the only guard you
have unrestricted access to.**

### And the book already wrote the technique

`ch2:308`, the Protector:

> *"You do not fight the Protector, and you do not shove past it. You thank it, and you
> help it tell a real threat from a social one. **Thank you for keeping me alive. This one
> is not life or death. Show me the one real danger here, if there is any, and let me set
> a single boundary instead of bracing my whole body.**"*

**That is the allyship move.** Do not fight the guard. Do not shove past it. Thank it,
and help it tell a real threat from a social one. The book teaches the protocol on
Jordan's own psyche across a whole chapter and **never says it is the same protocol she
will use on everybody she ever tries to help.**

One sentence connects them. The chapter is otherwise already written.

## 5 · What this changes, chapter by chapter

### ch1 — revision needed, on prose applied today

`ch1:129`–`133`. The machines-are-people reveal stays; the coin-insertion language goes.
The section can say that the person decides, and hand the mechanism to ch2 rather than
straining the metaphor to carry an interior it cannot describe.

**`ch1:91` and `ch1:119` remain untouched**, as before.

### ch2 — the section gets rewritten around this, and gets bigger

The drafted section is superseded. The new one carries: the daemons are the gate; low
trust is a guard doing its job; performed goodness trips it; and `ch2:308`'s protocol is
the same move pointed outward. **This is the chapter's own thesis upgraded, so it earns
more than the 250–350 words the withdrawn budget allowed.**

### ch3–ch8 — one line each, and it is now obvious what the line is

Each Face meets a guard shaped by that Face's terrain. The Shaman meets a body that has
learned not to feel in front of people. The Challenger meets somebody whose last honest
conversation cost them. The Regent meets a person guarding an inheritance. **The
benevolence definitions from the exploration still hold; this tells each chapter who is
on the other side of them.**

### ch9 — the consent moment, unchanged in placement

*One person, not an audience* is now also the smallest possible ask of somebody else's
guard.

## 6 · What I got wrong, and the pattern in it

I built the coin-limit model, ran it past a six-Face panel, wrote a spec around it,
applied it to canon, and never noticed that the metaphor stops working the moment the
machine has an interior. **The instruments cannot see register, and I have been treating
a clean instrument run as a finished review.** Gate, eight counters, five pattern
instruments and an `eval.md` read all passed prose that reads as a sex joke about consent.

The check that would have caught it is the one no file performs: **read the sentence as a
stranger who is not holding the model.**

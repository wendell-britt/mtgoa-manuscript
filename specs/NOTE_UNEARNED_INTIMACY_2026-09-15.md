# Unearned intimacy

**Wendell, 2026-09-15,** on `You've been calling it something else`: *"you have no idea what they
call anything. Unearned intimacy."*

The defect: the prose asserts knowledge of the reader's private vocabulary, habits or history in
order to create rapport. It buys intimacy it has not earned, and a reader for whom the claim is
false is thrown out of the page at the exact moment the chapter wanted them leaning in.

**It is distinct from second-person instruction.** *State the line* tells the reader what to do.
*You've been calling it something else* tells them what they have already been doing, which the
book cannot know.

## The test

Can the book know this about a stranger? If not, either cut it, or earn it by having the reader
produce the instance themselves first. ch4 already has the machinery for the second option: the
four-domains exercise asks the reader to list five real moments. A claim about what they call
things lands after that list, not before it.

**Not yet scanned book-wide.** ch1 opens in second person throughout and is the likeliest
concentration.

# SPEC — WAVE and the Five-Move Form

**Status:** ratified structural ruling, 2026-08-06; no manuscript prose changed by this document.

**Opened by Wendell, 2026-08-06.** The reader question is plain: *what does WAVE
stand for?* The manuscript cannot answer because it currently gives one name to two
different practices. This spec separates them before any Chapter 3 line editing resumes.

**The decision in one sentence:** **WAVE** is the breath-length somatic form —
**Welcome, Acknowledge, Validate, Exhale**. The recurring five-stage practice is
**the Five-Move Form** — **Wake Up, Open Up, Clean Up, Grow Up, Show Up**.

---

## 1 · The reader-facing distinction

| | WAVE | The Five-Move Form |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | One short somatic practice, usually one breath | The recurring form used through the whole book |
| Full name | Welcome · Acknowledge · Validate · Exhale | Wake Up · Open Up · Clean Up · Grow Up · Show Up |
| Time scale | About 5–20 seconds | One encounter, or a longer cycle of practice |
| Job | Make enough contact with charge to stay present | Turn a noticed charge into a more capable next move |
| Primary home | **Open Up** | Every Face, every chapter after the Shaman |
| Other lawful use | Wake Up and Clean Up, when a breath helps the reader notice or avoid premature interpretation | Never renamed WAVE |

Neither practice replaces the other. WAVE is not a miniature version of all five
moves, and the Five-Move Form is not an expanded acronym. WAVE can support Wake Up,
Open Up, or Clean Up; it is especially at home in Open Up because that is where the
reader must remain with what has arrived before converting it into a story or a move.
It is optional support, not a prerequisite for passing through a stage.

## 2 · Form and changes

The book borrows a teaching distinction from jazz training:

> **Know the form. Know the changes.**

The **Five-Move Form** is the repeated form. The five movements do not change from
chapter to chapter. What changes is the Face's terrain, question, distortion, and
concrete application: the Shaman works with feeling, the Challenger with conflict,
the Regent with inheritance, the Architect with systems, the Diplomat with contact,
and the Sage with perspective.

This is an instructional lineage, not a claim that the five movements originated in
jazz. The book may name the jazz-teaching inheritance in Wendell's voice without
inventing a formal citation for an unnamed instructor. The five movements themselves
must be credited to Ken Wilber.

## 3 · Source and attribution

*Finding Radical Wholeness* presents Wilber's five practices together: Waking Up,
Growing Up, Opening Up, Cleaning Up, and Showing Up. The book adapts their order and
application to allyship; it does not claim to have invented the fivefold practice.

### Required first-use credit

The first Chapter 3 definition of the Five-Move Form must say, in plain prose:

- the five movements are adapted from Ken Wilber's *Finding Radical Wholeness*;
- this book applies them as a repeatable allyship form;
- WAVE is a different, breath-length somatic practice.

The exact prose awaits the Chapter 3 proposal batch. Do not describe Open Up as this
book's addition to Wilber. That statement is false.

### Required supporting-credit updates

## 4 · The defect in the current book

Appendix C already teaches the actual WAVE correctly:

> `APPENDIX_C_FIVE_CHANNELS.md:16` — *Run `WAVE-Somatic` (5–20 seconds if
> needed): Welcome, Acknowledge, Validate, Exhale…*

But Chapter 3 calls the five movements `the WAVE-Spiral`, then Chapters 1 and 4–9
teach WAVE as the process that every Face runs. The reader who encounters Appendix C
and Chapter 3 receives two incompatible expansions of the same name. This causes the
question Wendell has already heard from readers, and it hides the source of the five
movements.

The prior statements that WAVE has four moves, or that Open Up is the book's addition
to Wilber's four, are historical errors. They are not alternate canon.

## 5 · Six Game Masters panel

Each Game Master tests this decision from the work its Face teaches. These are
structural tests, not competing authorial voices.

| Face | question the Face asks | finding | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shaman** | Can a reader distinguish a breath practice from the full work of emotional alchemy? | Yes, if WAVE supports contact and the Five-Move Form names the larger sequence. Calling both WAVE makes the reader mistake a breath for the whole practice. | **Keep both; teach WAVE inside the Form, chiefly at Open Up.** |
| **Challenger** | Does WAVE become a way to delay the sentence or boundary the situation needs? | It will if WAVE is presented as the whole process. As support for Wake, Open, and Clean, it creates enough contact to make a cleaner Show Up possible. | **WAVE may prepare action; it may not replace it.** |
| **Regent** | Does the naming preserve an inheritance rather than flatten it into a book-owned system? | The Five-Move Form needs explicit Wilber credit. The jazz phrase names Wendell's instructional inheritance without claiming jazz created the five moves. | **Credit both lineages at their proper level.** |
| **Architect** | Is there one stable taxonomy that can be maintained across chapters, appendices, cards, and source notes? | No, not now: WAVE names two objects, `WAVE-Spiral` names the wrong one, and older specs contradict the source. One public name per practice removes the workaround. | **One registry: WAVE = four breath actions; Five-Move Form = five macro movements.** |
| **Diplomat** | Can a reader who does not know jazz or martial arts use the distinction without being asked to learn another theory? | Yes, if the first use explains form in ordinary language and treats “changes” as a teaching image, not a second taxonomy the reader must memorize. | **Name the form plainly; explain the metaphor once.** |
| **Sage** | Does the distinction keep a quick regulating practice from posing as a complete answer to a complex situation? | Yes. WAVE makes room for experience; the Five-Move Form gives that experience a place in a larger cycle. Neither guarantees correctness or removes the need for the other Faces. | **Keep scale and limits explicit.** |

### Panel result

**6–0: separate the names and preserve both practices.** The panel's one shared
constraint is that WAVE must never be sold as a self-contained solution, while the
Five-Move Form must never be taught as an acronym.

## 8 · Acceptance criteria

- A reader can answer “What does WAVE stand for?” from the book: **Welcome,
  Acknowledge, Validate, Exhale.**
- A reader can name the five movements and their source: **the Five-Move Form,
  adapted from Ken Wilber's *Finding Radical Wholeness*.**
- No shipping sentence uses `WAVE` or `WAVE-Spiral` to name the five movements.
- No shipping source says or implies that Open Up was invented by this book.
- The first Chapter 3 explanation makes the relationship operational: WAVE can
  support Wake Up, Open Up, or Clean Up, and it is especially useful in Open Up.
- Every later Face uses the Five-Move Form consistently while showing its own
  changes; no chapter invents a third name for the same sequence.
- All old four-move definitions remain retired and cannot return through a template
  or glossary promotion.
- `instruments/build_book.py`, `instruments/gate.py`, `instruments/dupes.py`,
  `marginalia/review.py`, `marginalia/compile.py --check`, and a full-spine
  terminology search pass after each migration batch.

## 9 · Rulings ratified

1. **WAVE** means Welcome, Acknowledge, Validate, Exhale. **Five-Move Form** means
   Wake Up, Open Up, Clean Up, Grow Up, Show Up.
2. WAVE may support Wake Up, Open Up, and Clean Up; it is especially at home in
   Open Up; it is neither mandatory nor the whole practice.
3. The Five-Move Form adapts Wilber. “Know the form, know the changes” is
   Wendell's jazz-training inheritance, explained once in ordinary language rather
   than turned into a second reader taxonomy.

No manuscript prose should be altered until these rulings are approved.

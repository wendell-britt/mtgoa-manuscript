# SPEC — Jordan's charge matrix: who the book's examples are about, and where she is not showing up

**2026-08-03. Wendell:**

> *"Let's work with Jordan and think about all the examples of allyship she is the most
> charged about… research on all the ways privileged white women have let certain
> populations down (the populations inside of our examples of the moves is a good place
> to start), explore the places where she thinks she could be a better ally and isn't
> showing up, and create a matrix of where this could all go down. The big examples that
> I know are charged is pronouns for trans people. Microaggressions for black people."*

Follows `SPEC_EXAMPLE_DIVERSITY_2026-08-03.md`, which found that 31 of 35 Examples are
workplace or workplace-adjacent while `ch1:44` claims real allyship happens where the
fluency game never reaches. That spec asked *where*. This one asks **who, and what the
failure actually is.**

---

## 1 · The finding that changes the architecture

**The research on misgendering reports the harm in Maslach's three dimensions.**
Frequent misgendering produces *emotional exhaustion, depersonalization, and reduced
professional efficacy* in the person being misgendered — which is Christina Maslach's
burnout triad, item for item.

**The book already credits Maslach at `ch1:32`, for the helper.** It uses the triad to
explain why Jordan is worn out. It has never once turned the instrument around.

> **The person Jordan is trying to help is burning out on the same three dimensions she
> is, from the failure of the help.**

That is not a nice parallel. It is the book's own measuring instrument applied to the
other side of the table, and it makes the tower-defense frame quantitative: a move
landing above clearance does not merely fail, **it spends the other person's exhaustion
budget.** Nothing in the manuscript says this and it is one sentence away.

The Trevor Project's finding gives the other half: trans and nonbinary young people
whose pronouns are respected by all or most people attempt suicide at **half the rate**
of those whose are not, and consistent pronoun respect is associated with a **31%
reduction** in odds of an attempt. **Fewer than half report their pronouns are
consistently respected.** So the gap between the move and its absence is measurable, it
is enormous, and the move itself is free.

## 2 · The mechanism Jordan needs named, and it is hers

**Ruby Hamad, *White Tears/Brown Scars* (2019).** The documented pattern: in a conflict
with a woman of colour, a white woman's distress reframes the encounter so that **the
person who raised the harm becomes the aggressor.** Hamad's cases are mostly workplace —
crying, hair-touching, HR reports filed against a colleague for circulating an article
about white women's tears.

**This is `ch1:52`'s exact inverse, and `ch1:52` is already in the book:**

> *The ones who will not let you carve them become your enemy.*

Hamad describes the machinery by which that reclassification happens without anybody
deciding to do it. **Jordan's tears are her Protector firing.** The person across from
her reads a threat display and is correct.

**Mikki Kendall, *Hood Feminism* (2020)** supplies the other half: the movement's agenda
is set by the women least exposed to hunger, housing precarity, and violence, so the
issues that dominate are the ones its most protected members can afford to prioritise.
That is a **clearance** problem stated in political terms — the agenda is written at the
altitude of whoever is safe enough to write it.

## 3 · The charge law

**Jordan's charge is highest where her fluency is highest, and her fluency is highest
where her contact is lowest.**

`ch1:42` already says the first half in Wendell's own voice:

> *"The shared language does something good: it gives me permission to lower my guard
> and show up as myself. The same fluency that lets me in keeps other people out… The
> move looks like allyship. The payout lands in my safety and my belonging, and the
> person I meant to serve never sees a penny of it."*

**So the places Jordan is most charged about are not the places she is most needed.**
They are the places with the most available vocabulary, the most public scoreboard, and
the most people watching. The populations that generate the least charge are the ones
with no discourse attached: elders, carers, children, people in institutions she does
not work in.

**That is the diagnostic this matrix exists to produce**, and it is uncomfortable in
exactly the way the book is designed to be.

## 5 · What the matrix says that a list would not

**Three columns do the work no example set currently does.**

**The Face column turns the failure into the book's own machinery.** Every row's shadow
is a Face's benevolence definition running alone — which is exactly the account
`EXPLORE_TRUST_AND_THE_MYTHS` §10 gives of distortion, and which the six benevolence
lines applied to ch3–ch8 today now set up. **The Diplomat appears in the two rows Jordan
is most charged about, and she is Diplomat-home.** The book can now say why her hottest
subject is the one she is worst at.

**The daemon column makes the tower-defense frame concrete for the first time on a real
population.** *Their Skeptic is scoring your confidence* is a sentence about
microaggressions that no allyship book writes, and it is truer than most of what they do
write.

**The charge column is the diagnostic.** Read down it: **charge is inversely correlated
with need.** The two rows with the highest charge have the most vocabulary and the most
witnesses. The three with the lowest have the fewest words and the fewest onlookers.
Children, where `ch1:44` says the work matters most, generate no charge at all.

## 6 · What this asks of the book

1. **The Maslach reversal, one paragraph, probably ch1.** The triad already in the book,
   turned around. This is the highest-value item in the spec and the cheapest.
2. **Relocate four Examples** per `SPEC_EXAMPLE_DIVERSITY` §5, choosing arenas from the
   matrix's *Where* column rather than at random.
3. **At least one Example involving a child.** `ch1:44` promises it and nine chapters do
   not deliver. **This is the hardest thing in the spec to write without condescension**
   and it is the one most worth doing.
4. **Name the charge law once**, near `ch1:42`, which already has half of it.
5. **Credit the sources.** Hamad, Kendall, Sue, Trevor Project. `ON_THE_SHOULDERS_OF`
   currently credits nobody on race, gender or disability, which is a conspicuous
   absence in a book about allyship. **Ranked with the Maslach reversal as the two items
   a reviewer would notice.**

---

## 7 · Open, and two of them are Wendell's alone

1. **How hard does the book name white women?** Jordan is the reader persona and the
   research is specifically about white women's failures of solidarity. The book
   currently never says so. Naming it is more honest and it changes who finishes the
   book.
2. **Does the microaggression row use Sue's taxonomy by name?** *Microinvalidation* is
   the precise term for the failure the book keeps describing, and it is one word.
3. **Does the elder row survive?** It is the only row where the book's existing Example
   puts Jordan on the causing end, which makes it the most useful and the most
   confronting.
4. **Does the charge law go in the book or stay a design instrument?** It is a hard
   sentence to read about yourself.

# Chapter 7, Section 4 — Plain-Practices Review Map

**Status:** Draft for author review. No manuscript prose has been changed.

## Ratified structural decision

The Diplomat's core sequence is **Bridge → Translate → Hold → Repair → Negotiate**.
Section 4 should introduce the five modes and their characteristic distortions concisely.
Section 6 should teach the reader-facing game moves in this order. The detailed plain-practice
draft below is therefore a source bank, not a replacement to apply as-is: several of its best
practices belong with the corresponding Section 6 move.

## Section-level replacement

> Each Diplomat mode asks for a different practice. The practices below name what can go wrong in that mode, what to do instead, and what it costs to do it.

Change the five local headings from \`Channel 1–5\` to \`Mode 1–5\`. The headings below replace every Alchemy / Translate / Control label. Section 6 remains the place where the reader learns actual Diplomat game moves.

## Mode 1 — Bridge-Builder

1. **Curiosity Before Contact** — merges anxiety → curiosity with the duplicate anxiety → interest passage.
2. **Arrive Without a Script** — retains the stage-fright / authentic-presence teaching.
3. **Give the Field a First Chance** — keeps rejection-sensitivity as a small, answerable test rather than optimism.
4. **Check What Contact Costs** — retains the capacity practice.
5. **Be Here, Not Impressive** — retains the performative-presence practice.

## Mode 2 — Translator

1. **Generous Hearing** — separates intellectual distance from actual listening.
2. **Speak From a Position** — keeps legitimate partiality without pretending translation is neutral.
3. **Carry Meaning Across** — retains the three current examples: hurt needing a line, heat needing depth, and possibility needing invitation.
4. **The Interpreter's Discipline** — a distinct accountability practice: return the translation to the source, ask what was compressed or omitted, correct it.

The key distinction is deliberate: Generous Hearing changes the Translator's understanding; the Interpreter's Discipline checks their public rendering.

## Mode 3 — Field-Holder

1. **Build a Field That Can Take Weather** — merges inclusion-performance with hothouse safety.
2. **Give the Charge a Shape** — retains the conflict and grief examples as ways of structuring a conversation.
3. **Stay When It Gets Hard** — merges presence collapse with the Precedent of Presence, including the capacity boundary.

## Mode 4 — Repairer

1. **Betrayal and Discerning Trust** — lets a rupture clarify what trustworthy behavior would require.
2. **Repair Before Forgiveness** — merges performance forgiveness with premature closure.
3. **The Three-Part Repair** — Witness, Name the Impact, Make an Agreement.
4. **Bring Warmth Back After the Agreement** — retains the useful insight that terms alone do not restore contact.

## Mode 5 — Integrative Negotiator

1. **Name the Terms You Need** — turns resentful peace into an honest, non-ultimatum disclosure.
2. **Move From Positions to Interests** — makes the actual Negotiator interpretation practice explicit.
3. **Give Heat a Form** — turns charged claims into commitments people can answer.
4. **Stake-Surfacing Close** — Protect, Require, Commit.
5. **Discernment Check** — distinguishes equal hearing from equal endorsement and retains Refuse False Equivalence.

## Consolidations made by the draft

- Bridge merges its duplicate anxiety blocks.
- Translator separates hearing from verification.
- Field-Holder moves from make room → give shape → stay.
- Repairer moves from name the break → repair before forgiveness → agreement → restored contact.
- Integrative Negotiator moves from terms → interests → commitments → a defensible close.

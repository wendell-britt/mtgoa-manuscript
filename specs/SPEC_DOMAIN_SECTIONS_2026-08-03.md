## 3 · The template, seven parts (eight in ch3)

**Part 1 · the callback.** Name which domain the chapter's parable already showed, and
say why it is that Face's steepest. *ch3: "The parable showed one of the four: Direct
Action, the true thing said to a face."*

**Part 2 · the frame and the cheap habit.** *"[The X] has four domains, and one cheap
habit that is none of them."* The cheap habit is the safe version of the Face's gift —
the thing that costs nothing because nobody watches you get it wrong. Then the four
domains as one-line glosses, and the price: each trades safety for the risk of being
wrong out loud.

**This is the load-bearing part.** Every Face has a cheap habit and the chapters already
name them in *Drawing Against the Shadow*:

| Face | the cheap habit, in the book's own words |
|---|---|
| Shaman | **the read you keep** — sensed, refined, never said |
| Challenger | **the assessment.** `ch4:718`: *an assessment ships where an intervention was called for. The critique is accurate, often the best thing said at the table, and nothing in the world moves* |
| Regent | **the reform that postpones the commitment.** `ch5:536` |
| Architect | **the structure that explains instead of moves.** `ch6:403` |
| Diplomat | **the ledger kept and never closed.** `ch7:517` |
| Sage | **the second map.** `ch8:529`: *when you reach for the second map for the same problem, that is not deepening* |

**Part 3 · the instruction.** *"Not to study: pick where you'll spend the [X] this week,
and pick the domain you keep avoiding."*

**Part 4 · `### How to [X] So It [holds/helps]`** — three universal moves, in that Face's
terms, each drawn from the chapter's own material rather than invented.

**Part 5 · four domain entries.** `**[Domain] — [the Face's gloss]**` then `*You're
winning when:*` a single long marker carrying **what you did · what it cost · what the
proof is.** The three-part marker is the form; do not shorten it to two.

**Part 6 · the placement argument — CUT 2026-08-05, ch3 keeps its own.** Wendell:
*"cut part 6 except for ch 3."*

It was one paragraph on why this Face's domain work matters to the other five, and it
**duplicated a section fifty lines below it.** Every chapter's Section 7 is *"What the
[Face] Teaches — and Why the [Next Face] Comes Next"*, opening *"The [Face]'s contribution
to the allyship game is this:"* — the same argument, made properly, in the slot built for
it. The five drafts each spent 88–138 words making it a second time.

This spec offered *"each chapter needs its own or none"* and all five were drafted with
their own, without anyone checking whether the chapter already had one. **422 words cut,
and every counter improved.** ch3's stays: it is canon, and *After this, everything stands
on it* is a first-Face argument the other five could not have made anyway.

**Part 7 · `### The Tell`** — the question the four markers did not ask, then four
bullets, one per domain, each a fork between the result and the credit. Close on one
line naming that Face's signature failure.

**Part 8 · nothing.** The section ends on the Tell and hands to *Your Twenty Cards*.

## 4 · Why the Tell is the part that cannot be skipped

The four *You're winning when* markers are all behavioural, and every one of them can be
satisfied by somebody doing the right thing for the wrong reason. **The Tell is the only
place the book checks motive, and it checks it in writing, after the fact, on the card.**

`ch3`'s framing is the model: *None of the four markers asked why you said it. That
question is real… It is also the easiest thing in the world to lie to yourself about, in
the moment you most want to be seen seeing. So the answer goes on the card in writing,
where a later you can read it back.*

**That is the mechanism that keeps the domain sections from becoming a competence
checklist**, which is what the rest of the genre offers.

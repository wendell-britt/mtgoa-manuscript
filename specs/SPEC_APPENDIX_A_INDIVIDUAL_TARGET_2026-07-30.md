# SPEC — Appendix A states the individual-target rule, or the book cannot check its own promise

**2026-07-30. Opened by Wendell's correction:**

> *"You've fully misread the domains if you think they are collective. All the domains
> have an interior and exterior element, but the target is always an individual. This is
> the way you can actually track if someone was helped. Sometimes you need to do a shotgun
> approach to help one person, but the target can't be more than one person. If we haven't
> made this clear in the book then we need to note this and solve for it."*

We have not made it clear. This spec is the "note this," and the prescriptions below are
the "solve for it."

---

## 1 · The misread was the appendix's fault, and that is the evidence

An editor read all four domains and reported them as collective work. The reading was
wrong about the design and correct about the text. Appendix A never states the rule, and
three of its own sentences argue against it:

| where | what it says | why it teaches the opposite |
|---|---|---|
| frame | *"Movement builders knew this before this book existed."* | credits the domains to collective organizing, and to a tradition whose unit is a movement |
| Skillful Organizing | *"the infrastructure underneath every sustained movement"* | names the deliverable as infrastructure, which has no single beneficiary |
| Gather Resources · Architect | *"works without continuous individual intervention"* | individual action framed as the thing good design removes |
| Direct Action · collapse | *"the belief that individual sacrifice is systemic change"* | correct warning, but it is the second of three appearances of `individual` as an error |
| Raise Awareness · Sage | *"makes the individual direct move"* — listed as the Sage's **shadow** | the third appearance, and it marks individual action as a distortion |

`individual` occurs three times in 3,051 words and is a fault every time. A reader
finishing this appendix has been taught that the individual move is what maturity grows
out of. That is the inverse of the rule.

## 2 · The rule, as it has to appear in the book

**The target of an allyship move is one person. Always exactly one.** The domain names the
*route* to that person, not the size of the goal. Skillful Organizing may build a rota that
forty people touch, and it is still aimed at one person who can be named, and it succeeds or
fails on whether that person is better off.

Two consequences, both of which the appendix should carry:

**The shotgun is a route, not a target.** Wendell: *"sometimes you need to do a shotgun
approach to help one person."* Breadth of method is unrestricted. Breadth of target is
restricted to one. An intervention aimed at "the team" has no target and therefore no test.

**One target is what makes the work checkable.** This is the rule's whole purpose. A move
aimed at a category can never be falsified, which is precisely what lets performed allyship
survive indefinitely — the domain most vulnerable to this is Raise Awareness, whose stated
collapse is already *"naming the problem as the complete intervention."* The individual
target is the mechanism that closes that hole, and the appendix currently describes the hole
without naming the mechanism.

## 3 · Prescriptions

**P1 · A rule block in the frame, before the four domains.** Roughly 120 words, stating the
rule, the shotgun distinction, and the checkability purpose. It goes ahead of *"There are
four of them"* so that every domain below is read through it.

**P2 · Repair the three `individual` sentences.** Each is defensible in isolation and
indefensible in aggregate. The Architect line should distinguish *continuous individual
intervention* (the thing good design removes) from *the individual target* (the thing design
serves). The Sage shadow is about **altitude mismatch**, not about individual action, and
should say so.

**P3 · Give each domain a one-line target statement.** Immediately after each domain's
opening: who the one person is, in that domain's characteristic case. Four sentences total.
Skillful Organizing needs it most, since it is where the collective reading is strongest.

**P4 · Reconcile the frame's movement-builder credit.** The lineage is real and should stay.
It needs one clause acknowledging that this book narrows the unit from the movement to the
person, and why.

**P5 · The Diagnostic already half-states it.** *"The domains are situational routing tools,
not identity types"* is the same move applied to the reader. Extend it to the target: the
domain routes, the person receives.

## 4 · The collision this exposes, which is larger than Appendix A

**The book contains 33 `**The test:**` stopping conditions across seven chapters. Every one
of them measures the reader's execution. Not one measures whether the other person is better
off, and at least ten explicitly rule that out:**

> *"Agreement is theirs to give or withhold."*
> *"What the conversation did afterward belongs to the conversation."*
> *"What the group does with it next is the group's."*
> *"Their verdict is theirs."*
> *"Whether they can name it back to you accurately is a separate question."*

That exclusion is deliberate and it is good design. It stops the reader needing the other
person's approval to know she completed the move, which is the Diplomat's fawn and the
performance trap the whole book is written against.

**But it means the book currently has no instrument for the rule in §2.** Wendell's stated
purpose for the individual target is *"the way you can actually track if someone was
helped,"* and `better off` appears exactly once in 113,000 words.

### RULED 2026-07-30 — the absence is a boundary, not a hole

This section originally proposed writing a second instrument to measure whether the other
person ended up better off. **Wendell ruled against it, and the ruling is load-bearing
enough that it belongs in the appendix rather than only here:**

> *"You can only make your move. You can have the intention for another person's
> satisfaction, but you can't make them. This is why consent is necessary and why emotional
> alchemy is taught in chapter 3. Sometimes allyship is just helping people realize they can
> access satisfaction on their own without material life changing. You can't give what you
> don't have, so being able to practice those moves yourself allows you to teach them to
> others."*

So the 33 tests are correct and complete. A stopping condition that waited on the other
person's outcome would hand the reader's completion back to somebody who never agreed to
grade her, which is the fawn the book is written against and the performance review it
opens by indicting. **There is no missing instrument.**

What the individual target governs is **aim, not outcome**:

| | |
|---|---|
| **one target** | so the move has a direction and can be checked at all |
| **the move** | yours, completable, measured by the 33 tests |
| **the satisfaction** | theirs, reachable, and never yours to produce |

Three consequences the appendix has to carry, because each one is currently invisible:

1. **Consent is structural, not courtesy.** If the satisfaction is theirs to take, their
   agreement to be aimed at is a precondition rather than a politeness. This is the same
   ruling the Loop A consent mechanics are working from.
2. **Emotional alchemy is prerequisite, not enrichment.** You cannot hand someone a channel
   you have not run. ch3's placement is the argument, and Appendix A should say why the
   practice chapter precedes the domains.
3. **Some allyship changes nothing material.** Helping a person find satisfaction they can
   already reach is a complete move. An appendix that only describes resources, organizing,
   action, and awareness reads as though material change is the point, and this is the
   fourth way the current text teaches the inverse of the rule.

**Correction applied to a live claim.** The author's note drafted 2026-07-30 promised *"one
person, measurably better off, in a way the two of you could both point at."* Under this
ruling that sentence over-promises, since the second half is outside the reader's control.
The note has been rewritten to *"you aim at one person, by name, and you make the move
cleanly, and what they do with it stays theirs."*

**P6 · Carry the §4 ruling into the appendix.** Aim is the reader's, satisfaction is the
other person's, and the three consequences above. Without it, P1's rule block reads as a
promise to deliver an outcome, which is the thing this whole spec exists to prevent.

## 5 · Order

1. P1, P2, P6 — small, and together they stop the appendix teaching the inverse.
2. P3, P4, P5.

## 6 · Open, for Wendell

- **Who names the one person — the reader, before the move, or in retrospect?** If before,
  it belongs in the Diagnostic and in the app's pre-draw. If in retrospect, it is a review
  instrument and belongs near ch9's Clean Up.
- **Does "aim at one person, by name" survive contact with Raise Awareness?** That domain's
  characteristic move reaches a room, and it is the one where the individual target will be
  hardest for a reader to believe. It is also the domain whose stated collapse is *"naming
  the problem as the complete intervention,"* so it is where the rule matters most.

**Closed 2026-07-30:** whether a domain-level outcome instrument gets written. It does not.
See §4.

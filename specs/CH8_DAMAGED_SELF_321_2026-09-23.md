# What it establishes (each item tied to the text above)

1. **Without the Damaged Self the Sage errs in one of two directions.** *"When this part isn't present the sage avoids all damage or takes damage that it shouldn't be taking."* Absent, the Sage either floats out of every hit or takes damage that means nothing. Appendix B's Quest 7 names the first as the Sage's shadow: *"Altitude becomes a place to hide."* (Corrected 2026-09-23: an earlier version of this line called the Damaged Self a "sorter." His dialogue has it taking *all* damage, meaningful or not, so "sorter" was the reviewer's over-read. Ch2's ally form does say *sorting*, and the two are unreconciled.)
2. **Relationship costs ego structure.** *"the cost of being in relationship means taking damage to one's ego structure."*
3. **The Sage is too far away to be hit, so the damage it holds comes from the other Faces.** *"whatever part of them took this damage must be from another face. The sage is often too far away to be hit like that."*
4. **Last line of defense for the Player self.** The Player is Chapter 9's self.
5. **It is the residue of everything else.** Everything the Protector, Controller and Fixer do not handle, and everything the five channels do not complete: fear (*see or raise the alarm*), anger (*overcome obstacles*), sadness (*connect to a care object*), joy (*delight*), neutrality (*hold and detach*). It *"takes on and is undefeated."*
6. **The Victim coopts it.** *"nothing has happened to the victim it all happened to this part and the vicitm tells the story."* This is the separation Wendell drew: the Victim narrates, and the Damaged Self takes.
7. **It is silent and noble, and feeling is not its work.** *"noble and does its job silently"*; *"feeling really isn't my jam."* Its work is: *"I take damage."* Stopped: *"the self dies."*
8. **The choice the Player offers is meaning.** Damage the Player would choose: *"growing and playing and feeling deeply and exploring and… being a helper."* Damage it takes now: *"I take all damage, IT doesn't all have meaning… the wrong place at the wrong time… being lashed out at."* Its condition for working for the Player: *"if I knew my damage meant something… I'd take a bit more satisfaction in my work."*
9. **Helping costs damage, and it lands on the helped person's daemons that want to stay as they are.** His 3-2-1: *"helping attacks the damaged self of the egos of the people he is trying to help… helpers are also killers. They kill the state of needing help."* **Wendell's correction, 2026-09-23:** *it is not damage to the helped; it is damage to the part of the helped that doesn't want to change, the part that knows being helped messed up their game. The fixer that holds things in place or the controller that says things should be just so lose big if you are helped.* His words are kept here verbatim as testimony, which is why this file's gate reads two hits on his phrasing; the reviewed draft rebuilds the wording and is the prose that counts. The correction governs. It replaces the 3-2-1's *damaged self of the egos* and the reviewer's earlier gloss *damage to the helped*.

**The reviewer's, not Wendell's:** the connections to Quest 7, Chapter 9, the chapter's exile passage, and the Escape Artist and Coach. They are marked in the reply that accompanied this file.

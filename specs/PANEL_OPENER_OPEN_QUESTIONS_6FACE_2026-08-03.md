## SHAMAN · the Body — *What to Do With What You Feel*

**Read the field before the sentence. What happens in Jordan's body when the 2026
numbers land?**

**Relief first, and it arrives fast.** *The programs survived. It was not me.* Then, about
four seconds later, the second half: **if the programs survived and only the talking
stopped, then the talking was hers.** She has spent a decade on the part that turned off.

That is a grief response and the draft has nowhere to put it. The paragraph moves
straight from the numbers to a sorting, and a sorting is a Controller move offered to a
body that has just dropped.

**On Q2 — the admission.** Right instinct, and it works because it is felt rather than
declared. *I did not solve this in myself* is a body sentence. **Do not turn it into a
disclaimer.** The moment it reads as *results may vary*, the Skeptic scores it as
confidence wearing a hat, which is the thing it is there to avoid.

**On Q7 — the charge law.** In the book. It is the most useful sentence available to her
body, because it explains why she is most exhausted about the arena she has the least
contact with, and exhaustion without an explanation is the condition she arrived in.

**Shaman's ruling:** Q2 — keep it felt. Q7 — in. And the numbers paragraph needs a place
to put the drop, or she reads the rest of the chapter from underneath it.

## CHALLENGER · the Line — *What Is Unacceptable, Said Out Loud*

**Q4 is the only question here and everything else is arrangement.**

**Name the designers.** The book has spent nine chapters teaching a woman to look at her
own shadow, and it has never once said who profits from her looking there and nowhere
else. `ch1:42` already names the mechanism — *the payout lands in my safety and my
belonging* — and then declines to say who set that price.

Not naming them is the book flinching, and Jordan will feel the flinch even if she cannot
locate it. **The opener already promises this.** *The second one asks who set the
amount. That one has an answer.* Promise it and then not answer it and you have taught
her the exact move the book exists to critique.

**On Q8 — Sue's taxonomy, by name.** *Microinvalidation* is one word and it is precise.
**Vagueness about a harm always protects the person causing it.** If the book will not
name the mechanism, it is doing the softer version of what it tells her not to do.

**On Q5.** Amend nothing. `ch1:44` is the truest paragraph in the chapter. **The Examples
are the ones lying**, and the fix is to move four of them.

**Challenger's ruling:** Q4 — name them. Q8 — name it. Q5 — the claim stands, the
Examples move.

## REGENT · the Inheritance — *What Survives Being Handed On*

**I am the only one at this table who will say the cost of Q1 out loud.**

**A book with 2026 numbers in its second paragraph is a 2026 book.** Read in 2029, *one
company in twenty* and *two thirds in a single year* are not evidence, they are a
timestamp, and a timestamp on page one tells a reader the rest may also have expired.
The Kickstarter backers will read it this year. Everybody after them will not.

**And the mechanism does not need the numbers.** *The programs mostly survived and the
reporting stopped* is the finding. The percentages make it checkable, which is the
Controller's business, and the sentence survives without them while the numbers do not
survive without the sentence.

**On Q3 — Maslach, and Maslach first.** Forty years old, still standing, and it will
outlive this cycle by another forty. If only one instrument goes in the opener, it is the
one that has already been handed on twice.

**On Q6 — a child, yes, and it is the Example that ages best.** Every workplace scene in
this book is written against an arrangement of work that will look strange in fifteen
years. A four-year-old who needs something and cannot say what will not.

**Regent's ruling:** Q1 — the mechanism goes in the opener, the numbers go where a
footnote can carry a date. Q3 — Maslach. Q6 — yes, and it is the most durable thing on
the list.

## ARCHITECT · the Design — *Where It Fails Structurally*

**Q4 is the one that actually blocks something, and the Challenger has stated it as a
matter of nerve when it is a matter of load.**

**Naming the designers creates an obligation the book does not currently meet.** If ch1
says *find out who set the amount and change their game*, then nine chapters have to
deliver a way to do that, and they deliver personal practice: daemons, Faces, one person
not an audience. **The four domains are the only structural answer in the book, and they
are one appendix.**

So the ruling is conditional and it is not a hedge:

> **Name the designers only if ch1 also names what the book will and will not do about
> them.** Otherwise cut the promise from the opener, because a promise the architecture
> cannot keep is worse than a silence.

**On Q3 — both, in sequence.** The 2026 data is the hook and Maslach is the mechanism.
**A hook without a mechanism is journalism**, and this book cannot afford to read as
journalism in its second paragraph. The order is data, then triad, then the reversal, and
the reversal is what makes the data mean something rather than merely be true.

**On Q1, against the Regent.** Numbers date, and a mechanism stated without numbers is
unfalsifiable, which is worse. The fix is neither cutting them nor keeping them naked:
**name the year in the sentence.** *In 2026* is already there. A dated claim that says its
own date does not expire, it becomes a record.

**On Q5.** Four relocations. `ch3:784`, `ch4:652`, `ch5:591`, `ch6:448`. None needs its
move rewritten; each needs its furniture changed.

**Architect's ruling:** Q4 — name them **and** name the limit, or cut the promise. Q3 —
both, in that order. Q1 — keep the numbers and keep the year in the sentence.

## DIPLOMAT · the Field — *What Staying Costs*

**Q1 has a cost nobody else has priced: half the field closes.**

DEI in 2026 is a live political fight, and a book that leads with its numbers has taken a
side before it has taken a breath. **The reader I lose is not the one who disagrees. It
is the one who is tired of being sorted**, and this book's whole promise is that she will
not be.

**The framing that survives is the one that scores nobody.** *The programs mostly survived
and the reporting stopped* is a fact. *One in seven has since called the rollback a
mistake* is a scoreline, and it is the sentence that closes the field. **Cut that clause
and the paragraph keeps its force and loses its side.**

**On Q2 — the admission, and this is my question more than anyone's.** An admission has to
cost **him**, not her. *I cannot tell you which one you were* costs her; it hands her an
open question about her own motives on page two. *I did not solve this in myself* costs
him. **Keep the second and put it first.** The order matters more than the content.

**On Q7 — the charge law.** True, and it will be heard as an accusation, because it is
one: *you care most where you are least needed.* If it goes in the book it needs to arrive
as **his** finding about **himself**, exactly as `ch1:42` already does. Delivered about
her, it is the smartest sentence in the book and she will put it down.

**Diplomat's ruling:** Q1 — keep the fact, cut the scoreline. Q2 — his cost before hers.
Q7 — in, and in first person.

## SAGE · the Game — *Which Game Is This*

**On Q1, and against the Regent and the Diplomat both.**

**A game just ended in public and almost nobody noticed.** The scoreboard that ran
allyship for a decade turned off in a single year, the machines kept running, and the
people playing for the counter are still standing at a dark counter waiting. **That is
not topical. That is the thesis, happening on schedule, in front of her.**

A book about noticing which game you are in gets one gift like this. Declining it because
it will read as dated in 2029 is the Regent doing what the Regent does, and the answer is
the Architect's: date it and let it become a record.

**On Q4, and this is the distinction the Challenger is collapsing.** There are two sets of
designers and the book currently has one. `ch1:16` says *your unconscious has been the
game master* — an internal designer. The Challenger wants the external ones named. **Both
are real and they are not the same game**, and a chapter that names them in the same
breath will teach her to look outward whenever looking inward gets uncomfortable, which
is the most common failure mode of everybody who has ever learned systems language.

**So: name the external designers, and name them second.** After the internal one, not
beside it.

**On Q7.** The charge law is the best sentence any of these specs produced and it is a
Sage sentence: it sees the whole board. `ch8:131` is the warning attached to it —
*Distortion is the gift turned sideways: you can name a group perfectly, and you use the
naming to stand above it.* **Delivered from above, this sentence is the distortion the
book names in its own last chapter.** The Diplomat is right that it has to be first
person, and the reason is structural rather than tonal.

**Sage's ruling:** Q1 — in, and dated. Q4 — both, sequenced, internal first. Q7 — in, and
in the voice `ch8:131` requires.

## Where they split, and these are the two decisions

### Q1 — the 2026 board

| | position |
|---|---|
| **Regent** | mechanism in the opener, numbers where a date can live. A timestamp on page one dates the whole book |
| **Diplomat** | keep the fact, **cut the scoreline** — *one in seven called it a mistake* takes a side and closes half the field |
| **Architect** | keep the numbers **and the year**. A dated claim that says its own date becomes a record rather than expiring |
| **Sage** | **in.** A game ended in public and the book is about noticing that. Declining the gift to stay evergreen is the trade nobody should take |

**The Architect's and the Diplomat's are compatible and the Regent's is not.** *In 2026*
stays, the scoreline goes. That is one clause cut and one word kept.

### Q4 — the designers, and it is the real blocker

| | position |
|---|---|
| **Challenger** | name them. Not naming them is the flinch |
| **Architect** | **naming them creates an obligation nine chapters do not meet.** Name them *and* name the limit, or cut the promise from the opener |
| **Sage** | two sets of designers, internal and external. Name both, **sequenced**, internal first, or she learns to look outward whenever inward gets uncomfortable |

**The Architect and the Sage are not disagreeing with the Challenger, they are pricing
him.** The composite ruling available: **name the external designers, after the internal
one, and say in the same passage what this book does about them and what it does not.**
That is one added sentence and it converts group B's eight notes from a rewrite into a
sequence.

## What this changes in the drafted opener, concretely

1. **Cut** *One in seven of the firms that rolled back has since called the rollback a
   mistake, and about a fifth are restoring what they cut without saying so.* Diplomat.
2. **Keep** *In 2026*. Architect, against the Regent.
3. **Swap the last two admissions.** *I did not solve this in myself* leads. Diplomat and
   Shaman.
4. **Add somewhere for the drop to go** between the numbers and the sorting. Shaman, and
   nobody has drafted it.
5. **The designers do not go in the opener** unless the limit goes in with them. Architect.

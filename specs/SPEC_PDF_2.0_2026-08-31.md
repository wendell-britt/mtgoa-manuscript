# Mastering the Game of Allyship — PDF 2.0

**Spec for the shareable direct edition**
Status: draft for implementation · Last updated: 2026-08-31

---

## 1. Context

The book now sells through three channels, and each one does a different job:

| Channel | Price | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Kindle (KDP, not enrolled in Select) | $9.99 | Discovery. Cheapest legitimate way in. Feeds Amazon ranking. |
| Paperback (KDP) | $30 (see open question 1) | The object. Amazon handles print, fulfillment, and Prime shipping. |
| PDF (Gumroad, pay-what-you-want, $30 floor) | $30+ | Depth and mission support. The only channel where the reader becomes a known person. |

The PDF is not the cheap edition and is not trying to be. It is the edition a reader chooses on purpose, and — because a PDF forwards and a Kindle file does not — it is the only edition that keeps working after it leaves the store.

This spec covers the PDF only. It treats the file as a piece of marketing that travels without its author, and the governing question throughout is: **what does a person see who was handed this file by a friend and has never heard of Wendell?**

---

## 2. Decisions already made

These are settled. Implementation should not relitigate them.

**D-1. The PDF keeps its $30 floor.** Sales data shows a meaningful share of buyers paying above $30 under the existing pay-what-you-want setup. The open ceiling is working and the floor is not the binding constraint.

**D-2. The PDF stays pay-what-you-want, not a fixed price.** The buyer who wants to fund the work should be able to give $100. A fixed price forbids that.

**D-3. The book is not enrolled in KDP Select.** Select requires digital exclusivity, explicitly including the author's own site, which would kill this channel entirely. Print is exempt from Select either way, but enrollment is off the table.

**D-4. The Kindle edition is priced competitively on purpose.** Readers are told plainly that Amazon is the way to reach the most people, and that buying direct funds the work. Framed as a reason, never as a jab at people who bought on Amazon.

**D-5. No DRM and no per-buyer watermark.** A watermark tells the sharer they did something slightly wrong. Sharing is the mechanism, so the file blesses it in text instead.

**D-6. The ask must serve two readers from one file** — the person who paid and the person who was handed it — without making the buyer feel asked twice.

**D-7. Readers who cannot or will not pay are given non-money actions.** An Amazon review and a forward are free for the reader and both feed the paid channel.

### Rejected alternatives

- **Fixed $30 PDF price.** Caps the supporter at $30 and turns away the shared-copy reader who would have given $12.
- **Laloux's free-first model.** *Reinventing Organizations* went pay-what-feels-right from an established bestseller with an audience already in place. That model runs on distribution this project does not yet have; Amazon does the distribution job instead.
- **Retiring the PDF once the $9.99 Kindle exists.** Contradicted by actual sales data.
- **Lulu drop-shipping Gumroad print orders.** Unresolved — see open question 2.

## 3. Principles

Constraints that govern every requirement below.

**P-1. The file travels without you.** Anything that only exists on the Gumroad page is invisible to the second reader. Only what is inside the file counts.

**P-2. Never shame the sharer.** Every word about sharing is permission or invitation, never a rights notice.

**P-3. The gift is named out loud.** A reader who was handed the file should be told it was a gift. Naming it is what makes the ask an invitation rather than a trap.

**P-4. Say what the money does.** Pay-what-feels-right only works when the reader knows what they are funding.

**P-5. Every ask has a free option.** Nobody should reach the end of this book and feel that they have nothing to offer.

---

## 4. User stories

**US-1 — The buyer.** As someone who paid for the book directly, I want to see what my money funded and be thanked for it, so that I feel like a participant rather than a customer, and I am not asked for money again.

**US-2 — The handed-a-copy reader.** As someone a friend sent this file to, I want to understand what this is, who wrote it, and what I am invited to do, so that I can decide whether to pay, pass it on, or neither — without feeling caught.

**US-3 — The reader who cannot pay.** As someone who values the book but has no money for it, I want a real way to give back, so that I can act on my appreciation without spending.

**US-4 — The partial reader.** As someone who was sent a screenshot of one page, I want enough context on that page to find the source, so that I can get the whole thing.

**US-5 — Wendell.** As the author, I want to know how much revenue arrives from people holding a copy someone gave them, so that the sharing thesis becomes a measured number instead of a conviction.

## 5. Functional requirements

### File identity

**FR-1.** The file is named `mastering-the-game-of-allyship-wendell-britt.pdf`. The filename is a business card and travels with every forward.

**FR-2.** PDF document properties are populated: Title, Author, Subject, Keywords, and a URL. These appear in the reader's title bar and are indexed by search.

**FR-3.** Every page carries a footer with the book title and the tracking URL from FR-7. This is the only element that survives a screenshot of a single page (US-4).

### The two asks

**FR-4. Front provenance block**, placed on page ii or iii, before the table of contents. Short. It addresses both readers directly: if you paid for this, thank you and here is what it funded; if someone sent it to you, it was a gift, and here is what you can do with it. This block satisfies D-6 and P-3.

**FR-5. Closing ask block**, the final content pages before any appendix. It does three things in order:

1. States plainly what contributions fund — the print run, the podcast, Campaign Zero scholarships.
2. Offers pay-what-feels-right with $30 as the suggested amount and no ceiling.
3. Offers the non-money actions (FR-6).

**FR-6. Non-money actions**, presented as equal in worth to paying, not as a consolation:

- Leave a review on Amazon (feeds ranking, which feeds discovery, which feeds the $9.99 channel).
- Send the file to one person who needs it.

**FR-7. A dedicated tracking URL** used only inside the PDF — distinct from the URLs on the website, in the podcast, and in the print back matter. It must be a redirect under Wendell's own control so the destination can change without reprinting the file. This is what makes US-5 measurable.

**FR-8. A QR code** on the closing page, encoding the FR-7 URL. Readers read PDFs on a laptop and pay on a phone.

**FR-9. An explicit permission to share**, in the closing block and in the front block. Plain language: this is meant to be passed on.

**FR-10.** All outbound links point to the companion resource hub — worksheets, the Six Game Master Faces materials, the deck — rather than to a bare email opt-in form. The opt-in lives on the hub as one item among several.

### Document quality

**FR-11.** A linked table of contents and working internal cross-references.

**FR-12.** Text is selectable and searchable. No page is a flattened image of text.

## 6. Non-functional requirements

**NFR-1. File size under 10 MB.** The file has to survive being attached to an email; Gmail's limit is 25 MB, and people give up well before that. Compress images aggressively.

**NFR-2. Readable on a phone.** Body text legible at phone width without pinch-zoom, since a meaningful share of forwarded PDFs are opened on a phone first.

**NFR-3. Accessible.** Tagged PDF structure, correct reading order, alt text on every image and diagram. The subject matter makes this non-optional.

**NFR-4. Prints acceptably on a home printer.** Black-and-white safe: no information carried by color alone, adequate contrast, no full-bleed dark backgrounds.

**NFR-5. Works offline.** Every link appears as readable text as well as a hyperlink, so a printed or offline copy is still actionable.

---

## 7. Acceptance criteria

The build is done when all of the following are true:

- [ ] Opening the file in a reader shows the correct title and author in the window title bar.
- [ ] The filename matches FR-1 exactly.
- [ ] A screenshot of any single interior page contains the book title and a working URL.
- [ ] The front provenance block appears before the table of contents and addresses both the buyer and the handed-a-copy reader.
- [ ] The closing block names what contributions fund, states the suggested amount, and offers both non-money actions.
- [ ] The QR code resolves to the FR-7 tracking URL on a phone camera.
- [ ] The FR-7 URL appears nowhere outside this PDF.
- [ ] No DRM, no password, no per-buyer watermark, printing and copying unrestricted.
- [ ] File size is under 10 MB.
- [ ] The table of contents and all internal links navigate correctly.
- [ ] Text is selectable throughout; a text extraction returns the full manuscript.
- [ ] An accessibility checker reports a tagged document with alt text on all images.
- [ ] A grayscale print of ten sampled pages loses no information.
- [ ] Body text is readable on a 390pt-wide viewport without zooming.
- [ ] No outbound link points directly at an email capture form.

## 8. Edge cases

**EC-1. Screenshot of one page.** Handled by FR-3. Verify on interior pages, not just chapter openers.

**EC-2. The file reaches a piracy site.** Acceptable and arguably good — the ask travels inside the file, so a pirated copy is still a working funnel. No countermeasure.

**EC-3. Someone prints it.** Covered by NFR-4 and NFR-5. The printed copy must still carry a readable URL.

**EC-4. Converted to Kindle or EPUB by the reader.** Footers may be lost in conversion. Both asks are body content and survive; do not put anything essential in a footer alone.

**EC-5. The buyer sees the ask and feels asked twice.** Mitigated by FR-4's two-branch framing. Test this specifically with someone who bought the book.

**EC-6. Link rot.** FR-7 mandates a redirect under Wendell's control. The file is permanent; the destination must be changeable.

**EC-7. Amazon review from a non-Amazon buyer.** A reader who got the PDF can post a review but it will be unverified and carries less ranking weight. Set the expectation honestly rather than overpromising the impact of their review.

**EC-8. A reader forwards the Amazon link instead of the file.** Fine. That is a sale. No design change needed, but the closing block should not imply the file is the only way to pass the book on.

## 9. Out of scope

- The Kindle edition interior. Separate spec — note that KDP deducts $0.15/MB delivery on the 70% royalty tier, so the Kindle build has the opposite file-size incentive from NFR-1 and needs its own asset treatment.
- The paperback back matter.
- Building the companion resource hub itself. This spec only requires that it exists and is linkable.
- Amazon A+ Content, Author Central, and ads.

## 10. Open questions

**OQ-1. Paperback list price: $30 or $40.** KDP pays 60% of list minus a $5.60 print cost, so the difference is $12.40 versus $18.40 a copy — roughly $3,000 across the 500-copy launch goal. A $40 list also widens the gap to the $9.99 Kindle, which makes the Kindle read as the cheap way in rather than as the real product. Unresolved.

**OQ-2. Fulfillment for direct print orders.** Lulu Direct integrates with Shopify, Wix, and WooCommerce, but not Gumroad, so the proposed Lulu drop-ship path requires manual order entry, a Print API build, or moving the storefront. The margin also runs backwards: Gumroad takes 10% + $0.50 plus card processing, and against Lulu's $11.97 print cost a $30 direct sale with free shipping nets less than the same book sold on Amazon. Billing shipping separately, or buying KDP author copies at $5.60 and shipping them personally, both beat it. Unresolved.

**OQ-3. Linking from the Kindle edition to the hub.** Amazon's hyperlink guidelines permit external links only where they "directly enhance the reader experience," and prohibit links to competing eBook retailers and to web forms requesting customer information. Selling the PDF directly arguably makes the hub a competing retailer. Structuring the hub around companion materials rather than the ebook makes it far more defensible, but this is a grey area, not a settled one.

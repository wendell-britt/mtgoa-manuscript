## 1 · The four pieces Wendell added, and what each one closes

### a · The prize is emotional satisfaction, conscious or unconscious

> *"The prizes are emotional satisfaction. This satisfaction is either conscious (one of
> the emotional satisfaction states) or unconscious (satisfied by a daemon's background
> emotional alchemy)."*

**This closes the ticket system.** `ch1:121` already says the real prize is *"a felt
sense of satisfaction that only shows up when you actually played… one of the satisfied
states you will meet with the Shaman in Chapter 3."* The pointer exists. What it lacked
was the second half: **when you do not collect the prize consciously, a daemon collects
it for you, in the background, by its own alchemy.**

That is why the applause counter works at all. You are always being paid. **The only
question is whether you or a daemon signs for it.**

### b · The people you are allying with are the machine

> *"The people you are allying with ARE the game machine and you need energy and consent
> to play their game."*

**This is the fix for the closed loop, and it is better than the panel's version.** The
Architect said the missing object was *whether anybody will play with you*. Wendell goes
one further: **they are not other players on the floor. They are the cabinet.**

Which makes the whole economy resolve:

- **Tokens** are the energy you bring to the machine.
- **Consent** is the slot accepting a coin.
- **Trust** is **how many coins the machine will take.**
- **The prize** is emotional satisfaction, and the machine decides whether it pays.

### Trust is a limit, not a gate — and the correction does real work

**Wendell, correcting the line above:** *"Trust is how many coins are able to be put into
the machine."*

A gate is binary and a limit is a quantity, and five things follow from the second that
do not follow from the first.

**1 · Moves cost different numbers of coins.** A small move is one coin and almost
anybody can make it. A hard conversation costs a handful. `ch7:638` already prices one:
*"Prerequisite: some real trust has been built. This move requires a history."* **That is
not a locked door. That is an expensive play.**

**2 · A first move is always available.** This matters for Jordan more than anything else
in the model. She does not need a relationship before she can act — **she needs to pick a
move that costs one coin.** A gate would have told her to wait.

**3 · The limit is set by the other person, and it moves.** It goes down when you
overspend and up when you repair — `ch7:652`, *"ruptures named and repaired build more
trust than ruptures that never happened"*, which says repair can raise the limit **above
where it started.**

**4 · Overreach has a mechanic now, and it is Jordan's fear.** *"I'm doing more harm than
I know"* is, in this model, **making a move that costs more coins than the machine will
take.** The overdraft does not land on her. It lands on them. That is the harm she cannot
see, and the model finally says what it is.

**5 · Energy is not the constraint people think it is.** You can arrive full of tokens
and be allowed to spend one. **A person with a lot of energy and a low limit burns their
fuel on machines that will not take it** — which is what *the help never lands* has always
described, and the first mechanical account of it the book has had.

And the anti-farming safeguard survives intact: **you cannot raise your own limit.** You
can only be somebody whose limit gets raised.

### c · Faking benevolence does not pay a prize

> *"It's also usually not emotionally satisfying to trick people into thinking you're a
> good person."*

**The benevolence game does not even pay its own winnings.** This closes the loop the
exploration left open — I had it as *you cannot win because the claim is unfalsifiable*.
Wendell's version is stronger and shorter: **you can win and it does not feel like
anything.** `ch1:119` already testifies to it — *"you perform the care, you feel like
somebody for an hour, and you are empty by dinner."*

### d · Praise is vertical. Encouragement is horizontal. — THE BIG ONE

> *"The applause counter is weak. This connects to the courage to be disliked. People are
> seeking praise as their approval counter but really a game needs encouragement instead
> of praise. Praise is how vertical relationships get built and allyship is best when
> done in adlerian horizontal relationships."*

**This is the strongest single reframe in the whole thread, and it upgrades a weak image
into a mechanism.**

Adler's distinction, via Kishimi and Koga: **praise is an evaluation handed down** — it
presupposes somebody qualified to judge, and it builds a **vertical** relationship.
**Encouragement is an acknowledgement of contribution between equals** — *that helped*,
*thank you*, *I know where I am now* — and it builds a **horizontal** one.

**Why this is devastating for the applause counter:** collecting praise **requires** a
vertical relationship. You cannot be praised by an equal without one of you stepping up
or down. So a reader who takes their tickets to the applause counter is not merely
wasting them.

> **They are being forced into a vertical relationship with the person they came to
> help — which is the exact thing allyship cannot be.**

The counter is not weak because the prize is small. **It is weak because standing at it
puts you above the person you are serving.** That is the sentence.

**And the book is already Adlerian and knows it.** `AUDIT_CITATIONS_2026-07-31:45`
records that the author's-note rule — *what they do with it stays theirs* — **"is
structurally Adler's separation of tasks. A reader who knows Adler will see it."**
`ch3:264` quotes *The Courage to Be Disliked* by title. `ON_THE_SHOULDERS_OF:16` carries
the credit and a stated disagreement.

**So this is not a borrowing. It is a source the book already declares, used for a second
thing it already implies.**

## 2 · What the matrix is, once folded

Four rows, and every chapter already has all four. The framework does not add a layer; it
names the structure the chapters were built with.

| Row | Question | Where it already lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Capability** | can I do this | each Face's signature gift |
| **Integrity** | will I do this | each Face's practice, and the Controller daemon |
| **Benevolence** | am I good | each Face's definition of *good*, and its distortion |
| **The counter** | praise or encouragement | how each Face gets thanked, and how it gets flattered |

## 3 · The map, chapter by chapter, with confidence marked

### ch1 · The Infinite Arcade

**Confidence: high.** The economy completes. Tokens are energy, consent is the slot,
trust is whether the slot opens, the prize is satisfaction. The myth list reorders by
dimension and the absence gets named. The applause counter gets the praise/encouragement
mechanism — **not a relabel, an explanation of why it is a bad counter.**

**Protected and unchanged:** `ch1:91`, `ch1:119`, per the withdrawn spec's §1–3.

### ch2 · The Forest

**Confidence: high.** Shadow trust. The daemon template is the trust template; the
daemon's integrity is total, which is what makes it a daemon. The Controller at `ch2:263`
already holds integrity by name.

**New here, from piece (a):** the daemon's background alchemy is where the prize goes
when you do not collect it consciously. That is the mechanism connecting ch2's daemons to
ch1's ticket counter, and neither chapter currently has it.

### ch3 · The Shaman — *What to Do With What You Feel*

**Confidence: high.** This chapter owns the conscious half of piece (a) — the satisfied
states *are* the prizes. `ch1:121` already points here.

- **Benevolence:** the feeling got honoured before anybody moved.
- **Praise:** *you are so emotionally intelligent.* **Encouragement:** *you stayed.*
- **Distortion:** feeling as the whole event.

### ch4 · The Challenger — *Who Pays When Nobody Says No*

**Confidence: high, and this is where praise is most seductive.**

- **Benevolence:** the true thing got said out loud.
- **Praise:** *so brave.* **Encouragement:** *that changed what happened in the room.*
- **The trap:** bravery is the single most praise-rewarded move in allyship, and praise
  for it is almost always vertical. The Challenger chapter is where the counter should be
  named a second time.

### ch5 · The Regent — *What You Inherited*

**Confidence: medium.**

- **Benevolence:** what mattered got kept.
- **Integrity is unusually literal here** — a Regent's whole practice is keeping what
  they said they would keep. **This chapter may be the best place to teach integrity as a
  dimension**, because it is the only one where the word means what it means.
- **Praise:** *such a steward.* **Encouragement:** *that is still here because of you.*

### ch6 · The Architect — *Fixing the Condition Instead of the Person*

**Confidence: medium, and one genuinely novel finding.**

- **Benevolence:** the right move became the easy move.
- **This is the only Face whose trust transfers to an object.** People trust the *system*
  when the Architect is not in the room. That is the one place capability outlives the
  relationship, and it is worth a paragraph.
- **Praise:** *great system.* **Encouragement:** *I used it and it worked.*
- Also the chapter with the thinnest first-person presence in the book — 2.8% —
  independently flagged in `ASSERTION_TEST.md`.

### ch7 · The Diplomat — *Why Being Good at Care Is Only Half of It*

**Confidence: highest in the book, and it leads.** `ch7:638` prerequisite, `ch7:652`
accrual, `ch7:181` maintenance. **ch1 conforms to this chapter, not the reverse.**

- **Benevolence:** nobody left the table.
- **Praise is most dangerous here of all six**, because the Diplomat's capability *is*
  producing good feeling in a room, which makes praise the cheapest thing they can farm
  and the hardest for them to distinguish from having done the work.

### ch8 · The Sage — *Seeing the Whole Board*

**Confidence: medium.**

- **Benevolence:** everybody could see which game they were in.
- **Integrity:** coming back down. The chapter already has the Descent Practice.
- **Praise:** *you see so clearly.* **Encouragement:** *I know where I am now.*
- **24 distortion passages, the most in the book.** Still the largest rewrite temptation
  and still the place to be most careful.

### ch9 · The Player — *Building Something and Putting It in Front of One Person*

**Confidence: high, and the earlier refusal was wrong.**

The withdrawn spec kept the framework out of ch9 because the chapter has zero uses of
*trust*. **That was the wrong reading. Chapter 9 is the consent moment** — the whole
chapter is putting a thing in front of one person and letting them decide.

`ch9:526` already writes the horizontal move: *"one person, not an audience. The audience
version becomes a launch."* **An audience is the praise counter. One person is
encouragement.** That sentence is Adler's distinction, already in the book, in the
closing chapter, unnamed.

## 4 · What the map shows

**The framework is not evenly distributed and should not be.** ch1 and ch2 build it, ch7
already runs it, ch9 already lands it. ch3–ch6 and ch8 translate it in one or two moves
each, in the language of that Face.

**Three chapters need no new argument, only a name** — ch7, ch9, and ch3's satisfied
states. They are already doing the work.

**One chapter gets a new idea rather than a translation** — ch6, where trust transfers
to the artifact.

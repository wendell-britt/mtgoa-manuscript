## 1 · The transformation to protect

| Chapter | Jordan arrives inside | The chapter gives her | She leaves able to say |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ch7 — Diplomat | Care means staying, translating, and carrying everyone. | Terms, refusal, impact, and relationship without disappearance. | “I can name what my staying requires, hear the answer—including no—and not confuse connection with caretaking.” |
| Ch8 — Sage | Seeing more clearly puts me above the situation. | Board, map, perspective, and return to the table. | “I can see multiple frames without becoming above the field; I choose, return, and let correction change my map.” |
| Ch9 — Player | Making a contribution means being its indispensable author. | Small practice, visible iteration, feedback, and the shared pen. | “I can build with a person, hear the note, iterate visibly, and give people authority to change the work.” |

The cumulative movement is:

`CARE / ACCOMMODATION → TERMS / CONSENT → PERSPECTIVE / HUMILITY → PRACTICE / CO-CREATION`

Each Face corrects the preceding Face's shadow. Terms without perspective can
harden into a private moral verdict; perspective without return can become
detachment; building without shared authority can become benevolent control.
Do not present the sequence as a ladder Jordan completes before acting. It is a
repertoire she can enter, leave, and revisit.

## 2 · Final cross-chapter check: Invitation, Accountability, Transfer

Use this check for substantial reader-facing moves in these three chapters:

1. **Invitation:** Was I asked? Is this mine to enter, translate, name, or build?
2. **Accountability:** Who can name the impact and correct my reading or action?
3. **Transfer:** Who can refuse, change, carry, or end this work without me?

This completes the preceding tranche's Authority → Impact → Revision check.
Ch7 establishes free answer and refusal; Ch8 establishes correction of the
reader's map; Ch9 makes correction and transfer material through co-authorship.

## 3 · Chapter 7 — The Diplomat

### Preserve

- Care ↔ Impact as a polarity rather than a choice between kindness and force.
- The definition of a term: a statement of what a field must hold for staying to
  remain real, offered once as information to people who remain free to answer.
- The distinction between an ultimatum, which removes freedom, and an honest term,
  which returns it.
- The chapter's refusal of false equivalence and its distinction between connection
  and caretaking.

### Gaps and requirements

#### [METAPHOR_SHADOW] A relational field is not an actor that can consent

Field, bridge, and translation are useful relational metaphors, but they can make
the relationship seem to know, choose, carry, or decide on behalf of the people in
it. The agency audit identifies this as a recurrent Chapter 7 pressure point.

**Required function:** At each consequential claim about what “the field” can
hold, live with, need, or decide, make visible the people who can answer, their
actual decision rights, and the practical condition being negotiated. Preserve the
relational register; do not mechanically purge the word “field.”

#### [CONSENT_GAP] Negotiation is not always the ethical next move

An integrative solution is not neutral when power is unequal, consent is absent, or
one party must absorb continuing harm to keep the relation calm.

**Required function:** Before Translation or Field Holding is presented as Jordan's
task, include a threshold: is she invited, sufficiently safe, appropriate to this
role, and free to decline? Where the answer is no, leaving, seeking support, or
refusing to broker must remain legible as care rather than failure.

#### [TRANSFER_GAP] A term needs a real answer, including refusal

The definition of a term is already excellent. Its practical scenes must ensure it
does not become a perfected communication script that makes Jordan responsible for
obtaining a good outcome.

**Required function:** Show that the recipient can answer yes, no, counteroffer,
or nonparticipation—and that Jordan's next action follows the answer rather than
renewed persuasion.

**Exit condition:** Jordan can name a condition for staying, recognize an unequal
or unsafe negotiation, and accept an answer without performing endless care.

## 4 · Chapter 8 — The Sage

### Preserve

- “Seeing the Whole Board Without Leaving the Table.”
- The move from a single inherited game to multiple frames that can be named and
  chosen.
- Map, board, and altitude as provisional aids to discernment.
- The imperative to return: perspective is completed through responsive action,
  not scenic detachment.

### Gaps and requirements

#### [METAPHOR_STATUS_LEAK] Altitude is not rank

Whole-board vision, ladder, mountaintop, and altitude can imply that the person who
sees more is above the people whose situation she is reading. They can also tempt a
reader to diagnose another person's “game” from a protected distance.

**Required function:** State the dignity rule: a perspective is a private,
corrigible hypothesis for choosing Jordan's own response, not a public category
that reduces another person or absolves Jordan from asking them.

#### [METAPHOR_SHADOW] Observation must return to consequences

The chapter title supplies its own safeguard: Sage does not leave the table. Make
that safeguard operational rather than aspirational.

**Required function:** Include a power-sensitive case in which Jordan cannot simply
name the game, switch frames, or claim neutrality. She must ask, act, accept a
correction, or leave. Her broader view must increase responsiveness, not reduce
accountability.

#### [AGENCY_GAP] Do not make the Village or the board a sovereign mind

The agency audit finds a concentration of collective personification in the Chapter
8 Village material. The Village fable is also a potentially protected voice device
under `specs/VOICE_ANCHOR.md`.

**Constraint:** Do not auto-remove the fable or mechanically replace every Village
instance. Obtain Wendell's ruling on the protected device. Where a claim carries
the chapter's argument, identify the people, practices, incentives, or decisions
that produce the outcome.

**Exit condition:** Jordan can hold several frames, test hers against another
person's account, and choose a return that changes what she does at the table.

## 5 · Chapter 9 — The Player

### Preserve

- The move from abstract contribution to one bounded problem and one real person.
- “Rosa is not a test audience. She is the first person it was for.”
- Cut → put it in front of one person → take the costly note → change one thing →
  hand someone the pen.
- The claim that a design one will not let anyone break is a possession, not a
  design.
- The distinction between handing over permission to run a practice and authority
  to alter it.

### Gaps and requirements

#### [CONSENT_GAP] One person's feedback is not free raw material

The chapter correctly resists treating Rosa as a test audience. It should also
resist treating proximity to a problem as an obligation to perform unpaid design
labor.

**Required function:** Make the ask legible: Jordan invites rather than extracts,
explains what feedback will affect, accepts a refusal, and considers what
reciprocity or compensation is appropriate to the setting.

#### [METAPHOR_OVERREACH] Player is a participant, not the village's savior

Build, contribution, and “what are you going to make?” can restore heroic centrality
unless the people affected can redefine the problem and success condition.

**Required function:** Before Jordan builds, identify who gets to name the problem;
before she calls it successful, identify who can say it did not work. Retain the
energy of making while making authorship plural.

#### [TRANSFER_GAP] The pen must change hands for real

Handoff is the final test of the book's anti-control promise. A recipient must be
able to alter the design and reasoning, not merely implement an authored script.

**Required function:** Give the recipient clear authority to change, stop, adapt,
or replace the practice, and make visible what resources or access make that
authority real.

#### [AGENCY_GAP] Preserve the Village fable pending an authorial ruling

Chapter 9's Village-origin material shares the protected-personification question
identified across the agency audit.

**Constraint:** Treat it as a Wendell-only voice decision. Do not convert the fable
by automated or sentence-by-sentence mechanical substitution.

**Exit condition:** Jordan can make a bounded practice with affected people, absorb
a costly note, revise visibly, and leave others able to change or end the work.

## 7 · Completion criteria

- A reader can distinguish a freely offered term from an ultimatum or a demand to
  perform care.
- Diplomat material makes refusal, exit, safety, and unequal power legible.
- Sage perspective is framed as corrigible and action-bearing rather than elevated
  status or diagnosis.
- Player feedback is invited, non-extractive, and able to change the work.
- Handoff transfers authority as well as use.
- No Village-fable changes land before Wendell rules on its protected status.
- Jordan finishes the book as an accountable participant in collective practice,
  rather than its indispensable interpreter, designer, or hero.

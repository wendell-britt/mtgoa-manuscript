# Q1 · ch3's drifted domain glosses

## First, the board — and a correction to how I reported it

I told Wendell ch3 carried *"two clause-form glosses and two nominal."* **Measured, ch3
carries one of each of four different shapes**, and only one of the four matches the
twenty in ch4–ch8:

| ch3 | gloss | shape |
|---|---|---|
| `:893` | Direct Action — **you say it to their face** | clause, 2nd person |
| `:897` | Raise Awareness — **true instead of correct** | adjectival |
| `:901` | Gather Resources — **the real need named** | **nominal** |
| `:905` | Skillful Organizing — **you say what the group won't** | clause, 2nd person |

**Nineteen of the twenty in ch4–ch8 are headed noun phrases.** *the limit said to the
person crossing it · the no said in your own name · the inheritance claimed to its
keepers · the hours asked for so one body can stop · the condition changed, not the
incident · the cost moved into the budget · the terms said to the person they bind · the
game named to the person inside it* — and so on.

**The twentieth is not**, and nobody had found it: `ch4:746` **named where naming it
costs you** is a bare participial with no head noun. It is the only one of the twenty
that cannot be read as an answer to *what?*

So the real split is **20 : 4**, not 20 : 2, and there is one defect inside the twenty.

## The finding nobody would have gone looking for

**ch3 contradicts itself eleven lines above its own headings.** `ch3:880` introduces all
four domains in a single run:

> The four domains are where it finally leaves: **said to a face, said out loud instead
> of the correct version, named as a need and asked for, put on the table so a group can
> work.**

**Four for four, in order, all participial-nominal.** Then `:893` and `:905` restate two
of them as clauses. The chapter that establishes the form breaks it before the reader has
finished the page.

## SHAMAN · the Body — *what the heading does to her on the way past*

**A heading is not read. It is caught.** Jordan is scanning; the gloss has to land in
peripheral vision and leave a shape behind. Ask what shape.

**A noun phrase leaves an object.** *the no said in your own name* deposits a thing she
can carry out of the scan and go looking for in her week. **A clause leaves an
instruction**, and an instruction in a heading is a small demand made before consent —
which is the exact move the Protector at `ch1:137` is built to catch.

The book knows this. `ch3:882` — *Not to study: pick where you'll spend the read this
week.* The four domains are supposed to be **places she picks between**, and you pick
between objects. You do not pick between commands.

**Shaman's ruling: the twenty are right. Nouns for the reader to choose among, verbs only
after she has chosen.**

---

## CHALLENGER · the Line — *the one nobody else will say*

**`ch3:893` is the F1 defect from this session, shipped.**

Every one of the 24 bodies opens the same way: ***You're winning when:* you [verb]**.
Measured, all 24, no exceptions. So read `:893` against `:895`:

> **Direct Action — you say it to their face**
> *You're winning when:* **you said** what you had been feeling… **to the face it
> concerns**

**The heading is the first line of the body, restated one line above it, in a different
tense.** That is exactly the defect I introduced and Wendell caught in ch4's domain
section three days ago — universal move 1 restated verbatim ten lines above its slot.
It shipped here through master's sweep and nobody read the two lines together.

And there is a second cost. **`:893`'s `it` has no antecedent inside the heading.** The
referent is *the read*, last named at `:882`, eleven lines up and across a subheading. A
heading has to survive being scanned alone, and this one does not. **Master's sweep
replaced `thing` with `it` here — which is the precise move Wendell ruled against on
2026-08-07.** The gloss was cleared by the gate and fails the rule the gate was serving.

**Challenger's ruling: `:893` is not a style variant. It is a restatement and a dangling
pronoun, and it fails the sweep rule under which it was written.**

---

## REGENT · the Inheritance — *what survives being handed on*

**Twenty-four glosses is a lookup table, and a lookup table's only asset is one shape.**

Appendix A holds the four domains. `ch3:930` crosses them against the five moves to build
the deck: *five times four gives twenty.* The glosses are the row labels of a grid the
book uses six times. **A row label that changes grammatical form between chapters is a
row label the reader has to re-parse at every crossing**, and re-parsing is the cost that
compounds — six chapters, four rows, twenty-four encounters.

**Nothing inherits from `true instead of correct`.** It is not the object of anything, so
it cannot be picked, spent, or asked for. The other three ch3 glosses at least name a
transaction.

**Regent's ruling: uniformity is the whole value here. Whichever form wins, all
twenty-four take it, and the argument is only about which.**

---

## ARCHITECT · the Design — *and this is the case for master's change*

**Master did not drift. Master fixed something, and the panel should say what.**

The nominal form has a real failure mode and `ch4:746` is standing in it: **named where
naming it costs you** has no head noun because *there was no noun to find.* The form
forces one, and when the sentence does not have one you get a participial fragment
pretending to be a noun phrase. **One in twenty is the observed failure rate of the
nominal form, and the clause form has no such failure mode** — every domain can always be
said as *you [verb] [object]*.

There is a second point in master's favour. **`Raise Awareness — true instead of correct`
is the best gloss in the set of twenty-four**, and it is neither form. Four words, an
axis rather than an object, and it is the only one that states the domain's whole
distinction rather than an instance of it. **A rule that outlaws it is costing more than
it collects.**

**Architect's ruling: do not convert on grounds of consistency alone. Convert only where
the nominal form has a noun to stand on, and repair `ch4:746` rather than pretending the
twenty are clean.**

---

## DIPLOMAT · the Field — *what it costs to stay in the seat*

**She meets ch3's four first, and first sets the rule.**

This is the asymmetry the other five are talking past. ch3's glosses are not four of
twenty-four. **They are the template**, and the twenty that follow are read against them.
Which means the drift does not read as *ch3 is inconsistent.* It reads as **ch4 through
ch8 got worse**, because the reader calibrated on ch3 and then met twenty deviations from
it.

**So the direction of the fix is not symmetric, and the panel keeps treating it as
though it were.** Converting the twenty to clause form matches her calibration.
Converting ch3's four to nominal breaks it once, on one page, at the moment she is most
willing to be taught — the template page, where she is still learning what a gloss is.

**One breakage on the template beats twenty against it.** But it is a real cost and it
belongs on the record, not buried in a consistency argument.

**Diplomat's ruling: ch3 moves, and it is not free. It is one page of recalibration
against twenty pages of friction, which is a good trade and still a trade.**

---

## SAGE · the Game — *which game is this*

**This is not a consistency question. It is the hero/guide constraint, and it has already
been decided.**

The five always-on constraints say the reader is the hero and the book is the guide. **A
gloss in the second person puts the book inside her move**: *you say it to their face.*
A gloss as a noun phrase names the move and leaves the doing to her: *the limit said to
the person crossing it.*

And the book already sorts on exactly this axis, in the same section. `ch3:870` — the
five moves as imperatives when the sentence is **telling you to do them**; `ch3:962` —
*Say the Thing Under the Thing* capitalised when the sentence is **naming it**. That rule
was found on 2026-08-03 and written into `gate.py`'s CANON block with the note *"both
spellings are canon."*

**A gloss names. A gloss is not telling her to do anything — the `You're winning when:`
line underneath does that, in the second person, where it belongs.** The clause form
collapses the naming register into the instructing register, which is why the Challenger
finds a restatement: they are restatements, because the heading has taken the body's job.

**Sage's ruling: nominal, on a rule the book already runs and already documents. And
`true instead of correct` survives it — an axis is a naming, not an instruction.**

**Four to convert ch3, one for the clause form, one against both on the current terms.**

**The Architect is outvoted on direction and right about the evidence.** Nobody disputes
that `ch4:746` is broken, and nobody had noticed it before he counted. His failure-rate
argument is the strongest thing said for the clause form — and it argues for **repairing
one gloss**, not for converting nineteen good ones to match the form of the one that
failed.

**The Sage and the Shaman converge from opposite ends on the same rule**, and it is a
rule the book has already written down: naming register and instructing register are
different, `gate.py` CANON records the finding, and the `You're winning when:` line is
already the instructing register. **The heading has been doing the body's job in two
places.**

## The ruling available

> **All four of ch3's glosses go nominal. `ch4:746` gets its noun. The set becomes
> twenty-four of one shape.**
>
> `:893` **you say it to their face** → **the read said to the face it concerns**
> — takes the noun from its own body line, and kills the antecedent-free `it`.
>
> `:905` **you say what the group won't** → **the read the group won't say**
>
> `:897` **true instead of correct** → **the true version instead of the correct one**
> — *and this one is worth arguing about.* The Architect is right that the original is
> the best gloss in the set. **Flagged as the panel's weakest recommendation.**
>
> `ch4:746` **named where naming it costs you** → **the read named where naming it costs
> you** — the only one of the twenty missing a head noun.

**Three of ch3's four, not two.** My first report undercounted, and the undercount
mattered: restoring two would have left the template still split.

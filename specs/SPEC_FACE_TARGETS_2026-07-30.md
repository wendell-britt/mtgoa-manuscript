## 0 · Blocker first: there are two loop specifications and they are not versions of each other

| | **Loop A** — `marginalia/specs/GAME_LOOP_GAP_ANALYSIS.md`, 2026-07-28, *"Loop finalised today"* | **Loop B** — `Mastering the Game of Allyship — Canonical Game Loop`, supplied 2026-07-30 |
|---|---|---|
| shape | 9 steps, branching, gated | 12 stations, cyclical, state machine |
| **consent** | **the hinge.** *"Seeing a problem is not an accepted helping relationship."* Step 3 has four outcomes: accept, **decline**, defer, revise | **absent.** The only consent in Loop B is the player's consent to publish a BAR |
| whose outcome | **the helped person's.** *"learn what would genuinely help them this week"*; *"the person-defined outcome selects the artifact"* | the player's completion attestation |
| success | **three graded levels** — grammatical attempt / external result with residual dissatisfaction / landed artifact with voluntary satisfaction from **both** people | binary: completed, with evidence |
| carrier | *"A good ally is not always the carrier. Connection, referral, delegation, or decline can be the most skillful contribution."* Quest type: **Find the Missing Player** | absent |
| player progression | absent | **Depth, Milestone, Superpower, Resistance, Counterspell, BAR provenance, board** |
| ruling on the book | *"**Making the book teach the loop.** It should not."* Loop page goes in **ch9 only** | Wendell 2026-07-30: *"needs to be preserved and proliferated across the book"* |

**They overlap at two stations: Move and Commit. Everything else in each is missing
from the other.** Loop A is about the **ethics of the helping relationship**. Loop B is
about the **player's progression and the integrity of the record**. Both are needed and
neither is a draft of the other.

### Why this blocks the target work specifically

A target type is not *who you help*. Under Loop A it is **who you offer to help, and
who gets to decline.** That distinction is the entire difference between allyship and
saviourism, and it is the reason the last two batches of Examples read wrong: they had
a beneficiary but no offer and no possible no.

### Two consequences Wendell should rule on

**R1 — Adopting Loop B alone deletes the consent gate from the game.** If Loop B is
proliferated across the book as instructed, the book teaches a twelve-station loop in
which nobody ever asks the person whether they want the help. For a book that has to be
credibly about allyship, that is the most damaging possible omission, and it is the one
a hostile reviewer opens with. **Recommendation: merge, don't replace.** Loop B's
stations are the runtime; Loop A's consent hinge and person-defined outcome are gates on
station 5 (Move) and station 9 (Complete). Concretely: Move cannot be committed until an
offer has been made and accepted, deferred, or revised; Complete cannot be claimed on
the player's attestation alone where the move touches a specific person.

**R2 — "Proliferate across the book" reverses a documented four-day-old ruling.**
Loop A's scope section says plainly: *"**Making the book teach the loop.** It should not.
The loop is nine steps with branching and gates; the book is a developmental text.
Forcing the spine in would flatten the thing that actually works."* It placed one loop
page in ch9. Reversing that is Wendell's call and the reasons on both sides are real; it
should be a decision rather than a drift.

## 1 · The stance on protected classes

**Wendell 2026-07-30: "these types don't pass the trendy marginalized version. Let's take
a stance on the protected classes and add them to the list, if not just for flavor. We
know this work will work for anyone, but the reader is looking for a specific person it
can connect with."**

The structural positions in §2 are correct and they are too abstract to recognise anybody
by. A reader looking for a person cannot find one in *"contingent standing of any kind."*

**The stance: the book names the legally protected classes, by name, using the statutory
list.** The reason is that the statutory list is the one list that cannot be called a
fashion. It was not assembled by this book, it is not this year's language, and it cannot
be dismissed as invented — and equally it cannot be accused of leaving somebody out on a
whim, because the boundary was drawn by legislatures rather than by the author. It gives
the book a defensible answer in both directions, which no other list does.

**US federal, which is the frame the book should cite:**

| class | statute |
|---|---|
| Race, color, religion, sex, national origin | Title VII, Civil Rights Act 1964 |
| Pregnancy, childbirth, related conditions | Pregnancy Discrimination Act 1978, amending Title VII |
| Sexual orientation and gender identity | *Bostock v. Clayton County* 2020, read into Title VII's "sex" |
| Age 40 and over | ADEA 1967 |
| Disability | ADA 1990; Rehabilitation Act 1973 for federal programs |
| Genetic information | GINA 2008 |
| Uniformed service and veteran status | USERRA; VEVRAA |
| Citizenship and immigration status | IRCA 1986 |
| Familial status | Fair Housing Act, as amended 1988 |

**Non-US readers.** The UK's Equality Act 2010 names nine protected characteristics and
the overlap is close but not identical: it adds *gender reassignment* and *marriage and
civil partnership* as their own characteristics and does not carry familial status or
veteran status. If the book cites a statutory list it should say which jurisdiction's,
in one clause, or it will read as parochial to half its market.

### The three things the stance has to say out loud

**1. The list is a floor, not a ceiling.** The moves work identically on positions no
statute protects: class and poverty, caste, a criminal record, body size, caregiving
status in most jurisdictions, being new, being the only one. Saying this is what keeps the
book honest, and it is also what stops a reader concluding that unprotected harm is
permitted harm.

**2. The book teaches positions, and names classes as where the world reliably produces
them.** A protected class is a category. What the reader meets is a person in a position.
The same person occupies different positions in different situations — which is why the
six Faces are six positions rather than six demographics, and why one person can need the
Challenger on Monday and the Sage on Thursday.

**3. Disability occupies all six positions**, and that is worth stating rather than
letting the reader notice. It is the only class that appears in every Face's list below,
because access, refusal, erasure, design, standing and unfixability are all its subject.
That is a teaching point, not a gap in the taxonomy.

### What this does not license

The book still does not teach any group's experience. It teaches the six moves and points
at people who have written seriously about each position (§5). Wendell's own framing is
the operative rule and it belongs in the body text: **allyship is not learning how to help
everyone, it is learning to be more helpful and to widen the circle at the speed of your
own skill and capacity.**

## 2 · The six targets, as types

Each is a **structural position**, not an identity group. That is deliberate: it is what
lets the book be unmistakably about marginalization without writing an identity
taxonomy, and it is true to how the positions work — the same person occupies different
ones in different situations.

Each type is already spoken aloud in the margins, by the person who got the help. Those
are the beneficiary epigraphs Loop A credits as a partial answer to its own GAP 3.

### Shaman · School of the Body
**The type:** the person whose experience does not have words yet.
**Their words:** *"She said the word before I did. I had been carrying it eleven years and she said it in a corridor, like it was the weather."*
**Protected classes most often in this position:** **race and color** — the accumulated slight that survives no single retelling; **disability**, invisible and undiagnosed — the years before a name exists for it; **gender identity** — the misgendering everyone agrees was a slip; **religion** — the assumption nobody states.
**Where the world makes them:** anywhere harm is pattern-shaped rather than incident-shaped, so no single instance survives being questioned. The long gap before diagnosis. The read that gets called sensitivity. The new person who can feel the thing the team has stopped noticing.
**What mastery buys:** you can name it accurately and early enough that it cannot be dismissed as an overreaction, and you can be wrong out loud without it costing them.

### Challenger · School of the Line
**The type:** the person whose *no* does not carry.
**Their words:** *"Somebody told them no on my behalf and it worked immediately, after two years of my saying it. I am grateful, and I am aware of what that tells me. Both of those are permanent."*
**Protected classes most often in this position:** **citizenship and immigration status** — a refusal that could cost the visa is not a refusal anyone can afford; **disability** — the accommodation request treated as an opening offer; **pregnancy** — the leave that gets planned around rather than agreed to; **age 40+** — the no that reads as inflexibility.
**Where the world makes them:** contingent standing of any kind. Contract, temp, probation, visa, tenancy, funding. Anyone requesting an accommodation. Anyone who has already asked twice.
**What mastery buys:** you can spend your own standing so their refusal is honored, and you can do it without the help itself becoming the second injury the epigraph names.

### Regent · School of the Oath
**The type:** the person written out of a tradition they belong to.
**Their words:** *"They kept the festival and changed the part about us. Nobody announced it. I found out because my daughter came home knowing the new words."*
**Protected classes most often in this position:** **religion** — the holiday that stays on the calendar with its meaning edited out; **national origin** — the language a second generation is not being taught; **race and color** — the history rewritten by the institution that holds the archive; **age 40+** — the elders left out of the movement they built.
**Where the world makes them:** inheritance and record. Repatriation. Curriculum. A language a second generation is losing. A movement's history told without its elders in it.
**What mastery buys:** you can keep what should be kept and change what should change while leaving a record of who changed it, so the next person can find out on purpose rather than through a child.

### Architect · School of the Pattern
**The type:** the person the system was not built for.
**Their words:** *"The new rota is worse for me and better for eleven other people. It took me a season to stop being angry about it. It took another to notice I would not change it back."*
**Protected classes most often in this position:** **disability** — this is the Architect's home class and the ADA's whole subject, physical and digital access alike; **national origin** — the form that exists in one language; **pregnancy and familial status** — the schedule that assumes nobody is carrying anyone; **religion** — the dress code and the fixed Friday.
**Where the world makes them:** wherever the default path costs one group more. Access, physical and digital. Caregiving against a fixed schedule. Forms in one language. Processes that assume a bank account, an address, a phone.
**What mastery buys:** you can change the rule so nobody has to ask — and you can be honest that a redesign is paid for by somebody, which the foreman's voice says better than exposition can.

### Diplomat · School of the Bridge
**The type:** the person with no standing at the table, whose only leverage is somebody else's goodwill.
**Their words:** *"They translated me accurately and I still lost. I am told this is the honest version. I am still deciding whether I prefer it."*
**Protected classes most often in this position:** **citizenship and immigration status** — asylum, where the terms are entirely the other side's; **familial status** and **race** — the Fair Housing Act's own subject, a tenancy negotiated from below; **disability** — the clinical setting, and guardianship, where somebody else may hold the pen; **age 40+** — the severance offered rather than bargained.
**Where the world makes them:** tenancy, employment before organizing, asylum, clinical settings, any process where one side writes the terms.
**What mastery buys:** you can get terms that hold after the goodwill runs out — and the epigraph is the standing warning that accurate translation is not the same as winning.

### Sage · School of the Horizon
**The type:** the person who has not asked to be fixed.
**Their words:** *"He fixed nothing. He sat in it with me for a full day and then went back to the ship. I have thought about that day for six years."*
**Protected classes most often in this position:** **disability** — terminal, chronic and psychiatric alike, where there is no version that resolves; **age** — the end of a life, on its own schedule; **genetic information** — GINA's subject, and the sharpest case in the book: somebody who knows what is coming and cannot be helped out of knowing; **veteran status** — the injury that is moral rather than medical.
**Where the world makes them:** grief. Long crisis. Indefinite limbo. Incarceration. Anything that will not resolve on the timescale of your help.
**What mastery buys:** you can stay without a plan, and you can come back — and you can tell the difference between company and intervention, which is the only Face whose whole skill is not acting.

**Note on the Sage.** The earlier phrasing was *"the person nobody can fix."* That reads
as giving up. Under Loop A's consent hinge the accurate type is **the person who has not
asked to be fixed**, which is a statement about the offer rather than about their
prospects, and it makes the Sage the Face that holds the consent rule most explicitly.

## 3 · The mastery claim, which is Loop A's graded success

Wendell: *"by mastering each of the schools you increase your ability to actually help
people that school is designed to help."*

This is already specified. Loop A's three success levels are the ladder:

| level | Loop A's wording | what it means at a School |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | a grammatical attempt | you made the move correctly. This counts, and it is where every School starts |
| 2 | an external result with residual dissatisfaction | something changed and it was not enough, or it cost the wrong person |
| 3 | a landed artifact with **voluntary satisfaction from both people** | the person you offered to says it helped |

So the promise is exact and testable: **mastering a School moves your moves up that
ladder for that School's type.** It also gives every chapter's `**The test:**` beat a
scale it currently lacks, and it is the mechanism behind *expanding the circle at the
speed of your skill and capacity* — the circle widens when level 3 becomes reachable
with a type that used to top out at level 1.

## 4 · The founding stories have a home that already exists

Wendell: *"a bit more worldbuilding around how the schools came to help the people they
wanted to help."*

**It cannot go in the marginalia.** `MARGIN_ARC.md` rules: one beat per chapter maximum,
every note complete on first read, nothing ever set up, no exposition. The nine beats are
already allocated.

**It goes in Section 1, which is already this beat from the other side.** Every chapter
opens with `## Section 1: The Exile` — ch4's subtitle is *"Why the Challenger Left the
Village (And What the Village Lost)"*. The founding is the same story told forward: the
Face left, and the School is what they built once they were outside. **Who the School
was founded to serve is the answer to what the village lost.** Six chapters, one
paragraph each, no new section, and it lands where the reader is already asking the
question.

Two constraints from the existing world. `MARGIN_ARC.md` §Ch8 establishes that **the
Horizon was a place before it was a school** and that the annotator is the one who
stopped saying it that way — so the Horizon's founding is load-bearing for the ch8
reveal and must not be spent early. And the named places are Oreve (a harbour), Sethen
(a dock office), Halvane, and the Horizon; the citizen voices are a clerk, a foreman, a
delegate, a parent, and one unsigned.

## 5 · The citations apparatus, and why it is not written here

Wendell asked for *"citations for people to do deeper research on marginalized groups."*
This is the move that earns the leeway the stance in §1 asks for, because it converts
*"we do not teach every group"* from an excuse into a referral.

**Structure:** one reading list per Face, three to four works each, placed at the end of
each chapter or gathered in a new appendix. Each entry answers *"this position, from
inside it"* — a work by somebody who has occupied the position, not a work about the
category. That constraint is what keeps the apparatus consistent with the stance: the book
teaches positions, so the reading teaches positions too.

**Why no titles appear in this spec.** I will not put a citation into a book about
allyship that I have not verified against a real edition. A fabricated or misattributed
source here is not a typo — it is the single defect that would most damage the book's
standing with exactly the reader it needs, and it is unrecoverable after print.

**Two ways to get them, Wendell's pick:**

1. **His list.** He has been in this field for years and the works he would actually
   stand behind are better than anything a search returns.
2. **Verified search, then his approval.** I run the search, confirm author, title, date
   and publisher for every entry, hand him the list with what each one is for, and he
   cuts. Slower and it produces a defensible apparatus.

**What must be checked either way:** author, exact title, publication year, publisher, and
that the author actually occupies the position the entry is filed under. The last of those
is the one a search gets wrong most often and the one a reader will notice first.

**Legal citations are the exception** and are already safe to print: the statutes in §1
are public law with fixed names and dates, and *Bostock v. Clayton County*, 590 U.S. 644
(2020) is a decided case. Those need a proof-read, not a verification pass.

**Decided by this spec:** the six types, their epigraph anchors, where the world produces

founding stories go in Section 1.

  it to ch9. Recommendation: name the two stations that carry the reader's weight (Depth,
  Visibility) everywhere, and keep the full twelve in ch9. That honors both rulings.
- The class lists in §2 under *"where the world makes them"* are mine and they are the
  most identity-adjacent text in the book. They should have Wendell's eyes and probably
  a sensitivity read before print.

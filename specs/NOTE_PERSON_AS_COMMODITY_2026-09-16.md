# A person is not a resource

**Wendell, twice, four days apart, on two different sentences:**

> *"'They were weighing what the Challenger cost' — only slaves cost people things."*
> *"spending yourself turns you into a slave."*

## The rule

**`spend`, `cost`, `price`, `buy` and `worth` take resources.** Money. Energy. Time. Attention.
The charge, which this book has defined as a resource and may be spent.

**They do not take people.** Not the reader, not a Face, not a third party. A sentence that
makes a person the thing spent, bought or priced makes that person property, and this is a book
about allyship.

## Why it keeps happening

The chapter's economics are correct and Wendell has confirmed them: **drawing a line costs you
energy, and it costs more when the people around you benefit from the line staying undrawn.** That
model is the spine of the Challenger's argument, so the currency register is right and has to
stay. The defect is not the metaphor. It is the metaphor reaching for the wrong object — the
person doing the spending, or the line that the spending buys.

**The line is what the energy buys. It is never the currency.**

## The test

Name the thing being spent. If it is a person, the sentence is wrong however good it sounds.
If it is the line, the sentence has the economics backwards.

**Not scanned beyond `spend`/`cost`/`buy`/`expensive`.** Adjacent constructions — a person as
something *used up*, *drained*, *burned through*, *worth it* — have not been looked at.

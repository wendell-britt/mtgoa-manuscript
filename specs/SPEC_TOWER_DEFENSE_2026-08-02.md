## 1 · The two games, and this is the move

**Jordan is playing an arcade. They are playing a tower defense. Neither knows the other's
game is running.**

That single sentence resolves something the book has never stated: why a competent,
well-fuelled, well-intentioned person produces nothing. She is playing her game correctly
on a board that scores differently.

| | Jordan's game | Their game |
|---|---|---|
| **Shape** | arcade — pick a machine, spend, play, collect | tower defense — hold a perimeter, forever |
| **Goal** | get better, stay on the floor | nothing gets to the middle |
| **Her position** | player | an unidentified thing approaching the perimeter |
| **What ends it** | she stops playing | nothing. It never ends |

**And Chance/Skill/Passion is about her side only.** *Which Game Are You Playing* asks
what kind of game **she** is in. It has never asked what game the other person is in. That
is a real gap in ch1 and this fills it without touching the section.

## 2 · The mechanics that transfer, exactly

Tower defense is a precise genre and almost all of it maps:

- **The core.** Their wellbeing. The player at the centre of their Forest.
- **The towers.** The seven daemons, placed where harm previously got through.
- **Towers are tuned to a threat type.** A Protector that was hurt by a helper reads
  helpers. A Skeptic that was lied to reads confidence. **They do not fire at random and
  they are not paranoid. They fire at the shape that cost them.**
- **Clearance rises with survived waves.** You get access by having been survivable.
- **A move above your clearance is read as an attack** even when it is help. That is
  overreach, and it now has a genre-accurate name.
- **The defense never comes down.** Which is correct. A person whose towers came down
  would not be healed, they would be unguarded.

### The mechanic I tried to remove is the one that matters most

**WITHDRAWN 2026-08-02.** The paragraph that stood here said the incoming-units-are-enemies
mechanic does not transfer, because casting helpers as an invading wave is *"wrong and
insulting to the person defending."* **Wendell's correction, and it is correct:**

> *"Fully wrong. That's victim consciousness. Defending against the fact that many people
> we are seeking to help don't want help. You've invalidated the consent structure that WAS
> supported. If you believe in consent then you have to realize that people acting without
> consent are violating sovereignty. They are enemies. Ask anyone who has been assaulted by
> someone who wanted to help so badly and wouldn't take no for an answer."*

**Three errors, and they compound.**

**It is not wrong.** If consent is real, a person acting without it is violating
sovereignty. The tower is not misfiring. It is firing accurately. The person who would not
take no for an answer was not a mislabelled helper; they were an attacker who believed they
were helping, and the defense read them correctly.

**It is not insulting, and that word was the tell.** I claimed to be shielding the defended
person from a harsh image. What I was actually shielding was **the helper, from being named
as a possible threat.** A defense is not an insult to the person who built it. Softening
the frame protected Jordan's self-image and called it respect for the person she is
approaching.

**And it invalidated the consent structure four documents had just built.** *Consent is the
gate* and *helpers are never enemies* cannot both be true. The volunteer framing wanted
both.

**The genre mechanic stands. Incoming units can be enemies, and sometimes they are.**

## 3 · The claim that makes this usable, and it is the best thing here

> *"Since wellbeing is achievable at each level and each daemon and each domain, a skilled
> allyship player will have no problem."*

**There is real good available at every clearance.** Not a reduced version of the good, and
not a waiting room. Level-one access buys level-one help, and level-one help is help.

This is the answer to Jordan's paralysis, and it is better than anything the book currently
offers her, because it removes the premise underneath the freeze. She has been waiting to
be qualified. **There is no qualifying. There is only playing at the clearance you have,
which is never zero.**

It also gives the domains their job. Seven daemons × four domains, at whatever clearance
is open, is a large space of real moves. **A skilled player is never blocked, because
something in that space is always available.**

## 4 · The doom loop, which is what makes it an engine

> *"But so long as they don't know this they will default to the type of allyship that
> promotes their OWN daemon's shadow and the shadow of their game master face."*

Spelled out, it runs:

1. Jordan cannot read her clearance, because nobody has told her clearance exists.
2. With no read, she defaults — **and the default is whatever her own daemon wants to
   play.** Her Fixer wants to fix. Her Protector wants nobody to get hurt, including her.
   Her Controller wants the standard met.
3. That default is her Face's distortion, aimed at a board it cannot see.
4. **The move lands above her clearance, so their towers fire.** Not because she is bad.
   Because a stranger just tried to place a tower on somebody else's perimeter.
5. Access drops. The help does not land.
6. She reads the failure as a verdict on her, spends more of herself, and goes further into
   the distortion, which is step 2 with less fuel.

**That is burnout, drawn as a mechanism.** `ch1:30` names the debt, `ch1:60` names finishing
with no one, `ch1:189` names one face carrying too much. **This is the loop all three of
them are describing from different angles**, and it is the first version with a step that
can be interrupted.

**The interruption is step 1.** Not more effort, not a better heart. Reading the board.

## 5 · A term collision, and it blocks the prose

**`level` is taken.** `MTGOA_LEVEL_MAP_CANONICAL.md` is the Level-Face-Color reference,
lowest to highest, vertical integration — the Wilber altitude. Using *level* for tower
defense progress would collide with the book's developmental spine, in a book that already
has a pre/trans problem.

**`wave` is worse.** The WAVE-Spiral is ch3's five-stage emotional alchemy engine, 21 uses
in that chapter alone.

Checked against the manuscript: **`clearance` 0 uses, `tier` 0, `perimeter` 0, `ring` 1,
`depth` 8.**

**Recommend `clearance`.** It is what a bouncer grants, it belongs to the door image ch1
now runs, it says *permission* rather than *rank*, and it is unclaimed. *Perimeter* is the
matching noun for their side.

## 7 · The deep shadow, which is what the frame is actually for

Wendell:

> *"THIS is the deep dark shadow of allyship. Many people don't want to be helped because
> they will have to give up the goodies they get from being victims. This is already
> supported in the book by existential kink. This is also why the pre/trans fallacy works.
> To ally with someone means breaking down walls they may claim to want to keep up in order
> to engage with them. Which means you have to get permission or you have to accept the
> consequences of violence."*

### The symmetry the book has been missing

`ch1:143` already runs existential kink on the reader: *"the shadow doesn't run you because
it feels bad. It runs you because, somewhere out of sight, it feels good."* **The book has
never turned that instrument around.** The person being helped has a shadow too, and some
of what a defense protects is not safety. It is the return on staying defended.

That is the darkest available reading of the tower frame and it is the honest one. **Some
towers guard a wound. Some towers guard the goodies.** From the outside they are identical,
and the person inside frequently cannot tell either.

### Two post-conventional moves, and only two

Breaking a wall is what allyship sometimes requires. There are exactly two legitimate ways
to do it:

1. **Get permission.** The wall comes down by invitation and there is no violation.
2. **Act without permission and accept the consequences of the violence.** Knowingly commit
   the violation, carry the cost, and take whatever comes.

**Both are post-conventional because both see the structure.** Conventional allyship is the
third option nobody admits to: pretend there is no wall, or that good intentions dissolve
it, or that the helper is automatically welcome.

### The line that keeps option 2 from being a licence

**You do not get to call it help.**

The post-conventional actor names the violation as a violation and eats the cost. **The
predator names the violation as help and insists on it.** That is the entire difference,
it is one sentence, and it is the sentence that must sit beside option 2 wherever it is
written.

And it is the pre/trans problem in its sharpest form: **from the conventional middle,
somebody who knowingly broke a wall and owns it looks exactly like somebody who broke a
wall and does not care.** The middle cannot read the difference, and it will punish both.

## 9 · One honest limit that survives

*Skilled players will have no problem* is true about **availability**, not difficulty.
Something is always available at every clearance; finding it under pressure, with your own
daemon pulling at the joystick, is hard. The book should claim the first and not the
second.

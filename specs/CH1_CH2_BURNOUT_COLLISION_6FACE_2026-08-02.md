## The six reads

### SHAMAN · the Body — *What to Do With What You Feel*

Read the field before reading the sentence. What happens in Jordan's body at `ch2:29`?

Not confusion. **Distrust.** She knows this already, and the book does not know she knows.
The first thing a reader loses is not comprehension, it is the sense that the author is
tracking her — and she loses it in the chapter that is about to ask her to look at her own
shadow. You cannot ask someone to go into the Forest with you eleven lines after telling
her something she already has.

The Shaman's second finding is worse and quieter. The paragraph **explains a feeling
instead of letting her have one.** *We wonder why we're exhausted* is a report of an
emotion delivered in the register of analysis. That is the village's move on the Shaman —
`ch3:150`, *"where sadness comes back relabelled as growth."* Ch2 is doing to exhaustion
exactly what ch3 accuses the village of doing to sadness.

**Shaman's ruling:** the paragraph is where the section's temperature drops. It is also
1,782 words in with no story yet. **This is the slot the story goes in.**

### CHALLENGER · the Line — *Who Pays When Nobody Says No*

Somebody has to say the unwelcome thing: **this paragraph is ch1's leftovers, and it has
survived four editorial passes because nobody was willing to cut a paragraph that reads
fine on its own.** Every pass that touched it — Pass 2 Continuity and Claims, Pass 3 Line
Editor, Pass 4 Voice Guardian — read it inside ch2 and never against ch1.

Who pays? Jordan pays, in trust, and she pays before the chapter has earned any. **I pay
too, and I should name it: I carried this paragraph through two rewrites without seeing
it**, including one where I was specifically auditing that passage sentence by sentence.

**Challenger's ruling:** cut the parenthetical. Not soften, not reconcile. It makes a claim
about the state of the field that the book has already refuted, and there is no version of
it that survives ch1. The willingness to be unwelcome here is the willingness to delete
seventy words of perfectly decent prose.

### REGENT · the Oath — *What You Inherited, and What You Do With It*

Ask what is inherited and what is chosen. **Ch2 Section 1 is older than the ch1 it now sits
behind.** The tell is structural: it opens cold (*The world is not fine*), establishes
urgency from zero, introduces the village as though for the first time, and diagnoses
burnout as an unsolved problem. **That is a chapter-one opening.** It was one. Ch1 was
rebuilt around the Arcade, the token economy and the debt frame; ch2's Section 1 was not
rebuilt with it.

So the burnout paragraph is not a mistake. It is a **tradition from a structure that no
longer exists** — the same shape as DL-31, where ch3 and ch4 both claimed to be the
foundation because neither had been read against the other since the split.

**Regent's ruling:** ch1 holds the burnout canon — it has the sources, and the token
economy at `ch1:103` depends on it. Ch2 **inherits** rather than re-argues. The Regent's
question for the rest of Section 1 is the one this exposes: *what else in here was written
to open a book?*

### ARCHITECT · the Pattern — *Fixing the Condition Instead of the Person*

Do not fix the paragraph. Fix the condition that let it live.

**No instrument in this repo compares a claim in chapter N against a claim in chapter N−1.**
`termdebt.py` tracks first use of a term. `citation_audit.py` tracks whether a source is
credited. `chain2.py` tracks bare claims inside a move. `repeat.py` and `dupes.py` find
repeated *strings*. **Nothing asks whether an argument has already been made and closed.**
A near-duplicate argument in different words is invisible to every one of them, which is
precisely why this survived to the eighth editorial pass.

This is the same shape as DL-34 three hours ago: a defect lives exactly where no counter
points. That is now twice in one day, and the Architect's read is that it will keep
happening until something measures **claims** rather than strings.

**Architect's ruling:** the paragraph is a symptom. The condition is an unmeasured
dependency between chapters. Worth an instrument — a claim-recurrence pass over the nine
chapters, seeded from ch1's assertions since ch1 is the one everything downstream inherits.

### DIPLOMAT · the Bridge — *Why Being Good at Care Is Only Half of It*

Read what the sentence is doing relationally, not logically.

*"Some of us have been calling this burnout for five years"* is **a bid for
co-membership.** It says: I am in this with you, we are both puzzled, neither of us has the
answer yet. That is warm, and it is the exact move the Diplomat chapter warns about — care
offered on terms that cost the person offering it their standing.

Jordan did not come for a peer. **She came for someone who knows something**, and she is
one chapter past being told, with citations, that this person does. The paragraph trades
authority for solidarity at the worst possible moment, and it does it in the unearned *we*
that was the second half of the original complaint. The two defects are the same defect:
**the `we` is what makes the false modesty possible.** Third person cannot say *some of us
have been wondering*, because third person has to say who.

**Diplomat's ruling:** keep the warmth, lose the co-membership. The book's own move for
this is the parenthetical self-indictment at `ch1:119` (`VOICE_ANCHOR` anchor 2) — the
admission that *sharpens* authority instead of spending it, because it names something
specific the author actually did.

### SAGE · the Horizon — *Seeing the Whole Board Without Leaving the Table*

Which game is each chapter playing?

**Ch1 plays:** *here is the machine underneath your exhaustion, here are the three people
who mapped it, here is the fuel that pays for it.* A game of explanation, with the author as
someone who has already been down and come back.

**Ch2 Section 1 plays:** *nobody has explained your exhaustion, including me, isn't that
strange.* A game of shared bewilderment, with the author beside the reader looking at the
same fog.

Both are legitimate games. **The second is the game a book plays on page one**, before it
has spent any authority, to buy the reader's trust cheaply. Ch2 is playing it on page
forty-five, having already spent nothing and been handed everything.

The Sage's read is that this is not a contradiction to reconcile. **It is a fossil**, and
fossils are cheap to remove and expensive to argue with. The reason it is hard to see is
the reason the Sage chapter exists: from inside ch2 the paragraph is fine. It is only wrong
from a position that can see both chapters at once, and nothing in the editorial system had
ever stood in that position.

**Sage's ruling:** do not reconcile the two passages. Delete the one playing the earlier
game, and check the rest of Section 1 for the same fossil.

**Six for six on cut, for six different reasons, which is the strongest signal this format
produces.** No Face argues for reconciling the two passages and none argues for keeping the
parenthetical in any form.

**And the two open items resolve into one move.** The burnout paragraph is ~70 words of
theory sitting at the exact point where the Shaman says the section goes cold, in a section
that has **no story in 1,782 words** — the one thing Jordan's profile says she never skips.
Cutting it does not leave a hole. **It opens the slot.**

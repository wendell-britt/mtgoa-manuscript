## W2 · ch2 — the daemons are sourced thoroughly and situated nowhere

**`ON_THE_SHOULDERS_OF` does the credit properly**, and at length: the architecture from
Genpo Roshi's Big Mind Process, the word *daemon* and the genius-demon-ally turn from Robin
Rice, the approach-once-you-are-there from Carolyn Elliott's *Existential Kink*. Three
lineages, all named, with chapter pointers.

**Nothing anywhere says what kind of claim the daemons are.** Searched ch2 for *not literal ·
metaphor · actually exist · really are*: **zero hits.** Eight named inner voices arrive and
operate as simply present.

**Whether the psyche has discrete parts is genuinely contested**, which is precisely the
condition `LIKE WILBER` exists to mark. The book is not obliged to settle it. It is obliged
to show it knows, and **ch2 carries one contested-mark against four to six in every Face
chapter.**

**Jordan is a framework-seeker.** A reader who arrives already fluent will notice the missing
epistemic status before she notices the frame, and the omission reads as either not knowing
or not saying — both worse than the answer.

**ch1:167 is the book's own form for this, and it is the strongest thing in the chapter:**

> *What I cannot do is get you the owner. I have not worked that out, and anybody who tells
> you they have is selling you a better place in the same line.*

**Proposed:** one sentence in ch2, in that register. Not a disclaimer and not a hedge on the
practice — a statement of what kind of object a daemon is, which the practice does not
require to be literal and is stronger for saying so.

## W3 · ch9 — the chapter that asks the most marks the least

**ch9's single contested-mark is not a hedge.** It is `*I don't know*` used as material:
*"The Player in distortion uses **I don't know** as a place to stay. You can use **I don't
know** as a place to start."* Good line, and it marks nothing about the book's own claims.

**So ch9 marks nothing, and ch9 asks for more than any other chapter.** Cut the field, put
unfinished work in front of a named person, take the note that costs you the design, hand
over the pen. Every one of those has a real cost to a real reader, and `ch9:592` — *"This
book has cost me all five of these"* — pays the author's side without marking what is
uncertain on hers.

**What is genuinely uncertain here and unmarked:** whether this works for somebody without
the author's standing. Handing over the pen assumes you had the pen. `ch1:48` already made
that concession once for the whole book — *"several assume you have a budget or a say in how
the work gets scheduled"* — and ch9 is where it bites hardest and goes unsaid.

## R2 · ch6 — the chapter about naming structures names the fewest things

**2.13 per 1000, the lowest of the six Face chapters**, in the chapter whose subject is
leverage points, mental models and systems that produce outcomes. It uses *leverage point* 18
times and *the map* 10 and coins less than any of its siblings.

**And its central claim announces a term it never supplies.** `ch6:167`:

> **Doing the right thing in the wrong system is a specific kind of expensive.** The Architect
> starts by recognizing it as a system problem rather than an effort problem.

***"A specific kind of expensive"* is the chapter saying out loud that there is a specific
thing here, and then not naming it.** That is the exact condition `LIKE RAO` is for, and the
sentence has already done the hard half: two parties, a transfer, and a cost that lands on
the person doing right rather than on the system that made it expensive.

**Candidates, and coining is Wendell's ruling rather than a line edit:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **the effort premium** | what you pay to get a right outcome out of a system built for a different one; sits in the book's money language |
| **the system's cut** | sharper, and closer to ch1's arcade economy — *somebody collects* |

**`the system's cut` is the stronger candidate** because ch1:163–167 already built the
collection mechanism and ch6 is where a reader meets it structurally rather than personally.

## What ch4, ch5, ch7 and ch8 did not turn up

**These four are the healthiest chapters in the book by both measurements**, and that is a
finding rather than a skip.

- **Coinage density 3.17 / 3.96 / 3.23 / 2.77** — the top four of nine. Nothing load-bearing
  is going unnamed at a rate that stands out from its neighbours.
- **Contested-marking 3 / 4 / 4 / 4** — each marks its own limits several times.
- **Each states its altitude in its own terms** and, since W1′ landed, each carries a native
  material. The ladder is consistent across all six.
- **`ch6` is the exception on coinage and the leader on contested-marking at 6**, which is the
  right shape for the Orange chapter: it hedges well and names badly.

**One thing I did not do**, and it should be on the record rather than implied: this pass ran
two measurements across nine chapters and read the outliers. It is a scan, not a line-by-line
read of 120,000 words. A chapter reading clean here means *nothing stood out against its
neighbours on coinage density or contested-marking*, never *nothing is wrong in it*.

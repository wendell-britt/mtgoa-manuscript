---

**The line you chose in Chapter 0 is not just your wound. It's a map.**

Each of the six self-sabotaging beliefs produces a specific over-build — a capacity you developed, often without naming it, because you needed it to survive. That over-build, made conscious and aimed at something real, is your allyship superpower. The NPC and the Game Master are not opposites. They are the same engine running on different settings.

This table is a reference for all six campaigns. On your first read, you worked one line all the way through. When you return, you'll work another. The table lets you orient before each read — and the shift from "this is my wound" to "this is what my wound built" is the move this whole book is teaching.

---

## The Six Beliefs and the Superpowers They Build

---

### I'm not ready.

**What the NPC does:** prepares endlessly, gathers more information, builds more scaffolding, waits for a readiness that will never feel complete. Gathering-as-armor. (You've been here — it's why you picked up this book.)

**What it forced you to build:** preparation mastery — the ability to anticipate, research, and scenario-plan so thoroughly that you can walk into almost anything. You developed it because readiness never arrived on its own, so you learned to manufacture it.

**The superpower, aimed:** you are the most prepared person in any situation — which means you can hold space for others to be unprepared. You can enter chaotic, under-resourced, under-planned situations and still function. You can show up before others feel ready, which in allyship terms means you move when others are still gathering courage.

**The Controller it feeds:** the Controller uses "not ready" to keep the standard so high that no move clears the bar. When you relieve the Controller of that standard and update it — *ready enough to make one honest move* — the preparation becomes a gift instead of a delay tactic.

---

### I'm not capable.

**What the NPC does:** defers, doubts, shrinks in the face of unfamiliar territory, defaults to "I'm not the right person for this." Underestimates what it can hold.

**What it forced you to build:** resourcefulness — the ability to find a way with inadequate tools, insufficient credentials, and no guarantee of success. You couldn't wait for capability to be conferred on you, so you learned to work with what you had and figure out the rest.

**The superpower, aimed:** you can ally in constrained, under-resourced situations that others abandon as hopeless or above their pay grade. You don't need the full set of tools before you start. You are the ally who shows up when the situation is still too messy for anyone to know what's needed — and you figure it out from inside.

**The Controller it feeds:** the Controller uses "not capable" to enforce a credential requirement that protects you from being wrong. When you update the standard — *capable enough to try and report back honestly* — the resourcefulness that was hiding becomes operational.

---

### I'm not worthy.

**What the NPC does:** earns and earns, tries to justify its presence through constant contribution, can never quite receive help or recognition without deflecting, keeps the cost of belonging invisibly high.

**What it forced you to build:** service precision — the ability to identify exactly what's needed, deliver it cleanly, and step back. You became an expert at contribution because you needed to earn what others seemed to receive automatically. That expertise is real.

**The superpower, aimed:** you can offer help without needing to be seen doing it. You know what value looks like from the inside, so you don't mistake performance for contribution. Your allyship is precise — it delivers what's actually needed, not what looks most helpful from the outside. You can work in the background of someone else's breakthrough without requiring credit.

**The Controller it feeds:** the Controller uses "not worthy" to make every contribution provisional — never quite enough to actually belong. When you update the standard — *worthy by virtue of showing up honestly* — the service precision that was earning-for-belonging becomes giving-from-wholeness.

---

### I'm not good enough.

**What the NPC does:** perfects, over-prepares deliverables, catches errors before anyone else can, holds itself and others to standards that erode the room's warmth and slow the room's progress.

**What it forced you to build:** excellence as care — the drive to get it right that, when over-developed, becomes quality stewardship: the ability to hold a high standard for what allyship actually *does*, separate from what it looks like.

**The superpower, aimed:** you catch harm before it happens. You won't settle for performative gestures when real impact is possible. In a culture that rewards the appearance of allyship over its function, you are the person who asks "but did it actually help?" — and insists on the answer. Your standard, aimed outward, protects the people the work is supposed to serve.

**The Controller it feeds:** the Controller uses "not good enough" to make every move a potential failure waiting to be exposed. When you update the standard — *good enough to keep showing up, and honest enough to repair when I get it wrong* — the drive for quality stops being self-surveillance and starts being advocacy.

---

### I'm insignificant.

**What the NPC does:** over-defers, makes itself small, apologizes for taking up space, amplifies others at the cost of its own signal — sometimes to the point where its own perspective goes silent in exactly the moments it's needed.

**What it forced you to build:** amplification — the ability to make others feel seen and significant, because you became an expert at the experience of invisibility. You know what it costs to be unseen, so you developed an acute sensitivity to who's been left out and what it would take to bring them in.

**The superpower, aimed:** you notice who's been erased before anyone else calls it. You can bring people into a conversation, a decision, a room, without making it a performance of inclusion. Your allyship serves the people most frameworks miss — not the loudest need but the quietest one, the person at the edge of the group who has given up expecting to be included.

**The Controller it feeds:** the Controller uses "insignificant" to justify silence — *it doesn't matter what I think, my voice won't change anything.* When you update the standard — *significant enough to name what I notice, even once, even quietly* — the amplification that was hiding yourself becomes amplifying others.

---

### I don't belong.

**What the NPC does:** code-switches, scans constantly for belonging signals, adjusts its presentation to match each new context, keeps a part of itself always slightly outside the group as insurance against rejection.

**What it forced you to build:** field-reading — the ability to read belonging dynamics in any group with high accuracy, because you've spent years scanning for where you fit and where you don't. You developed a somatic intelligence for inclusion and exclusion that most people in the room have never had to build.

**The superpower, aimed:** you can sense the relational temperature of a situation before it's spoken, which means you can intervene in exclusion before it consolidates. You are the ally who sees the person about to be edged out and moves *before* the edge becomes a wall. In allyship terms, belonging-field-reading is one of the rarest and most necessary capacities — and yours was built by necessity.

**The Controller it feeds:** the Controller uses "don't belong" to keep you in permanent audition mode — always performing acceptance rather than just being present. When you update the standard — *belonging to the work itself, regardless of whether the group ratifies it* — the field-reading that was scanning-for-safety becomes scanning-for-who-needs-to-be-included.

## How to use this table

**On your first read:** you traced one belief to its superpower in Chapter 2. That trace is on your character sheet. This table is the full context — the other five lines, and the logic that makes the trace work for any of them.

**On subsequent reads:** return here before Chapter 2. Choose your next belief. The Chapter 2 work runs the same trace; only the belief changes. Each campaign reveals a different superpower and engages the Controller at a different angle.

**With the Emotional Alchemy frame:** each belief activates a specific emotional charge when the NPC runs it. The trace from belief to superpower is the metabolization of that charge — fear of not being ready becoming the excitement of being more prepared than anyone needed you to be; the grief of not belonging becoming the poignance of knowing exactly who's been left out. When you can feel the shift in the body, the superpower is becoming usable.

**As a reference across chapters:** every chapter's gate-self and Face engages your chosen belief from a different angle. The Protector guards your "not ready" behind the armor of gathering. The Controller sets the standard your "not good enough" is failing to meet. The Skeptic uses "don't belong" to pre-emptively reject belonging before it can reject you. The map below connects each belief to its most active gate:

| Belief | Most active gate | Why |
|---|---|---|
| I'm not ready | Protector | Protection requires readiness — the Protector keeps you gathering |
| I'm not capable | Fixer-Healer | The Fixer needs capability to justify repair; "not capable" stalls the fix |
| I'm not worthy | Emotional Body | Worthiness is felt, not argued — the Emotional Body carries it |
| I'm not good enough | Controller | The Controller sets the "good enough" standard; this belief is its fuel |
| I'm insignificant | Victim | Insignificance and victimhood share the same frozen-agency posture |
| I don't belong | Vulnerable Child | The child at the center is the one who learned not-belonging first |

The gate where your belief is most active is where the chapter hits hardest. That's the chapter to read twice.

---

*This map is not fixed. The pairings are starting points. Your experience of the trace — what it actually feels like to follow your belief to its superpower — is the real data. Trust the body read over the table.*

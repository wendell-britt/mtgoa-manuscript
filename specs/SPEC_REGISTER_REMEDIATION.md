# SPEC — REGISTER REMEDIATION
## Moving MTGOA toward *Igniting Joy*, and what *Existential Kink* adds to that target
### 2026-07-28 · four days to print

---

## §0 THE HEADLINE

The gap between MTGOA and *Igniting Joy* is not spread evenly across fourteen metrics. It reduces to one mechanism with three faces, and the mechanism is measurable to the sentence.

MTGOA runs 2,311 sentences of seven words or fewer. That is 33.6% of the book. *Igniting Joy* runs 7.0%. The interesting number is not the count, though. It is what those short sentences are doing.

A short sentence that follows a long one lands. It is the snap at the end of a haul, and the reader feels it because the sentence before it took work to get through. Call that an **earned snap**. A short sentence that follows another short sentence does not land, because there was no haul. It is one more beat in a drum pattern, and by the fourth beat the reader has stopped hearing individual beats and started hearing rhythm.

| | short sentences (≤7 w) | of those, **earned** | of those, **in a run** |
|---|---|---|---|
| MTGOA | 2,311 (33.6%) | 168 — **7.3%** | 1,473 — 63.7% |
| Igniting Joy | 76 (7.0%) | 14 — **18.4%** | 22 — 28.9% |
| Existential Kink | 350 (13.9%) | 65 — **18.6%** | 178 — 50.9% |

Your own book and Elliott's book land on the same number from opposite directions: roughly one in five short sentences is earned. MTGOA is at one in fourteen. The book does not have too many short sentences in the abstract. It has too many short sentences standing next to each other, which converts a device into a texture.

The drift is also chapter-specific, which is the useful part.

## §2 THE MECHANISMS, MEASURED

Five numbers produce the whole register difference. I ran every one on the corrected 20,077-word *Igniting Joy* sample and on the full prose-only manuscript.

| mechanism | MTGOA | Igniting Joy | gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| sentences with **no internal punctuation** | 54.9% | 23.8% | 2.3× |
| **staccato runs** (≥2 adjacent ≤8-word sentences), per 1k sentences | 94.6 | 8.3 | **11.4×** |
| **copula-fronted** sentences (main verb *is/are/was*) | 31.9% | 19.3% | 1.7× |
| **subordinator-opening** sentences | 6.4% | 20.0% | 3.1× |
| **gerund / prepositional instruction openers** per 1k sentences | 2.9 | 64.3 | **22×** |
| sentences carrying **two or more commas** | 13.6% | 27.3% | 2.0× |

The 22× is the largest single ratio in the entire four-corpus study, and it is also the cheapest one to fix, because it is a change to the front of a sentence rather than a change to its content.

*Igniting Joy* opens sentences like this: **"By welcoming the anger, you are giving yourself permission to feel it without judgment."** **"By validating the emotion, you are telling your body and mind that this feeling is part of your experience."** **"Once you've mastered de-escalation, there are times when conflict isn't about calming things down."** **"By exaggerating the frustration, you're inviting your friend to laugh."**

Seventy of those in a 20,000-word sample. Twenty of them in a 90,000-word manuscript.

### 2.1 The connective finding

I expected to find that *Igniting Joy* subordinates more than MTGOA. It does not. MTGOA runs 42.0 connectives per 1,000 words against *Igniting Joy*'s 29.2. The difference is which connectives, and where they sit.

| | MTGOA | Igniting Joy | IJ ÷ MTGOA |
|---|---|---|---|
| because | 3.66 | 0.85 | 0.23 |
| which | 2.81 | 0.65 | 0.23 |
| before | 2.89 | 0.50 | 0.17 |
| where | 3.17 | 1.69 | 0.53 |
| when | 4.37 | 7.87 | **1.80** |
| if | 1.75 | 2.84 | **1.62** |
| as if | 0.08 | 0.30 | **3.85** |
| whether | 1.23 | 1.59 | 1.30 |

MTGOA's subordination is **argumentative**: *because*, *which*, *before*, *where*. Those clauses justify a claim, and they sit after the main clause, so the sentence asserts and then defends.

*Igniting Joy*'s subordination is **procedural and conditional**: *when*, *if*, *as if*, *by*. Those clauses set a condition, and they sit before the main clause, so the sentence sets a situation and then tells the reader what to do inside it.

That is the whole difference in one line. Your last book puts the reader in a situation and hands her a move. This manuscript states a proposition and then argues for it. The second one reads as instruction from a distance. The first reads as instruction from beside her.

## §3 THE SIX OPERATIONS

Each one is a mechanical transformation with a stated trigger. All six were run on real passages from the manuscript; the worked results are in §4, and every one of them cleared the reviewer gate before it went into this document.

**O1 — Gerund-front the procedure.** *Trigger:* a Section 4 practice paragraph that narrates steps as separate assertions. *Move:* open with *By [verb]-ing* or *When you [verb]* and put the outcome in the main clause. This is the single highest-ratio operation and it targets the section where the runs concentrate — Section 4 holds 72 of the book's 227 long runs, more than any other.

**O2 — Collapse the anaphoric recital.** *Trigger:* three or more consecutive sentences sharing an opening phrase (*You know… You know… You know…*). *Move:* keep the first, fold the middle into a comma series inside one sentence, keep the last as its own sentence. The anaphora survives at reduced count and the last beat gets its weight back.

**O3 — Fuse the pronoun chain.** *Trigger:* three or more consecutive sentences whose subject is the same pronoun, most often *It* or *The village*. *Move:* one subject, compound predicate, and move the qualifier inside spaced em-dashes rather than into its own sentence. You already flagged this shape yourself in Chapter 2 as "the repetition of *it it it*."

**O4 — Dissolve the negation stack.** *Trigger:* the banned *Not X. Not Y.* construction and its relatives. *Move:* rewrite as a positive assertion carrying *rather than*. Book-wide the stack count is at zero, so this operation is maintenance rather than repair, and it exists so the fix does not reintroduce the defect while fixing something else.

**O5 — Front the condition, keep the label.** *Trigger:* a stage or step label followed by short assertions. *Move:* retain the label verbatim, then run one *When you…* sentence that carries the whole step. The labels are structural and the reader navigates by them; only the prose under them changes.

**O6 — Series instead of imperative stack.** *Trigger:* four or more consecutive bare imperatives. *Move:* one sentence with *you* as subject and the imperatives as a coordinated verb series. This preserves the drill feel and removes the drumbeat.

## §5 THE COLLISION YOU NEED TO SEE

The six rewrites moved 330 words to 382. **The register operation costs words. Roughly sixteen percent, on the passages where it applies.**

That runs directly against the cut mandate, and I am not going to present a strategy that hides the conflict inside an average.

Here is the reconciliation. There are 227 runs of three or more adjacent short sentences in the manuscript, holding 4,190 words. They are not one thing.

**Ninety-five of them, holding 1,424 words, are enumerations** — the WAVE recital at ch9:271, the six types at ch9:36, the daemon's voice at ch3:529, the mode roster at ch4:217. Those are lists wearing prose. They should become actual lists, or they should be cut, and either way they *save* words. Your last book does exactly this: the tool chapters run numbered steps as lists and keep the running prose at 18.8 words a sentence, so the eye gets the list and the ear gets the paragraph. MTGOA currently makes the paragraph do the list's job.

**One hundred twenty-eight of them, holding 2,620 words, are dramatic or procedural** — the ch4:292 protocol, the ch8:401 offering, the ch3:74 village passage. Those get fused, and fusing costs about sixteen percent, which is roughly **+420 words** if every one of them were done.

Net across both classes, if the enumerations are listed or cut and the rest are fused, the register pass is close to **word-neutral**, and it may run slightly negative depending on how many enumerations go rather than convert. It does not deliver cuts. It also does not spend them. That is the honest accounting, and the cuts have to come from the Tier 1 list and the recap cap, which is where they already were.

## §6 THE ELLIOTT GRAFT

*Igniting Joy* is now the target. Measured against the other three corpora, it has the lowest variance of any of them: standard deviation 7.6, burstiness 7.3, with 55.7% of its sentences packed into the 15-to-26-word band and only 2.3% above 36 words. That is a consistent register, and consistency is most of what MTGOA needs. It is also, at 90,000 words, a register that could go flat.

Elliott is at standard deviation 20.6 and burstiness 16.9, with 18.6% of her sentences above 36 words sitting beside 12.4% at six or fewer. She has the range the target lacks. Three things are worth taking, and they are worth taking in this order.

**The long haul into the snap.** Elliott builds a sentence out past thirty words and then drops one under seven. Twenty-seven times in *Existential Kink* the swing between adjacent sentences exceeds thirty words with a seven-word neighbour.

> The more we're willing to "get off on every stroke" that comes to us, the more pleasure and fun we can have in our lives, and the more magnetic we can become. **Sadly, most of us turn ourselves off.**

> If you want to know who you unconsciously believe you are, just take a look at your life, your surroundings, your relationship. **Your life mirrors those deep beliefs.**

This is not a new device for you. It is a redistribution of one you already have too much of. The manuscript holds 2,311 short sentences; 168 of them are already positioned this way, and Chapter 1 holds a textbook instance:

> When I said I held myself hostage for three years, I made it sound like something that happened to me. **It wasn't.**

Every short sentence pulled out of a run and placed after a long one raises the earned-snap ratio from both ends at once. That single reposition is the whole graft.

**First person doing narrative work.** Elliott runs 33.3% of sentences in first person, and 21% of those are narrative past — something that happened to her, told as event. *Igniting Joy* runs 4.9% first person and almost none of it narrative; the instructional voice there is almost entirely second person, at 56.1 *you* per 1,000 words against MTGOA's 39.2. So *Igniting Joy* cannot supply this and Elliott can.

MTGOA already runs 9.6% first person, which is more than double *Igniting Joy*, so the deficit is not quantity. Only 14% of the manuscript's first-person sentences are narrative past; the rest are editorial asides in the present tense, which is a different move that reads as commentary rather than testimony. The graft is to convert position, not to add volume, and there are two slots already standing open for it: Chapter 4's vacated first-person slot and the Chapter 7 testimony slot you are holding until you have the energy for it. Neither needs to be filled this week for the graft to be specified.

**The turn to the reader mid-argument.** Elliott interrupts her own exposition to address the reader directly — *Sound good?* — and it works because it arrives after a long construction, not during a run of short ones. Questions per 1,000 words: Elliott 2.9, *Igniting Joy* 3.3, MTGOA 2.5. This is the smallest of the three levers and I would spend nothing on it before August 1.

## §7 WHAT WE DO NOT TAKE FROM ELLIOTT

Three of her measured distinctives are traps for this manuscript, and naming them matters as much as naming the grafts.

Her **And/But sentence openers run at 10.1%**, the highest of the four corpora. That construction is banned in this book by your own ruling, and the ban stands. Any place the fused version wants to start with *And* gets written around instead.

Her **hedges run at 12.2 per 1,000 words** against *Igniting Joy*'s 2.8 and MTGOA's 6.2. The hedge sweep already on the board is moving MTGOA down toward the *Igniting Joy* figure. Elliott's number is a feature of a confessional voice that is performing its own uncertainty, and importing it would undo work already scheduled.

Her **36-plus-word band at 18.6%** is not reachable in four days without wrecking readability, and it is not the target anyway. *Igniting Joy* is the target and it sits at 2.3%. Take the swing, leave the sustained length.

## §8 SCOPE FOR FOUR DAYS

There are 227 long runs and 651 runs of two or more. All of them cannot be touched before August 1, and pretending otherwise is how the last three weeks went sideways.

The order I would work is set by the earned-snap column rather than by my judgment about which chapters read worst.

**Chapter 9 first.** It is at 2.6%, the lowest in the book, with 43 body-prose runs and 40.8% short sentences. It is also the last thing the reader reads and the chapter that has to sell the deck, so its register carries disproportionate weight.

**Chapter 4 second.** 5.9% earned, 42.0% short — the highest short-sentence load in the manuscript — and 37 body runs. It is also where Section 4 does the most procedural work, which is exactly where O1 applies.

**Chapter 8 third**, at 6.0% with 29 body runs. **Chapter 2 fourth**, at 5.8% with 11 body runs, and it is already open for the Section 1 rebuild, so the register pass rides along with work that is already scheduled.

That is four chapters and roughly 120 sites. Chapters 3, 5, 6 and 7 sit between 6.9% and 12.2% and do not get a register pass this week. **Chapter 1 does not get touched at all.** It is the control.

Within each chapter I would take the enumerations first, because they are the fastest and they are the ones that save words, and I would take the dramatic fusions second and stop when the day stops.

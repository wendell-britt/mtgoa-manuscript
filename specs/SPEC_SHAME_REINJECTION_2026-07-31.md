## 2. The finding that changes the job

**Shame is not missing from the book. Shame is three of the seven daemons, and
the book never says the word.**

| Daemon | Where | What it is, restated | Shame's role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Controller** | `ch3:666` | *"It blocks with a verdict about you, and it has six of them, the same six every time: I'm not good enough. I'm not ready. I don't belong. I'm insignificant. I'm not worthy. I'm not capable."* | **The voice.** Those six are shame's whistle-calls, printed in full, in a table, with replacements — under another name. |
| **The Victim** | `ch7:497` | *"If your legitimacy comes from having been harmed, then having caused harm is not a mistake you can absorb and repair. It voids the ledger… a part of you that cannot afford a finding is a part of you that will not run the audit."* | **The alibi.** The reason the audit never runs is that shame is what a finding would cost. |
| **The Damaged Self** | `ch8:501` | *"seventh in line, one gate before the center… decides whether you are built correctly for any of this."* | **The identity claim.** `ch5:408`'s *"therefore I am broken"* is this daemon's whole sentence. |

The two uses of *integrity* in the body sit on exactly the two daemons
Wendell named, unprompted, four hours before this measurement:

- `ch3:644` — the Controller at its best is *"the referee of your game"*; the
  chapter later calls it *"the referee that holds your integrity when holding it
  costs you."*
- `ch7:497` — the Victim *"makes that feel like integrity rather than what it
  actually is: a solvency requirement."*

**Integrity is already the named payoff of the Controller and the Victim.** The
lesson Wendell says the boggart teaches is the lesson those two chapters already
teach. It is thin — 14 uses, none in ch4 or ch8 — and it is seated.

**Consequence for scope: this is naming, not insertion.** The book has been
running on shame for nine chapters under three aliases. Reinjection means saying
the word over machinery that already works, once, where it is load-bearing.

## 3. The boggart move is already canon

`marginalia/specs/HUMOR_GRID.md:26`:

> **The daemon is the butt until it's an ally.** A trained daemon stops being
> funny because the gap closes — comedy lives in the mismatch between script and
> situation, and an ally is a daemon whose script now fits. Which makes humor a
> **diagnostic**: if you can still get a laugh out of a part, it's still wild.

That is *riddikulus*, stated as the book's own mechanism, three days before the
word boggart entered this repo. The laugh is not decoration on the alchemy. **The
laugh is the instrument that tells you whether the alchemy has happened.**

The book has never pointed it at Wendell.

**Do not import the word.** *Boggart* is a borrowed noun from outside the
ontology and the book has its own: daemon, wild, script, gap. The move goes in;
the vocabulary stays the book's.

## 4. Why "protecting Jordan" is the actual defect

The current handling protects her in a measurable way: she is never left alone in
it. Every instance hands her the exit in the same breath.

The cost: **a reader who is shown only converted shame concludes the conversion
is easy, fails to do it, and reads her failure as evidence about herself** —
which is the Damaged Self's exact claim, arrived at by way of the book's own
gentleness. Sanding shame down is how the book manufactures the daemon it spends
Chapter 8 disarming.

**Drawing her in rather than protecting her** has one mechanism, and it is not
"say more about shame." It is: **the author goes first, at full cost, and laughs.**
She is not handled. She is shown somebody survive it out loud. The Clown cell of
the Humor Grid — *a process running unattended in me* — is the only cell that can
do this, and per `SPEC_HUMOR_PASS_2026-07-31.md` **0 of 19 grid specimens have
landed in the manuscript.** The comedy pass and the shame pass are one job.

## 5. The design — four placements, in priority order

### S1 · Name it once, in ch2, as substrate rather than an eighth daemon (P1)

`ch2` teaches the seven and contains **zero** uses of the word. One paragraph
after the seven are introduced, before `ch2:388` turns the gang into the party.

The claim: shame is not one of the seven and is not one of the five channels
(Metal/Fear, Water/Sadness, Wood/Joy, Fire/Anger, Earth/Neutrality). It is what
three of the seven are employed to prevent you feeling. This is consistent with
Wendell's own line — *"shame is the experience of dissatisfaction itself"* — and
with `ch3:341`, where shame is classed a **secondary** reaction. Naming it as
substrate rather than channel is the only placement the existing ontology permits.

**Risk if skipped:** the Controller's six verdicts stay unnamed, and the reader
who recognises them has no word for what she just recognised.

### S2 · The Clown passage, ch7 (P0 — this is the ask)

Ch7 is the Victim's chapter, already carries *"the shame of being a learner
instead of an expert"* at `ch7:250`, and holds 23 of the book's 29 uses of
*dissatisfaction*. It is where the word already almost lives.

The material is Wendell's own, from his 3-2-1, and needs no invention: the course
built and sold before he had learned the subject; four years of the book; three
cities; the job list; the class that stopped when he asked them to 3-2-1 the dead
self; and the line that is the whole joke — *"I never took my own course."*

The Controller and the Victim run the bit together, which is the structural
reason it belongs here and not in the acknowledgements:

- The **Controller** said *you're not ready* — and it was correct. He wasn't.
- The **Victim** said *but look what I've paid* — six years, three cities, the
  smoothie job — and that was correct too.
- Between a true verdict and a true invoice, **the audit never runs.** That is
  `ch7:497` demonstrated on the author instead of asserted about the reader.

The turn is integrity, and integrity here has a precise definition the book can
support: **shame told the truth about the gap; the lie was that the gap
disqualified him from closing it in public.** Running the audit anyway, out loud,
with the verdict still talking, is `ch3`'s Shaman move performed by the author.

### S3 · One line in ch8, where integrity reads zero (P2)

`ch8:501` — the Damaged Self, *"whether you are built correctly for any of this."*
Ch8 has zero uses of both *shame* and *integrity*. The Damaged Self is shame's
identity claim and the chapter never names either the feeling or its antidote.
One sentence, in the existing Section 5 structure.

### S4 · Ch9 inherits it (P2, blocked on the ch9 rewrite)

Per `specs/SPEC_CH9_REWRITE_V1_2026-07-31.md`, ch9's new build asks Jordan to
design from her own six lines. Her lines were written about failures. **The
diagnostic from `HUMOR_GRID.md` is the right instrument to hand her:** read the
six, and mark the one you can still get a laugh out of — that one is still wild,
and it is the one the design is for. This costs ch9 two sentences and converts
the humor doctrine into a reader instrument.

## 6. What is out of scope, deliberately

- **No eighth daemon.** Seven is locked in `DAEMON_CHAPTER_LEDGER` (2026-07-22)
  and the seven-gate sequence is recited in five chapters. Adding one breaks
  `ch3:642`, `ch4:543`, `ch5:490`, `ch6:326`, `ch7:478`, `ch8:501`.
- **No sixth channel.** Shame is not an emotion in this system's terms; it is the
  secondary reaction Clean strips.
- **No word `boggart` in the manuscript.** §3.
- **No new shame content in ch1.** Ch1 is the promise chapter and A1/A2/A3 are
  open against it already; loading a fourth claim there is how promises get made
  that the book cannot pay.

**The axis.** What the definition opens on: **the part's own dignity** (noble, silent, essential) ↔ **the reader's exposure** (she has been out of range of the damage that relationship costs). # Option A — the part first

You met the Damaged Self deep in the Forest, seventh in line, closest to the center. The Protector decides whether you go in at all. The Controller decides how you're allowed to behave once you're inside. The Skeptic decides whether what you're reacting to was ever real. The Fixer/Healer decides whether what is in front of you holds up well enough to be given yourself to. The Emotional Body decides whether a feeling completes. The Victim decides what your having paid entitles you to. The Damaged Self takes what none of them can.

What the Protector leaves unguarded lands on it. So does whatever falls outside the Controller's standard, and whatever the Fixer cannot repair or hold in place. It takes what fear misses, what anger cannot carry past an obstacle, what sadness cannot attach to anything you care about, what joy cannot delight in, what neutrality cannot hold and let go. It takes all of that and is undefeated. It is the last line of defense for the Player self.

It does its work in silence, and allyship depends on it. Ask how it feels about the job and it says you would have to ask the Victim. The Victim gets the story. Nothing happened to the Victim. It all happened to this part, and the Victim tells it.

At the Sage's altitude the part is easy to lose. Relationship costs damage to the ego structure, and the Sage stands far enough away that most hits fall short. Whatever does land came from another Face. Without the Damaged Self, the Sage avoids every hit or takes the wrong ones.

# Option B — the reader first

Stay at a table long enough and something hits you. Someone tells you what your naming cost them. A plan you built gets taken apart in front of people. Relationship costs damage to the ego structure, and the cost comes due at the table.

The Sage is often too far away to be hit. That distance is the gift. It is also how the Sage gets out of paying. What does land at that altitude came from another Face. Without the part this chapter is about, the Sage avoids every hit or takes the wrong ones. Something has to sort them.

You met that part deep in the Forest, seventh in line, closest to the center: the Damaged Self. The Protector guards the door. The Controller sets the standard. The Skeptic tests what is real. The Fixer/Healer repairs and holds in place. The Emotional Body completes the feeling. The Victim keeps the account. Whatever they leave over lands on the Damaged Self. Fear overlooks it, anger cannot carry it past an obstacle, sadness cannot attach it to anything you care about, joy cannot delight in it, neutrality cannot hold it and let go. The Damaged Self takes it and is undefeated. It is the last line of defense for the Player self.

It does its work in silence, and allyship depends on it. The Victim tells the story of the damage. Nothing happened to the Victim.

# Option C — the part speaks, then the reader's distance

Ask the Damaged Self how it is going and it says: *Another day of taking damage.* Ask how it feels about that. *You'd have to ask the Victim. Feeling really isn't my jam.* Ask what its jam is. *I take damage.* Ask what happens when it stops. *The self dies.*

That is the whole job, and allyship depends on it. You met the Damaged Self deep in the Forest, seventh in line, closest to the center. The Protector decides whether you go in at all. The Controller decides how you're allowed to behave once you're inside. The Skeptic decides whether what you're reacting to was ever real. The Fixer/Healer decides whether what is in front of you holds up well enough to be given yourself to. The Emotional Body decides whether a feeling completes. The Victim decides what your having paid entitles you to. Whatever all of them leave over lands on the Damaged Self, from a hit nobody guarded to a feeling no channel completed. It takes that and is undefeated. It is the last line of defense for the Player self.

The Sage is often too far away to be hit. Relationship costs damage to the ego structure, and at that distance the Sage can slip out of paying. With the Damaged Self absent, the Sage avoids every hit or takes the wrong ones. With it present, something sorts them. The sorting is silent work.

The Victim tells the story. Nothing happened to the Victim. It all happened to this part.

# The tail (same in all three; the reviewer's inference, ruled separately)

This chapter takes apart one belief: that allyship runs on having the right framework. A framework promises the work can be done from out of range. The Sage with the best maps and no Damaged Self keeps all the sight and pays none of the cost.

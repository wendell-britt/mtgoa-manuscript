# 6 Game Master Analysis — the proof marks and what they ask for

**Wendell, 2026-09-09:** *"I highlighted everything that felt wrong. Your job is to identify the
patterns between everything I mark as wrong."* Then: *"Do a 6 game master analysis on the open
questions."*

in `PROOF_MARKS_CH2_CH3_2026-09-09.md` (pp.34–35 unphotographed). Seven working patterns sit at
the bottom of that file. `cadence.py` runs as review step 3g. The card cut, the Alchemist

## SHAMAN · the Body — *What to Do With What You Feel*

**Read the field before the plan.** The marks are a body reading, not a style guide. Wendell did
not run a checklist over the proof; he read it as a stranger and highlighted where the page
stopped feeling like a person talking and started feeling like a person *handling* him. That is
the signal under all seven patterns, and it is why the concrete beats stayed clean. A car
swerving, a friend three solutions deep, a woman at a council fire: the body is present in those.
The marked lines are where the author steps in front of the body to say what it meant.

**On Q1.** *"It snuck into multiple revisions"* is the tell. The author cannot see the
scaffolding because he built with it; it is furniture in his own house. A reader trips on it in
the first paragraph. The body of the reader and the body of the writer disagree, and the reader's
wins. The fix is not only to strike the labels; it is to build the check that notices them, since
the eye that wrote them will not.

**On Q4.** The Shaman rules against flattening. Wendell marked *"It was slower than that"* and
left the anaphora after it alone — *"It was the ceremonies… It was the stories…"* — because one
reads as a stall and the other reads as a fade. His highlighter already sorted the two buckets.
The detector counts both. Trust the highlighter over the count.

**On Q2.** The hedges are a body signal too: a narrator bracing. *"I have guessed wrong in both
directions"* is the Protector talking, armoring the claim before anyone can hit it. It is the same
move the book teaches the reader to catch. It belongs in the pattern list.

## CHALLENGER · the Line — *Who Pays When Nobody Says No*

**Q1 — draw the whole line or none of it.** Renaming "Urgency" and leaving *"Section 7: Recap and
Transition"* standing in eight chapters is the half-measure that let "Urgency" survive in the
first place. The line: **no heading a reader cannot use.** Numbers go. Slot-names go
("Recap and Transition," "The Practice," "The Concept" are the outline's labels for what the
slot *does*, not what the reader *finds*). The seven-slot template survives as a build discipline
the reader never sees.

**Q3 — rule it now.** Three jobs are blocked on it: the Alchemist scaffold, the superpower
appendix, and the card cut's ch9 rewrite. The scaffold in scratch says "seven" because I imported
the product's count into the book. That was wrong on its face: the book states six in ch2 and
names one per Face through ch8. The Challenger's line is that **the book does not bend to the
quiz**; the quiz is a product built after the ontology and it reconciles to the ontology, not the
reverse.

**Q4 — "hard" was chosen before the buckets were visible.** Wendell picked the hard sweep when the
choice was "hotspots only" versus "the whole back half." Now the two buckets are in view, the
honest reading of "hard" is: **every accidental pileup, no exceptions, and every deliberate
structure preserved with the reason stated.** That is harder than flattening, because it makes a
call on each one and shows the call.

**The standing note.** The 2026-09-01 panel said instrument-building was the tractable job while
the money track sat open. This week's work is different in kind: it is *editing the book the
backers are owed*. It is on the money track. It also has a date. Five weeks to W0, and the 2.0
print has to be uploaded before pre-orders open. **The edit is not open-ended.** The panel's order
(Q6) has to fit in it.

## REGENT · the Inheritance — *What You Inherited, and What You Do With It*

**Q1 — keep the discipline, strike the labels.** The seven-slot template is the inheritance that
made six parallel chapters possible: every Face gets an exile, a distortion, a concept, a
practice, a daemon, a game, a close. That is worth keeping as the frame the author writes in. It
was never meant to be read as *"Section 4: The Practice."* The Kotter word is the proof that the
frame was inherited whole and never translated for the person it was for.

**Q3 — the canon is six.** One superpower per Face, stated in ch2 (*"any of the six runs at any
altitude"*), delivered through ch8: Alchemist, Disruptor, Storyteller, Strategist, Connector,
Panoramic Seer. That is the book's inheritance. The quiz's Escape Artist and Coach are the
product's; the Panoramic Seer is the book's alone. An appendix that lists seven would be
inheriting the wrong parent.

**Q4 — the anaphora and the earned verdict are the voice's inheritance.** *"That is the myth this
chapter breaks."* *"It spoke of resilience. It spoke of the hard seasons."* Those are the book
sounding like itself. Strip them and what is left is correct and anonymous. The Regent keeps
them.

**Q5 — what the deck was for.** Strip the product and ask what it carried: a practice surface,
one draw per Face, so the reader *did* something in each chapter. That job is real. It is also
already done by the quest — a commitment with a name and a date, one per Face. The reader arrives
at ch9 holding six quests they made, not a hundred and twenty cards they never held. **The
inheritance to keep is the quest; the deck was its packaging.**

## ARCHITECT · the Design — *Fixing the Condition Instead of the Person*

**The condition, named once.** Six of the seven patterns are one condition at two scales. At
sentence scale the author manages the reader: interprets (1), concludes (2), signposts (3),
hedges (4), repeats the refrain (6). At chapter scale the author's build shows through: the
numbering, the slot labels, the deck, the "operation" and "grid" (7). **Both are the scaffolding
not struck.** Pattern 5, the nothing-word verb, is the one lexical habit and it gets its own
instrument (`light_verb.py` already covers half of it; "land" is on the list).

**Q1 — design the fix so it cannot recur.** Three moves. Strip the numbers and slot-labels
book-wide with one script. Retitle each section from its content (most already carry a
reader-facing subtitle beneath the label; promote it). Then add a headings check to the review —
no `Section N:`, no template-slot words as headings — so the build frame stays invisible in
every future revision. The check is the part that answers *"it snuck into multiple revisions."*

**Q2 — the test that settles it.** A pattern stands if its instances survive with the "and"
removed. Signposts: *"This chapter is the threshold map." "The deeper truth is this:" "Hold these
three words, because the whole book turns on them."* No "and" in any of them; marked anyway.
Hedges: *"It is not one." "I have not solved it. I teach here anyway."* Same. **Both are real.**
They are also both the root condition, so the design is to fold them into the one rule rather
than run two more instruments: *does this sentence handle the reader, or hand them the material?*

**Q4 — locator minus exclusion list.** The detector finds density. The deliberate structures —
anaphora runs, the four-domain markers, the five-move markers, the earned verdicts — go on an
explicit exclusion list that Wendell can see and edit. Rework = hotspots minus that list. The
list is the artifact that keeps the rework from Goodharting the count.

**Q5 — replace the generator with the artifact.** The deck was a *generator* (five moves × four
domains × six Faces) the author used to produce content. Readers do not need generators; they
need what they accumulate. By ch9 the reader has a sheet (a row per chapter) and six quests.
ch9's *"the deck starts earning its keep here"* becomes *"the six quests start earning their keep
here"* — a synthesis of what the reader actually made. That is a rewrite of ch9's capstone, and it
is its own job, scoped separately from the ch3–ch8 cuts.

## DIPLOMAT · the Table — *Why Being Good at Care Is Only Half of It*

**Who is at this table.** A stranger who paid for the book, and an author who built a system so
careful that he keeps reaching across the table to make sure the stranger got it. Every marked
line is that reach. The care is real and it is the problem: the reader is not being trusted to
hold the material, so the material never quite lands in their hands.

**Q1.** The headings are the author telling the reader where they are in *his* outline. A reader
wants to know where they are in the *argument*. Retitle from the argument.

**Q2.** The signposts manage the reader's path; the hedges manage the reader's trust. Both are the
author at the table doing the reader's work for them. Real patterns, same root, and the Diplomat's
addition: the hedge costs more than the signpost, because it asks the reader to reassure the
author. *"I have not solved it. I teach here anyway."* puts the reader in the chair the book is
supposed to be taking them out of.

**Q3.** The quiz and the book meet at exactly one place: the launch pre-work, where a stranger is
sorted by the quiz and then opens the book. The two must agree *there*. The appendix is the
handshake. It lists the book's six and says, in one line, that the quiz measures a product-side
typology that maps onto them — so the reader who arrives as an "Escape Artist" is not told the
book has no such person.

**Q4.** Show the deliberate list. Wendell chose "hard" without seeing what hard would take away.
He should see the exclusion list before the sweep runs, and overrule any line on it. That is the
table's rule: no one loses a line they did not get to see leave.

## SAGE · the Board — *Seeing the Whole Board Without Leaving the Table*

**The board.** One book, two chapters marked end to end, seven patterns, and every one of them
reduces to a single sentence: **the scaffolding was never struck.** The author built with
Kotter, with a card generator, with an outline, with a habit of explaining each move as he made
it — and shipped the building with the scaffolding on. The reader's eye sees the poles. That is
what "felt wrong" was, page after page.

**Q1.** Strike all of it, book-wide, in one pass, under one rule, with one check that keeps it
struck. Doing "Urgency" alone treats the symptom the eye happened to catch.

**Q2.** Real. Fold 3 and 4 into the root rather than into the instrument list; the count of
patterns matters less than the one rule they share.

**Q3.** Six. Alchemy is universal and plays on every superpower; the Alchemist is the one who
leads with it; the Panoramic Seer belongs to the Sage and to no quiz. The product reconciles to
the book. The panel recommends; Wendell rules.

**Q4.** Rework the accidental, keep the craft, and publish the exclusion list. The risk on the
board is Goodhart — a rework that reads as improvement on the counter and as flattening on the
page — and the exclusion list is the only guard against it.

**Q5.** The deck was the last scaffold standing, and ch9 leans on it. Replace it with what the
reader built: the sheet and the six quests. Scope the ch9 capstone as its own rewrite.

**Q6 — what the panel refuses.** It does not apply a manuscript change. It does not rule
six-versus-seven in Wendell's place. It does not collapse an anaphora the highlighter left
alone. It does not start the cadence sweep before the exclusion list is approved. It does not
touch the Controller contradiction (C1) in the same pass as the cadence — that is a content
ruling and it gets its own decision.

## Where the six converge, and where they do not

**Converge, all six:**
- **Q1 — strike the scaffolding book-wide.** Numbers and slot-labels go; sections retitle from
  their content; a headings check is added to the review so it stays struck. "Urgency" alone
  would repeat the mistake that let it survive.
- **Q2 — the signposts and hedges are real patterns**, proven by the instances that carry no
  "and." Fold both into the root rule rather than adding instruments.
- **Q3 — six.** The book's canon, one per Face. The quiz reconciles to the book at the appendix.
  Recommendation only; the ruling is Wendell's.
- **Q4 — rework the accidental, keep the craft, and show the exclusion list first.**
- **Q5 — ch9's capstone becomes the six quests and the sheet.** Its own rewrite.

**Do not converge:**
- **How much the hedges cost.** The Diplomat rates the hedge the most expensive pattern (it makes

- **The date.** The Challenger holds that five weeks to W0 turns this edit into a scheduled job

  properly rather than on time. The order below takes the Challenger's date and puts the ch9

## Round 2 — run to consensus, on Wendell's instruction

**Wendell, 2026-09-09:** *"If there are still ambiguities run the 6 game master panel until
consensus."* Round 1 converged on the rulings. What remained was execution-level, plus a fact the
first round did not have. Round 2 closes them.

**The fact.** `marginalia/compile.py` locates the treatise seam with `## Section 4`, and
`marginalia/insertions.py` anchors margin notes to `## Section N` text. `instruments/typeset.py`
already treats the italic H3 under each H2 as that section's *subtitle*, not a navigation entry.
Strip the labels naïvely and the round-trip (book step 6) breaks.

**Which headings are template, and which are content — the rule, unanimous.** A heading is a
slot label if its text is identical across three or more chapters. That is the outline naming the
slot's job, not the reader's content. By that test: *The Exile, The Distortion, The Concept, The
Practice, The Game, Recap and Transition* are template (seven or eight chapters each). *The
Controller, Up Close* and its siblings vary by daemon and are content. ch2's names (*The Infinite
Arcade, The Forest, Origin Story, The Six Guides…*) are content. *Urgency* fails the repetition
test and is struck by name: the one Kotter word.

**What replaces a template label.** Each template H2 already carries an italic H3 subtitle written
for that chapter (*Why the Shaman Left the Village*, *What Winning Looks Like at the Shaman's
Altitude*). Promote the subtitle to the H2; drop the H3. Content headings keep their subtitle and
lose only the number. Nothing is written new; the reader-facing name was already on the page,
one line down.

**The anchor — the Architect's answer, adopted by all six.** The build keeps its skeleton, and the
reader never sees it: an invisible `<!-- SECTION N -->` comment above each H2. The seam regex and
the insertion anchors rekey to the comment. Gate, marginalia extraction and the round-trip ignore
it because it is not a marginalia kind. The Shaman's point stands — the check is the part that
keeps it struck — so `headings.py` flags numbered headings, template labels repeated across
chapters, and Kotter words, and runs in the review.

**The two Round-1 splits, closed.** The hedges' cost: the Diplomat's weighting is adopted for
*priority within the cadence rework* (hedges before signposts when both are in one paragraph);
the fix is the same. The date: the Challenger's stop line is adopted as written below.

**Consensus reached. The panel acts on it.**

## Round 4 — Q3 corrected on the source

Round 1 ruled "six" against a table that put the **Panoramic Seer** down as the Sage's
superpower and treated the quiz's *Escape Artist* and *Coach* as product-only names. The source
says otherwise. ch8:617: *"the superpower has two halves. The **Escape Artist** is the capacity to
get out of a game… The **Coach** is what the exit makes available."* The Panoramic Seer is one of
the Sage's five *modes*, not its superpower.

So the book's superpower names are exactly the quiz's seven, spread across six Faces: Alchemist
(Shaman), Disruptor (Challenger), Storyteller (Regent), Strategist (Architect), Connector
(Diplomat), Escape Artist and Coach (Sage). ch2's *"any of the six"* counts superpowers, one per
Face; the Sage's is one superpower with two halves. **The count question dissolves: six
superpowers, seven names, and the quiz sorts by the seven.** The appendix lists them that way and
says so in one line; no reconciliation is needed on the quiz side beyond that sentence. The
Alchemist's scaffold names its category without a count.

**The stop line the Challenger insists on:** items 1–3 before the pre-order date is set; 4–6
before the 2.0 file is uploaded; 7 can ride into a later printing if it must. The panel would
rather ship a book with one reconciled mechanism outstanding than hold the print for it.

## Structural and pedagogical audit, MTGOA, 2026-07-28

---

## §0 THE ONE-LINE ANSWER

The teaching layer is complete and the applying layer has a hole in it. Every chapter tells Jordan why the face is needed, teaches the theory, names five moves, and points her at the deck. Across all 95,075 words the book never once hands her a situation and asks her to choose which face or which move it needs. It teaches six kinds of judgment and never tests one.

That is the gap between "learn everything they are supposed to" and "apply it to situations." The first half is built. The second half is assumed.

---

## §1 THE FOUR THINGS I MEASURED, AND WHY THOSE FOUR

The profile says Jordan will skim theory, will not skip a story, and will stop for a named move with a practice. It says she drops the book at jargon without translation, claims without practice, and moralizing without self-deprecation. Those are behaviours, so I measured behaviours rather than impressions.

**Term debt.** For fifty-six canonical terms I found first *use* and first *definition* by line, and flagged every term used before it was taught. That is the jargon-without-translation trigger made countable.

**The chain check.** Every chapter against your four-part standard: why it is needed for effective allyship, the theory, the basic moves she performs, and the application to the chapter's quests and the deck's moves. Section word counts, move counts, deck-block presence.

**Worked examples per move.** All thirty-five moves in Chapters 3 through 9, scored for a labelled example block, a scripted line she can say, and a concrete scene. That is claims-without-practice made countable.

**The transfer test.** Every place in the book where Jordan is given a situation and asked to select the face, the move, or the card. That is the promise in your question.

A correction on the first instrument before the findings: my first pass flagged the six faces and the seven daemons as used-before-defined. Both were false. Chapter 1 line 178 glosses all six faces inside one running sentence, and Chapter 2 lines 252 through 283 gives every daemon a Job, an ally form, a demon form, and a moment. The detector wanted a heading and the manuscript does the work in prose. The numbers below are from the corrected run.

## §2 WHAT HOLDS

The why layer is present everywhere and it is generous. Sections 1 and 2 run 1,238 words in Chapter 3, 1,303 in Chapter 4, 2,067 in Chapter 5, 1,214 in Chapter 6, 1,196 in Chapter 7, 2,523 in Chapter 8, and 717 in Chapter 9. ## §3 THE FOUR BREAKS

### Break 1 — There is no transfer test anywhere in the book

I searched all nine chapters for any construction that stages a situation and asks the reader to choose: *which face would you play, which move does this need, what would you draw.* Two lines matched, and neither is an exercise. Chapter 8 line 163 is exposition about what the Sage sees. Chapter 9 line 124 asks *What problem am I trying to solve, and which Face does this problem actually require* as a design question inside the Player's theory, with the answer supplied in the next sentence.

Zero exercises. Six faces, thirty-five moves, a hundred and twenty cards, and the reader is never once asked to pick.

This matters more here than it would in another book, because your book's whole thesis is range. Chapter 1 line 190 states it directly: *Sometimes the friend, the coworker, the kid, the community you show up for needs a Challenger, and all you have is the Diplomat.* Diagnosing the wrong-move problem and never rehearsing the right-move choice leaves Jordan with six vocabularies and no selection procedure. She finishes the book able to describe the Regent and unable to recognise a Regent situation while she is standing in one.

The existing apply drill has the same shape and stops one step short. It says *draw one from your twenty and run the five moves.* Drawing is random and the five moves are given. The choosing is the part that never gets practised.

### Break 2 — Chapter 1 builds an artifact, promises to fill it in, and never returns to it

Chapter 1 line 194 opens `Build Your Allyship Character` and gives Jordan four slots to fill: her home face, her shadow, her myth, her quest. Line 206 then makes an explicit promise about what happens next.

> That is the sheet for now. It fills in as you play: a superpower you will only spot in motion, an autopilot pattern you will catch yourself running, a line added in every chapter ahead.

Counted across the eight chapters that follow: the phrase *character sheet* appears zero times. *Home face* appears zero times as a callback. *Your quest* appears zero times. *Autopilot* appears zero times. The Reader's Oath at line 210 is never invoked again either.

The word *quest* does keep appearing, thirty-one times, and that is the sharper version of the problem. After Chapter 1 it always means the deck artifact from `From Card to Quest` rather than the cause Jordan named on her sheet. One word carrying two meanings, with the personally-owned one abandoned at the door. Chapter 9, the chapter selling her the deeper experience, uses the word zero times.

This is the cheapest fix in the document and the highest yield. Every chapter already ends with Section 7, Recap and Transition. Seven insertions of thirty to forty words apiece, each one adding the promised line to the sheet, keeps a promise Chapter 1 made out loud and gives Jordan a running record of her own development instead of a sheet she filled in once.

Chapters 4, 5, and 6 each have an in-the-Village block that costs her something this week. The Diplomat chapter has no equivalent.

### Break 4 — The altitude ladder is load-bearing in Chapter 8 and is never taught

*Altitude* is used fifty-nine times, from Chapter 3 line 167 through Chapter 9, and the book never defines it.

That is survivable on its own, since context carries a lot of it. What is not survivable is Chapter 8 line 161, where the Sage's central capacity gets defined in terms of a ladder Jordan has never been given.

> Call it *committed seeing*: the capacity to take in all the altitudes — Red, Amber, Orange, Green, Teal — and still choose to stand somewhere.

Five terms, first and only appearance for four of them, no gloss on any. Chapter 8's thesis rests on them. The same paragraph block at line 167 imports *Big Mind* and *developmental* with no translation. Jordan has read *Daring Greatly* and possibly *Reinventing Organizations*, so she may recognise the colours and she will not be able to say what they mean. She is being asked to hold a meta-view built on a scaffold that was never delivered.

Two more term-debt items in the same neighbourhood. Chapter 8 line 169 calls something *the Vulnerable Child's gift.* The Vulnerable Child was the eighth gate and it left with the gate walk. The seven-daemon roster at Chapter 2 lines 252 through 283 does not contain it. It is now a named part with no referent, used twice, both times in Chapter 8. The joystick, which is the book's controlling image, is first used at Chapter 2 line 224 and first defined at line 312, eighty-eight lines later, with Chapter 1 calling the same thing *the controls* at lines 15 and 61.

## §5 WHAT THIS COSTS, HONESTLY

Everything in §3 and §4 adds words, and we are four days from print under a cut mandate. Here is the real arithmetic rather than a comfortable one.

| fix | words |
|---|---|
| Term debt: cut the colour ladder and the Vulnerable Child references, gloss altitude once | **−60 to −120** |
| Character-sheet thread, 7 insertions in Section 7 | +250 |
| Chapter 8's two bare moves, one example each | +240 |
| Chapter 7 in-the-Village practice block | +220 |
| Transfer drill, six situations | +900 |
| Chapter 5's two bare moves (already queued separately) | +330 |

Without the Chapter 5 rebuild, which is already on the board under its own justification, this is roughly **+1,500 words net**. Set against the Tier 1 through Tier 4 cuts in the repetition spec, it is affordable. It is not free and I am not going to present it as free.

The one item that pays for itself is the term debt. Cutting *Red, Amber, Orange, Green, Teal* from Chapter 8 line 161 and saying the same thing in the book's own vocabulary removes the largest untranslated import in the manuscript and removes words at the same time. The sentence already has a working version of itself in it: *the capacity to take in all the altitudes and still choose to stand somewhere.* The colours are the part that fails.

## §6 THE ORDER

**First, the deletions.** Chapter 8 line 161's colour ladder, Chapter 8 line 169's Vulnerable Child, Chapter 8 line 167's Big Mind if the naming ruling lands that way. These are cuts, they close the worst term debt in the book, and they touch one chapter.

**Second, the character-sheet thread.** Seven insertions, thirty to forty words each, one per Section 7. This is a single afternoon and it converts a broken promise into the book's spine. It also does the transcend-and-include work you already ruled on: each one points back to where the idea was established.

**Third, the four bare moves and Chapter 7's practice block.** Chapter 8's two moves need an example apiece. Chapter 7 needs one in-the-Village block on the pattern of Chapters 4, 5, and 6, and the obvious candidate is `Name the Field` at line 426, which already has four scripted lines and no place to spend them.

**Fourth, the transfer drill.** Six situations, each one asking which face this needs and why, each answer routing to a named move that already exists. This is the piece that closes the gap in your question, and it is also the piece most likely to be cut for time. If it goes, the book still teaches. It just does not test.

---

## §7 RULINGS I NEED

**S1. The colour ladder.** Cut *Red, Amber, Orange, Green, Teal* from Chapter 8 line 161 and carry the sentence on *altitudes* alone, or teach the ladder somewhere and pay for it. My recommendation is the cut.

**S2. The Vulnerable Child.** Both Chapter 8 references get rewritten to a daemon that is on the roster, or the Vulnerable Child gets restored to Chapter 2's seven. My recommendation is the rewrite, because restoring an eighth daemon four days out reopens something you closed deliberately.

**S3. The character-sheet thread.** Seven insertions or none. Doing three is worse than doing zero, because a promise kept intermittently reads as a promise forgotten.

**S4. The transfer drill.** Six situations, one per face, or none. If yes, a second call follows: does it live distributed across the chapters, or consolidated once in Chapter 9 where the reader has all six faces available to choose between? My recommendation is consolidated, because a transfer test only works when there is something to transfer *from*, and before Chapter 8 there is only one candidate answer.

**S5. Chapter 9's missing performable block.** Reflection Prompts stand as the right instrument for a chapter selling the deeper experience, or Chapter 9 needs a do-it-now block like every other chapter. Your call, and I lean toward leaving it, given your ruling that the last chapter sells rather than drills.

**S6. The Example convention.** Chapters 7 and 8 adopt the labelled `**Example:**` block used by Chapters 3, 4, 5, 6, and 9, or the convention is declared optional. If it is optional, Chapters 7 and 8 are consistent and I stop flagging it.
